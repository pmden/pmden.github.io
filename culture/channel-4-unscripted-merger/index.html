Broadcasting10 Sep 2026

Channel 4 folds three teams into one. Four of six rebuilds cost a commissioner.

The broadcaster is merging documentaries, reality and specialist factual commissioning while cutting 340 jobs, more than a quarter of its staff.

4 min read

What happened

Channel 4 plans to merge three of its biggest unscripted commissioning departments, documentaries and factual entertainment, reality and entertainment, and specialist factual, as part of the 340 job cuts it confirmed on 9 September; the teams between them hold The Great British Bake Off, Married at First Sight, 24 Hours in Police Custody and The Piano.

(Deadline, 10 Sep 2026)

Channel 4 told staff on 9 September that it will cut 340 roles, about 28% of its 1,270 employees, before the end of the year, with a consultation to begin shortly and the rest of the strategy to follow in the coming months (BBC, 9 Sep 2026). The detail that decides what gets made emerged a day later: three unscripted commissioning departments, documentaries and factual entertainment, reality and entertainment, and specialist factual, are to be folded into one (Deadline, 10 Sep 2026). Those three teams commission Bake Off, Married at First Sight, 24 Hours in Police Custody and The Piano.

The merger lands on a commissioning operation that is already half rebuilt. Chief content officer Ian Katz announced his exit in April, and in July Channel 4 set out the structure that replaces him from November: a director of programmes alongside two newly created director roles for Louisa Compton and Kiran Nataraja (Deadline, 2 Jul 2026). Candidates for the director of programmes job were sitting first-round interviews with chief executive Priya Dogra in the week the cuts were announced (Guardian, 8 Sep 2026).

Almost nothing on Channel 4 is made in-house, so its staff are gatekeepers rather than programme-makers. The broadcaster ran a £640m programming budget last year, £480m of it on British originals, against a third consecutive annual loss (Guardian, 8 Sep 2026). Dogra has told staff the redundancies are meant to push more of Channel 4's £1bn income on screen (BBC, 9 Sep 2026). Collapsing three genre desks into one changes which ideas get a hearing, and the record says it usually changes who is sitting at the desk.

The Pattern

Announcements since 2020 in which a UK broadcaster merged, closed or stripped out commissioning departments, newest first. Held means a named commissioning editor, genre head or content director at that broadcaster was reported leaving within 90 days of the announcement.

2026
24 June 2026: the BBC said it would close its Entertainment, Factual Entertainment and Events and Daytime commissioning teams in the autumn and create three new heads of commissioning under director of entertainment Ed Havard; no commissioning departure has been reported in the 78 days since.
2026
14 May 2026: Sky rebuilt its entertainment commissioning structure under a newly created head of entertainment commissioning, Shirley Jones, and said commissioning editor Barbara Lee would leave in October after 14 years.
2024
7 January 2024: Channel 4 announced its deepest cuts in 15 years and brought television drama under the Film4 commissioning lead; five weeks later drama boss Caroline Hollick and E4 head Karl Warner were reported to be leaving under the consultation.
2022
18 May 2022: the BBC created a single unscripted commissioning directorate under Kate Phillips; eleven weeks later Storyville commissioning editor Philippa Kowarsky left the corporation after a year.
2021
27 January 2021: Channel 4 pulled all commissioning under Ian Katz as chief content officer and dispersed its features slate to other departments; head of features and formats Sarah Lazenby, who commissioned The Great British Bake Off, left the same day after nearly six years.
2020
8 December 2020: the BBC abolished the BBC One, BBC Two and BBC Four controller roles and handed commissioning power to genre directors; no controller departure was reported before the replacement portfolio editors were named in March 2021.
4 of 6
Four of the six commissioning rebuilds by UK broadcasters since 2020 were followed within 90 days by a named commissioning or content executive leaving: Sky in 2026, Channel 4 in 2024 and 2021, and the BBC in 2022. The two that were not are both BBC exercises in redistributing genre power, in December 2020 and June 2026. Channel 4 is four for four on its own record, in 2021 and 2024. One caveat sits on the count: the BBC's June 2026 window does not close until 22 September, so it is scored on the public record to 10 September and could still turn.

Four of the six commissioning rebuilds since 2020 produced a named departure inside 90 days. Both exceptions are the BBC, where genre power was redistributed rather than removed.

The Read

The weighted case on the exit market is yes. Channel 4 is merging three departments into one, which leaves at least two genre heads without a department, and its own two previous rounds both produced named departures inside the window: Sarah Lazenby on the day in 2021, Caroline Hollick and Karl Warner five weeks in during 2024. The consultation that produced the 2024 exits ran 45 days, and this one is larger. The 72% start sits below the four-in-six base rate only because a merger can be settled by promotion rather than exit, which is what the BBC did twice.

The honest counter-case is that Channel 4 has already taken its senior scalp. Katz went in April and the July structure promoted Compton and Nataraja rather than replacing them, so the broadcaster may absorb the merger into the November reshuffle and let redundancies fall below commissioning-editor level, where the trade press does not name people. The prediction also fails on reporting rather than fact: if the consultation runs quietly to Christmas, no exit will be confirmed inside 90 days even if several are agreed. The director-of-programmes market is the softer of the three, because Dogra controls the timing entirely and has said the wider strategy will come in the coming months.
