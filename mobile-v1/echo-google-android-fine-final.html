Google's last appeal dies. The claims queue forms.

Europe's top court dismissed Google's final appeal against the record €4.1bn Android fine, ending an eight-year fight and opening the way to damages claims.

4 min read

What happened

The European Court of Justice dismissed Google's appeal against the €4.1bn ($4.7bn) Android fine, leaving the record penalty permanently in place.

(Court of Justice of the EU, 2 Jul 2026)

The European Court of Justice ruled on 2 July that Google's appeal against the European Commission's Android penalty should be dismissed, leaving the €4.1bn fine standing in full. The decision is legally binding and final: Google has no further right of appeal. The case dates to 2018, when Brussels concluded that Google had abused Android's market power by requiring phone makers who wanted the Play Store to pre-install Google Search and Chrome on every device Court of Justice of the EU, 2 July 2026.

The judgment ends the largest antitrust penalty the EU has ever imposed, and the last of Google's three landmark Brussels cases still in the courts. The General Court had already trimmed the fine from €4.34bn to €4.125bn in 2022 while upholding the Commission's core findings; the top court has now confirmed that outcome Bloomberg, 2 July 2026.

The finality matters beyond the fine itself. A sealed infringement judgment is the strongest foundation available for follow-on damages claims by rivals and consumers in national courts, and litigation funders moved quickly after Google's Shopping judgment became final in 2024. Meanwhile a separate front remains open: the Commission fined Google €2.95bn over its ad-tech business in September 2025 and demanded remedies to end its conflicts of interest — a review that was still unresolved when the Android judgment landed European Commission, 5 September 2025.

The Pattern

Big EU competition penalties usually survive the courts — but not always. The final-appeal record of landmark fines since 2022 favours Brussels without being unbroken, and the exceptions were not small ones.

2024
The ECJ gave final judgment upholding Google's €2.4bn Shopping fine, closing the EU's first big Google case after seven years.
2024
On the same day, the ECJ ruled Apple must pay €13bn in Irish back taxes, reversing a lower-court win for Apple and the Irish state.
2024
The ECJ dismissed the Commission's appeal in the Intel case, leaving the annulment of Intel's €1.06bn fine in place — a rare, total defeat for Brussels.
2022
The General Court annulled Qualcomm's €997m fine outright, citing procedural failings in the Commission's investigation.
2022
The General Court upheld the core of the Android decision but cut the fine from €4.34bn to €4.125bn — the judgment the ECJ has now confirmed.

Three of the five landmark EU competition penalties that reached final judgment since 2022 survived. Intel and Qualcomm walked away with annulments. The pattern favours Brussels, but it is genuinely split — which is why the Android outcome was never a formality.

The Read

The weighted case: follow-on claims arrive fast after final judgments. Sweden's PriceRunner sought €2.1bn from Google while the Shopping case was still on appeal, and a sealed judgment removes the biggest legal risk for claimants and their funders. With the Android infringement now beyond challenge, a filing inside five weeks is more likely than not — claimant firms have had four years since the General Court ruling to prepare. On ad-tech, the sober call is inertia: Commission remedy reviews of this size run through market testing, and August is Brussels' dead month. 'No decision' carries the weight.

The honest counter-case: damages claims are announced on lawyers' timetables, not news cycles, and July-August is peak court vacation across the EU. A claim being prepared is not a claim being filed, and five weeks is a short window for something funders may prefer to launch in September with full press attention. On ad-tech, the wildcard is political: transatlantic trade friction has accelerated Commission decisions before, and a rejection announcement before recess would be a statement of intent. The 22 per cent on escalation is not noise.
