Scotland31 Aug 2026

Scotland asks for its own visa for the fifth time. The first four got nothing.

The Scottish Government published proposals on 29 August for a Scottish Visa, a Rural Visa and a Scottish Graduate Visa.

4 min read

What happened

The Scottish Government publishes proposals for a Scottish Visa, a Rural Visa and a Scottish Graduate Visa, and invites the UK Migration Minister to discuss them.

(Scottish Government, 29 Aug 2026)

Business Minister Tom Arthur published the paper on 29 August, setting out a Scottish Visa, a Rural Visa and a Scottish Graduate Visa, and said he had invited the UK Migration Minister to constructive discussions so that the immigration system is tailored to Scotland's needs (Scottish Government, 29 Aug 2026). The paper names the closure of the adult social care visa route as a risk to workforce supply in rural and island communities, and submitting the proposals was one of the government's hundred-day commitments.

The Scottish Visa as now drafted would be an additional route inside the UK system: holders would need a Scottish tax code and residence in Scotland, the Scottish Government rather than an employer would nominate applicants, and the visa could run five years and three months to give a clear path to indefinite leave to remain (Scottish Government, Migration: supporting the future of Scotland's economy and communities, 29 Aug 2026). The demographic case uses National Records of Scotland figures: populations fell in 20 of Scotland's 32 council areas between mid-2024 and mid-2025, 21.0 per cent of the population was 65 or over at mid-2025 against 16.3 per cent twenty years earlier, and the share aged 0 to 15 fell from 18.2 to 15.8 per cent (Scottish Government, 29 Aug 2026).

None of that changes where the power sits. Immigration is reserved, so a Scottish visa exists only if a UK minister creates one, and the paper is a request rather than a plan. That is what makes it countable: the ask is published, dated and specific, and so is the answer whenever it comes.

The Pattern

Action type: the Scottish Government publishes a formal proposal to the UK Government for a Scotland-specific migration route or devolved migration powers. Four cases since 2018 with the UK position now on the record. The test is single: did a Scotland-specific immigration route come into existence within 18 months. The August 2026 paper is not in the count.

2025
Migration: meeting Scotland's needs, published 22 January 2025, proposed a Scottish Graduate Visa giving international graduates of Scottish institutions an additional two years to reach the skilled worker salary threshold, and asked the UK Government to develop it jointly. The UK immigration white paper of 12 May 2025 instead shortened the UK-wide graduate route to 18 months.
2022
The Rural Visa pilot proposal of 2022 asked for a community-driven, employer-based route for rural and island areas delivered jointly by the two governments and local partners. The Conservative UK Government blocked the pilot, and in October 2024 a UK Government source said the new administration was not at all considering a Scottish visa system.
2020
Migration: helping Scotland prosper, published 27 January 2020, proposed a Scottish Visa whose holders would remain resident in Scotland and pay tax locally, with the Scottish Government assessing applications and a route to settlement. No such route was created; the UK Government's position in October 2024 was that it was not considering one.
2018
Scotland's population needs and migration policy, a discussion paper published 7 February 2018, argued that Scotland's migration needs were distinct and explored options for devolving powers over migration to the Scottish Parliament. No devolution and no Scotland-specific route followed.
0 of 4
None of the four decided proposals since 2018 produced a Scotland-specific route. Three were put to Conservative governments; the fourth, in January 2025, was put to a Labour government whose white paper four months later cut the graduate route to 18 months rather than extending it. Four cases: History is not shown below six, and most of the record describes one party's revealed position; the 2025 case is the one test of the other.

0 of the 4 decided proposals produced a Scotland-specific route. One was blocked outright, one was answered by a white paper moving the other way, and two produced no route and no published answer that this record can find.

What this pattern excludes

The August 2026 paper itself
No UK Government response to it had been published on gov.uk as of 31 August 2026. Scoring an undecided case would be scoring a guess, and the October 2024 position is an answer to an earlier proposal, not to this one.
Scotland's Migration Service and the Addressing Depopulation Action Plan
Initiatives the Scottish Government runs within devolved powers (gov.scot, 29 Aug 2026). They are not migration routes and needed no UK decision.
Fresh Talent, the post-study work scheme of the 2000s
It predates the window. The 2018 paper cites it as the one Scotland-specific scheme that did exist, which is why the count starts after it.

The Read

The near market is about whether Whitehall engages in writing, and the record says it mostly has not. The 2020 and 2022 proposals drew a stated position rather than a document, and the 2025 one was answered by omission and then by a white paper moving the other way. An invitation to a minister is easier to accept in a meeting than in a publication, which is why the gov.uk market sits low.

The November market is where the base rate does most of its work and where it should be held loosest. None of four is a thin record, and three of the four answers came from one party. What has not changed is the structural reason: a route defined by where someone lives requires enforcement of where they stay, and the August paper's answer, a Scottish tax code as the residence test, is the same one offered in 2020. Until a UK minister publishes something that engages with that mechanism rather than with the principle, the pattern is the better guide.
