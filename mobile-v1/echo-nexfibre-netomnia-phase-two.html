Competition31 Aug 2026

Britain's fibre challengers merge. Phase 2 has a December date.

The CMA is running a full Phase 2 inquiry into nexfibre's purchase of Netomnia and its sister networks, with a statutory deadline of 15 December. Rival operators have filed against it and the merging parties answered in mid-August.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

The CMA publishes the merging parties' joint response to its areas of focus, the latest filing in the Phase 2 inquiry into nexfibre's acquisition of Substantial.

CMA, 14 Aug 2026
2 · The event

Four members, one deadline, no minister

nexfibre, the joint venture of Liberty Global, Telefónica and InfraVia, is acquiring Substantial, the holding company for the Netomnia, brsk and YouFibre altnet networks. The CMA accepted a fast-track reference request and sent the deal to a Phase 2 inquiry on 1 July 2026, appointing a group of four under Cyrus Mehta and publishing its terms of reference the same day (CMA, 2026). Interim undertakings freezing integration were accepted on 24 July.

The areas of focus document went out on 7 July with a response deadline of 20 July. Filings followed from BT's Openreach, Sky, CityFibre, Grain Connect and Hyperoptic, and the CMA published the merging parties' joint response on 14 August (CMA, 14 Aug 2026). The commercial question is whether combining two of the largest challengers to Openreach removes the main competitive pressure in the areas where their footprints overlap.

The decision machinery is tightly bounded. An independent group of four decides, applying the substantial lessening of competition test under the Enterprise Act 2002, against a hard statutory deadline of 15 December 2026 that only the CMA itself can extend, and by a limited amount. There is no ministerial call-in on competition grounds and no default outcome if the group fails to report. Ofcom is consulted but holds no veto.

3 · The pattern

How UK telecoms mergers end at Phase 2

Inclusion rule, fixed before the outcome: every merger in UK telecommunications referred to a CMA Phase 2 inquiry group under the Enterprise Act 2002 since the CMA took over merger control in April 2014, up to 31 August 2026, where a final report has been published. The outcome scored is the one recorded in that final report: prohibited, cleared subject to remedies, or cleared unconditionally. Cases decided by any other authority, and cases resolved at Phase 1, fall outside the rule and are listed in the exclusions.

2016
BT / EE. Final report 15 January 2016. The group found no substantial lessening of competition anywhere in the UK and cleared the deal unconditionally.
2021
Telefónica / Liberty Global, the Virgin Media–O2 joint venture. Final report 20 May 2021. Concerns about wholesale access and backhaul were rejected and the deal was cleared unconditionally.
2022
Cellnex / CK Hutchison UK towers. Final report 3 March 2022. The group cleared the deal with remedies, requiring the sale of over 1,000 sites (GOV.UK, March 2022).
2024
Vodafone / Three. Final report December 2024. A four-to-three mobile merger was cleared with binding remedies: an eight-year network investment commitment plus time-limited retail and wholesale protections.
4 of 4
4 of 4 UK telecoms mergers that reached a CMA Phase 2 final report were cleared, and none was blocked. The base is small, and three of the four predate the current fibre overbuild question entirely. Half the set needed binding remedies to get through, so the record says more about the CMA's appetite for remedies than about the odds of unconditional clearance.

4 of 4 UK telecoms mergers taken to a CMA Phase 2 final report have been cleared. Two went through unconditionally; two were cleared only after the parties signed binding commitments.

What this pattern excludes

Telefónica / Hutchison, the O2 and Three merger prohibited in May 2016
Decided by the European Commission under the EU Merger Regulation, not by a CMA inquiry group; different decision-maker, different test and a different appeal route.
Telecoms deals cleared at Phase 1 without reference
No inquiry group is appointed and no final report is published, so there is no outcome the inclusion rule can score.
The CMA's cloud services market investigation, concluded 31 March 2026
A market investigation rather than a merger reference; it has no merging parties, no substantial lessening of competition finding and no prohibition power over a transaction.
Sabre / Farelogix, prohibited at Phase 2 in 2020
A Phase 2 prohibition, but in airline distribution technology rather than UK telecoms; it fails the sector limb of the inclusion rule.

5 · The read

The read

The weighted case is clearance with conditions. The CMA has never blocked a UK telecoms merger at Phase 2, and in the one case where it found a real problem, Vodafone and Three, it took commitments rather than prohibition. The fast-track route matters too: the parties asked to skip Phase 1 and go straight to a full inquiry, which is a move made by buyers who expect to negotiate remedies rather than argue for a clean bill. The interim undertakings accepted on 24 July keep the businesses separable, which is the practical precondition for a structural remedy if the group wants one.

The counter-case is that this deal is different in kind. BT/EE and Virgin Media–O2 were vertical or convergence deals; Vodafone/Three was a mobile consolidation with a network investment story attached. This one removes a direct rival in fixed overbuild, where the CMA's own theory of harm is easiest to state and hardest for the parties to answer, and the objectors are not fringe players but Openreach, Sky, CityFibre and Hyperoptic. That is what holds the prohibition branch at 18% rather than the near-zero the raw record would imply, and why the small size of the base should be read as a warning rather than a comfort.
