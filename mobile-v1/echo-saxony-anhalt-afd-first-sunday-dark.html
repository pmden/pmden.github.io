AfD polls above 40% in Saxony-Anhalt. In three eastern votes it has finished first once.

Saxony-Anhalt votes on Sunday 6 September with the far-right AfD at around 42% in the final polls, twenty points clear of the governing CDU.

4 min read

What happened

Germany's far-right AfD is eyeing an election breakthrough in Saxony-Anhalt on Sunday, with polls putting it around 42%, roughly twenty points ahead of the CDU that leads the state government.

(Al Jazeera, 4 Sep 2026)

Saxony-Anhalt elects its ninth Landtag on Sunday 6 September. The AfD has led every poll of the campaign and the final surveys put it at about 42%, against roughly 22% for the CDU of Minister-President Sven Schulze, 12% for Die Linke and 7% for the SPD (Al Jazeera, 4 Sep 2026). On those numbers the governing CDU-SPD-FDP coalition would fall well short of a majority and the AfD would hold close to half the seats.

The state is the first of three eastern contests in a fortnight. Mecklenburg-Vorpommern and Berlin both vote on 20 September, and the AfD leads the most recent surveys in the former by a few points over Manuela Schwesig's SPD, while Berlin is a four-way race between the CDU, Die Linke, the AfD and the Greens.

The question Sunday answers is whether a poll lead of this size survives contact with the count. Every other party has ruled out governing with the AfD, so a first place does not by itself produce an AfD-led government; it does produce a blocking minority in a state parliament for the first time, and a template for the two votes that follow (Al Jazeera, 4 Sep 2026).

The Pattern

The last full round of eastern state elections, in September 2024, gives three tests of the same question: the AfD entered each one at or near the top of the polls, and finished first in one.

2024
September 2024: Olaf Scholz's SPD took about 31% to the AfD's 29% in Brandenburg, holding first place by under two points after polls had shown the two level (Al Jazeera, 22 Sep 2024).
2024
September 2024: the CDU won Saxony with 31.9% to the AfD's 30.6%, a margin of 1.3 points, after a campaign in which the AfD had led several polls (Al Jazeera, 1 Sep 2024).
2024
September 2024: the AfD won Thuringia with 32.8% against 23.6% for the CDU, the first far-right party to top a German state election since 1949. The other parties refused to form a coalition with it (Al Jazeera, 1 Sep 2024).
2021
June 2021: at the last Saxony-Anhalt election the CDU won 37.1% and the AfD 20.8%, a 16-point gap. The final polls of that campaign had shown the two parties far closer (Al Jazeera, 4 Sep 2026).
1 of 4
In 1 of the last 4 eastern state elections the AfD finished first: Thuringia in September 2024. It lost Saxony by 1.3 points and Brandenburg by under two, and in Saxony-Anhalt's own election in 2021 it finished 16 points behind the CDU. Sunday's polling gap of twenty points is larger than any margin in the set, in either direction.

One first place in four eastern contests, and the polls overstated the AfD in 2021.

The Read

The weighted case says first place is close to settled and the real question is the number. No party in the dataset has come from twenty points down in the final polls, which is why the first-place market sits at 90%. The 40% line is a different proposition: the AfD's best result in any state election is 32.8%, and in 2021 Saxony-Anhalt's voters gave it seven points less than its polls. The market prices that shortfall as roughly even money.

The counter-case is the 2021 precedent itself, when a late CDU rally under Reiner Haseloff turned a close race into a rout. Sven Schulze is a newer and less known figure, but the mechanism that helped Haseloff, voters of other parties consolidating behind the strongest alternative, is the same one that saved the CDU in Saxony and the SPD in Brandenburg in 2024. The prediction fails if that consolidation lifts the CDU above 30% and clips the AfD to the low thirties.
