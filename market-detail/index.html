The pattern behind it

2 of 8
Two of the last eight ten-year highs carried into a higher average two months later: August 2023 (4.42 to 4.53) and March 2026 (4.78 to 5.03). The other six, October 2023, January, August and September 2025, and April and May 2026, were followed by a lower average. Across all twelve highs since September 2022 the later month was higher in 4. Highs have clustered, four in 2023 and three in the spring of 2026, and the month two on from a cluster has more often eased than pushed on.

Inclusion rule, fixed before the count: the Bank of England's monthly average ten-year nominal par yield (IUMAMNZC) from January 1990, taking every month in which the average was higher than in any of the preceding 120 months, the shape of August 2026 at 5.07 per cent. The test is single: was the monthly average two months later higher than in the month of the high, the distance from August to the Budget month. Twelve cases meet the rule, all since September 2022 because yields fell for most of the previous three decades; the last eight are shown and the full record is counted. August 2026 is the event under test and is not counted.
