G717 Jun 2026

Évian set the target. China still holds the magnets.

The G7 summit in Évian created a Critical Minerals Resilience and Production Alliance: no single outside supplier above 60% of rare earths and magnets by 2030.

3 min read

What happened

G7 leaders at Évian established a Critical Minerals Resilience and Production Alliance and a crisis platform, targeting below-60% dependence on any single outside supplier for rare earths and magnets by 2030.

(Élysée, 17 Jun 2026)

The 52nd G7 summit ran from 15 to 17 June in Évian-les-Bains, and produced nine declarations covering Ukraine, the Middle East, AI and global economic imbalances (Council of the EU, 17 June 2026). With Volodymyr Zelensky present, leaders committed to increased military support for Ukraine including air defence systems and long-range capabilities, and France pulled partner countries India, Kenya, South Korea, Brazil and Egypt into the working sessions.

The measurable commitment is on minerals. Leaders established a non-binding Critical Minerals Resilience and Production Alliance, with coordination on financing, traceability, stockpiling and recycling, and set a target of reducing dependence on any single supplier outside the G7 and partner countries to below 60% for rare earths and permanent magnets by 2030, with an ambition of 50% as soon as possible (Élysée, 17 June 2026). Traceability starts with pilots on lithium and nickel, extending to five new minerals each year.

The declaration never names China, but the sequencing does: it follows Chinese export curbs on permanent magnets that disrupted carmakers and defence suppliers and exposed the scale of the dependence. China processes roughly 90% of the world's permanent magnet output, which makes the 60% ceiling a commitment to displace around a third of current supply share in under four years.

The Pattern

G7 summits produce economic pledges every June. Whether they deliver depends less on the ambition than on the plumbing: pledges with a named financial mechanism and a hard number have a materially better record than umbrella initiatives. Four recent cases split cleanly.

2025
The Kananaskis summit adopted a Critical Minerals Action Plan. A year later it had survived a change of chair and escalated into the Évian alliance with a numeric target, a rare case of a G7 initiative gaining force over twelve months.
2024
Apulia pledged $50bn in ERA loans to Ukraine funded by profits on frozen Russian assets. The mechanism was legally novel and delivery happened: disbursements began within months and continued through 2025.
2022
The Partnership for Global Infrastructure and Investment promised to mobilise $600bn by 2027. Tracked commitments run far behind the target, at roughly a tenth of the headline figure by most counts.
2021
Build Back Better World, the Cornwall summit's infrastructure answer to Belt and Road, produced almost no capital and was quietly rebranded into PGII within a year.
2 of 4
Two of the last four headline G7 economic pledges delivered on schedule. The split is genuine and it tracks design: the ERA loans and the minerals action plan had mechanisms and owners; B3W and PGII had press releases and aggregates. The Évian alliance has a number but is explicitly non-binding, which puts it between the two camps.

Two of four recent headline G7 pledges delivered; the ones with named mechanisms and hard numbers did, the umbrella initiatives did not.

The Read

The weighted case for early follow-through is thin, and the price says so. Summit initiatives typically go quiet through the European summer, the alliance is explicitly non-binding, and nothing in the Évian texts commits anyone to a July deliverable. The historical pattern is that even the successful pledges took months to grow plumbing: the ERA loans agreed in June 2024 disbursed from December. Five weeks including August's dead zone is a demanding window for seven governments plus partners to agree membership and a programme.

The counter-case is that this initiative is unusually pre-built. It escalated from an action plan that already survived a year and a chair handover, the traceability pilots on lithium and nickel were specified in the declaration itself, and the commercial pressure is live: Chinese magnet export curbs are an ongoing cost to G7 industry, not a hypothetical. France has an incentive to show its presidency produced machinery rather than language, and partner countries recruited at Évian give it early wins to announce. If any G7 economic pledge moves before autumn, it is likelier to be this one than most.
