Echo #168Sep 4

Two world records in an hour in Zurich. The fastest ratification in the last eight took 29 days.

Alison dos Santos ran 45.80 for the 400m hurdles and Masai Russell 12.09 for the 100m hurdles at the Weltklasse on 27 August, taking 0.14 and 0.03 seconds off marks held by Karsten Warholm and Tobi Amusan. Of the eight world records World Athletics ratified in its 2025 press releases, none was confirmed within 22 days of being set.

3 min read
1 · The event

What happened

Alison dos Santos won the 400m hurdles at the Weltklasse Zurich on 27 August in 45.80, 0.14 seconds inside Karsten Warholm's record from the Tokyo Olympics, and within the hour Masai Russell won the 100m hurdles in 12.09, 0.03 inside Tobi Amusan's mark (World Athletics ) (Al Jazeera ). Audrey Werro set a Diamond League record in the 800m and Kenny Bednarek a world lead of 19.62 in the 200m on the same night.

Neither mark is yet a world record in the formal sense. World Athletics ratifies records only after the timing, wind, track and doping-control documentation is verified, and announces each batch by press release; the Zurich report itself does not mention the process (World Athletics ).

The federation's own releases give the clock. In 2025 it ratified eight records in three batches, and the gap between performance and confirmation ran from 29 days for Duplantis's 6.29m to 489 days for Wanyonyi's road mile (World Athletics ) (World Athletics ).

3 · What it means
0 of 8
Base rateIn 0 of the 8 world records ratified by World Athletics in 2025 did confirmation come within 22 days of the performance, the gap between Zurich and 18 September. The fastest was 29 days, for Duplantis's 6.29m; four of the eight took more than two months, and one, Wanyonyi's road mile, took 489 days. The process is administrative, and it does not hurry for headline marks.

How to read it

The weighted case for the first market is a no, and it is not about doubt over the marks. Eight for eight in 2025 took longer than the window allows, and the fastest case, a pole vault record at a major meeting with the federation's own timing partner, still needed four weeks. The market is priced in single digits accordingly.

The counter-case is that Zurich is a Diamond League final with full documentation on site, and the two records are the athletics story of the year; the federation could choose to move quickly. The 31 October market is where the real uncertainty sits: roughly half the 2025 cases cleared inside 65 days, and the prediction of a double ratification by then fails if either sample or paperwork follows the slower half of the set.
