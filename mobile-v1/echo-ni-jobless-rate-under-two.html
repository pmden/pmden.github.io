Northern Ireland03 Sep 2026

Northern Ireland's jobless rate is under 2 per cent. Four in ten adults are outside the labour market.

The latest Labour Force Survey puts Northern Ireland's unemployment rate at 1.96 per cent, the third consecutive print below 2 per cent. The same table puts economic inactivity at 40.7 per cent. The next release lands on 15 September.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

NISRA's Labour Market Report puts Northern Ireland unemployment at 1.96% for April to June 2026, with economic inactivity at 40.7% and 628,000 people economically inactive.

NISRA, 18 Aug 2026
2 · The event

One eighteen-thousand, one six hundred and twenty-eight thousand

The headline tables published by NISRA at 7am on 18 August 2026 record 18,000 people unemployed in Northern Ireland in the April to June quarter, an unemployment rate of 1.96 per cent, against 898,000 in employment and an employment rate of 58.2 per cent (NISRA, 18 Aug 2026). Unemployment is down 4,000 on the year. Economic inactivity is up 10,000 on the year, at 628,000 people and 40.7 per cent of those aged 16 and over.

Those two movements are not independent of each other. Someone who stops looking for work leaves the unemployment count and joins the inactivity count, which lowers one headline and raises the other in the same step. Northern Ireland has run the highest inactivity rate of the four nations for most of the period NISRA has measured, and the record high in this series is 42.4 per cent in the spring of 2009, which is 1.7 percentage points above where it stands now.

The next reading is dated. NISRA's published schedule puts the Labour Market Report, the monthly Labour Force Survey, the claimant count, redundancies and the HMRC payrolled employees figures on 15 September 2026, with the quarterly Labour Force Survey two days later. The release will cover the May to July rolling quarter, which overlaps the current one by two months.

3 · The pattern

What happens after a print below 2 per cent

Inclusion rule, fixed before the count: this pattern uses the whole published NISRA seasonally adjusted series for the unemployment rate of those aged 16 and over, running from the January to March 1995 rolling quarter to April to June 2026, which is 376 observations. The rule takes every observation below 2.0 per cent that has a successor in the series, and asks whether the next observation was also below 2.0 per cent. No episodes are selected and no window is chosen. The base is the series.

1995
The series opens at 11.2 per cent in the January to March quarter of 1995. For the next twenty-eight years it never once printed below 2 per cent.
2023
July to September 2023 is the first observation in the series below the line, at 1.90 per cent. Every sub-2 per cent reading Northern Ireland has ever recorded has come since that quarter.
2024
August to October 2024 is the record low at 1.55 per cent, inside a run of twelve consecutive prints below 2 per cent that ran from March 2024 to the February to April quarter of 2025.
2026
The rate went back above the line at 2.06 per cent in January to March 2026, then dropped below it again for three prints: 1.78, 1.85 and 1.96 per cent. The current reading is rising within that run.
14 of 16
14 of 16. The limitation is severe and has to be stated: rolling quarters overlap by two months, so consecutive observations share two thirds of their sample and are not independent trials. The number describes persistence in a smoothed series, which is real information, but it is not the same as sixteen separate coin flips and should not be read as one.

14 of the 16 sub-2 per cent observations with a successor were followed by another sub-2 per cent observation. 17 of 376 observations in the series have been below the line, and all 17 have come since 2023.

What this pattern excludes

The claimant count series
It counts people receiving a benefit rather than people meeting the survey definition of unemployment, and it is classed as experimental. Two different definitions in one base rate would make the number unreadable.
The 16 to 64 table
It gives a different rate for the same population, 2.50 per cent for April to June 2025 against 2.36 per cent on the 16 and over basis. Mixing the two would let the answer depend on which table was open.
UK-wide comparisons
The question is what a Northern Ireland print does next, not how Northern Ireland compares. A UK series would answer a different question with a different mechanism behind it.

5 · The read

The read

The unemployment market is priced off persistence rather than off any forecast. In a series where each observation shares two of its three months with the last one, a reading of 1.96 per cent does not jump to 2.3 per cent in a month without something breaking, and the published record says a sub-2 print is usually followed by another. What argues the other way is direction: the last three prints have moved up, from 1.78 to 1.85 to 1.96, and one more step of that size crosses the line.

The inactivity market is the more interesting one and it sits low for a reason. The rate has not printed above 41 per cent at any point in the last year, though it came within a twentieth of a point in the December to February quarter, and the moves in this series are usually a tenth or two rather than a third. Read the two together and the picture is a labour market that is not producing unemployed people, it is producing people who have stopped counting as being in it. That is the number the Executive's economy department has to move, and it is the one no monthly release has moved yet.
