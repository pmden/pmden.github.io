Sky wants ITV. Six weeks after comments closed, the CMA has not started the clock.

The Competition and Markets Authority opened its file on Sky's acquisition of ITV's media and entertainment business on 23 July and closed the comment window on 6 August. The statutory Phase 1 clock only begins when the authority formally commences its investigation, and it has not.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

The CMA case page for Sky's anticipated acquisition of ITV M&E Holdings Limited records that the formal Phase 1 investigation has not yet been launched, six weeks after the invitation to comment closed.

GOV.UK, 3 Sep 2026
2 · The event

Opened, consulted, and still not commenced

The case was opened on 23 July 2026, with Sky UK Limited as the acquirer and ITV M&E Holdings Limited as the target, and written representations on competition issues were due by 6 August 2026. The case page states that Phase 1 has not been formally launched and that it will be updated when the investigation commences (GOV.UK, 3 Sep 2026). Until that happens there is no statutory deadline attached to the deal at all.

That distinction is the whole of the timing question. An invitation to comment is information-gathering with no clock; the 40 working day Phase 1 period starts only when the CMA formally commences by notice to the parties, which it does once the parties have satisfied its pre-notification requirements. A merger can therefore sit in public view for months without any legal deadline being engaged, and the length of that wait is set by the authority and the parties rather than by statute.

The regulator is carrying a second large media file at the same time. The Daily Mail and General Trust acquisition of Telegraph Media Group has been open since 13 February 2026, after the Secretary of State issued a public interest intervention notice on 12 February under section 42 of the Enterprise Act 2002; the CMA delivered its report on 21 April 2026 against a deadline of 10 June for both the CMA and Ofcom, and the case remains open (GOV.UK, 2026).

3 · The pattern

How long the CMA takes to start the clock

Inclusion rule, fixed before the count: this pattern counts every merger inquiry opened by the CMA in 2026 whose case page shows both an opening date and either a Phase 1 commencement date or a wait of more than 100 days without one, as published on the CMA case finder on 3 September 2026. The test is stated in advance: was the formal Phase 1 investigation commenced within 60 days of the case being opened. Cases opened less than 60 days ago are excluded, because they cannot yet fail the test, which is why the two subjects of this Echo are not in the count.

2026
AkzoNobel and Axalta. Opened 16 April, comments closed 1 May, and as of 3 September the case page still says the formal investigation has not been launched. 140 days and no clock.
2026
nexfibre and Substantial. Opened 23 April, Phase 1 commenced 29 June, 67 days. Two days later, on 1 July, the CMA referred it straight to a Phase 2 investigation under its fast-track procedure.
2026
Co-operative Group and Southern Co-operative. Opened 4 June, Phase 1 commenced 30 July, 56 days, with a statutory decision deadline of 28 September. An initial enforcement order had been served on 23 July, a week before the clock started.
2026
E.ON and OVO. Opened 8 July, Phase 1 commenced 2 September, 56 days, with a statutory decision deadline of 28 October. The same interval as the Co-operative case, to the day.
2 of 4
2 of 4, which is the floor of a base rate and should be read as a description rather than a forecast. Three caveats: four cases is thin, the interval is partly set by the parties' own readiness rather than by the regulator, and the two cases that did commence were smaller and simpler than a merger of two national broadcasters. What the count does establish is that a six-week silence is normal and tells you almost nothing.

2 of the 4 commenced Phase 1 within 60 days of the case opening, and both did so at exactly 56 days. One took 67 days and one has passed 140 with no clock started.

What this pattern excludes

Sky and ITV, and McCormick and Unilever
Both were opened in late July 2026 and are 42 and 44 days old, so neither can yet pass or fail a 60-day test. Scoring a case that has not reached the threshold would be scoring a guess, and one of them is the subject of the market.
Cases opened before 2026
The CMA's pre-notification practice and its published guidance on commencement have changed, and mixing years would blend two regimes. A single-year window is narrow but it is a window in which the same rules applied throughout.
The DMGT and Telegraph Media Group case
It is running under a public interest intervention notice, which puts the Secretary of State and Ofcom into the timetable alongside the CMA. That is a different mechanism with a different clock, and it belongs in the story rather than in the count.

5 · The read

The read

The 17 September market is not really about Sky and ITV; it is about whether the two 56-day intervals this year were a coincidence or a working practice. Fifty-six days from 23 July lands on 17 September exactly, which is why the date was chosen for the market, and the honest position is that two identical intervals out of four cases is weak evidence for a rule. Against it: this is a far larger transaction than either of those two, the parties control pre-notification, and AkzoNobel shows that a file can sit unstarted for four and a half months.

The intervention notice market is the one that would change the shape of the whole thing. A merger between the largest commercial broadcaster and the largest pay-television operator in the country raises media plurality questions that competition law alone does not answer, and the government has an intervention notice live on a newspaper deal already. If a notice is issued, Ofcom joins the timetable, the Secretary of State takes the decision rather than the CMA, and the DMGT case becomes the precedent for how long that takes. On the evidence of that case, which was opened in February and is still open in September, the answer is a long time.
