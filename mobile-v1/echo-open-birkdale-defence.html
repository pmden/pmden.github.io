Scheffler comes to Birkdale. The Jug hasn't stayed put since 2008.

Final Qualifying closed the field for the 154th Open at Royal Birkdale. Scottie Scheffler arrives on 16 July to defend the Claret Jug.

4 min read

The field is set for Birkdale

Twenty players earned places at Royal Birkdale through Final Qualifying at four courses; Sergio Garcia, Matt Wallace and Wesley Bryan were among those who missed out.

(Sky Sports, 30 Jun 2026)

Final Qualifying for the 154th Open took place on 30 June at Burnham & Berrow, Dundonald Links, Royal Cinque Ports and West Lancashire, with 288 players contesting 20 places at Royal Birkdale (PGA Tour, 30 June 2026). Sergio Garcia and Matt Wallace failed to advance, while PGA Tour winner Wesley Bryan bogeyed the last at West Lancashire to miss a play-off by a stroke (Sky Sports, 30 June 2026).

The championship runs 16-19 July at Royal Birkdale in Southport — the club's eleventh Open and its first since Jordan Spieth's 2017 victory (PGA Tour, 12 July 2026). The R&A has added a first-ever Last Chance Qualifier, in which twelve golfers will contest one final place at Birkdale on the Monday of championship week (The Open, 2026).

Scottie Scheffler arrives as defending champion after winning at Royal Portrush in 2025, and as world number one — though his most recent major outing ended in a tie for fourth at the US Open at Shinnecock Hills in June, where Wyndham Clark led wire to wire to deny Scheffler the career Grand Slam.

The Pattern

Two patterns collide at Birkdale. Recent Opens have gone overwhelmingly to Americans at the top of the world game. But the specific thing Scheffler is attempting — winning it twice running — has not been done for eighteen years, and the last man to do it did it on this course.

0 of 16
No Open champion has retained the Claret Jug in the sixteen editions since Pádraig Harrington's 2008 defence at Birkdale. That is the honest weight against Scheffler the defender — even as Americans have won four of the last five Opens and Scheffler remains world number one.

Americans have won 4 of the last 5 Opens — but 0 of the last 16 champions have defended the title.

The Read

The case for Scheffler leading the market is simple: he is the world number one, he has won a major-level share of everything in sight for three seasons, and his links game passed its examination at Portrush a year ago. Birkdale is regarded as the fairest of the Open rota courses — dune-framed but honest — which historically rewards the best ball-striker in the field, and no one disputes who that is. Twenty-one per cent for one man in a 156-player field is an enormous statement, and the market makes it anyway.

The counter-case is the defence problem and the depth problem. Sixteen consecutive champions have failed to retain this trophy, including prime Woods, McIlroy and Scheffler-grade favourites; links golf's weather draw randomises outcomes in a way no other major allows, which is why 'Another player' carries 58 per cent. Scheffler also arrives off a major where he was out-executed wire to wire by Wyndham Clark. The forecast, the draw and one bad Friday gust matter more at Birkdale than world ranking — that is precisely what the 0-for-16 base rate is measuring.
