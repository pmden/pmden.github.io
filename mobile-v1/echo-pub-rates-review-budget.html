Business rates31 Aug 2026

A review for pubs and hotels. The Budget has never skipped this bill.

The Treasury opened an independent review of how pubs and hotels are valued for business rates on 24 August, with a call for evidence closing on 16 October. John Healey's first Budget lands twelve days after that.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

HM Treasury launched an independent review of business rates valuation methodology for pubs and hotels, led by Jerry Schurder, alongside a call for evidence running to 16 October 2026, with the review reporting by the end of March 2027.

HM Treasury, 24 Aug 2026
2 · The event

A valuation problem the Budget cannot fix in time

The review announced on 24 August examines how the Valuation Office arrives at rateable values for public houses and hotels, two sectors valued on trading potential rather than floor area. Both saw significant increases at the 2026 revaluation, largely because valuations that had been depressed by the pandemic period rolled off (HM Treasury, 24 Aug 2026). Jerry Schurder leads the work and reports to the Treasury by the end of March 2027.

The consultation window is the part that matters for the autumn. The call for evidence opened on 24 August and closes at 23:59 on 16 October, gathering submissions from landlords, brewers, hoteliers and operators (HM Treasury call for evidence). Twelve days later the Chancellor delivers his first Budget. The review itself cannot report in that time, so any relief this autumn has to come through the multiplier or a discount rather than through valuation.

The sector is not starting from zero. From 1 April 2026 retail, hospitality and leisure properties with rateable values below £500,000 pay multipliers set five pence below the national equivalents, a change legislated at the November 2025 Budget (HM Treasury, Budget 2025). Pubs and live music venues additionally received a 15% cut to bills from April 2026 followed by a two-year real-terms freeze, worth about £1,650 to the average pub in 2026-27 (HM Treasury, 24 Aug 2026).

3 · The pattern

What autumn Budgets do to hospitality rates

This is a decision, not a threshold: a chancellor either announces a business rates measure for retail, hospitality and leisure in England or does not. Inclusion rule, fixed before the count: every autumn fiscal event delivered by a UK chancellor from 2021 to 2025 inclusive, five consecutive events, no gaps. The autumn event is the point in the calendar at which multipliers and reliefs for the following April are set, which is why the window is drawn there and not across all fiscal statements. The test is whether the event carried a new or extended rates measure aimed at the retail, hospitality and leisure sector.

2021
Autumn Budget, 27 October. A new 50% relief for retail, hospitality and leisure in 2022-23, capped at £110,000 per business, alongside a freeze in the multiplier. Measure delivered.
2022
Autumn Statement, 17 November. Relief raised to 75% for 2023-24 with the same £110,000 cap, and the multipliers frozen again. Measure delivered.
2023
Autumn Statement, 22 November. The 75% relief extended into 2024-25 and the small business multiplier frozen. Measure delivered.
2024
Autumn Budget, 30 October. Relief reduced to 40% for 2025-26, but paired with a commitment to permanently lower multipliers for the sector from 2026-27. Measure delivered.
2025
Autumn Budget, 26 November. Permanently lower multipliers legislated: retail, hospitality and leisure properties under £500,000 rateable value set 5p below the national rates from April 2026. Measure delivered.
5 of 5
Five autumn events since 2021, five business rates measures for retail, hospitality and leisure. The unbroken record is less impressive than it looks in one respect: two of the five were reductions in generosity dressed as continuity, with relief cut from 75% to 40% in 2024 before the permanent multipliers arrived. The direction of travel is not guaranteed even when the presence of a measure is. What raises the odds this year is the Treasury having just conceded, in its own words, that the valuation system is not reflecting the market for pubs and hotels, and having set a review that cannot report until five months after the Budget.

All 5 autumn fiscal events since 2021 carried a business rates measure for retail, hospitality and leisure. None passed the sector over.

What this pattern excludes

The Growth Plan, 23 September 2022
It carried no business rates measure and was substantially reversed within eight weeks by the November Autumn Statement. Counting both would place two events in a single autumn and score the same decision twice.
Spring Statements and spring Budgets, March 2022 to March 2026
The multiplier and relief settlement for the following April is made at the autumn event. Spring statements confirm or implement it, so including them would add observations where the decision was not available to be taken.
The Budgets of March 2020 and March 2021
The 100% reliefs announced there were emergency measures tied to closure orders under pandemic restrictions. That mechanism no longer operates, so the cases do not transfer even though the policy label is the same.
Business rates in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland
Rates are devolved. The Budget sets policy for England only, and the markets resolve on English measures.

5 · The read

The read

The weighted case is another measure. Business rates has become the standing hospitality item in the autumn statement of accounts, and the Treasury has now put its own name to the argument that pub and hotel valuations are wrong. The review reports in March 2027, which leaves a five-month gap in which the sector will be told that help is coming but not yet arriving. Chancellors have generally filled gaps of that kind with a discount rather than let them run, and the sums involved are small in Budget terms: the 2026-27 pub package is worth roughly £1,650 per house.

The counter-case is arithmetic. The permanently lower multipliers introduced in April 2026 were presented as the settled answer, and the Treasury has an argument for saying the sector has already had its structural change this cycle. This is also a Budget being framed around a fiscal gap, with reports through August of ministers examining bank and energy levies rather than new giveaways. The 20% probability on relief being reduced reflects a real possibility that the freeze is left to expire on its existing schedule and the review becomes the answer to every question.
