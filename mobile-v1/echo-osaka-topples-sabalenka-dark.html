Wimbledon05 Jul 2026

Sabalenka falls. Wimbledon belongs to no one.

Naomi Osaka beat world No. 1 Aryna Sabalenka 6-2, 7-6(2) in the fourth round, wiping the last of the top three seeds from the women's draw. A ninth consecutive first-time champion is now guaranteed.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

Naomi Osaka beat world No. 1 Aryna Sabalenka 6-2, 7-6(2) in the fourth round — Sabalenka's first straight-sets defeat at a major since the 2020 US Open.

ESPN, 5 Jul 2026
2 · The event

What happened

Fourteenth seed Naomi Osaka produced the result of the fortnight on Centre Court, dismissing world No. 1 Aryna Sabalenka 6-2, 7-6(2) to reach the first Wimbledon quarter-final of her career. Sabalenka had won the pair's last three meetings, including a straight-sets fourth-round win at Roland Garros only weeks earlier, and had not lost in straight sets at a Grand Slam since the 2020 US Open (ESPN, 5 July 2026).

The defeat completed a clear-out at the top of the women's draw. Second seed Elena Rybakina and third seed Iga Swiatek had already departed, so Sabalenka's exit means none of the top three seeds reached the last eight (Sky Sports, 12 July 2026). Every woman left in the draw is chasing a first Wimbledon title, guaranteeing a ninth consecutive first-time champion at the All England Club.

Osaka, a four-time major champion who had never previously passed the third round on grass, next faces tenth seed Karolina Muchova for a place in the semi-finals. The women's semi-finals are scheduled for 9 July and the final for 11 July.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

Wimbledon's women's draw has become the most open title in tennis. Since Serena Williams's 2016 victory, no woman has lifted the trophy twice — every champion has been winning Wimbledon for the first time, often from deep in the seedings.

2025
Iga Swiatek won her first Wimbledon, dismantling Amanda Anisimova 6-0, 6-0 in the most one-sided final of the Open Era.
2024
Barbora Krejcikova, seeded 31st, beat Jasmine Paolini in three sets for her first Wimbledon title.
2023
Marketa Vondrousova became the first unseeded woman in the Open Era to win Wimbledon, beating Ons Jabeur in the final.
2022
Elena Rybakina, seeded 17th, came from a set down to beat Jabeur for her first major title.
2021
Ashleigh Barty won her first Wimbledon, beating Karolina Pliskova in three sets — the first of what is now eight straight first-time champions.
8 of 8
The last 8 of 8 Wimbledon women's champions were winning the title for the first time. With every former champion out of the 2026 draw, the streak reaches nine whatever happens — the live question is whether the winner again comes from outside the top ten seeds, as in 3 of the last 4 editions.

Eight consecutive first-time Wimbledon women's champions since 2017 — and 2026 makes it nine by default.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weight of the pattern favours chaos over pedigree. Eight straight first-time champions, three of the last four from outside the top ten seeds, and a 2026 draw with no former champion left in it: the structural case says the market's 41% on "another seeded player" understates how often this tournament rewards whoever holds their nerve in week two rather than whoever the rankings prefer. Osaka's serve on fast grass and her 4-0 record in major finals make her the most dangerous player left by résumé, and the market's 24% reflects that.

The counter-case is that Osaka's grass-court body of work remains thin — this is her first Wimbledon quarter-final in eight visits — and Muchova, an all-court player with a Roland Garros final behind her, is precisely the awkward type who has troubled big hitters here. One statement win over Sabalenka does not overwrite years of early exits on this surface. The final-four price on Osaka at 34% is a call on her ceiling; the counter is her floor.
