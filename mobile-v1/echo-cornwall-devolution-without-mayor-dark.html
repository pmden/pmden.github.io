Devolution03 Sep 2026

Cornwall is offered a deal with or without a mayor. The record says the mayor is the money.

The Prime Minister told the Commons on 1 September that work has begun with Cornwall Council and its six MPs on a devolution deal, and that the deal need not come with an elected mayor. The same statement reserved fiscal powers, including a visitor levy, for mayors.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

In a Commons statement the Prime Minister says work has begun with Cornwall Council and its six MPs on a devolution deal, with or without an elected mayor.

GOV.UK, 1 Sep 2026
2 · The event

An offer to Cornwall, and a levy reserved for mayors

The Prime Minister told the Commons that Cornwall, like many rural economies, lacks a basic level of infrastructure which harms growth, and that he had heard the cross-party call for a devolution deal. He said he had asked Number 10 North to begin work with Cornwall Council and its six MPs on a deal, in his words with or without an elected Mayor, and extended the same offer to every part of England without devolved power (GOV.UK, 1 Sep 2026).

The same statement drew a line around the powers on offer. Devolution will include post-16 technical education, employment support and fiscal devolution, and the first fiscal power named is the ability to set an overnight visitor levy, to commence towards the end of the 2027-28 financial year. The statement attaches those powers to mayors. That is the distinction a Cornwall deal has to survive: an area can have a deal without a mayor, or it can have the money-raising power, and so far in England those have not been the same thing.

Cornwall has already banked part of the answer. At its meeting on 29 July 2026 the council's cabinet took item CAB/569, Cornwall Foundation Strategic Authority, and accepted the government's intention to designate Cornwall as a single Foundation Strategic Authority, while continuing to ask the government to maximise and accelerate the quantum of devolved functions and funding available to such authorities (Cornwall Council, 29 Jul 2026). Foundation is the lowest of the tiers, and the cabinet's own wording, asking for more functions and more funding, says so.

The next two dates are on the council's own calendar. Full council meets on Tuesday 15 September at 10.30am in the Council Chamber at County Hall in Truro, and cabinet meets again on 23 September; the democracy portal says each agenda is displayed in the week before the meeting (Cornwall Council, 2026). In parallel the council signed a memorandum of understanding with Homes England on 2 September naming five priorities, and citing more than 20,000 households with Cornwall connections registered in housing need and around 800 households in temporary or emergency accommodation (Cornwall Council, 2 Sep 2026).

3 · The pattern

What an English devolution deal carries when there is no mayor

Inclusion rule, fixed before the count: this pattern counts every devolution deal published by the UK Government for an English area where the agreement did not create a directly elected mayor, and where the deal document is on gov.uk. The test applied to each is single and stated in advance: did the deal transfer a power to raise revenue locally, as distinct from a power to spend money granted from the centre? Mayoral deals are excluded, because the question is precisely what happens without a mayor. Deals announced but never published are excluded, because the powers cannot be read.

2015
The Cornwall devolution deal was published on 22 July 2015, the first for a rural county and agreed without an elected mayor. It covered transport, skills, business support and other areas. It carried no revenue-raising power.
2023
The Lancashire deal, published on 22 November 2023, created a combined county authority for Lancashire, Blackpool and Blackburn with Darwen with no mayor. It devolved the adult education budget and £20 million of capital funding, and no power to raise money.
2024
The Devon and Torbay deal, published on 25 January 2024, created a combined county authority with no mayor. It devolved the adult education budget and £16 million of capital funding, and no power to raise money.
2024
The Warwickshire deal, published on 6 March 2024, was a Level 2 agreement with no elected mayor. It devolved the adult education budget from 2026-27 and carried no capital pot at all, and no power to raise money (GOV.UK, March 2024).
0 of 4
0 of 4. The honest limits: four cases is still a thin base, the deals span nine years and two different statutory frameworks, and the English Devolution and Community Empowerment Act 2026 has since changed the structures available, so the mechanism that produced this record may not be the mechanism that decides Cornwall.

0 of the 4 published non-mayoral deals carried a power to raise revenue locally. Three carried a training budget and a capital pot decided in Whitehall; the fourth carried the training budget alone.

What this pattern excludes

Mayoral combined authority deals from Greater Manchester 2014 onwards
They are the comparison group, not the sample. Including them would answer a different question, namely whether devolution works at all, rather than what an area gets when it declines a mayor.
Deals announced in principle but with no published agreement
Powers can only be scored from a document. Anything described only in a speech or a press notice is left out rather than inferred from the description.
Reports that Cornwall rejected an elected mayor in 2022 or 2023
Widely repeated but not confirmed against a primary record at the time of writing, so it is left out rather than used. The council's own position on record is the cabinet decision of 29 July 2026.

5 · The read

The read

The near-term markets are about paperwork, not politics. A full council agenda published a week ahead is a narrow window for an item that was announced in the Commons a fortnight earlier and has no agreed content yet, which is why the agenda market sits near evens rather than high. The gov.uk market sits lower still, because the department that publishes deals tends to publish them when they are signed, not while they are being scoped.

The long market is the one that matters and the record argues against it. Three times England has written a devolution deal without a mayor and three times the area got a training budget and a capital allocation rather than a lever of its own. The Prime Minister's own statement keeps fiscal devolution and the visitor levy in the mayoral column. The counter-case is that this government has explicitly broken with the mayor-or-nothing rule, has legislated new structures in 2026, and has a political reason to make Cornwall the proof that the rule is gone. If the deal lands with a levy attached, the pattern above stops describing the present and becomes history, which is exactly the sort of break the base rate cannot see coming.
