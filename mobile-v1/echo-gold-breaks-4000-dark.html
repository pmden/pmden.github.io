Gold lost $4,000. The floor is now the question.

Gold fell below $4,000 an ounce on 24 June for the first time since November 2025, down nearly 30% from January's $5,589 record.

4 min read

What happened

Gold fell below $4,000 an ounce for the first time since November 2025, extending a slide driven by a hawkish Fed, a stronger dollar and fading war risk.

(Reuters, 24 Jun 2026)

Gold broke below $4,000 an ounce on 24 June, its first visit under that level since November 2025 and the low point of a slide that began with a tech-led liquidation earlier in the week (Bloomberg, 23 June 2026; Reuters, 24 June 2026). The metal set its all-time record of $5,589 on 28 January, crossing $5,500 intraday, which puts the June low nearly 30% beneath the peak.

Three forces did the damage. The Federal Reserve under new chair Kevin Warsh held rates at 3.50-3.75% on 17 June but published a hawkish dot plot, with nine of nineteen officials pencilling in a hike by year-end and futures pricing a December move at roughly 80% probability. The dollar strengthened almost 1% in a week, raising the cost of bullion for non-dollar buyers. And the US-Iran de-escalation, with ships moving through the Strait of Hormuz again, removed the geopolitical bid that had supported the metal since February.

The break did not hold cleanly. Bank forecasters spent the final week of June cutting their 2026 targets.

The Pattern

Gold has fallen 20% or more from a record peak four times in the modern era. The outcomes split cleanly into two camps: twice the metal reclaimed its record within about two years, and twice the peak marked the start of a bear market that lasted the better part of a decade or more.

2020
Peaked at $2,075 in August 2020, fell nearly 20% to about $1,680 by March 2021, then ground back to a new record by December 2023, roughly two and a half years under the peak.
2011
Peaked at $1,920 in September 2011, crashed through $1,550 in a two-day 13% fall in April 2013, and did not reclaim the record until July 2020, a nine-year bear market.
2008
Peaked at $1,030 in March 2008, dropped about a third to near $680 in the October crash, then set a new record by September 2009, back inside eighteen months.
1980
Peaked at $850 in January 1980, lost over 40% within months as Volcker's rate hikes bit, and the nominal peak stood until 2008, twenty-eight years later.
2 of 4
In 2 of 4 major peak-to-trough episodes gold reclaimed its record within about two years; in the other 2 the peak held for nine and twenty-eight years. The pattern is split down the middle, and both bear cases (1980, 2011) began exactly like this one: real rates rising after a mania.

Four falls of 20%+ from gold records: two reclaimed the peak within about two years, two began decade-class bear markets.

The Read

The weighted case favours the range holding rather than a snap recovery. Every input that broke the price is still in place: the Warsh Fed's dot plot points up, December hike probability sits near 80%, the dollar is firm, and the Hormuz de-escalation is deepening rather than reversing. Rising real yields are the single most reliable headwind gold has, which is why only a third of the market prices a move back through $4,200 this month, and the modal outcome sits in the $3,800-$4,200 bands.

The counter-case rests on what has not changed. Central bank buying, the structural bid that carried gold from $3,300 to $5,589 in a year, does not follow the dot plot, and the 2008 and 2020 precedents show post-peak falls reversing quickly once positioning cleans out. Gold closing back above $4,014 on 30 June, within a week of the break, is the kind of failed breakdown that has previously marked lows. A soft US inflation print or one hostile headline from the Gulf would test $4,200 fast.
