Taiwan Strait20 Jun 2026

Taiwan rehearses the invasion. Beijing rehearses the blockade.

Taipei confirmed its biggest annual defence cycle yet as Chinese coast guard ships pushed into waters east of the island for the first time.

4 min read

Two militaries, two rehearsals

Taiwan's drills will test relocating arms production while under Chinese attack — the next phase of a summer of escalating readiness exercises, with Han Kuang No. 42 scheduled for early August.

(Reuters, 28 Jul 2026)

Taiwan's Ministry of National Defense confirmed on 19 June a restructured training calendar: an immediate five-day combat readiness drill from 23 June, a one-week joint defence exercise from 13 July as a prelude, and Han Kuang Exercise No. 42 from 5 August, with a live-fire segment expected to run ten days and nine nights (Reuters, 28 July 2026). This year's cycle tests whole-of-society resilience through civil-military integration and, for the first time, brings military intelligence units into the tabletop war games. It also imports reuters.com/world/china/taiwan-drills-test-relocating-arms-production-under-chinese-attack-2026-07-28/">Reuters, 28 July 2026).

Beijing spent June rehearsing something else. The carrier Fujian made its third transit of the Taiwan Strait on 23 June, and China Coast Guard vessels conducted repeated patrols into restricted waters around Pratas and Kinmen — while at least two CCG ships maintained a continuous presence in Taiwan's claimed eastern exclusive economic zone for the first time, a pattern analysts read as blockade coordination: the coast guard covers the strait while the navy covers the eastern approaches (Reuters, 28 July 2026). The PLA flew 134 aerial incursions into Taiwan's air defence identification zone in June, down from the 300-plus monthly average that followed President Lai's May 2024 inauguration.

Taipei is planning for exactly that scenario. On 25 June, government agencies ran tabletop exercises simulating a PRC maritime 'quarantine', practising countermeasures to coast guard boardings and vessel detention. The political backdrop sharpens the tension: Legislative Yuan Speaker Han Kuo-yu led a multipartisan delegation to Washington on 21–26 June, returning on the first-ever direct commercial flight between Washington and Taipei, and Taiwan's November local elections are already the target of a suspected PRC network of fake social media accounts.

The Pattern

Since 2022, the PLA's named exercise campaigns have followed a consistent trigger: Taipei's political moments — visits, inaugurations, speeches — not its military calendar. Han Kuang itself has never drawn a named response. The pattern is split by design, and the split is the story.

2025
Strait Thunder-2025A, 1–2 April: launched after President Lai designated China a 'foreign hostile force'; 76 aircraft detected around Taiwan in a single day.
2024
Joint Sword-2024B, 14 October: launched after Lai's National Day speech; a then-record 153 PLA aircraft detected in 25 hours.
2024
Joint Sword-2024A, 23–24 May: launched three days after Lai's inauguration, encircling the island as declared 'punishment'.
2022
August drills after the Pelosi visit: 11 ballistic missiles fired and live-fire zones ringing the island — the largest PLA exercises around Taiwan to that date.
0 of 5
Zero of the past five Han Kuang exercises triggered a named PLA counter-exercise. Beijing's set-piece drills answer speeches and visits, not Taiwan's training calendar. The honest caveat: 2026 is the first year Beijing has held continuous coast guard positions east of the island while that calendar runs.

Named PLA campaigns follow Taipei's political moments; 0 of the last 5 Han Kuang cycles drew one.

The Read

The weighted case: the trigger record is unambiguous — five Han Kuang cycles, zero named responses — and Beijing's current strategy is the cheaper one: normalising coast guard presence east of the island, transiting a carrier and calling it routine. Grey-zone pressure erodes Taiwan's position without handing Taipei a rallying moment before November's elections. The 22 on a named exercise and the 55 on a middling ADIZ count are the same forecast stated twice: pressure, not spectacle.

The honest counter: 2026 has no clean precedent. A continuous CCG presence in the eastern EEZ is new, the Fujian transit landed mid-schedule, and Han Kuo-yu's Washington trip — capped by a first-ever direct commercial flight — is precisely the kind of political moment that has triggered named campaigns before. If Beijing decides the delegation, the flight or Han Kuang's US-style methods amount to a status-quo change, the past pattern of answering politics with exercises argues the other way. Unlikely is not unthinkable, and five weeks is a long time in the strait.
