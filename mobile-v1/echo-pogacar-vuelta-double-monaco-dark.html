Cycling21 Aug 2026

Three riders own the Tour–Vuelta double. Pogacar starts at his front door.

Tadej Pogacar begins the 81st Vuelta a España on Saturday with a time trial in Monaco, the city where he lives, chasing the seventh Grand Tour win that would make him only the fourth man to take the Tour and the Vuelta in the same year. The record of reigning Tour champions at the autumn Vuelta is less kind than his form suggests.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

Pogacar starts the Vuelta a España on Saturday as overwhelming favourite, one month after winning the Tour de France by nearly six and a half minutes, and stands one victory from becoming only the ninth rider to win all three Grand Tours.

The Guardian, 21 Aug 2026
2 · The event

What happened

Seven years after his last appearance there, Pogacar returns to the Vuelta on Saturday seeking the one Grand Tour missing from his palmarès. Victory in Granada on 13 September would be his seventh Grand Tour win overall and would make him only the ninth rider to have won all three of cycling's three-week races (The Guardian, 21 Aug 2026). He last rode the race in 2019, finishing third and winning three stages as a 20-year-old debutant (ESPN, Aug 2026).

The 3,275km route opens with an individual time trial in Monaco on 22 August, crosses Provence and enters Spain via Andorra and the Pyrenees, with a mountain finish at Font Romeu on stage three and a run of summit finishes before the finale in Granada (The Guardian, 21 Aug 2026). The organiser confirms 21 stages between 22 August and 13 September, with rest days on 31 August and 7 September (La Vuelta, 2026). The start could hardly be more convenient for the favourite: Pogacar lives in Monaco (ESPN, Aug 2026).

One month ago Pogacar took a record-equalling fifth Tour de France title, and he won the 2024 Giro d'Italia by a wide margin; he brings a UAE team built around Almeida, Vine and Sivakov (ESPN, Aug 2026). The opposition is led by four-time Vuelta winner Primoz Roglic, Richard Carapaz, who took two Tour stages in July including Alpe d'Huez, and Scotland's Oscar Onley, fourth at the 2025 Tour (The Guardian, 21 Aug 2026).

3 · The pattern

The pattern

Three riders have completed the same-year Tour–Vuelta double in the race's history — and the calendar has always decided how hard it is. Since the Vuelta moved from spring to the end of the season in 1995, only one man has managed it.

1963
Jacques Anquetil took the first same-season Vuelta–Tour double, in the era when the Vuelta ran in spring and doubled as Tour preparation (The Guardian, 10 Sep 2017).
1978
Bernard Hinault repeated the double as a Grand Tour debutant — the last time it was done with the Vuelta in spring (The Guardian, 10 Sep 2017).
2016
Chris Froome, the reigning Tour champion, chased the autumn double and finished 2nd — one of his repeated near-misses at the Vuelta before his breakthrough (The Guardian, 10 Sep 2017).
2017
Froome converted at last: 2min 15sec over Vincenzo Nibali, the first double since Hinault in 1978 and the first ever achieved since the Vuelta moved to late season in 1995 (The Guardian, 10 Sep 2017).
2023
Jonas Vingegaard, that year's Tour winner, finished 2nd at the Vuelta behind his own team-mate Sepp Kuss, as Jumbo-Visma swept all three Grand Tours and locked out the podium (The Guardian, 17 Sep 2023).
1 of 3
Since the Vuelta moved to the end of the calendar in 1995, three reigning Tour de France champions have ridden it in the same year: Froome in 2016, Froome in 2017, Vingegaard in 2023. One converted the double; all three stood on the final podium. Pogacar arrives with a bigger Tour margin than any of them — and a seven-year absence from the race he now starts as favourite.

5 · The read

How to read it

The case for 68% rests on margin and floor. Pogacar won the Tour by nearly six and a half minutes a month ago, won the 2024 Giro by a wide margin, and faces a Vuelta field missing the Tour's other podium finishers. The autumn-era base rate supplies the floor: every reigning Tour champion who has started this race finished on the podium, so the downside scenario is second place, not collapse. The route helps too — a home-city time trial to take early time, then summit finish after summit finish to suit the strongest climber in the race.

The honest counter-case is that the double has beaten better-rested men than this. Froome in 2016 and Vingegaard in 2023 arrived as dominant Tour winners and were outlasted in the third week by fresher rivals; Roglic has won this race four times and skipped the Tour. Pogacar has not ridden a Grand Tour into September since 2019. The prediction fails if he concedes serious time in the Monaco time trial or at Font Romeu on stage three, if UAE surrender the jersey deliberately to ease the workload — which resolves the rest-day market NO without changing the overall picture — or if a crash or illness ends the attempt entirely, the one outcome no base rate prices.
