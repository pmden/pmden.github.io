The Knicks end 53 years. The repeat question starts now.

New York beat San Antonio 94-90 in Game 5 to win the NBA Finals 4-1 — the franchise's first championship since 1973. Jalen Brunson scored 45 in the clincher and took Finals MVP.

2 · The event

What happened

The New York Knicks won the 2026 NBA Finals four games to one, beating the San Antonio Spurs 94-90 in Game 5 on 13 June to claim the franchise's third championship and its first since 1973 (ESPN, 13 June 2026). Jalen Brunson scored 45 points in the clincher and was named Finals MVP.

The series, played 3-13 June, was historically clutch: New York rallied from double-digit deficits in all four of its victories, closing the title out on the road (NBA.com, 14 June 2026). For San Antonio, a first Finals appearance of the Victor Wembanyama era ended one round short of a sixth banner.

The wait New York ended — 53 years — was among the longest in major American sport, and the title extends a defining feature of the modern NBA: no franchise has repeated as champion since Golden State in 2018, and the Knicks are the eighth different champion in eight seasons.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

The dynasty era is over. Since 2019 the NBA has crowned a different champion every season, and long-suffering franchises keep cashing in: the past decade has ended more title droughts than the previous three combined.

8 of the last 8 NBA Finals produced a first-time-in-this-era champion — no franchise has gone back-to-back since Golden State in 2018. The pattern is unanimous, which is exactly why the Knicks' 2027 repeat question opens against the grain.

5 · The read

How to read it

The result validated the era's structural story. Under the current collective bargaining rules — a punitive luxury-tax apron that breaks up expensive rosters — sustained superteams have become nearly impossible to hold together, and the championship has circulated to eight different franchises in eight years. New York's build, anchored on a mid-first-round pick in Brunson rather than an assembled trio of stars, is the model the rules now favour. The 53-year drought ending in five games was the least surprising shock in recent Finals history: the Knicks had the East's most durable core and the Finals' best player.

The honest counter is that the series result flattered the margin. Four comeback wins from double-digit deficits is as much variance as dominance — flip two of those clutch stretches and this is a Spurs series. San Antonio arrives next season with the best young player in the sport and a Finals education, while the champions face the pattern's other edge: 8 of 8 recent title-winners failed to repeat. The banner is permanent; the throne, on recent evidence, is rented by the year.
