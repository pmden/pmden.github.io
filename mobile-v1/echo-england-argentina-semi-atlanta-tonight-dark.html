World Cup15 Jul 2026

Messi meets England at last. Atlanta decides who faces Spain.

England and Argentina meet in a World Cup semi-final tonight in Atlanta — their first match in 21 years, and the first time Lionel Messi has faced England.

4 min read

A 21-year wait ends in Atlanta

England face Argentina in the second World Cup semi-final at Mercedes-Benz Stadium in Atlanta, with kick-off at 15:00 local time and the winner meeting Spain in the 19 July final.

(FIFA, 15 Jul 2026)

England meet Argentina tonight for the first time since 2005, in a World Cup semi-final at Mercedes-Benz Stadium in Atlanta. It is the first time Lionel Messi has faced England in his international career, and the winner plays Spain at MetLife Stadium on 19 July (Al Jazeera, 13 Jul 2026).

Argentina arrive as the only semi-finalist yet to drop a point, having beaten ten-man Switzerland 3-1 after extra time in the quarter-final, with Alexis Mac Allister heading in Messi's tenth-minute cross and Julián Álvarez scoring from 25 yards in the 112th minute (Sky Sports, 11 Jul 2026). England needed extra time too: Jude Bellingham scored twice, at 45+2 and 93 minutes, to overturn Norway's lead in a 2-1 quarter-final win in Miami (FIFA, 11 Jul 2026).

The numbers frame a near-even contest. Messi sits joint-top of the Golden Boot standings on eight goals; Harry Kane and Bellingham have six each. Jordan Pickford made his 18th World Cup appearance against Norway, an England goalkeeping record.

The Pattern

This is the sixth World Cup meeting between the sides, a fixture that has produced a red card, a hand of God and a redemption penalty. The full record cuts both ways.

1962
Group stage, Rancagua: England won 3-1, their first World Cup win over Argentina.
1966
Quarter-final, Wembley: England won 1-0 after Argentina captain Antonio Rattín was sent off; Geoff Hurst headed the only goal.
1986
Quarter-final, Mexico City: Argentina won 2-1 through Diego Maradona's Hand of God and the Goal of the Century, scored four minutes apart.
1998
Round of 16, Saint-Étienne: 2-2 after extra time, with David Beckham sent off in the 47th minute; Argentina won the shoot-out 4-3.
2002
Group stage, Sapporo: England won 1-0 through Beckham's 44th-minute penalty, four years after his red card.
3 of 5
England have won 3 of 5 World Cup meetings with Argentina, yet just 1 of the 3 knockout ties between the sides.

England have won three of the five World Cup meetings — but only one of the three knockout ties, with Argentina taking both that went to the wire.

The Read

The case for England rests on form and simulation. Opta's model gives them 52.3% to advance, Bellingham has scored four in two knockout rounds, and the overall head-to-head record reads three wins from five. Both of England's front-line scorers, Kane and Bellingham, are within two goals of the tournament lead.

The honest counter is that the head-to-head narrows exactly where it matters. In knockout ties England have beaten Argentina once in three attempts, and both recent eliminations — 1986 and 1998 — came in matches England did not trail on the run of play. Argentina are the only side in the tournament yet to drop a point, and Messi has eight goals. A market sitting at 51 is a coin toss priced as one.
