Kerr calls his shot. The mile record has held for 27 years.

Josh Kerr will attack Hicham El Guerrouj's mile world record of 3:43.13 at the London Athletics Meet on 18 July — a publicly announced assault, branded Project 222, on the oldest standing men's middle-distance mark.

3 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

Josh Kerr confirmed 'Project 222' — an attempt to run a 222-second (3:42) mile at the London Diamond League on 18 July, targeting El Guerrouj's 3:43.13 world record.

London Diamond League, 28 Mar 2026
2 · The event

A record attempt, announced in advance

Kerr, fresh from retaining his world indoor 3000m title in Toruń, announced that he will attempt to break the men's mile world record at the Novuna London Athletics Meet on 18 July, the eleventh stop of the 2026 Diamond League (Wanda Diamond League, 2026). The target is Hicham El Guerrouj's 3:43.13, set in Rome in July 1999 and untouched since.

Kerr has framed it as a race rather than a time trial, saying rivals such as Cole Hocker and Jakob Ingebrigtsen are welcome in the field: 'I believe I've been in shape to run this world record a couple of times already.'

Kerr's personal best of 3:45.34 is the British record, set when he beat Ingebrigtsen in the 2024 Bowerman Mile — 2.21 seconds shy of the target. He will make the attempt in front of a home crowd at London Stadium, the world's biggest one-day athletics meeting (London Diamond League, 28 Mar 2026).

3 · The pattern

The record that outlasts its challengers

The men's mile world record was rewritten constantly through the Coe-Ovett-Cram era, then froze. Since Rome 1999 it has repelled every generation of challengers, including announced, paced, engineered attempts by the best 1500m runners alive.

2024
Kerr won the Bowerman Mile in 3:45.34, breaking Steve Cram's 39-year-old British record and beating Ingebrigtsen head-to-head — still 2.21 seconds outside the world record.
2023
Jakob Ingebrigtsen ran 3:43.73 in Eugene, the third-fastest mile ever, and missed the record by 0.60 seconds with Yared Nuguse (3:43.97) alongside.
1999
Hicham El Guerrouj set 3:43.13 in Rome, with Noah Ngeny (3:43.40) also under the old mark in the same race.
1985
Steve Cram ran 3:46.32 in Oslo — a British record that stood for 39 years.
1981
Sebastian Coe closed the Coe-Ovett duel with 3:47.33 in Brussels — the mile record fell three times in ten days that summer.
0 of 27
Zero men's mile world records in 27 years. In that span only two men have come within a second of 3:43.13 — Ngeny in the record race itself and Ingebrigtsen in 2023 — and no announced record attempt has succeeded. Kerr needs to find 2.21 seconds on his lifetime best. The base rate is brutal and the market should reflect it.

27 years, zero new records — the two closest challengers since 1999 missed by 0.60s and 0.84s.

5 · The read

How to read it

The case for Kerr is that announced attempts of this kind are no longer romantic gestures — they are engineering projects, and the engineering has moved. Super-spikes and aerodynamic suits have already dragged records down across the distance events, wavelight pacing removes tactical waste, and Kerr is a championship racer who has beaten Ingebrigtsen at his own game more than once, including for that 3:45.34 in Eugene. He is a double world indoor champion in the form of his life, at home, with fourteen weeks of preparation aimed at one night.

The counter-case is arithmetic. Kerr must improve his lifetime best by 2.21 seconds in a single race — a leap neither Ingebrigtsen nor Nuguse could make when they were within a second of the mark and running the races of their lives. El Guerrouj's record has outlived every shoe revolution to date, and London in mid-July offers no altitude, no guarantee of still air, and the pressure of a promise made in March. A 16 per cent market on the record is, if anything, generous to the announcement; the 62 per cent on Kerr simply winning the race is where the confidence properly belongs.
