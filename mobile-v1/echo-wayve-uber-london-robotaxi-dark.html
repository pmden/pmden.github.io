Robotaxis08 Jun 2026

The robotaxis are funded. London's kerb decides who wins.

Wayve has banked a $1.2 billion Series D and Uber is committed to a London robotaxi launch this year. Waymo says it is coming too, and the race is live.

4 min read

Three fleets, one capital

Uber has opened public sign-ups for London robotaxis, with the first rides — built on Wayve's self-driving system — expected within months.

(Reuters, 8 Jun 2026)

Wayve, the London-headquartered autonomy company spun out of Cambridge research, closed a $1.2 billion Series D in February at an $8.6 billion post-money valuation, with milestone-based capital from Uber taking the total package to $1.5 billion (FT, 25 February 2026). Microsoft, Nvidia, Mercedes-Benz, Nissan and Stellantis all participated, and the company says the money funds commercial robotaxi trials this year with supervised autonomy in consumer cars from 2027 (Wayve, 24 Feb 2026).

The venue for the first commercial test is London. Uber and Wayve aim to introduce their first robotaxi offering in the capital in 2026 before expanding to more than ten markets, while Waymo has separately announced plans to bring its service to London. That sets up a direct contest on some of the hardest urban roads in the world (Reuters, 8 June 2026).

Government policy has moved to meet them. The Department for Transport accelerated its timetable so that pilots of self-driving taxi and bus-like services could begin from spring 2026, ahead of full implementation of the Automated Vehicles Act expected in 2027. The regulatory door is open; what remains is for an operator to actually carry a paying member of the public without a driver.

The Pattern

The industry's own history is the sharpest check on this year's promises. Announced robotaxi launch windows have slipped far more often than they have held, and even the launches that happened arrived hedged with safety drivers and tiny service areas.

2025
Tesla launched a limited robotaxi service in Austin in June 2025 with a small supervised fleet, roughly five years after Elon Musk's 2019 pledge of a million robotaxis by 2020.
2023
Cruise, which had been running paid driverless rides in San Francisco, suspended all driverless operations in October 2023 after an incident in which a pedestrian was dragged; GM wound down the robotaxi business entirely in December 2024.
2020
Waymo opened fully driverless rides to the public in Phoenix in October 2020, about 11 years after the Google self-driving car project began in 2009.
2018
Waymo One launched commercially in Phoenix in December 2018, but with safety drivers on board, softening the fully driverless service that had been trailed for that year.
2016
nuTonomy ran the world's first public robotaxi trial in Singapore in August 2016 and Uber put self-driving cars on Pittsburgh streets in September 2016; both required safety drivers and neither scaled into a lasting driverless service.
2 of 6
2 of 6 headline robotaxi launch windows since 2016 were met on schedule, and both of those launches were smaller in scope than announced. The base rate leans firmly towards slippage.

Of six headline robotaxi launch windows announced since 2016, roughly two opened to the public on schedule, and both launched narrower than promised.

The Read

The weighted case is patience. Every prior robotaxi race has run long: Tesla missed its own deadline by five years, Waymo took eleven years from project start to driverless fares, and Cruise collapsed after moving too fast. London adds complications Phoenix never had, from roundabouts and rain to a regulator, TfL, that revoked Uber's licence twice in living memory. A 2026 'launch' most plausibly means a supervised trial late in the year, not a public driverless fare by August.

The honest counter is that this cycle is different in kind. Wayve's capital is milestone-tied to an Uber launch, which puts commercial pressure behind the date; the DfT has actively pulled its pilot framework forward to spring 2026 rather than waiting for 2027; and Waymo's arrival threatens whoever moves second, punishing delay. When a government fast-tracks and two rivals race, announced windows hold more often than the long-run base rate suggests.
