Manchester United15 Sep 2026

A 15-year-old asks to leave Manchester United. 4 of 7 such exits closed in a month.

JJ Gabriel, 15, is in Manchester United's academy. He wants the club to cancel his registration, the record that ties him to United, and let him go.

3 min read

What happened

On 15 September BBC Sport reported that JJ Gabriel, Manchester United's 15-year-old striker, has sent the club formal notice that he wants his registration cancelled with immediate effect, a request United can challenge.

(BBC Sport 15 Sep)

JJ Gabriel, a 15-year-old striker in Manchester United's academy, has sent the club formal notice that he wants his registration cancelled with immediate effect. A registration is the record that ties a young player to one club; without it he is free to join another. Gabriel was the Premier League's Under-18 player of the year last season while still 14, scored a hat-trick on his Uefa Youth League debut last week and had been expected to play against Brighton in the Carabao Cup on Wednesday. (BBC Sport 15 Sep; Independent 15 Sep)

United can say no. Under Premier League youth rules a registration can only be cancelled if the club, the player and a parent all agree; if they do not, the Premier League makes a binding decision. Gabriel's registration runs to the end of the season. He cannot sign a scholarship with any club until he turns 16 on 6 October, and cannot sign a professional contract until he is 17. Chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox are talking directly to his family. (Sky Sports 15 Sep; BBC Sport 15 Sep)

The club says it does not know what caused the break. Gabriel did not train with Michael Carrick's squad at the end of last week amid problems between his family and the club, and he has told United he would rather leave than play for it again at any level. He got eight minutes in pre-season, against Atletico Madrid on 1 August, while academy team-mates and an 18-year-old newcomer from Tottenham got more. He could have joined Manchester City in the summer of 2025 and stayed after United promised him a route to the first team. (BBC Sport 15 Sep; The Athletic 15 Sep; Sky Sports 15 Sep)

Four of the seven forced exits ended with a registration at a new club within a month of the news; the three English cases since 2017 all took between six and eleven weeks.

The Read

The notice is a lever. A registration is what lets United hold Gabriel until June, and a formal request to cancel it forces a choice: agree, or take the case to the Premier League. On the player's side are a family United says it cannot read, a pre-season of eight minutes while others played more, and clubs, Manchester City and Arsenal among them, that have been linked with him. On United's side are a Carabao Cup tie on Wednesday that was meant to be his debut and the two executives, Berrada and Wilcox, who talked him into staying in 2025. Nobody can register him for a scholarship before 6 October. (BBC Sport 15 Sep; Sky Sports 15 Sep; The Athletic 15 Sep)

The market opens at 30, below the four in seven. The four that held were cases where the news and the signing arrived together, or the boy's old deal had already run out; Gabriel's has not, and United can contest. The three English cases since 2017 that most resemble his, a refused deal made public before a new club could register him, took 42, 57 and 78 days. Ngumoha is the closest match: public in July, unable to sign until his 16th birthday, registered within a week or so of it. Gabriel turns 16 on 6 October, ten days before this market closes.

The honest counter-case is that United ends this the way Chelsea could not: with a deal to stay. The club has not given up, both executives are in the room, and a Carabao Cup debut would change the mood; that is the second market. The prediction of a new club fails if United refuses to cancel and the Premier League takes longer than a month to rule, which is the normal course. The long market fails if the League sides with United and Gabriel stays registered until June; none of the seven cases shows that, but none of them involved a club refusing to release a player still under registration.
