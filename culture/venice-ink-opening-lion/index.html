Boyle's Ink opens Venice in competition. Openers win Volpi Cups, not Lions.

Danny Boyle's Ink, with Guy Pearce as Rupert Murdoch, is the opening film in competition of the 83rd Venice Film Festival, premiering on 2 September.

4 min read

What happened

La Biennale di Venezia confirms Danny Boyle's Ink as the opening film, in a world premiere in competition, of the 83rd Venice International Film Festival, screening on Wednesday 2 September in the Sala Grande at the Palazzo del Cinema after the opening ceremony.

(La Biennale di Venezia, 1 Sep 2026)

Ink is directed by Danny Boyle and written by James Graham from his own stage play about Rupert Murdoch's purchase of The Sun in 1969 and the editor, Larry Lamb, he put in charge of it. Jack O'Connell plays Lamb, Guy Pearce plays Murdoch and Claire Foy plays Jules Davies. StudioCanal financed the film and will release it in the United Kingdom, Italy, France and Germany among other territories. It screens on 2 September in the Sala Grande as the opening film, in competition, of a festival that runs to 12 September (La Biennale di Venezia, 1 Sep 2026).

The field was announced by the festival's director, Alberto Barbera, in July. Martin McDonagh's Wild Horse Nine, Werner Herzog's Bucking Fastard, Florian Zeller's Bunker and Nanni Moretti's first Venice film since 1989 are all in the lineup, and Alex Gibney's four-hour documentary on Elon Musk screens out of competition. Only one female director has a film in competition this year (The Guardian, 23 Jul 2026).

The decision is the competition jury's alone. The official awards of the Venezia 83 competition, from the Golden Lion down to the Volpi Cups for acting and the Marcello Mastroianni Award, are announced by the jury at the closing ceremony on 12 September and published by the Biennale the same evening, as they were for the 82nd festival on 6 September 2025 (La Biennale di Venezia, 6 Sep 2025). The opener screens before the jury has seen a single rival.

The Pattern

Inclusion rule, fixed before the count: every Venice International Film Festival from the 73rd (2016) to the 82nd (2025) whose opening-night film screened in the Venezia competition, as recorded in the Biennale's own opening-film announcement or in whitelisted coverage of opening night. The single test is whether that film was named in the official awards of the competition jury: Golden Lion, Silver Lions, Special Jury Prize, Volpi Cups, Best Screenplay or the Mastroianni Award, as published by the Biennale on closing night. Eight festivals meet the rule; the 77th (2020) and 81st (2024) are excluded because their openers were not in competition. Newest first.

2025
Paolo Sorrentino's La Grazia opened the 82nd festival in competition on 27 August (La Biennale, 27 Aug 2025). The Golden Lion went to Jim Jarmusch's Father Mother Sister Brother; Toni Servillo took the Volpi Cup for best actor for La Grazia.
2023
Edoardo De Angelis's Comandante opened the 80th festival in competition on 30 August, having taken the slot vacated by Challengers (Deadline, 30 Aug 2023). The Golden Lion went to Yorgos Lanthimos's Poor Things and Comandante was not named in the official awards.
2022
Noah Baumbach's White Noise opened the 79th festival in competition on 31 August, the first Netflix film to do so (La Biennale, 31 Aug 2022). The Golden Lion went to Laura Poitras's All the Beauty and the Bloodshed; White Noise took nothing.
2021
Pedro Almodóvar's Madres paralelas opened the 78th festival in competition on 1 September (La Biennale, 1 Sep 2021). The Golden Lion went to Audrey Diwan's L'Événement; Penélope Cruz won the Volpi Cup for best actress for Madres paralelas.
2019
Kore-eda Hirokazu's La vérité opened the 76th festival in competition on 28 August (La Biennale, 28 Aug 2019). The Golden Lion went to Todd Phillips's Joker; La vérité was not named in the awards.
2018
Damien Chazelle's First Man opened the 75th festival in competition on 29 August (La Biennale, 29 Aug 2018). The Golden Lion went to Alfonso Cuarón's Roma; First Man took nothing.
2017
Alexander Payne's Downsizing opened the 74th festival in competition on 30 August (La Biennale, 30 Aug 2017). The Golden Lion went to Guillermo del Toro's The Shape of Water; Downsizing was not named in the awards.
2016
Damien Chazelle's La La Land opened the 73rd festival (The Guardian, 31 Aug 2016). The Golden Lion went to Lav Diaz's The Woman Who Left; Emma Stone was named best actress for La La Land.
3 of 8
Three of the last eight in-competition openers took an official award: La La Land (2016), Madres paralelas (2021) and La Grazia (2025), each through a Volpi Cup for its lead actor. The other five, Downsizing, First Man, La vérité, White Noise and Comandante, went home empty-handed. Zero of the eight won the Golden Lion. The eight festivals are the full record counted here.

Three of the last eight in-competition openers were named in the official awards, and in all three the prize was a Volpi Cup for an actor. None of the eight won the Golden Lion, the Silver Lions, the Special Jury Prize or the screenplay award.

What this pattern excludes

The 77th festival (2020) and the 81st festival (2024)
The opening films of those years did not screen in the Venezia competition, so the jury could not have rewarded them and the test cannot score them. The Biennale's awards pages for both years (12 September 2020 and 7 September 2024) name no opening film.
Collateral, Orizzonti and independent-jury prizes
Awarded by other juries under different rules; only the Venezia competition jury's official awards count.
Festivals before 2016
Not fetched and not counted; the record here is the eight most recent in-competition openers, all confirmed on the Biennale's own pages or in whitelisted coverage.

The Read

The record says the opener's realistic prize is an acting award, and the market opens a little above the base rate for that reason. Three of eight openers were rewarded and all three through a Volpi Cup for the performance at the centre of the film, which is where Ink's case sits: Pearce as Murdoch and O'Connell as Lamb are the kind of showy, single-figure roles that acting juries have rewarded on night one before. Against that, five of eight openers took nothing at all, and Ink screens before the jury has a comparison point.

The Golden Lion is a different question and the record is blunt about it: zero of eight. The opening slot is chosen months ahead for red-carpet value and a film seen on night one competes against ten days of accumulating enthusiasm for something newer. A British newspaper story in English, in an Italian festival with Moretti, McDonagh and Herzog in the field, has the extra handicap of being about a proprietor the European press has written about for fifty years. The pattern fails if the jury reads Ink as a film about the manufacture of consent rather than a period newspaper drama.
