Immigration03 Sep 2026

Burnham says the summer crossings halved. The Home Office series says he is right.

In his Commons statement on 1 September the Prime Minister said arrivals over the summer had halved on last year and that 2026 is the lowest year to date since 2021. Both claims check out against the Home Office time series. September is where the claim gets its first real test.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

In a Commons statement the Prime Minister says numbers arriving over the summer halved on last year, making this year to date the lowest for crossings since 2021.

GOV.UK, 1 Sep 2026
2 · The event

Two claims, one published series

The Prime Minister told the Commons that numbers arriving over the summer had halved on last year, making this year to date the lowest for crossings since 2021, and thanked France for cooperation that has prevented crossings (GOV.UK, 1 Sep 2026). Unusually for a political claim about migration, both halves of it can be checked against a single published file.

The Home Office publishes a daily and weekly time series of small boat arrivals, updated every day for the last seven days and weekly on a Friday for the full run (GOV.UK, 3 Sep 2026). In the edition published on 28 August, arrivals from 1 June to 27 August 2026 total 7,249 against 14,191 across the same dates in 2025, which is 51 per cent of last year. The year to 27 August stands at 16,391 against 29,003 in 2025, 19,819 in 2024, 19,801 in 2023 and 25,065 in 2022. The only recent year lower at this point was 2021, at 12,482. The statement holds on both counts.

The same file shows why September matters. The week to 23 August recorded 250 arrivals in three boats, one of the quieter weeks of the summer, and then 27 August alone recorded 494 arrivals in seven boats. This is a series with long flat stretches and sudden spikes, driven by weather windows rather than by anything that moves smoothly, and 14 of the 34 completed weeks of 2026 have carried more than 500 arrivals.

3 · The pattern

What the first half of September has done since the route became continuous

Inclusion rule, fixed before the count: this pattern uses the published Home Office weekly series and takes, for each year from 2020 to 2025, the week ending nearest to 11 September. The test is whether that week recorded more than 500 arrivals. The period starts in 2020 because that is the first year annual arrivals passed 5,000 and the crossing became a continuous route rather than an occasional one, which is a change in the thing being measured rather than a choice about the answer. The base is taken from the full published series, not from selected episodes.

2020
Week ending 13 September: 565 arrivals. Above the line in the first year the route ran continuously, when the annual total was 8,462.
2022
Week ending 11 September: 568. The lowest early-September week of the peak years, in the year with the highest annual total on record at 45,755.
2023
Week ending 10 September: 2,017, the largest early-September week in the series, in a year whose annual total fell to 29,437.
2025
Week ending 14 September: 862, with 1,166 the week before. Annual total 41,472.
6 of 6
6 of 6 since 2020. Two limits belong next to it. Six is a small denominator for a weather-driven series, and the years inside it are not independent of each other in the way separate trials would be. And 2026 has run at roughly half of 2025 all summer, so the honest reading is that the seasonal pattern points one way while the level of the year points the other.

6 of the 6 years from 2020 to 2025 recorded more than 500 arrivals in the week nearest 11 September. The two intervening years not listed above, 2021 and 2024, recorded 1,862 and 1,388.

What this pattern excludes

2018 and 2019
Annual arrivals were 299 and 1,843, and the equivalent September weeks recorded 0 and 0. The route was not yet continuous, so the mechanism producing the numbers was different. Stated plainly: including them would make the base rate 6 of 8 rather than 6 of 6, and the reader should hold that alternative in mind.
Preventions recorded by French authorities
They are published in the same file but count a different event. Mixing arrivals and preventions would let the total move without anyone landing.
Quarterly immigration system statistics
Those are the final authoritative figures and are published months later. Using them would make no market resolvable inside a fortnight, and switching between two sources mid-series would produce a number nobody could check.

5 · The read

The read

The two weekly markets sit above evens but not far above, and the reason is that two honest reference classes disagree. Take the calendar and the answer is clear: every early September since the route became continuous has cleared 500, and the smallest of those weeks was 565 in the year with the smallest annual total. Take 2026 and it is much less clear: this year has run at half of last, only 14 of 34 completed weeks have cleared 500, and several weeks have recorded nothing at all because the weather closed the window.

What would settle it is not policy but wind. The series moves in bursts, with 494 arrivals on a single day in late August after a fortnight of near silence, and the French prevention effort removes a share of attempts before they start rather than smoothing the total. The third market is the one with the least uncertainty in it: 2026 is 12,612 arrivals behind 2025 at the end of August, and closing that gap in September would take the largest month in the history of the series. The interesting question is not whether the year stays lower. It is whether the government keeps saying halved once the September weeks land.
