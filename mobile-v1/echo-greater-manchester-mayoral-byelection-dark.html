By-elections30 Jun 2026

Burnham left Manchester. Reform wants the keys.

Greater Manchester elects a new mayor on 30 July after Andy Burnham's return to Westminster forced him out of the job. Labour's Bev Craig defends a mayoralty Labour has never lost — but every one of those wins belonged to Burnham personally.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

The full candidate list for the 30 July Greater Manchester mayoral by-election is confirmed: seven names, with Labour's Bev Craig and Reform UK's Sian Astley the principals.

Manchester City Council, Jul 2026
2 · The event

What happened

Andy Burnham's victory in the Makerfield by-election on 18 June disqualified him from the Greater Manchester mayoralty — the mayor holds police and crime commissioner powers and cannot sit as an MP — and he formally resigned the office on 19 June, triggering a by-election on 30 July House of Commons Library, Jun 2026. The winner serves until the next scheduled mayoral election in May 2028.

Labour moved fast, announcing Bev Craig, leader of Manchester City Council since 2021, as its candidate on 23 June LabourList, 23 Jun 2026. Reform UK selected Sian Astley, a recently elected Baguley councillor. The Greens run Trafford group leader Geraldine Coggins, the Conservatives Trafford deputy leader Phil Eckersley, and the Liberal Democrats Didsbury West councillor Richard Kilpatrick, with Restore Britain's Marlon West completing the field Manchester City Council, Jul 2026.

The contest reverts to the supplementary vote: voters mark a first and second preference, and if no candidate clears 50% of first preferences, the top two go to a second count where second preferences are redistributed Greater Manchester Combined Authority, Jul 2026. Every previous Greater Manchester mayoral election except 2024 used this system — and in all three, Burnham won on first preferences alone.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

Two patterns collide here, and they point in opposite directions. Labour has won every Greater Manchester mayoral contest, each time by a landslide. But every one of those landslides was Andy Burnham's, and the recent by-election record shows what happens to Labour majorities when insurgent turnout meets a low-salience contest.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case favours Craig. Greater Manchester is structurally Labour terrain — every mayoral race won by 26 points or more — and Craig brings a real machine: five years running Manchester City Council, name recognition in the region's biggest borough, and a selection completed within four days of the vacancy. Burnham himself held Makerfield by 20 points in far worse conditions for Labour than a mayoral contest, and his implicit endorsement transfers more cleanly to a mayoral successor than to a Westminster one. The supplementary vote also helps the front-runner: Green and Liberal Democrat second preferences historically break toward Labour, not Reform.

The counter-case is that this is a July by-election with no Burnham on the ballot, and by-elections are turnout contests. Runcorn showed Reform converting a 14,696-vote Labour deficit into victory when the governing party's voters stayed home; Makerfield showed Reform reaching 34.5% in the county even against the most popular Labour politician in Britain. Craig's 57% price assumes Labour's mayoral coalition belongs to the party rather than the man — the one assumption these three landslides have never had to prove. A first-preference majority looks unlikely either way, which is why the second-count mechanics matter more here than in any previous Manchester race.
