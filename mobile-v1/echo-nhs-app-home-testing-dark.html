NHS tech20 Apr 2026

The NHS App wants your blood. The GP queue is the target.

NHS England has opened a market engagement exercise for HomeTest, a national service that would let patients order, take and receive diagnostic tests at home through the NHS App, across seven clinical specialities.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

NHS England is exploring at-home health tests ordered through the NHS App, opening a market engagement exercise for suppliers — the first step towards home testing at scale.

The Daily Telegraph, 21 Apr 2026
2 · The event

What happened

NHS England opened a market engagement exercise on 20 April 2026 for HomeTest, a proposed national service letting patients order diagnostic tests, complete them at home and receive results through the NHS App. Supplier responses closed on 5 May. The initial scope covers seven specialities: sexual health, gastroenterology, urology, gynaecology, orthopaedics, rheumatology and primary care tests including cholesterol The Daily Telegraph, 21 April 2026.

The ambition is explicit in the numbers NHS England cites: users already view GP records through the app more than 30 million times a month, including 10 million views of test results. HomeTest would push the app from displaying diagnostics to initiating them, with an operational target of 2027/28, contingent on feasibility work and ministerial approval The Daily Telegraph, 21 April 2026.

The move sits inside the 10 Year Health Plan's promise to make the NHS App a "doctor in your pocket". GP leaders have already set conditions, warning that expansion must be evidence-based and clinically validated, with concerns that unclear results could drive anxiety and extra GP demand rather than relieve it. No operating model, national or locally commissioned, has been fixed.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

NHS digital programmes have one of the most polarised delivery records in British public services. The same organisation that ran the largest civilian IT failure in UK history also shipped one of its fastest-adopted digital products. The split is consistent: incremental features on the NHS App deliver; grand centrally-procured programmes struggle.

2025
The 10 Year Health Plan (July 2025) commits to the NHS App as the service's front door, pledging to shift two-thirds of outpatient appointments to digital-first pathways.
2021
The Covid Pass turns the NHS App from a niche tool into national infrastructure, driving registrations past 16 million in a single year.
2019
The NHS App launches in January 2019 to slow uptake; pre-pandemic adoption stays in the low single-digit millions.
2011
The National Programme for IT is dismantled after nine years and roughly £12.7bn, the benchmark failure for centralised NHS technology procurement.
2 of 4
Of four flagship NHS digital initiatives since 2002, two delivered at national scale (the NHS App and the Covid Pass) and two either failed outright (NPfIT) or remain unproven pledges (the 10 Year Plan's outpatient shift). The split is honest: HomeTest's app-first design puts it on the better side of the pattern, but its national procurement puts it on the worse one.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case for caution on the short horizon: market engagement exercises are the earliest rung of public procurement, and the stated pathway, feasibility recommendations then ministerial approval then a 2027/28 launch, leaves little reason for a formal next-stage notice within weeks. NHS England's own language is exploratory, with no commitment to national rollout or specific conditions. The anniversary market is different: governments rarely let a flagship plan's first birthday pass silently, and the App is the plan's most photogenic asset, so some announcement is the likelier branch.

The honest counter-case: the political wind is unusually strong behind this programme. The Health Secretary has personally championed home testing as faster diagnosis, the 10 Year Plan needs visible progress before its anniversary, and departments have a habit of accelerating announceable items into exactly this kind of window. A HomeTest pilot confirmed as part of a July anniversary package would resolve both live markets at once, and precedent exists: the Covid Pass went from concept to national deployment in months when ministers wanted it to.
