India's biggest share sale starts trading on 28 October. Will Jio close day one up?

Jio Platforms, the telecoms arm of Mukesh Ambani's Reliance Industries, sells new shares to the public until 23 October. Trading starts on 28 October.

5 min read

What happened

Jio Platforms opened India’s biggest share sale on 21 October, aiming to raise 302bn rupees ($3.2bn) while the Nifty 50 index is down more than 12% this year.

Jio Platforms opened its first sale of shares to the public on 21 October. It aims to raise 302bn rupees ($3.2bn), and is expected to be the biggest such sale in India’s history. The money will repay loans taken to build Reliance Jio Infocomm, India’s largest private telecoms operator. Buyers pay between 1,065 and 1,119 rupees a share, and the sale closes on 23 October.

At the top price Jio would be worth 10.3tn rupees, the fourth-largest company in India. That is far below the more than £110bn expected when it filed its first draft papers in the summer. The sale comes as the Nifty 50, the main index of 50 leading Indian companies, has fallen by more than 12% this year.

Big funds bid early. On 19 October, 212 large investors, among them BlackRock and the sovereign funds of Singapore and Abu Dhabi, bid about 30 times the shares set aside for them, according to Reuters. Reliance is also offering its more than 400,000 staff a discount of 54 rupees a share.

By the evening of 21 October, the first day, bids on both Indian exchanges covered the whole sale 3.2 times. Funds and banks had bid for 9.4 times their part. Ordinary buyers, who can bid up to 200,000 rupees each, had bid for 0.73 times the shares kept for them.

The Pattern

We looked for cases where a company sold shares to the public in India for the first time in what was then the country’s biggest such sale. We found seven since 2004. We compared each first-day closing price with the sale price, and with the first price of the day.

3 of 7
In three of seven record first share sales in India since 2004, the shares closed their first day above the sale price. The last three, in 2021, 2022 and 2024, all closed below it.
2024 Hyundai Motor Indianot held
Hyundai Motor India sold shares at 1,960 rupees, with orders 2.4 times the shares on offer, as the market fell. On 22 October 2024 it opened at 1,934 and closed at 1,845 rupees, down 7%.
2022 LIC, state insurernot held
State insurer LIC sold shares at 949 rupees, and the market fell up to 8% after the price was set. On 17 May 2022 it opened at 872 and closed at 873 rupees, down 8%.

The Read

Demand is strong. By the end of the first day, funds and banks had bid for 9.4 times their part of the sale. Of the past record sales with orders above seven times the shares, two of three closed day one higher. Reliance Power in 2008 was the exception, with orders 73 times the shares, because the whole market crashed in the three weeks before it listed.

There are reasons for doubt. The last three record sales, Paytm, LIC and Hyundai, all closed below the sale price. All seven past sales were in India, and the rules changed over 20 years: the wait between the sale and the first day of trading fell from about four weeks to seven days. Ordinary buyers had bid for less than three quarters of their part by the first evening.

The second market asks about the shape of the day. Five of the seven past record sales closed below their first price of the day. Tata Consultancy Services and DLF both ended above the sale price but gave back part of their early gains. Coal India was the one that kept rising all day, from 287.75 to 342 rupees.

Sources: National Stock Exchange of India, TIME, BBC, Al Jazeera, Rediff, The Hindu
