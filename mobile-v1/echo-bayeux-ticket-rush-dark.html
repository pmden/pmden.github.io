80,000 queued for the Bayeux Tapestry. Now the museum has a capacity problem.

The British Museum sold £2.5m of Bayeux Tapestry tickets in a single day — the biggest ticketing day in its history — and every 2026 slot is gone. History says blockbuster sellouts force museums to conjure extra capacity. The question is how fast.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

Bayeux Tapestry tickets generate a record £2.5m for the British Museum in one day, with the online queue peaking above 80,000 and the September–December 2026 run selling out.

The Guardian, 3 Jul 2026
2 · The event

The exhibition of the century meets a finite building

Public tickets for The Bayeux Tapestry went on sale on 1 July 2026, as announced by the British Museum, covering entry dates from the 10 September opening through December. The response broke the institution's records: £2.5m taken in a single day, website traffic at 4.7 times the daily average, and an online queue that peaked above 80,000 people, per The Guardian, 1 Jul 2026. Director Nicholas Cullinan called it 'the biggest ticketing day for any exhibition in the British Museum's history'.

The 70-metre embroidery, on loan from France for its first UK appearance in nearly 1,000 years, will show from 10 September 2026 to 11 July 2027. Top-price adult tickets cost £33, with £25 super-off-peak slots in term time, pricing confirmed by the British Museum, 21 May 2026. The museum says tickets for 10 September to 31 December 2026 have sold out, with two further releases planned — in October for January–March, and in January for April–July 2027.

That leaves a five-month gap in which demand has nowhere official to go. Every recent British blockbuster that sold out this fast ended with the host institution improvising capacity — extended hours, on-the-day releases, returns schemes. The British Museum has made no such announcement yet.

3 · The pattern

What museums do after they sell out

A total sellout is rare enough that the precedents are famous. In each modern case, the institution responded to sold-out demand by adding capacity within weeks — but the mechanism differed every time, from dawn queues to round-the-clock opening.

2023
Vermeer at the Rijksmuseum sells out its entire run within days; the museum extends opening hours — running late into the night in the final weeks — and total attendance reaches a record 650,000.
2019
Tutankhamun: Treasures of the Golden Pharaoh at the Saatchi Gallery draws more than 580,000 visitors, adding extended and late-night openings as allocations sell through.
2011
Leonardo da Vinci: Painter at the Court of Milan at the National Gallery sells out its advance allocation; the gallery releases 500 on-the-day tickets each morning, with queues forming before dawn.
1972
Tutankhamun at the British Museum itself: 1.69 million visitors over nine months — still the museum's all-time record — with queues that became a national event in their own right.
3 of 3
In all three modern blockbuster sellouts — Leonardo 2011, Tutankhamun 2019, Vermeer 2023 — the host institution added capacity after selling out. The base rate is unanimous, but the mechanism (day tickets, late hours, extended run) differed every time, and none faced the conservation limits of a 900-year-old textile.

3 of 3 modern blockbuster sellouts ended with the museum adding capacity within the run.

5 · The read

The Hunch read

The weighted case says capacity comes. The precedent is 3 for 3, the run is ten months long, and the museum has already normalised late opening on Fridays. A returns scheme in particular costs the institution nothing and answers the touting pressure that follows every sellout — the National Gallery's 2011 day-ticket queue is the template, and Cullinan, who has framed this as a once-in-a-millennium show, has every incentive to be seen widening the door rather than guarding it. Sixty-three per cent for an announcement inside five weeks is defensible.

The honest counter-case is conservation. A 900-year-old, 70-metre embroidery is not a Vermeer wall — French lenders set strict environmental and footfall conditions, and the viewing route's capacity may be genuinely fixed in a way earlier blockbusters' were not. If the constraint is the object rather than the building, the museum's answer to sold-out demand is silence until the October 2027-dates release — which sits outside this market's window and resolves it NO by default.
