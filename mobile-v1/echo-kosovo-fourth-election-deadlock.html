Kosovo voted again. The deadlock now has a deadline.

Kosovans returned to the polls on 7 June for their second snap election in six months. Vetëvendosje won 47% and 53 seats — but the presidency fight that collapsed the last Assembly is still unresolved.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

Final results give Vetëvendosje 53 of 120 seats; with nine minority MPs it can reach the 61 votes needed to govern — but PDK and AAK refuse any coalition.

2 · The event

What happened

Kosovo held its second snap parliamentary election in six months on 7 June, with turnout of 37.03% — the lowest recorded in the country's history (Al Jazeera, 7 June 2026). Prime Minister Albin Kurti's Vetëvendosje finished first for the fifth consecutive time, taking 47.13% — 382,865 votes and 53 seats — in final results announced ten days after the vote (RFE/RL, June 2026).

The election was forced by a constitutional failure, not a policy dispute. The previous Assembly could not elect a president: an initial attempt on 5 March collapsed for lack of quorum, opposition parties boycotted later sessions, and when the deadline set by the Constitutional Court expired, the Assembly was dissolved on 28 April. Acting president Albulena Haxhiu then called the 7 June vote (Politico, 29 April 2026).

The arithmetic has barely moved. With the backing of nine minority-seat MPs, Vetëvendosje can reach the 61 votes needed to constitute the Assembly and confirm a government (Euronews, 7 June 2026). But the PDK and AAK have categorically refused any coalition with Kurti's party — and electing a president requires a two-thirds quorum that the opposition can deny, the same wall that broke the last parliament.

3 · The pattern

The Echo

Post-election government formation in Kosovo is among the slowest in Europe. Five parliamentary elections since 2017 have produced everything from a five-week handover to a 199-day deadlock that ended in fresh elections.

2025
After the December snap vote, Albin Kurti was sworn in for a third term on 11 February 2026 — about six weeks.
2025
The February election produced 199 days of deadlock: the Assembly needed dozens of attempts just to elect a speaker in August, and no government ever won confidence. The stalemate ended in new elections.
2021
Vetëvendosje's February landslide was followed by a confirmed government on 22 March — 36 days, the fastest of the era.
2019
The October election took until 3 February 2020 to produce the first Kurti government — 120 days.
2017
June's vote led to Ramush Haradinaj's confirmation on 9 September — 90 days.
2 of 5
In 2 of Kosovo's last 5 parliamentary elections, a government won a confidence vote within two months of polling day. The split is real: 36 days at the fastest, 199 days of pure institutional deadlock at the worst.

5 · The read

How to read it

The case for speed rests on the numbers Kurti did not have last time. Fifty-three seats plus nine minority MPs clears 61 — enough to constitute the Assembly, elect a speaker and confirm a government without a single opposition vote. December's precedent is the template: the last snap election produced a sworn-in government in about six weeks, which from a 7 June vote lands within the window of these markets. On that reading the speaker vote passes in July and only the presidency remains stuck.

The honest counter-case is that the presidency, not the premiership, is what broke the last Assembly — and nothing about 7 June fixed it. Electing a president needs a two-thirds quorum the opposition can still deny, PDK and AAK have already refused cooperation, and Kosovo's modal formation time since 2017 is closer to three months than six weeks. The lowest turnout ever recorded weakens every mandate claim in the room. Two of the last five elections delivered a government inside two months; three did not.
