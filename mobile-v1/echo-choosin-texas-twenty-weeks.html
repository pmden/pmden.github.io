Twenty weeks at No. 1. Only a Christmas song has lasted longer.

Ella Langley's 'Choosin' Texas' logged a twentieth week atop the Billboard Hot 100 on the chart dated 5 September, passing the 19-week runs of 'Old Town Road' and 'A Bar Song (Tipsy)'. The four longest reigns before it all ended within three weeks of reaching week sixteen.

3 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

Ella Langley's 'Choosin' Texas' rules the Billboard Hot 100 for a 20th week on the chart dated 5 September, the sole longest run among non-holiday songs, passing the 19 weeks of 'Old Town Road' and 'A Bar Song (Tipsy)'.

Billboard, 1 Sep 2026
2 · The event

What happened

'Choosin' Texas' spent a twentieth week at No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 dated 5 September, which makes it the longest-leading non-holiday single in the chart's history. Billboard notes that only Mariah Carey's 'All I Want for Christmas Is You', at 22 weeks accumulated across successive Decembers, has spent longer at the summit (Billboard, 1 Sep 2026).

The song, which Langley wrote with Luke Dick, Miranda Lambert and Joybeth Taylor, was released in October 2025 and entered the Hot 100 dated 1 November (Billboard, 1 Sep 2026). Its sixteenth week, in August, coincided with country songs holding the entire top five for the first time (Billboard, Aug 2026).

The chart is a weekly count of streams, sales and radio, and the question for the next one, announced on Tuesday 8 September, is arithmetic: whether the margin over the No. 2 song survives another seven days of decay. The record the song has just broken is the evidence for how such runs end.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

Four songs before this one reached sixteen weeks at No. 1. Billboard's own reports record where each run stopped.

2024
November 2024: Shaboozey's 'A Bar Song (Tipsy)' reached a record-tying 19th week at No. 1 and was replaced the following week (Billboard, Nov 2024).
2019
August 2019: Lil Nas X's 'Old Town Road' set the record at 19 weeks, passing the 16-week mark held by two songs, and was dethroned the next chart (Billboard, Aug 2019).
2017
August 2017: 'Despacito' by Luis Fonsi, Daddy Yankee and Justin Bieber tied the record at 16 weeks and did not add a seventeenth (Billboard, Aug 2017).
1996
March 1996: Mariah Carey and Boyz II Men's 'One Sweet Day' became the first Hot 100 No. 1 to reach 16 weeks, a mark that stood for 21 years (Billboard, Aug 2019).
4 of 4
In 4 of the 4 previous runs of sixteen weeks or more at No. 1, the song was gone from the top within three weeks of its sixteenth week: 'One Sweet Day' and 'Despacito' stopped at 16, 'Old Town Road' and 'A Bar Song (Tipsy)' at 19. 'Choosin' Texas' has already outlasted the whole set, which means the base rate has stopped predicting and the weekly margin is all that is left.

Four runs of sixteen weeks or more; none lasted more than three weeks past sixteen.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case for next week is another week. Runs at this length are ended by a challenger's debut rather than by slow decay, and the country top-five sweep in August showed the song's format has few rivals at the moment. That is why the first market sits above 70%. Each additional week is a fresh coin with worse odds, which is why the 25-week market is priced below 30%.

The honest counter-case is the dataset itself: the four longest runs in history ended abruptly and close to where they were, and every one was ended by a release nobody had priced a week earlier. The prediction fails if a major debut lands in the tracking week of 4 to 10 September, or if the song's streaming total, which Billboard reported as still growing at week sixteen, has finally turned.
