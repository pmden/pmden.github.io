England win the series. Carse is still waiting on a verdict.

England beat Pakistan by 194 runs at Lord's to take the series. Brydon Carse was not there: the Cricket Regulator is investigating an incident in Derby.

4 min read

A series won, a squad still one bowler short

England won the second Test at Lord's by 194 runs on the fourth day, bowling Pakistan out for 194 in pursuit of 389 and taking an unassailable 2-0 lead in the three-match series.

(ESPNcricinfo, 30 Aug 2026)

England completed a 194-run win at Lord's on the fourth day, dismissing Pakistan for 194 chasing 389, and now lead the series 2-0 with one Test to play (ESPNcricinfo, 30 Aug 2026). It followed an innings victory at Headingley (Al Jazeera, 27 Aug 2026).

Brydon Carse played no part. The ECB referred the events of the night of 22 August in Derby to the Cricket Regulator and confirmed on 24 August that he was unavailable for selection for the second Test pending the outcome, with Sonny Baker taking his place in the squad (ECB, 24 Aug 2026). Footage circulated of the bowler being led away in handcuffs outside a nightclub in Derby city centre after Durham players had celebrated a County Championship win over Derbyshire; Derbyshire Police said a man in his 30s was arrested on suspicion of being drunk and disorderly and de-arrested shortly afterwards having agreed to leave the area (Sky Sports, 24 Aug 2026).

The clock is short and the incentive has changed. No finding has been published, and the third Test at Edgbaston runs from 9 to 13 September, eighteen days after the incident (Sky Sports, 24 Aug 2026). With the series decided, a selection that would once have carried a sporting cost now carries almost none.

The Pattern

Inclusion rule, fixed before the outcome: every case since 2017 in which an England men's international was removed from, or made unavailable for, a named England squad while a governing-body conduct investigation was open. Injuries, rest, ordinary selection calls and sanctions imposed after a completed investigation are out. The measured outcome is single: did the player appear for England again within four weeks of being stood down?

2017
Ben Stokes was withdrawn from the one-day squad against West Indies after his arrest in Bristol, lost the vice-captaincy and was left out of the Ashes tour. He did not play for England again until the following year (ESPNcricinfo, Sep 2017). No.
2019
Alex Hales was removed from the provisional World Cup squad and from all England selection after a 21-day ban for recreational drug use, weeks before the tournament (ESPNcricinfo, Apr 2019). No.
2020
Jofra Archer was excluded from the second Test against West Indies for breaching bio-secure protocol, isolated for five days, was fined and formally warned, and played the third Test eight days later (Sky Sports, Jul 2020). Yes.
2021
Ollie Robinson was suspended from international cricket pending investigation on his Test debut, missed the rest of the New Zealand series, received an eight-match ban with five suspended, and returned against India on 4 August, eight weeks later (ESPNcricinfo, Jun 2021). No.
1 of 4
One of the four comparable cases since 2017 saw the player back inside four weeks: Archer in 2020, whose breach was admitted at once and dealt with at a hearing within days. The other three ran longer than the window Carse has. The distinguishing feature is not the seriousness of the conduct but whether the facts were settled quickly: Archer's were, and a fine and a warning followed in 48 hours. Carse has eighteen days between the incident and the Edgbaston team sheet, and the regulator has published nothing.

1 of the 4 England players stood down mid-investigation since 2017 was back in the side within four weeks. The other three missed at least the rest of the series they were withdrawn from.

What this pattern excludes

Alex Hales, Bristol 2017
He faced no charge and his removal followed a blanket decision covering both players while police worked, rather than a disciplinary case opened against him.
Brydon Carse, betting ban 2024
The three-month suspension, served between 28 May and 28 August 2024, followed a completed investigation in which he admitted 303 charges; there was no live inquiry and no mid-series withdrawal.
Ben Stokes' Crown Court trial, 2018
The outcome was produced by a jury rather than a cricket panel, so the deciding mechanism is different even though the underlying incident is the same.
Bio-secure and Covid absences, 2020-21
Withdrawals for testing, isolation or bubble rotation involved no conduct investigation and no disciplinary decision-maker.

The Read

The weighted case is absence. The record says these cases run past the next team sheet unless the facts settle in days, and here they have not: the ECB has referred the matter out, the regulator has said nothing, and the police element resolved without charge but also without a public account. England no longer need him either. The series is won, Sonny Baker has been given the shirt, and a selector who leaves a bowler out of a dead rubber pending an inquiry is criticised by nobody.

The honest counter is that Carse was de-arrested the same night and no charge followed, which is the closest thing in this sample to Archer's position in 2020: an admitted, self-contained breach with no external process to wait for. If the regulator concludes there is no case to answer, there is nothing left to hold him out for, and England have used the third Test of a decided series to give bowlers overs before a winter. The prediction fails if a clearance is published in the first week of September; watch the ECB news page rather than the squad announcement.
