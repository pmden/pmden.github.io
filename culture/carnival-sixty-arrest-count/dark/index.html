London29 Aug 2026

The Met widens facial recognition for Carnival's sixtieth year. Three of the last seven counts passed 400.

The Met set out its plan for the 60th Notting Hill Carnival on 28 August, with more live facial recognition cameras inside the event and on the approaches, after 528 arrests last year. The force's end-of-Carnival total has passed 400 in three of the last seven editions on the record.

4 min read

Rescued in March, policed in August

The Met publishes its policing plan for the 60th Notting Hill Carnival, with additional live facial recognition cameras at the event and on the approaches to it, and sets out 2025's outcomes, including 528 arrests.

(Metropolitan Police, 28 Aug 2026)

Europe's largest street festival reached its sixtieth year only after City Hall found the money. On 27 March 2026 the Mayor of London announced £4.66 million in additional funding, on top of £946,300 already committed, to pay for barriers, stewarding and crowd management plans required by an independent review commissioned in 2024 (Mayor of London, 27 Mar 2026). The 2025 event had gone ahead on £958,000 of one-off money from City Hall, Kensington and Chelsea and Westminster after the first part of that review (Notting Hill Carnival Ltd, 9 Jul 2025).

The Met set out its operation on 28 August. It said last year's proactive approach had brought a reduction in serious violence, that live facial recognition had identified 98 people in 2025 leading to 61 arrests and checks on 30 registered sex offenders, and that additional cameras would be deployed this year both at Carnival and on the approaches. Its summary of 2025's outcomes begins with 528 arrests across the two main days (Metropolitan Police, 28 Aug 2026).

The number the force publishes after the bank holiday has become the shorthand by which the event is judged, and it moves with policing tactics as much as with behaviour. Facial recognition on the approach routes turns existing wanted-person cases into arrests that would not otherwise be counted at Carnival, which is why the total can rise in a year the Met itself describes as calmer.

The Pattern

Inclusion rule, fixed before the outcome: every Notting Hill Carnival held on the streets since 2015 for which the Metropolitan Police's final arrest total is recorded on a whitelisted page, using the force's own statement where it is available and the Guardian's report of the force's figure otherwise. The test is the one the first market asks: did the total exceed 400. The 2018 and 2019 editions are omitted because no whitelisted page recording their totals was found, not because they were held off the streets; 2020 and 2021 were not held. Seven editions qualify; all are shown.

2025
The Met's summary of 2025 policing outcomes, published with its 2026 plan, records 528 arrests across the two main days and 61 arrests from live facial recognition (Metropolitan Police, 28 Aug 2026).
2024
The Met's end-of-Carnival statement records 349 arrests over the weekend, 102 on Sunday and 247 on Monday, and eight stabbings; an interim figure of 334 had been reported on the Tuesday morning (Metropolitan Police, 27 Aug 2024; Independent, 27 Aug 2024).
2023
275 arrests over the two days, 165 of them on the Monday, for offences including weapons, assaults on officers, drugs and sexual offences (Guardian, 30 Aug 2023).
2022
The return after two cancelled years: 209 arrests by early Tuesday, including 46 for assault, 36 for drugs and 33 for offensive weapons, in a year marked by a fatal stabbing (Guardian, 30 Aug 2022).
2017
313 arrests across the two days, down from 454 the year before, after a pre-Carnival crackdown in which 656 other arrests were made in the preceding three weeks (Guardian, 29 Aug 2017).
2016
More than 450 arrests, then a record, with the commander saying the Psychoactive Substances Act had inflated the count; the Police Federation called for a review (Guardian, 30 Aug 2016; Guardian, 29 Aug 2017 gives the final 454).
2015
The Met's updated total of 407, including 106 for drugs and 57 for bladed weapons, which the Mayor called too many; the force said it would review its policing of the event (Guardian, 1 Sep 2015).
3 of 7
Three of the last seven Carnival arrest totals on the record exceeded 400: 407 in 2015, 454 in 2016 and 528 in 2025. The four below it include the 2017 crackdown year and the three post-pandemic editions of 2022 to 2024, whose totals rose each year. The count tracks how the event is policed at least as much as how it is behaved at, and 2026 adds cameras on the approach routes.

3 of the 7 editions on the record produced a total above 400: 2015, 2016 and 2025. The three below 300 were the crackdown year of 2017 and the two post-pandemic returns. The run since 2022 rises every year, 209, 275, 349, 528, and the last step was the largest.

What this pattern excludes

The 2018 and 2019 Carnivals
Held on the streets, but no whitelisted page recording the Met's final totals was found after searching the Met newsroom by date and the Guardian's day archives; they are omitted rather than filled from memory.
The 2020 and 2021 Carnivals
Not held on the streets, so there was no policing operation and no end-of-Carnival arrest total.
Arrests from pre-Carnival warrant operations
Reported separately by the force in the days before the event, as with the 656 of 2017, so including them would mix two different counts.
Stop and search totals
A different measure that moves with tactics rather than with the number of people taken into custody.

The Read

The weighted case was another high number, and the count delivered it: 636, the highest on the record, with 193 arrests on the approaches and 121 from facial recognition. The two mechanical drivers pointed the same way from the start. A larger operation funded by the March package and a wider camera deployment reaching the approach routes convert wanted-person cases into Carnival arrests that would not otherwise be counted there. That is why the record allows the count to climb in a year the Met describes as its second with markedly less serious violence.

The counter-case, that 528 was an outlier and that barriers and stewarding prevent the disorder that generates arrests, did not hold for the headline number, though it may hold for the injuries behind it: the force reported two knife assaults, neither life-threatening. The funding market is the one still open. City Hall confirmed money in July of 2025 and March of 2026, each time months into the event year and each time as one-off support; a commitment for 2027 before December would be a change of habit, which is why it sits at one in five.
