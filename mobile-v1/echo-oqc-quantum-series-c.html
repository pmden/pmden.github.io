Britain's biggest quantum round lands. The ownership question follows.

Oxford Quantum Circuits has closed a £260m Series C, the largest private quantum computing raise in European history. The money is British-anchored; the pattern for what happens to UK deep-tech champions afterwards is not reassuring.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

OQC announces a £260m Series C, the largest fundraise ever completed by a European quantum computing company.

OQC, 3 Jun 2026
2 · The event

What happened

On 3 June 2026 Oxford Quantum Circuits announced an oversubscribed £260m ($350m) Series C, which the company describes as the largest private fundraise by any quantum computing firm in Europe. Bullhound Capital led the round, with the British Business Bank committing £100m and Oxford Science Enterprises and Chevron participating OQC, 3 Jun 2026.

Founded in 2017 as a University of Oxford spinout, OQC builds superconducting quantum computers and already operates systems in the UK, US, Japan and Spain.

The state's £100m holding makes OQC an explicit test of whether Britain can keep a strategic technology champion at home.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

Britain is exceptional at founding deep-tech companies and historically poor at keeping them. The recent record of UK deep-tech champions who reached escape-velocity scale points mostly one way: foreign ownership or a foreign listing, with one partial exception.

2024
Graphcore, once valued at $2.8bn after raising over $700m, is acquired by SoftBank for a reported sum around $600m, below the capital invested.
2024
Wayve raises $1.05bn in a SoftBank-led round, the largest ever for a European AI company; it stays headquartered in London but on Japanese capital.
2016
Arm, Britain's flagship chip designer, is sold to SoftBank for £24bn; when it returns to public markets in 2023 it lists on Nasdaq, not London.
2014
DeepMind is acquired by Google for around £400m, moving the UK's premier AI lab under US ownership before its breakthroughs arrive.
3 of 4
Three of the four UK deep-tech champions above ended under foreign ownership or listed abroad. The pattern is not unanimous: Wayve has stayed independent and London-based so far, but it took a Japanese-led megaround to do it. British Business Bank money in OQC's round is a deliberate attempt to break the sequence.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case: the momentum behind UK deep tech in mid-2026 is genuine, not statistical noise. June's £1bn month, the state co-investing at £100m scale, and AI-and-deep-tech dominance of deal flow all suggest the follow-on question, another £200m+ UK round within weeks, resolves YES more often than not. The capital is present, the pipeline of late-stage UK AI and science companies is deeper than at any point this decade, and megarounds cluster.

The honest counter-case: single months distort. June 2026 was a record precisely because rounds of this size are rare, and July and August are historically quiet months for European venture announcements as firms pause for summer. On the bigger question the pattern is against sentiment: raising the money has never been Britain's problem, and every previous champion raised plenty before ownership moved abroad. A £260m round changes OQC's runway, not the gravitational pull of US capital markets and acquirers.
