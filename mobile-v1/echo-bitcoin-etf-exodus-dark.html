The ETFs turned seller. Bitcoin hunts a floor.

Bitcoin opened July at $57,950, a 21-month low and roughly half its October 2025 peak, after the worst month on record for US spot ETFs saw about $4bn leave.

4 min read

What happened

Bitcoin fell to $57,950 on Wednesday, its lowest level in 652 days, opening the second half of 2026 roughly $47,000 below where it stood a year ago.

(Bloomberg, 1 Jul 2026)

Bitcoin touched $57,950 on 1 July, its lowest print in 652 days, after closing June down about 20% (Bloomberg, 1 July 2026). The slide dates precisely to the Federal Reserve's 17 June meeting, where new chair Kevin Warsh held rates at 3.50-3.75% and took this year's expected cut off the table; within a week Bitcoin had broken below $60,000 for the first time since 2024, and it now sits roughly 54% beneath the October 2025 peak near $125,600 (Bloomberg, 24 June 2026).

The distinctive feature of this drawdown is who is selling. US spot Bitcoin ETFs bled about $4 billion in June, the worst calendar month since the products launched in January 2024, following a record streak of 13 consecutive daily net outflows from mid-May; over the full losing streak more than $6 billion has been withdrawn, and 2026 net flows have turned negative for the first time in the products' history. BlackRock's IBIT alone accounted for roughly three quarters of June's outflow.

Corporate holders added to the unease. Strategy, the largest corporate Bitcoin treasury, disclosed a sale of 32 Bitcoin in June, its first disposal since 2022, with the price trading below the company's average cost basis of $75,699 (Bloomberg, 1 June 2026). One analyst summarised the structural shift: the marginal buyer is no longer a retail investor but an ETF allocator or corporate treasury, and in June both were on the other side.

The Pattern

Bitcoin has traded 50% below a cycle peak four times before. The halfway mark is emotionally loaded but historically ambiguous: twice it was close to the bottom and new highs followed within months, and twice it was merely the midpoint of a collapse that ran far deeper.

2022
Fifty percent below November 2021's $69,000 peak by mid-2022. Not the floor: the slide continued to $15,500 in November, a 77% total drawdown, and the old peak stood until early 2024.
2021
Fell 55% from April's $64,800 to about $29,000 in July. That was the bottom: a new record above $69,000 arrived within four months.
2020
The COVID crash halved the price in two days to about $3,900 in March. It was the low of the cycle; Bitcoin ended the year above $29,000.
2018
Halved from December 2017's $19,700 by February 2018. The real bottom came ten months later at $3,200, an 84% total drawdown, and recovery to the old peak took three years.
2 of 4
In 2 of 4 previous 50% drawdowns, down-half proved to be near the low and new records followed within months. In the other 2, the decline roughly doubled from there. The pattern is a genuine coin-split, and this is the first drawdown where ETF flows, not exchanges, set the marginal price.

Four prior 50% drawdowns: twice the halfway mark was roughly the bottom, twice the price went on to fall 77-84% from peak.

The Read

The weighted case is that the flow picture has to turn before the price does, and it has not turned. The seller is structural: ETF allocators de-risking against a Fed that may hike into year-end, a corporate treasury sector sitting on losses against a $75,699 average cost basis, and no obvious marginal buyer beneath them. That is why the market puts just 41% on July inflows and concentrates the price distribution in the $55,000-$65,000 band; the 2018 and 2022 precedents say halfway down is where the second leg starts.

The counter-case is that record outflows are themselves the capitulation signal. Whale wallets absorbed roughly 270,000 coins during the ETF exodus, the exact transfer from weak to strong hands that marked the 2021 and 2020 lows, and a single dovish surprise from the Warsh Fed would flip the flow overnight. In both prior cases where the halfway mark held, the recovery to new highs took under a year. If July's first fortnight shows even modest net inflows, the 8% priced above $75,000 will have been the wrong tail to sell.
