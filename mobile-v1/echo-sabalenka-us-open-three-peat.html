US Open04 Sep 2026

Sabalenka is two titles deep. The last three defending champions lost by the fourth round.

Aryna Sabalenka, the top seed and champion in 2024 and 2025, is through to the third round in straight sets.

3 min read

What happened

Defending champion Aryna Sabalenka won her second-round match in straight sets at the 2026 US Open, following a first-round win over Camila Osorio, to reach the third round as the top seed.

(US Open, 2 Sep 2026)

Aryna Sabalenka moved into the third round with a straight-sets win on 2 September (US Open, 2 Sep 2026), after beating Camila Osorio in a first round that included a wobbly second set (US Open, 31 Aug 2026). She is the No. 1 seed, drawn in the top half with Jessica Pegula and Linda Noskova (US Open, 27 Aug 2026).

She is going for a third consecutive title, having won in 2024 and retained it in 2025. That defence was itself unusual: in each of the three years before it, the reigning champion lost by the fourth round.

The women's singles final is on Saturday 12 September. A third title would be the first women's three-peat at the US Open since Serena Williams won in 2012, 2013 and 2014.

The Pattern

The last four defending champions in the women's singles and what happened to each in New York the following year.

2025
September 2025: Sabalenka retained the title, beating Amanda Anisimova in the final, the first successful defence since 2014 (US Open, 31 Aug 2026).
2024
September 2024: Coco Gauff, the 2023 champion, lost to Emma Navarro 6-3, 4-6, 6-3 in the fourth round, hitting 19 double faults (ESPN, 1 Sep 2024).
2023
September 2023: Iga Swiatek, the 2022 champion, lost to Jelena Ostapenko in the fourth round (ESPN, 1 Sep 2024).
2022
August 2022: Emma Raducanu, the 2021 champion, lost to Alizé Cornet in the first round, only the third defending US Open champion to go out in round one (ESPN, 1 Sep 2024).
1 of 4
In 1 of the last 4 years the defending women's champion kept the US Open title, and it was Sabalenka in 2025. Gauff in 2024 and Swiatek in 2023 lost in the fourth round; Raducanu in 2022 lost in the first. A third title in a row would be the first since Serena Williams's 2012 to 2014 run.

One successful defence in four, and it was Sabalenka's own.

The Read

The weighted case against a three-peat is simple arithmetic: seven rounds, a top seed who has already dropped a set in New York, and a recent record in which three of four defences ended early. The market prices the title at roughly one in three, well above the 2022 to 2025 base rate of one in four because the one success in the set was hers.

The counter-case is that the base rate counts three different players and Sabalenka is not Raducanu. She has reached the final in each of the last three years at Flushing Meadows and the top half of the draw has lost none of its seeds so far. The prediction of an early exit fails if the second set against Osorio was the only wobble; the title prediction fails on any hard-court upset of the kind the tournament has produced every year in this set.
