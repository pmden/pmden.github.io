Wales03 Sep 2026

Wales puts £145m behind ending two-year waits. That queue has grown in two of the last four months.

The Welsh Government announced £145 million on 2 September to cut waiting times, tied to health boards changing how they work. The number it has to move is published monthly, and over the last four official months it has gone up as often as down.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

The Welsh Government announces £145 million of extra NHS investment this financial year, with £100 million to reduce waiting times, £25 million for surgical and diagnostic hubs and £20 million for estate maintenance.

Welsh Government, 2 Sep 2026
2 · The event

£145 million, and a condition attached to it

The Health and Care Minister, Mabon ap Gwynfor, announced £145 million of extra investment this financial year: £100 million to reduce waiting times, £25 million for new surgical and diagnostic hubs and £20 million for essential estate maintenance. The stated aims are to end two-year waits for treatment and bring the overall waiting list to pre-pandemic levels before the end of this Senedd term (Welsh Government, 2 Sep 2026).

The Minister attached a condition and a warning to the money in the same sentence. Waiting two years for planned care, he said, is not acceptable, and the investment is tied to health boards changing how they work permanently so the progress he expects to see does not slip back as it has in the past. The named mechanisms are weekend operating lists, earlier clinical review of referrals, patient-initiated follow-up in place of routine appointments, and a rule that the longest waiters are treated next unless there is a clinical reason not to.

The measurement is monthly and it is public. The accredited official statistics released on 20 August put just under 698,400 patient pathways open in June 2026, with just over 95,100 waiting more than a year and just under 4,100 waiting more than two years, the last figure 94.2 per cent below the peak in March 2022; emergency department performance in July was 64.1 per cent seen within four hours against a 95 per cent target (Welsh Government, 20 Aug 2026).

3 · The pattern

What the two-year queue has actually done this year

Inclusion rule, fixed before the count: this pattern uses only the official month in each edition of the NHS activity and performance summary published on gov.wales, taking every edition from 23 April 2026 to 20 August 2026. That gives five official months, February to June 2026, and four month-on-month changes. The test is direction: did the number of pathways waiting more than 104 weeks fall. Provisional months are excluded, because each is revised before it becomes official and using both would count the same month twice on two different numbers.

2026
February, published 23 April: just over 4,500 pathways waiting more than two years, out of just under 688,000 open pathways, with just under 105,600 waiting more than a year.
2026
March, published 21 May: just under 2,600, the lowest of the five months, with the total list also at its lowest, just under 666,700. A fall of roughly 1,900 in a single month.
2026
April and May, published 18 June and 23 July: back up to just under 3,700 and then just over 4,200. Two consecutive monthly increases, while the total list rose to just over 689,700.
2026
June, published 20 August: just under 4,100, a small fall, with the total list at its highest of the five months at just under 698,400 and one-year waits at just over 95,100.
2 of 4
2 of 4, which is as close to no signal as a base rate gets, and that is the honest finding rather than a failure to find one. Four changes over five months is a very thin series, the months are seasonal, and one of the four moves is far larger than the other three. What the series does say clearly is that the two-year figure is volatile in both directions while the total list has moved steadily one way.

2 of the 4 month-on-month changes were falls. Over the same five months the total waiting list rose in three months out of four, from just under 666,700 in March to just under 698,400 in June.

What this pattern excludes

The commitment to return the waiting list to pre-pandemic levels
It cannot be scored. The accredited series does not carry a published February 2020 figure that a market could resolve against, so any number put on the pre-pandemic level would be a choice made by whoever was doing the resolving. The two-year count is used instead because it is published monthly and unambiguous.
Provisional months
Every edition publishes one official month and one provisional month, and the provisional figure is revised before it becomes official. Counting both would put the same month in the series twice at two different values.
Individual patients rather than pathways
The releases state that pathways are not patients, because one person can hold several open pathways. February management data put patients at about 543,400 against just under 688,000 pathways. Mixing the two measures would change the answer without changing the facts.

5 · The read

The read

The money arrives too late to show up in the July figures, which is the first thing to hold on to. An announcement made on 2 September cannot change a queue measured in July, so these three markets are about momentum rather than about the policy. On momentum the series is genuinely split: two falls and two rises in four months, with the single largest move a fall of about 1,900 in March that has since been given back in full.

The total-list market is the one that carries the argument. The two-year number is small enough to be moved by a handful of health boards clearing a handful of lists, but the total has risen in three of the last four official months and stands about 31,700 higher in June than in March. A programme that promises to end two-year waits while the list underneath keeps growing is promising to change the shape of the queue rather than its size, and both can be true at once. The Minister's own line, that progress must not slip back as it has in the past, is a recognition that the March number was not held. Watch whether the two-year figure and the total move in the same direction in the next release: they have not done so in three of the last four months.
