Gaza09 Jun 2026

Cairo opens phase two. Phase one never closed.

Palestinian factions and mediators from Egypt, Qatar and Turkey convened in Cairo to negotiate the second phase of the Gaza plan — eight months into a ceasefire that has been violated on most of the days it has existed.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

Talks on phase two of the Gaza agreement opened in Cairo as Israel pressed deeper into the enclave; disarmament versus withdrawal remains the core standoff.

Al Jazeera, 9 Jun 2026
2 · The event

A second phase for a first phase still in dispute

Palestinian factions arrived in Cairo in the week of 8 June to meet Egyptian, Qatari and Turkish mediators on the second phase of the ceasefire agreement, working from a roadmap prepared by Nikolay Mladenov, the Board of Peace high representative for Gaza (Al Jazeera, 9 June 2026). Hamas has signalled it would hand governance to a technocratic national committee and keep weapons off Gaza's streets, but will not surrender its arms outright, tying any decommissioning to an Israeli withdrawal. Israel and Mladenov condition the next phase on disarmament. Hamas and other factions insist Israel must first meet phase-one commitments on humanitarian relief and aid trucks.

The ceasefire being extended exists more on paper than on the ground. Israeli operations have killed 1,005 Palestinians since the truce took effect on 10 October 2025, according to Gaza's health ministry (Al Jazeera, 17 June 2026). Around two million civilians live under Hamas administration in roughly 40% of the strip, with the Israel Defense Forces controlling the rest; advances of the 'Yellow Line', marked with cement blocks towards populated areas of Gaza City, have triggered new displacement (UK Home Office bulletin, June 2026).

Pressure on the talks is rising from outside the room. A UN commission of inquiry found on 23 June that Israel continues to deliberately target and kill Palestinian children — one child a day on average since the ceasefire began (Al Jazeera, 23 June 2026). UN humanitarian reporting warns that fuel shortfalls are forcing partners to ration life-saving services. The mediators have set no public deadline, which makes the next month the market's real test: movement, stalemate or collapse.

3 · The pattern

Phase two is where Gaza deals stall

Every multi-stage arrangement between Israel and Hamas since 2012 has followed the same trajectory: a first phase that partially delivers, then a second phase that is never reached. The record is unusually clean — and it is the single most important number in this story.

2025
The January 2025 ceasefire: phase one ran from 19 January to 1 March, exchanging 33 Israeli hostages for around 1,900 Palestinian prisoners. Phase two was never substantively negotiated; Israel resumed full military operations on 18 March.
2023
The November truce: 7 days, around 105 hostages freed in staged exchanges. Extension talks collapsed and the war resumed on 1 December.
2014
After the 50-day war, Cairo talks on a durable arrangement — port access, easing the blockade — were repeatedly postponed and fizzled without agreement.
2012
The Egypt-brokered November understanding promised an easing of movement restrictions; the easing clauses were never fully implemented and no follow-on phase materialised.
0 of 4
No multi-stage Israel-Hamas agreement since 2012 has advanced past its first phase — 0 of 4. That unbroken record is exactly why mediators built the Board of Peace structure this time, and exactly why the market prices agreement low.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case: the 0-for-4 record meets positions that have not moved in six months — disarmament-first against withdrawal-first — and a phase-one scoreboard that both sides cite as the other's breach. Even the mediators frame the current work as roadmap discussion rather than deal drafting, and there is no forcing deadline on the calendar before August. Seventeen percent for a signed phase two is not pessimism; it is the base rate doing its work.

The honest counter: this arrangement is structurally different from its four predecessors. There is a standing Board of Peace with an empowered envoy, three mediators in one room rather than shuttling, and Hamas has already conceded the governance question — something it never did in 2014 or 2025. Deals nobody expected have landed twice in this war under concentrated US pressure, and the October 2025 agreement itself was one of them. If Washington decides it wants a phase-two signature before September, the probability moves fast.
