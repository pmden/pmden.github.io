Bank of Japan04 Sep 2026

Tokyo spent 15 trillion yen in a month. The Bank has answered once in four.

Japan's finance ministry disclosed 15.4 trillion yen of yen-buying between 30 July and 26 August, after the first joint operation with Washington since 2011.

4 min read

What happened

Japan's Ministry of Finance disclosed foreign exchange intervention of 15,399.3 billion yen for the period 30 July to 26 August 2026, its largest monthly total on record.

(Ministry of Finance, Japan, 28 Aug 2026)

The Ministry of Finance disclosed on 28 August that it bought 15,399.3 billion yen in the market between 30 July and 26 August (Ministry of Finance, 28 Aug 2026). The operation began with a joint intervention confirmed by Washington and Tokyo on 3 August, when the yen had fallen to forty-year lows; the currency rose to 155.20 per dollar in the days after, and finance minister Satsuki Katayama's ministry said it would not hesitate to intervene jointly again (Al Jazeera, 3 Aug 2026) (Ministry of Finance, 3 Aug 2026).

The Bank of Japan's policy rate stands at 1%, a 31-year high reached in June (Al Jazeera, 3 Aug 2026). Its next policy meeting is on 17–18 September. On the sidelines of the G20 finance meeting in North Carolina, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent met Governor Kazuo Ueda and the Treasury said afterwards that it strongly supported Japan's decisive market and monetary steps to correct the yen's undervaluation, noting yen weakness was adding to inflation (Bloomberg, 1 Sep 2026).

The mechanism is the rate gap. Intervention buys time; only a narrower gap between Japanese and US rates changes the direction of the currency. That is why the market has begun to price a September hike, and why the history of what the Bank did after past interventions matters.

The Pattern

Japan has intervened to move the yen in four episodes since 1998. The dataset is what the Bank of Japan did at its next policy meeting.

2024
April–July 2024: the ministry spent a record 9.8 trillion yen at the end of April and a further 5.5 trillion in July (Ministry of Finance, monthly releases). On 31 July 2024 the Bank raised its policy rate to around 0.25% and set out a plan to halve bond purchases (Bank of Japan, 31 Jul 2024).
2022
September 2022: Japan bought yen on 22 September for the first time in 24 years. At its next meeting on 28 October the Bank kept the short-term rate at minus 0.1% and yield curve control unchanged (Bank of Japan, 28 Oct 2022).
2011
August 2011: the ministry sold yen on 4 August to check its rise. The Bank ended its policy meeting a day early and expanded its asset-purchase programme from 40 to 50 trillion yen the same day (Bank of Japan, 4 Aug 2011).
1998
June 1998: the US bought $833 million of yen alongside Japan on 17 June (New York Fed, 30 Jul 1998). On 9 September the Bank of Japan cut its overnight call rate target to 0.25% (Bank of Japan, 9 Sep 1998).
1 of 4
In 1 of the 4 intervention episodes since 1998 the Bank of Japan tightened at its next policy meeting: July 2024. In 1998 it cut, in 2011 it eased the same day, and in 2022 it held. The one hike came when intervention was defending a weak yen and inflation was above target, which is the configuration now, and it is the most recent case in the set.

One tightening in four post-intervention meetings, and it was the most recent.

The Read

The weighted case is a hike, and it rests on two things the earlier episodes lacked: the Bank is already in a tightening cycle, with five increases behind it, and the United States has publicly asked for decisive monetary steps rather than merely tolerating intervention. Governor Ueda has said the board will pay greater attention to upside price risks. That is why the market sits above 70% despite a base rate of one in four.

The counter-case is Ueda's other sentence: five hikes already, and the cumulative effect still to be judged. The 2022 precedent shows the Bank willing to let the ministry carry the burden alone, and 15 trillion yen in a month suggests the ministry is prepared to keep carrying it. The prediction fails if the board decides intervention has bought enough time to wait for the October outlook report.
