World Cup03 Jul 2026

Nagelsmann is gone in four days. Klopp's phone is ringing.

Germany's first-ever World Cup shootout defeat, to Paraguay in the round of 32, cost Julian Nagelsmann his job within four days. Jürgen Klopp has confirmed talks over the succession.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

Julian Nagelsmann resigned as Germany head coach four days after the shock last-32 exit to Paraguay, with Jürgen Klopp reported ready to take over.

Sky Sports, 3 Jul 2026
2 · The event

A shootout Germany had never lost, then a resignation

Julio Enciso headed Paraguay in front against the run of play in Boston on 29 June; Kai Havertz levelled shortly after half-time. In extra time, Jonathan Tah's header was ruled out after a VAR review for a soft foul on goalkeeper Orlando Gill. In the shootout, Gill saved from Havertz and Nick Woltemade before Tah blazed over: Paraguay won 4-3, and Germany lost a World Cup penalty shootout for the first time in their history (Sky Sports, 29 Jun 2026).

Nagelsmann initially said he would love to continue, describing the exit as bitter and Paraguay as ultra-defensive (ESPN, 29 Jun 2026). The position did not hold. Under intense pressure from the DFB board, he resigned on Friday 3 July, four days after the defeat (Sky Sports, 3 Jul 2026).

Jürgen Klopp has confirmed talks over the vacancy (ESPN, Jul 2026), and reports say he has agreed in principle, pending the terms of his departure from Red Bull, with a remit covering playing style and youth development. No official DFB unveiling has taken place. The open question is not whether German football moves on — it is how fast the federation signs the successor.

3 · The pattern

How fast does the DFB replace a Bundestrainer?

Germany does not leave the national team job open. Across the last four changes of head coach, the DFB confirmed a successor within six weeks in three cases — the 2021 handover being the slower, pre-planned exception.

2023
Hansi Flick sacked on 10 September; Julian Nagelsmann confirmed 12 days later, on 22 September.
2021
Joachim Löw announced his departure on 9 March; Hansi Flick was confirmed on 25 May — roughly 11 weeks, the outlier in a planned transition.
2006
Jürgen Klinsmann stepped down after the World Cup; assistant Joachim Löw was promoted within days in July 2006.
2004
Rudi Völler resigned on 24 June after the Euro 2004 group exit; Klinsmann was appointed on 26 July — about a month.
3 of 4
Three of the last four Germany head-coach successions were confirmed within six weeks of the vacancy opening. The split is honest: when the exit is a crisis rather than a plan, the DFB moves fast.

5 · The read

The Hunch read

The weighted case is speed. Crisis successions at the DFB resolve in days to weeks — Löw in 2006, Nagelsmann himself in 2023 — and this vacancy comes with a successor who has already confirmed talks and, per multiple reports, agreed in principle. Klopp is out of club management, publicly available, and the German federation has every incentive to end the story before the new season. An 84 per cent call on confirmation by 7 August reflects that alignment.

The honest counter is that the remaining obstacle is not football but contracts. Klopp holds a global role at Red Bull, and unwinding it involves a company with no incentive to hurry. The 2021 case shows a DFB process can stretch past two months when the terms are complicated, and a deal reported as done is not a deal announced. Sixteen per cent on NO is a position about paperwork, not appetite.
