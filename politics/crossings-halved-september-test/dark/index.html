Immigration03 Sep 2026

Burnham says summer crossings halved. September tests the claim.

In his Commons statement on 1 September the Prime Minister said summer arrivals had halved on last year and 2026 is the lowest year to date since 2021.

4 min read

What happened

In a Commons statement the Prime Minister says numbers arriving over the summer halved on last year, making this year to date the lowest for crossings since 2021.

(GOV.UK, 1 Sep 2026)

The Prime Minister told the Commons that extra French police numbers on the beaches were delivering results, that arrivals over the summer had halved on last year, making the year to date the lowest for crossings since 2021, and that French cooperation had prevented more than 48,000 people crossing (GOV.UK, 1 Sep 2026). Unusually for a political claim about migration, both halves of it can be checked against a single published file.

The Home Office publishes a daily and weekly time series of small boat arrivals, updated every day for the last seven days and weekly on a Friday for the full run (GOV.UK, Home Office time series). Arrivals from 1 June to 27 August 2026 total 7,249 against 14,191 across the same dates in 2025, which is 51 per cent of last year. The year to 27 August stands at 16,391 against 29,003 in 2025, 19,819 in 2024, 19,801 in 2023 and 25,065 in 2022. The only recent year lower at this point was 2021, at 12,482. The statement holds on both counts.

The same file shows why the next month matters. The week to 23 August recorded 250 arrivals in three boats, one of the quieter weeks of the summer, and then 27 August alone recorded 494 in seven boats. This is a series with long flat stretches and sudden spikes, driven by weather windows rather than by anything that moves smoothly: 14 of the 34 completed weeks to 23 August carried more than 500 arrivals, and the thirty days to 31 August carried 1,987 in all (GOV.UK, Home Office time series).

The Pattern

Inclusion rule, fixed before the count: a case is a public statement by the Prime Minister or a Home Office minister that puts a proportion on a fall in small boat arrivals against the year before. The record starts in 2023, the first year arrivals fell on the year before, and a statement made within thirty days of one already counted is folded into it. The test is single: were arrivals in the thirty days after the statement below the same thirty days a year earlier by at least the proportion claimed, on the Home Office daily series. Four statements meet the rule, the minimum Hunch publishes on and below the six needed to show History.

2024
On 12 June 2024 Rishi Sunak said on Sky's leaders' programme that crossings were down by a third last year and by a little less over the last twelve months; PA Media put the twelve-month fall at 26.4 per cent. From 13 June to 12 July 2024 arrivals were 2,838 against 4,286 a year earlier, a fall of 33.8 per cent (Home Office daily series).
2023
On 15 November 2023, after the Supreme Court's Rwanda judgment, Rishi Sunak said crossings to the UK were down by a third. From 16 November to 15 December 2023 arrivals were 2,068 against 2,989 a year earlier, a fall of 30.8 per cent, short of the third claimed (Home Office daily series).
2023
On 5 September 2023 the immigration minister Robert Jenrick said arrivals were down 20 per cent on the year before and August's fall was more than a third. From 6 September to 5 October 2023 arrivals were 3,650 against 6,014 a year earlier, a fall of 39.3 per cent (Home Office daily series).
2023
On 5 June 2023 Rishi Sunak said at Dover that arrivals were down 20 per cent this year and the plan to stop the boats was starting to work. From 6 June to 5 July 2023 arrivals were 3,823 against 2,916 a year earlier, a rise of 31.1 per cent (Home Office daily series).
2 of 4
Two of the four ministerial claims since 2023 were matched by the thirty days that followed: Jenrick's 20 per cent in September 2023 (a 39 per cent fall) and Sunak's third in June 2024 (a 34 per cent fall). Sunak's 20 per cent at Dover in June 2023 was followed by a 31 per cent rise, and his third after the Supreme Court judgment in November 2023 by a 31 per cent fall, just short. Four cases is the full record and below the six Hunch requires to show History.

Two of the four claims were matched by the following thirty days, in September 2023 and June 2024. The June 2023 claim was followed by a rise on the year and the November 2023 claim by a fall just short of the third promised.

What this pattern excludes

The Prime Minister's remarks of 7 December 2023
Made 22 days after the 15 November statement, so folded into it under the thirty-day rule. Counted separately it would add a true case: 347 arrivals from 8 December to 6 January against 1,625 a year earlier.
Home Office data releases reported as falls without a ministerial statement: the 2023 total of 29,437, 36 per cent down, reported on 1 January 2024, and the first-half 2026 total of 11,884, 41 per cent down, reported on 1 July 2026
They are the department's figures rather than a minister's claim. Counted, they would add one false case (1,335 arrivals in January 2024 against 1,180) and one true one (2,316 from 2 to 31 July 2026 against 5,014), and make the record three of six.
Statements without a proportion, such as the Prime Minister's remarks at Dover on 3 August 2026 that progress was being made and the Home Secretary's on 20 August that the France deal was having a real impact
They cannot be scored against the test, because the test needs a number to hold the following month to.
The early-September calendar pattern used in the earlier version of this Echo
Every early-September week from 2020 to 2025 cleared 500 arrivals, but that is the season, not the action in the news. The two weekly markets it produced are kept as adjacent.

The Read

The direct market sits a little above evens because two honest reference classes disagree. The record of ministerial claims is two of four, and the two that failed did so for different reasons: one was made in June before the summer peak, the other set a bar the next month missed by two points. The 2026 numbers are on the claim's side: the largest thirty-day run this year is 3,428 from 22 July, and the thirty days to 31 August carried 1,987, so the bar of 2,611 sits between the year's worst month and its August.

What settles it is wind, not policy. The series moves in bursts, with 494 arrivals on a single day in late August after a fortnight of near silence, and the French prevention effort removes a share of attempts before they start rather than smoothing the total. The two weekly markets are the short version of the same question. The interesting one is not whether the year stays lower than 2025, which it will by a wide margin. It is whether the government keeps saying halved once the September weeks land.
