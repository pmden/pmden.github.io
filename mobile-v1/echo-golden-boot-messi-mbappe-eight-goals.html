Messi and Mbappé locked on eight. Three matches decide the Boot.

The Golden Boot race reaches its final week with Lionel Messi and Kylian Mbappé tied on eight goals, and Mbappé ahead only on the assists tiebreaker.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

Kylian Mbappé and Lionel Messi lead the World Cup Golden Boot race on eight goals each, with Mbappé ahead on the assists tiebreaker and Harry Kane and Jude Bellingham two goals back on six.

Sky Sports, Jul 2026
2 · The event

Eight apiece, and the fixtures are running out

The 2026 Golden Boot standings show Mbappé and Messi level on eight goals, with Mbappé holding first place on the assists tiebreak (Sky Sports, Jul 2026). Erling Haaland's challenge ended on seven when Norway lost 2-1 to England in the quarter-final on 11 July; Kane and Bellingham both sit on six, and Spain's Mikel Oyarzabal moved to five with his penalty against France.

The remaining calendar shapes the race. Mbappé was held scoreless in Spain's 2-0 semi-final win over France in Dallas, leaving him one fixture: the bronze final against tonight's Atlanta loser, at Hard Rock Stadium in Miami on 18 July (FIFA, 14 Jul 2026). Messi, Kane and Bellingham each have up to two matches left — the semi-final tonight, then either the final at MetLife or the bronze final.

The award's mechanics matter in a race this tight. FIFA separates tied scorers first by assists, then by fewer minutes played (FIFA, 14 Jul 2026). One Messi goal tonight would put him clear outright; Kane or Bellingham would need three, or two plus a tiebreak swing, across their remaining games.

3 · The pattern

How Golden Boot races have finished

The instinct is that the Boot goes to a finalist adding goals on the last day. The record of the past six World Cups says the lead more often survives from the sidelines.

2002
Ronaldo won with 8 goals, scoring both in Brazil's 2-0 win in the final — the last winner to score on the final day until 2022.
2006
Miroslav Klose won with 5, none scored after the quarter-final; Germany finished third.
2010
Thomas Müller won on the assists tiebreaker, level on 5 goals with David Villa, Wesley Sneijder and Diego Forlán.
2014
James Rodríguez won with 6 despite Colombia's quarter-final exit — the lead held for two full rounds from the sidelines.
2018
Harry Kane won with 6, his last goal coming in the round of 16; England finished fourth.
2022
Mbappé took the Boot 8-7 from Messi with a hat-trick in the final — and still lost the trophy on penalties.
2 of 6
Only 2 of the last 6 Golden Boot winners played in the final; 3 held the lead without scoring after the quarter-finals.

5 · The read

The Hunch read

The case for Messi is arithmetic. He is level with Mbappé on eight, has up to two matches left against Mbappé's one, and a single goal tonight puts him clear outright against an England defence that has conceded in both knockout rounds. Mbappé, by contrast, must score in a bronze final against opponents with nothing decided, three days after being held without a goal by Spain.

The honest counter is that leads like Mbappé's survive more often than they fall. Three of the last six winners added nothing after the quarter-finals and still collected the award, and Mbappé holds the assists tiebreak, meaning Messi must outscore him rather than merely match him. The 2022 final — where Mbappé took the Boot from Messi with a hat-trick on the last day — is the reminder that this race is not settled until the final whistle at MetLife.
