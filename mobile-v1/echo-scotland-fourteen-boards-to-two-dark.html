Scotland03 Sep 2026

Scotland puts fourteen health boards into two. The last merger of this size lost its centre.

The Programme for Government published on 1 September commits to replacing Scotland's 14 territorial health boards with two strategic boards, alongside a wider cull of public bodies. The vehicle is a Public Service Renewal Bill that has not yet been introduced.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

The Programme for Government 2026 to 2031 commits to replacing the 14 territorial health boards with two strategic health boards and to substantially reducing the number of public bodies.

Scottish Government, 1 Sep 2026
2 · The event

Fourteen into two, and a Bill that has not been written

The Programme for Government sets out the commitment in one line: the government will simplify the NHS by replacing the current 14 territorial health boards with two strategic health boards, which alongside other measures will free up more time and capacity for treatment (Scottish Government, 1 Sep 2026). The clinical promise attached to it is that by the end of 2031 no one waits more than 26 weeks for treatment (Scottish Government, 1 Sep 2026).

The health boards are one part of a wider consolidation. The same document counts 130 public bodies in Scotland and says the number will be substantially reduced, naming the merger of SEPA, NatureScot, Zero Waste Scotland and Scottish Forestry into a single environmental body and the return of Transport Scotland into the Scottish Government. The named legislative vehicle is the Public Service Renewal (Scotland) Bill, one of nine Bills listed for 2026-27 and described as supporting reform through restructuring and the potential merging of public bodies (Scottish Government, 1 Sep 2026).

Nothing structural has happened yet. The two strategic boards have no names, no boundaries and no chief executives, the Bill has not been introduced, and territorial boards are statutory bodies that cannot be abolished by ministerial announcement. That is the useful thing about this commitment for anyone trying to price it: every step from here leaves a document, and the first documents are due long before any board disappears.

3 · The pattern

When Scotland has legislated to merge its territorial public bodies

Inclusion rule, fixed before the count: this pattern counts every case since devolution in 1999 where the Scottish Government proposed abolishing or merging a tier of territorial public bodies and put a Bill before the Scottish Parliament to do it. The test applied to each is the same: did the structural change reach the statute book in the form announced. Reorganisations achieved without primary legislation are excluded, because the mechanism is different and easier. Proposals never introduced as a Bill are excluded, because there is nothing to score. Four cases meet the rule.

2004
The National Health Service Reform (Scotland) Act 2004 dissolved Scotland's NHS trusts and folded their functions into single-system health boards, adding community health partnerships. The structure announced is the structure that passed.
2012
The Police and Fire Reform (Scotland) Act 2012 replaced eight regional police forces with the Police Service of Scotland and consolidated the fire and rescue boards into one national service. Delivered in full, and the largest merger of territorial bodies Scotland has attempted.
2014
The Public Bodies (Joint Working) (Scotland) Act 2014 created integration authorities to run health and social care jointly across board and council boundaries. Delivered, though as a layer added on top rather than a tier removed.
2025
The National Care Service Bill, introduced on 20 June 2022, proposed moving social care from councils to a national body. The structural transfer was removed; the Bill was renamed the Care Reform (Scotland) Bill at Stage 2 on 4 March 2025 and passed on 10 June 2025 by 116 votes to none.
3 of 4
3 of 4. Read it with the caveats: four cases is the minimum that counts as a base, three of the four were passed by governments with a working majority or a settled coalition, and the single failure is the one whose subject matter, care and health, matches this proposal most closely. A small base pointing one way with its nearest analogue pointing the other is a weak signal, and it should be priced as one.

3 of the 4 reached the statute book with the structure the government announced. The one that did not is the most recent, and it is the closest in kind to this one.

What this pattern excludes

The 2016 Health and Social Care Delivery Plan's regional planning arrangements
They were pursued administratively rather than through a Bill, so they fall outside the rule fixed above. Including them would let a softer mechanism into a count about legislated structural change.
The creation of new national bodies such as Revenue Scotland and Social Security Scotland
These built a body where none existed. Nothing territorial was abolished, so the constraint that makes these cases hard, which is removing an existing statutory tier, was absent.
Local government reorganisation before devolution
The 1996 reorganisation was made by the UK Parliament before the Scottish Parliament existed. Different legislature, different mechanism, out of period.

5 · The read

The read

The fortnight market is close to a formality. Naming two new health boards means naming their boundaries, which means telling fourteen chairs and fourteen workforces which side of a line they sit on, and no government does that in the two weeks after the announcement. The autumn markets are the live ones. A named Bill in a nine-Bill programme is a strong signal of intent, but the Budget Bill takes the front of the queue every year and the renewal Bill has to carry environmental mergers and the return of Transport Scotland as well as the health boards.

The case for pricing the structural change lower than the base rate suggests rests on the newest case in the set. The National Care Service proposal also promised to lift services out of local bodies into a national one, also had a named Bill, and also had a government behind it, and what survived was the parts that did not move anybody. Health boards employ people, hold budgets and have local political sponsors, and the last time Scotland tried to move that kind of function upwards the structure came out of the Bill rather than the Bill coming out of the Parliament. The counter-case is the 2012 precedent: eight police forces into one was a bigger merger than fourteen health boards into two, and it happened on time.
