House prices are stirring. The Bank holds the thermostat.

Nationwide says annual house price growth edged up to 2.2% in June, the strongest reading since spring — with a Bank of England rate decision on 30 July that will set the tone for the autumn market.

3 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

Annual house price growth edged up to 2.2% in June from 1.7% in May, with the average home at £278,784 and prices broadly flat on the month.

Nationwide, 1 Jul 2026
2 · The event

What happened

Nationwide's June index put annual UK house price growth at 2.2%, up from 1.7% in May, with the average home at £278,784 and prices broadly flat month-on-month once seasonal effects are stripped out Nationwide, 1 Jul 2026.

The rate backdrop is the swing factor. The Bank of England's Monetary Policy Committee held Bank Rate at 3.75% on 18 June by a 7–2 vote — but the two dissenters, Megan Greene and Huw Pill, voted to raise, not cut, with CPI inflation at 2.8% in May and energy prices still volatile after events in the Middle East Bank of England, 18 Jun 2026. A hawkish tilt at the 30 July meeting, which comes with a full Monetary Policy Report, would feed straight into fixed mortgage pricing.

Halifax's rival index tells a cooler story — 0.5% annual growth in May, with the average home at £298,806 — underlining how thin the market's momentum is. The next month decides whether June was a turn or a blip: Halifax's June reading, the Bank's decision, and Nationwide's July print all land within five weeks.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

Does a mid-year uptick in house price growth carry into the second half? Nationwide's own index over the last four years gives a genuinely split answer — June momentum has been extended twice and broken twice.

2024
June annual growth of +1.5% built steadily to +4.7% by December as rate-cut expectations fed through to mortgage pricing.
2023
June growth of −3.5% — the depths of the rate shock — moderated to −1.8% by December. The direction improved; prices still fell.
2022
June growth of +10.7% collapsed to +2.8% by December after the September mini-budget sent mortgage rates through 6%.
2021
June growth of +13.4% — the stamp-duty-holiday peak — faded to +10.4% by December as the incentive expired.
2 of 4
In two of the last four years, Nationwide's annual growth rate ended the year higher than its June reading; in two it ended lower. The pattern is genuinely split — the deciding variable each time was what happened to mortgage rates in the second half.

Split pattern: in 2 of the last 4 years, December's annual rate came in higher than June's.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case for the uptick holding: the June acceleration happened despite mortgage rates sitting above their new-year lows, which suggests underlying demand rather than a financing effect. Wage growth continues to outrun prices, the 2024 precedent shows how quickly annual comparisons flatter once soft months drop out of the calculation, and a hold at 3.75% with any dovish language would let lenders keep trimming fixed rates into the autumn market.

The honest counter-case: two MPC members are voting to raise rates, not cut them, and inflation at 2.8% gives the hawks the argument. The month-on-month reading was flat — the annual acceleration is arithmetic as much as it is momentum — and Halifax's near-zero 0.5% shows how little the indices agree. If 30 July produces a hawkish hold or, at the tail, a raise, the 2022 lesson applies in miniature: mortgage pricing moves within weeks, and 2.5% growth by the July print becomes a stretch.
