Six of seven US state votes backed ID at the polls. Will California join them?

California votes on 3 November on Proposition 39, which would make voters show ID at polling stations. Its campaign has raised more than $36m.

4 min read

What happened

Billionaires across the US have given more than $36m to the campaign for California’s Proposition 39, which would make voters show ID before casting their ballots.

The campaign for Proposition 39 has raised more than $36m. That includes $17m from Richard Uihlein, a conservative donor, and $8m from Joe Gebbia, a co-founder of Airbnb, according to the news site CalMatters. The yes side has raised up to $13m more than its opponents.

Today California voters do not show ID at the polls. Prop 39 would require ID at polling stations, and postal voters would write the last four digits of a government ID number on the envelope. Republican lawmaker Carl DeMaio wrote it after the state’s Democratic-led legislature did not pass his bill.

A YES vote on this measure means: Voters would be required to present additional identifying information to elections officials each time they vote.

California Official Voter Information Guide, Proposition 39

Thirteen points is how far Prop 39 trailed in a poll by the UC Berkeley Institute of Governmental Studies. Other polls from late September showed it ahead. The state Democratic party, the American Civil Liberties Union and the League of Women Voters oppose it.

The official guide prints both sides. Supporters ask for a yes vote to create a national model for voter ID. The argument against, signed among others by US Senator Alex Padilla, says STOP TRUMP’S POWER GRAB. State analysts put the yearly cost at tens of millions to low hundreds of millions of dollars.

Election day is 3 November. The Secretary of State certifies the official count, called the Statement of Vote, on 11 December.

Sources: The Guardian, California Secretary of State (2), California Secretary of State (2)

The Pattern

We looked for cases where voters across a whole US state decided, on general election day, whether people must show ID to vote. We found seven with official results, from 2004 to 2024. All were in other states, because California has never voted on this. Votes that state lawmakers themselves put to voters are left out.

The Read

Earlier votes point to a yes. Voters backed an ID rule in six of seven earlier state votes, five of them by at least 55%. Prop 39 also has far more money behind it than the no side, and its supporters say a majority of voters from both parties want it.

There are reasons for doubt. None of those votes was in California, a state run by the party that campaigns against this one, with Senator Alex Padilla signing the argument against. A UC Berkeley poll had Prop 39 13 points behind, while other polls in late September had it ahead. Arizona said yes to a similar rule in 2004 and no in 2022.
