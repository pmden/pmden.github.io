Lebanon04 Sep 2026

Two truces in eight months. Israel has kept striking through both.

Israeli aircraft hit al-Mansouri, Sarbine, Beit Lif and Yater on 26 August while US mediators pushed Syria-Israel talks in Jordan. Lebanon's health ministry counts 4,350 dead since 2 March. Of the last four Israel-Lebanon ceasefires, three were followed by continued Israeli strikes within weeks.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

Israel carried out air attacks on al-Mansouri and Sarbine, artillery fire on Wadi Zibqin and strikes on Beit Lif and Yater in southern Lebanon, and shelled positions in Syria's Damascus countryside, as US-mediated Syria-Israel talks took place in Jordan.

Al Jazeera, 27 Aug 2026
2 · The event

What happened

On 26 and 27 August Israeli aircraft struck al-Mansouri and Sarbine in the Tyre and Bint Jbeil districts, artillery hit Wadi Zibqin, and further strikes were reported at Beit Lif and Yater, with drones over Beirut and its southern suburbs. Across the border Israeli forces shelled Bat al-Warda hill near Beit Jinn and raided Taranja in the Quneitra countryside (Al Jazeera, 27 Aug 2026).

The strikes came as Syria's foreign minister Asaad al-Shaibani met Israeli intelligence officials in Jordan in US-mediated talks aimed at ending Israeli operations in Syria. Lebanon's health ministry put the toll since 2 March at 4,350 killed and 12,310 wounded (Al Jazeera, 27 Aug 2026).

The framework that is meant to govern this was agreed in US-led talks in June: a phased Israeli withdrawal from southern Lebanon contingent on the Lebanese army deploying and armed groups disarming (Al Jazeera, 4 Jun 2026). It replaced a ten-day truce announced by Trump on 16 April (Al Jazeera, 16 Apr 2026), which itself followed the November 2024 ceasefire that was supposed to be permanent. The mechanism has not changed: each agreement makes Israeli withdrawal conditional on disarmament Hezbollah says it did not negotiate.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

Four Israel-Lebanon ceasefires in twenty years. Three were followed within weeks by continued Israeli strikes; one, in 2006, was followed by a withdrawal.

2026
June 2026: Israel and Lebanon agreed a US-brokered framework tying withdrawal to Lebanese army deployment and disarmament (Al Jazeera, 4 Jun 2026). Air strikes on the south were still being reported in the last week of August (Al Jazeera, 27 Aug 2026).
2026
April 2026: Trump announced a ten-day Israel-Lebanon ceasefire on 16 April, separate from the US-Iran pause (Al Jazeera, 16 Apr 2026). Strikes resumed and a new framework had to be negotiated in June.
2024
November 2024: a ceasefire took effect on 27 November that Washington called a permanent cessation (Al Jazeera, 27 Nov 2024). Israel bombed Beirut's southern suburbs the following April (Al Jazeera, 27 Apr 2025) and carried out nearly 1,600 strikes across Lebanon between January and late November 2025 (Al Jazeera, 18 Dec 2025).
2006
August 2006: the Security Council adopted resolution 1701 on 11 August calling for a full cessation of hostilities (UN, 11 Aug 2006). Israeli forces completed their withdrawal from southern Lebanon on 1 October (Al Jazeera, 2 Oct 2006).
3 of 4
In 3 of the last 4 Israel-Lebanon ceasefires, Israeli strikes continued within weeks of the agreement: November 2024, April 2026 and June 2026. The exception is 2006, when resolution 1701 was followed by an Israeli withdrawal within seven weeks. The difference in the set is enforcement: 1701 came with an expanded UN force, the three recent agreements with a conditional withdrawal and no enforcement mechanism.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case says the strikes continue, because the June framework has the same conditional structure as the two agreements before it and none of them stopped the air campaign. What the market prices at less than even is the specific escalation to Beirut: strikes on the capital have been the marker of each breakdown, in April 2025 and again in May 2026, but they have been rarer than strikes on the south.

The honest counter-case is diplomacy on the Syrian track. Talks in Jordan with US mediation are the first structured channel between Damascus and Israel in years, and an agreement there would give Washington leverage to restrain the Lebanese front. The Beirut prediction fails if that channel produces a deal quickly; the Syria market fails if, as in Lebanon, the talks produce a framework rather than a signature.
