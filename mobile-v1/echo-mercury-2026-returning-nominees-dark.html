Music03 Sep 2026

Four of the twelve have been here before. The Mercury has rewarded that once in five years.

The 2026 Mercury Prize shortlist returns Kojey Radical, Olivia Dean, RAYE and Nia Archives to a list they stood on before, alongside Paul McCartney and Suede.

4 min read

Twelve albums, a Tyneside arena, and four familiar names

The 2026 Mercury Prize shortlist of twelve Albums of the Year is published, with the ceremony set for 22 October at the Utilita Arena in Newcastle.

(Mercury Prize, 3 Sep 2026)

The twelve Albums of the Year are Dave's The Boy Who Played the Harp, Dove Ellis's Blizzard, Florence + the Machine's Everybody Scream, JADE's That's Showbiz Baby!, Knats's A Great Day In Newcastle, Kojey Radical's Don't Look Down, Nia Archives's Emotional Junglist, Olivia Dean's The Art of Loving, Paul McCartney's The Boys of Dungeon Lane, RAYE's This Music May Contain Hope, Suede's Antidepressants and War Child's HELP(2). The ceremony is on 22 October at the Utilita Arena in Newcastle (Mercury Prize, 2026).

Four of the twelve have stood on this list before within the five shortlists the prize publishes. Kojey Radical was nominated in 2022 for Reason to Smile, Olivia Dean in 2023 for Messy, RAYE in 2023 for My 21st Century Blues and Nia Archives in 2024 for Silence Is Loud (Mercury Prize, previous shortlists). None of them won in those years.

The venue is doing work of its own. Moving the ceremony to Newcastle a year after Sam Fender, a North Shields songwriter, won it puts a regional frame around a prize that has spent most of its life in London, and one of this year's twelve is a Newcastle record by name. Whether that matters to twelve judges is not something a base rate can answer, and it is not what the markets below are about.

The Pattern

Inclusion rule, fixed before the count: this pattern uses the five shortlists published on the prize's own previous shortlists page, covering 2021 to 2025, and the winner named on each. The test is stated in advance: had the winning artist appeared on any earlier shortlist inside that same window. The window is the five years the prize itself displays, which is a limitation of the source rather than a choice about the answer, and it is stated here so a reader can discount the number accordingly.

2021
Arlo Parks won with Collapsed In Sunbeams from a list that included Wolf Alice, Ghetts, Berwyn, Celeste and Mogwai. First appearance in the window.
2023
Ezra Collective won with Where I'm Meant To Be, beating Arctic Monkeys, RAYE, Olivia Dean and Loyle Carner. First appearance, and the first jazz record to take the prize.
2024
English Teacher won with This Could Be Texas over Charli xcx's BRAT, Beth Gibbons and The Last Dinner Party. First appearance, and the second consecutive win for a debut-scale act over a household name.
2025
Sam Fender won with People Watching, having been shortlisted in 2022 for Seventeen Going Under. The only winner in the window who had been nominated before, and he beat Pulp, Wolf Alice, FKA twigs and Fontaines D.C. to do it.
1 of 5
1 of 5, and the number carries two warnings. Five is the smallest sample this method will publish, and the window is set by what the prize chooses to display rather than by anything about the prize itself. Read alongside the exclusions, the honest range is one or two winners in five, which is roughly the same as the share of returning nominees on a typical list. In other words the record so far says returning nominees are neither favoured nor punished.

1 of the 5 winners in the window had been shortlisted in an earlier year. Five artists appeared more than once across those five lists, CMAT three times, and four of the five never won.

What this pattern excludes

Shortlists before 2021
The prize's own archive page shows five years, so an artist nominated in 2019 counts here as a first-timer. This understates the number of returning nominees and therefore probably understates the base rate. It is a limit of the source and it should be held against the number.
The 2022 result
Little Simz won with Sometimes I Might Be Introvert and is widely reported to have been shortlisted before 2021, but that earlier nomination falls outside the published window and is not confirmed from the source used here. It is left out of the cases rather than asserted, and if it is true the base rate becomes 2 of 5.
Sales, streaming and critical reception
They are the usual way people forecast this prize and they are not in the count, because the judging panel is explicitly asked to ignore commercial performance. A base rate built on chart position would be measuring something the judges are told not to measure.

The Read

The returning-nominee market is close to what a coin would give you, and that is the finding rather than a failure to find one. Four of twelve nominees are returners, so a shortlist drawn at random would deliver a returning winner a third of the time, and the five-year record delivers one in five. Those two numbers are not far enough apart, on samples this small, to say the prize leans either way. What the record does say clearly is that the Mercury does not reward persistence for its own sake: CMAT has been shortlisted in three of the last four years and has not won.

The McCartney market is the one where the prize's own history argues hardest. The last four winners were an emerging poet, a jazz septet, a rapper and a Newcastle songwriter, and in 2024 the panel passed over the most commercially dominant British album of the decade in favour of a first record. A prize that has spent five years choosing the less obvious name is not the natural home of a legacy artist, however good the album. The venue market is included because it is the only part of this that can be settled by a fact rather than a judgement, and because prize ceremonies do move.
