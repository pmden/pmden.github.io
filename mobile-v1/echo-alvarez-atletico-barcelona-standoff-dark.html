Atlético say Álvarez is not for sale. Barcelona's offer is still on the table.

Atlético Madrid have rejected nine-figure bids from Real Madrid and Barcelona for Julián Álvarez inside five weeks, publicly mocking both rivals and pointing to a €500m release clause. Barcelona insist their offer stands — but say it is not open-ended.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

Barcelona refuse to give up on Julián Álvarez and could raise their bid to around €130m plus bonuses after the World Cup, despite Atlético Madrid's refusal to sell and relations between the clubs at an all-time low.

Sky Sports, 15 Jul 2026
2 · The event

What happened

Spain's two biggest clubs have both bid for the same Atlético Madrid striker and both have been turned away. Real Madrid's €150m (£129m) offer for Álvarez was rejected on 10 June, with Atlético responding on X: 'You make us laugh even more than Barcelona' (Sky Sports, 10 Jun 2026). Barcelona followed with a firm offer north of €100m, which president Joan Laporta confirmed 'remains on the table and the deal could still go through, but the offer is not open-ended' (ESPN, 1 Jul 2026). Two rejections at that level in five weeks make this the defining standoff of the European window.

Atlético's refusal is total, and increasingly theatrical. The club says it will 'neither study nor consider' any offer for a player contracted to 2030 with a €500m (£431m) release clause (Sky Sports, 10 Jun 2026).

Yet the pursuit has not stopped, and the calendar now shapes it. Barcelona are considering raising their offer to around €130m plus bonuses once the World Cup ends, with Arsenal and Paris Saint-Germain also monitoring — though Álvarez and his family are reported to prefer staying in Spain (Sky Sports, 15 Jul 2026). Laporta has framed Atlético's 'no' as conditional rather than absolute: 'Atlético said no because they didn't have a replacement. If they get one, we will see' (ESPN, 1 Jul 2026). Álvarez is due to holiday after Argentina's campaign, then report back to Atlético for pre-season — the moment any private request to leave would surface.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

Since 2013, six star players have been publicly declared off the market by their clubs mid-window while nine-figure suitors circled. How the standoffs ended is remarkably consistent.

2013
Liverpool refused Arsenal's infamous £40,000,001 offer for Luis Suárez, the owner declared him not for sale at any price — and Suárez stayed the full season, moving only the following summer.
2015
Manchester United's David de Gea had agreed to join Real Madrid, but the clubs' standoff ran to deadline day and the paperwork failed at the deadline. He stayed and re-signed.
2017
Barcelona insisted Neymar was not for sale. PSG simply paid the full €222m release clause and announced him on 3 August — the only mechanism that overrode the club's refusal.
2017
Liverpool rejected three Barcelona bids for Philippe Coutinho, the last near €150m, and refused his transfer request. He stayed that summer, moving only in January.
2019
Atlético themselves lost Antoine Griezmann when Barcelona deposited his €120m release clause on 12 July. Atlético protested the fee was too low and filed complaints — but the clause settled it.
2021
PSG rejected Real Madrid bids rising to €180m for Kylian Mbappé in late August, despite one year left on his contract. He stayed, eventually leaving for nothing in 2024.
2 of 6
2 of 6 players publicly declared 'not for sale' mid-window moved that same summer — and both required the full release clause to be paid.

Two of six moved that summer — and both moves happened only because the buyer paid the full release clause. No club that publicly declared a star not for sale was ever negotiated down from that position within the same window. Álvarez's clause is €500m; nobody pays that.

5 · The read

The Hunch read

The case for a move rests on three live wires: Barcelona's offer is formal and standing, Laporta has framed Atlético's refusal as conditional on finding a replacement, and the player — who Laporta claims has wanted the move since his Manchester City days — reports back to a club he has just watched publicly ridicule his suitors. If Álvarez asks to leave in pre-season and Atlético identify a striker, the 'no' can soften quickly, and Sky reports Barcelona are ready with €130m plus bonuses the moment the World Cup ends.

The counter is the weight of history. In six comparable standoffs, no club was ever talked down from a public 'not for sale' within the same window; the only two players who moved went for the full release clause, and Álvarez's is an unpayable €500m. Atlético have escalated publicly against both bidders — behaviour that makes a climbdown before August politically costly — and Álvarez himself has not agitated. Sixteen per cent on a Barcelona announcement inside 18 days prices the noise honestly: loud, but so far all one-directional.
