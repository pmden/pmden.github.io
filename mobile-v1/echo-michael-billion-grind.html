Michael passed Oppenheimer. The last $23m is the hard part.

Antoine Fuqua's Michael Jackson biopic overtook Oppenheimer's $975m to become the highest-grossing biopic ever made. It now sits at $977m worldwide, within sight of a line that has stopped better-positioned films.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

Michael passed Oppenheimer's $975m to become the highest-grossing biopic ever, standing at $977m worldwide.

Variety, Jun 2026
2 · The event

What happened

Michael, Antoine Fuqua's account of Michael Jackson's rise from the Jackson 5 to global stardom, passed $977m worldwide in late June, overtaking Oppenheimer's $975m to become the highest-grossing biopic in box-office history (Variety, Jun 2026). The film, which stars Jackson's nephew Jaafar Jackson in his acting debut, opened in April to $97m domestic and $217m globally, itself a record start for a musical biopic.

The milestone capped a run of records. Earlier in June the film passed Bohemian Rhapsody's $910.8m to become the biggest music biopic ever (Billboard, Jun 2026), and it danced past the Hunger Games and Twilight franchises to become Lionsgate's highest-grossing release in the studio's history (Deadline, 28 Jun 2026). Its split — roughly $370m domestic against $607m overseas — shows unusually deep international reach for the genre.

The run has come despite mixed reviews and sustained criticism of the film's handling of the abuse allegations against Jackson. The commercial question left is narrow and binary: $23m more makes Michael only the second 2026 release to cross $1bn, after The Super Mario Galaxy Movie. Films at this altitude have stalled before.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

Films that approach $1bn on long legs rather than monster openings split into two camps: those whose word-of-mouth carried them over, and those that ran out of screens just short of the line. The final $30m is the hardest stretch in the business.

2024
Despicable Me 4 ground upward all summer and finished at $969m — never crossed.
2023
Oppenheimer ran for seven months and stalled at $975m, the figure Michael just passed — never crossed.
2022
Top Gun: Maverick crossed $1bn in week five on word-of-mouth legs and finished at $1.496bn.
2019
Joker crossed $1bn in week seven, the first R-rated film to do so, finishing at $1.078bn.
2018
Bohemian Rhapsody, the previous music-biopic record holder, stalled at $910.8m after a five-month run.
2 of 5
Of five recent films that ground toward $1bn on legs rather than openings, two crossed and three stalled within $90m of the line. The pattern is genuinely split — but no stall-out was ever this close: Michael needs $23m with weeks of international play still booked.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case is a strong yes on the $1bn crossing. The base rate looks split until distance is priced in: Oppenheimer stalled $25m short after its momentum had already died, while Michael needs $23m with active bookings across international territories where it has consistently over-indexed — $607m of its total came from overseas. Even a steep decay to $4–5m a week worldwide clears the line by early August. The 84% the forecasters have settled on reflects execution risk on timing, not on outcome.

The honest counter-case is about pace, not direction. Films nine weeks into release lose screens abruptly; if exhibitors clear space for August tentpoles faster than expected, Michael's weekly adds could fall to $2–3m and leave it at $995m on resolution day — a crossing that happens in September rather than by 7 August. Bohemian Rhapsody's tail is the warning: music biopics skew older, and older audiences stop coming all at once. On the year's chart, Michael has no realistic path past Mario or a Toy Story 5 still adding $70m weekends; that market is really a two-horse race about whether Pixar's pace holds.
