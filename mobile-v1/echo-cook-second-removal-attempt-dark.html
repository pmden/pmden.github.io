Central banks28 Aug 2026

The Court carved out the Fed in June. The White House came back in August.

Lisa Cook's lawyers filed their rebuttal on 27 August, three weeks after a formal White House warning. The Supreme Court blocked her removal in June, in a ruling that let the President fire almost every other agency head at will.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

Federal Reserve governor Lisa Cook's legal team submitted a detailed rebuttal to the White House on 27 August, calling the mortgage fraud allegations baseless and an attempt to force the central bank to bend to the President's will.

Al Jazeera, 27 Aug 2026
2 · The event

What happened

The White House issued a formal warning to Cook on 5 August, giving her three weeks to respond to allegations of mortgage fraud. Her lawyers filed on 27 August, arguing that an inadvertent error is not fraud and that the case is an attempt to force the Federal Reserve to bend to the President's will (Al Jazeera, 27 Aug 2026). It is the second attempt to remove her (Al Jazeera, 7 Aug 2026).

The first attempt began in August 2025, when the President purported to fire Cook, the first Federal Reserve governor removed in the institution's 111-year history. She sued. The Supreme Court temporarily blocked the removal on 1 October 2025 (Al Jazeera, 1 Oct 2025) and appeared reluctant to allow it at argument in January 2026 (Al Jazeera, 21 Jan 2026).

On 29 June 2026 the Court delivered a split answer. It upheld the President's power to dismiss the heads of independent agencies at will, overriding statutes that require cause, and extended that logic to the National Labor Relations Board, the Merit Systems Protection Board and the Consumer Product Safety Commission. It made the Federal Reserve the single exception, allowing Cook to stay while she fights (Al Jazeera, 29 Jun 2026; Supreme Court of the United States, 25A312). The next rate meeting is on 15 and 16 September (Federal Reserve, 2026 calendar).

3 · The pattern

The pattern

The removal question has been tested repeatedly since 2025, and the answers form a clean line. Everywhere the President moved against an independent agency head, the courts eventually let him. Everywhere means everywhere except one building.

2025
In August the President purported to dismiss Cook, the first Federal Reserve governor removed in 111 years. She sued and the Supreme Court blocked the removal on a temporary basis on 1 October (Al Jazeera, 1 Oct 2025).
2026
In June the Court upheld the removal of Federal Trade Commission member Rebecca Slaughter, dismissed without cause despite a statute requiring one (Al Jazeera, 29 Jun 2026).
2026
The same ruling extended to board members dismissed at the National Labor Relations Board, the Merit Systems Protection Board and the Consumer Product Safety Commission, unwinding much of a 91-year-old precedent limiting executive removal power.
2026
The Court held that outside the central bank, presidents have free rein to dismiss agency heads at will. Cook stayed in post by 5-4, the only official the ruling protected (Supreme Court of the United States, 25A312).
4 of 5
Of the five independent-agency removals tested at the Supreme Court in this cycle, four were allowed to stand and one was blocked. The one was the Federal Reserve, and the Court said so explicitly rather than on a technicality: monetary policy was carved out of a doctrine that swept everything else away. That makes the base rate a poor guide to the second attempt, because the first attempt already produced the precedent that governs it.

4 of 5 tested removals of independent agency officials were allowed to stand; the Federal Reserve was the only body where the Court intervened.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case is that Cook is still in her seat and voting in September. The June ruling was not a procedural stay but a substantive carve-out, and a second attempt using the same allegations runs into the same judgment. The three-week warning letter and the detailed rebuttal are the shape of a process being built for a future court rather than an imminent dismissal, and the Federal Reserve has no mechanism to seat a replacement while the question is live. That is why both near-dated markets price above 80%.

The honest counter is that the June decision protected the Fed as an institution rather than any individual, and it did not say what happens if a governor is removed for cause that a court accepts as genuine. Mortgage documentation is a factual matter, and a factual finding is exactly the route that would let the administration argue this is not a policy dispute at all. For a British reader the transmission is through the long end: a contested Fed governorship widens the term premium on Treasuries, and gilts have tracked that premium all year. The prediction fails if a court accepts the for-cause finding before the September meeting.
