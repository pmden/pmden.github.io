US midterms06 Oct 2026

Greg Abbott trails in a Texas poll. Governors in his position usually won again.

A New York Times and Siena poll puts Democrat Gina Hinojosa ahead of Texas Governor Greg Abbott, 49% to 46%. Texas votes on 3 November.

4 min read

What happened

A New York Times and Siena poll taken from late September to early October found Democrat Gina Hinojosa ahead of Republican Governor Greg Abbott in Texas by 49% to 46%.

Democrat Gina Hinojosa leads Texas Governor Greg Abbott by 49% to 46%, according to a New York Times and Siena poll taken from late September to early October. Texas last elected a Democrat as governor in 1990. Abbott, a Republican, has been governor since January 2015.

The same polls put Democrats ahead in three other states that have not chosen a Democratic governor for at least 20 years: Iowa by 52% to 41%, Ohio by 49% to 45% and Alaska by 52% to 48%. Kansas was the only state in the survey where the Republican led.

President Donald Trump’s approval rating is near historic lows, as voters are unhappy about the cost of living and the war in Iran. Some Republicans warn that polls have been wrong before. The Trump administration is pointing voters to Trump’s tax cuts and his record on immigration.

Early voting for the November 3 Election runs Oct. 19-30.

Texas Secretary of State, election notice

The vote is on 3 November and early voting starts on 19 October. Unless the race changes, the result will be known on election night, when the state publishes its first count.

Sources: The Independent, National Governors Association, Texas Secretary of State

The Pattern

We looked for cases where a governor from the president’s party ran again in the middle of the president’s term, after winning the job at least twice, in a state where the other party had not won it for more than 20 years. We found nine, from 1942 to 2014, in eight states. Four were in the South, where one party had ruled for decades.

8 of 9
Eight of nine such governors won again. The one who lost, Mario Cuomo in New York in 1994, went down on a night his party lost across the country.
2014 Oregon, Kitzhaberheld
4 November 2014: Democrat John Kitzhaber, elected three times before, won with 49.9% to 44.1%. No Republican had won the job since 1978. He resigned 106 days later.
1994 New York, Cuomonot held
8 November 1994: Democrat Mario Cuomo, elected three times before, ran again. Republicans had not won the job since 1970. He lost to a little-known Republican on the night Republicans won the US Senate.

The Read

Governors in Greg Abbott’s position usually won again. Eight of nine were re-elected, from Tennessee in 1942 to Oregon in 2014, and each time the result was known on election night. This year the best-known poll a month out puts the challenger ahead, and for most of those past elections there is no similar poll to compare.

There are reasons for doubt. Four of the eight wins came in Southern states where one party had ruled for decades, and vote shares are known for only three of the nine elections. In Colorado and New York the other party last had the job in the 1970s, but only after taking over from a governor who left, not by winning a vote. The one loss, New York in 1994, came when fewer than half of Americans approved of the president, and his party lost the Senate the same night.
