Liverpool agree £120m for Barcola. The window shuts on Tuesday.

Liverpool have agreed a fee of about €140m with Paris Saint-Germain for Bradley Barcola, their second-biggest signing ever. The player is still waiting on PSG to clear a medical, and the Premier League window closes at 11pm on 1 September.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

ESPN reports Liverpool have agreed a fee of around €140m with Paris Saint-Germain for Bradley Barcola, with a five-year contract in place and the club awaiting PSG clearance for a medical.

ESPN, 27 Aug 2026
2 · The event

What happened

Liverpool have agreed a fee reported at €140m, around £120m, for the 23-year-old France forward Bradley Barcola, with at least £100m guaranteed and a five-year contract agreed. PSG's initial asking price was reported at £145m. The deal would be Liverpool's second-most expensive signing after Alexander Isak at £125m (ESPN, 27 Aug 2026).

The move is not complete. Liverpool are waiting for PSG to clear the player for a medical, and the manager said publicly that he hoped for good news but that the club were not there yet (ESPN, 27 Aug 2026). ESPN also reports Liverpool tracking Ismaïla Sarr as the market moves behind the Barcola deal (ESPN, 28 Aug 2026).

The deadline is fixed. The Premier League summer window closes at 11pm BST on Tuesday 1 September, having opened on 15 June, with a correctly submitted deal sheet buying up to two extra hours for paperwork (Premier League, 2026). Liverpool play Nottingham Forest at Anfield on 28 August (Al Jazeera, 28 Aug 2026).

3 · The pattern

The pattern

The final days of a window produce two kinds of story: the record fee that completes, and the one that dies at the last hour. The dividing line is not the money. It is whether the selling club has solved its own problem.

2022
Manchester United agreed £86m with Ajax for Antony and completed the signing on deadline day, then the biggest deadline-day fee on record (Sky Sports, Sep 2022).
2023
Chelsea completed the signing of Enzo Fernández from Benfica for a then British record £106.8m on the final day of the January window, after an earlier attempt in the same window had collapsed (Sky Sports, Feb 2023).
2025
Liverpool completed the British record signing of Alexander Isak from Newcastle for £125m on deadline day, after an earlier £110m offer had been rejected (Premier League, Sep 2025).
2025
On the same day Liverpool's £35m move for Marc Guéhi collapsed. The fee was agreed, the medical was permitted and a deal sheet was submitted, and Crystal Palace pulled out at the last hour after failing to sign a replacement (Sky Sports, Sep 2025).
3 of 4
Three of the four comparable late-window deals since 2022 completed, including two on deadline day itself. The exception is the most instructive: Liverpool's £35m move for Marc Guéhi had an agreed fee, a sanctioned medical and a submitted deal sheet, and still died when Crystal Palace could not find a replacement defender. That is the live risk here. PSG's willingness to lose a forward, not Liverpool's willingness to pay, is what decides this one.

3 of 4 nine-figure or record deals agreed in the closing days of a window completed; the one that failed died because the selling club could not replace the player.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case is completion. The fee is agreed at €140m against an opening ask of £145m, which is the shape of a negotiation that has finished rather than one still running, and a five-year contract with the player is in place. Liverpool have done this exact thing twice in twelve months, landing Isak on deadline day after a rejected opening bid. PSG have depth in the position and a squad-cost incentive to bank a nine-figure fee for a player with two years left on his deal.

The honest counter is that the last outstanding item is PSG's, not Liverpool's. Clearance for a medical is the club's to give, and the Guéhi case a year ago showed that a selling club with an unsolved replacement problem will walk away from an agreed fee at the final hour, deal sheet or no deal sheet. PSG are defending European champions with a Champions League league phase starting on 8 September and no obvious incentive to weaken the front line in the four days before it. The prediction fails if PSG identify no replacement of their own before Tuesday evening.
