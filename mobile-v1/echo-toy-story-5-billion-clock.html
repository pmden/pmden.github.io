Toy Story opens biggest ever. The billion-dollar clock starts.

Pixar's fifth Toy Story film took $160m in its opening weekend, the largest debut in the franchise's 31-year history and the biggest of 2026. The question now is not whether it is a hit, but how fast the legs carry it to ten figures.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

Toy Story 5 held the top spot in its second weekend with $70m as Supergirl opened below expectations at $38m.

The Hollywood Reporter, 28 Jun 2026
2 · The event

What happened

Toy Story 5 earned $160m from 4,425 North American cinemas over the weekend of 19–21 June, the biggest domestic debut of 2026 and the largest opening in the franchise's history, well clear of the $120m record set by Toy Story 4 in 2019 (Variety, 21 Jun 2026). Overseas the film added $152m for a $312m global start, the second-largest animated opening weekend ever recorded, behind only Incredibles 2's $182.7m domestic bow in 2018.

The debut delivered the year's first $200m three-day frame for the overall box office, a milestone exhibitors had been waiting for all spring (Box Office Mojo, Jun 2026). Cinemark reported its highest-grossing June weekend in company history on the back of the release.

A week later the film proved its staying power, holding the top spot with roughly $70m in its second frame while Warner Bros' Supergirl opened to a soft $38m (The Hollywood Reporter, 28 Jun 2026). Family films with holds like this tend to run deep into August, and the $1bn threshold — passed by both previous Toy Story instalments — is now the measure that matters.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

Pixar sequels that open big do not fade. Every one of the studio's sequels to debut above $110m domestic has gone on to cross $1bn worldwide — the only variable has been how quickly.

2024
Inside Out 2 opened to $154.2m domestic and finished at $1.698bn worldwide, becoming the highest-grossing animated film ever.
2019
Toy Story 4 opened to $120.9m, then the franchise record, and closed its run at $1.073bn worldwide.
2018
Incredibles 2 opened to $182.7m — still the animated record — and finished at $1.243bn.
2016
Finding Dory opened to $135.1m and reached $1.029bn worldwide.
2010
Toy Story 3 opened to $110.3m and became the first animated film to pass $1bn, finishing at $1.067bn.
5 of 5
All five Pixar sequels to open above $110m domestic have crossed $1bn worldwide. The unbroken record hides one nuance: each of those runs had unlimited runway, and the near-term market below asks whether it happens on a seven-week clock.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case says yes on both counts. Toy Story 5 opened 32% ahead of Toy Story 4, held its second weekend better than most tentpoles, and enters July with schools out and no direct family competitor until mid-August. Toy Story 4 needed roughly eight weeks to reach $1bn from a $120m start; a film pacing a third ahead of it should compress that timetable. On the domestic market, matching Toy Story 4's day-by-day curve scaled to the bigger opening puts the film past $434m by late July with room to spare.

The honest counter-case is the calendar, not the film. Worldwide totals depend heavily on staggered international play, and 2026's family audience has already spent heavily — The Super Mario Galaxy Movie took over $1bn from the same demographic this spring. Second, front-loaded openings mean lower multipliers: if Toy Story 5 legs out at 2.8x its opening rather than Toy Story 4's 3.6x, the $1bn crossing slips past 10 August even though the final total gets there eventually. The domestic call is the safer of the two; the worldwide deadline is a genuine race.
