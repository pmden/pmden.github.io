BIGBANG is back. 31 stadiums start filling.

The K-pop trio confirmed a 20th-anniversary world stadium tour on 11 June, their first since 2017. Thirty-one shows begin 21 August at Goyang Stadium.

4 min read

What happened

BIGBANG confirm the 20/26 World Tour: 31 stadium shows from 21 August in Goyang, their first tour since 2017.

(Billboard, 11 Jun 2026)

BIGBANG announced a 31-show world stadium tour on 11 June to mark the group's 20th anniversary, opening 21 August at Goyang Stadium in South Korea and running through February 2027 (Billboard, Jun 2026). It is the group's first tour since 2017, and the first with the current three-man line-up of G-Dragon, Taeyang and Daesung.

The itinerary spans Asia, North America, Europe and Australia, with stops including Oakland, East Rutherford, Paris, London, Sydney, Bangkok, Hong Kong, Osaka, Tokyo, Kuala Lumpur, Jakarta and Kaohsiung (Variety, 11 Jun 2026). The reunion was first teased on stage at Coachella in April before the full routing landed.

The commercial question is scale. BIGBANG's previous outings were arena tours; this one books stadiums across four continents in a market where nostalgia acts have set records — and where at least one major act has cancelled a tour over soft demand (Variety, 11 Jun 2026).

The Pattern

Comeback and farewell stadium runs by legacy acts have mostly been demand events of the decade: dates sell out fast and promoters add shows within weeks. But the pattern has one loud recent exception, and it came from overestimating room size.

2025
Oasis's reunion tour sold more than 900,000 UK and Ireland tickets in a single day when it went on sale, and extra dates were added within days.
2024
The counter-example: The Black Keys scrapped their entire North American arena tour weeks after tickets went on sale, amid reports of weak demand for rooms that were too big.
2023
Elton John's Farewell Yellow Brick Road tour closed at $939m, briefly the highest-grossing tour in history, after repeatedly extending its routing.
2019
BTS sold out two Wembley Stadium shows — roughly 120,000 tickets — in around 90 minutes, proving K-pop could fill Western stadiums.
2016
Guns N' Roses' Not in This Lifetime reunion ran three years and grossed $584m, then the third-highest-grossing tour on record.
4 of 5
Four of the five comparable legacy-act stadium campaigns since 2016 sold out their initial dates and added shows within weeks. The fifth cancelled. The pattern favours expansion, but it is not unanimous.

4 of 5 comparable legacy-act stadium runs since 2016 sold out and expanded; one cancelled outright.

The Read

The weighted case says expansion. G-Dragon's 2025 solo arena tour sold out across Asia, the Coachella tease generated a measurable streaming spike for the back catalogue, and every precedent from Oasis to BTS shows that scarcity plus a long absence converts into day-one sellouts. Promoters routinely hold back second dates in key cities precisely so they can announce them as 'demand' shows; a nine-year gap and a 20th anniversary is the strongest possible setup for that play.

The honest counter is room size. BIGBANG has never toured Western stadiums as a headliner; its previous US and European shows were arenas. The line-up is a trio now, its core fanbase is a decade older, and The Black Keys' 2024 cancellation shows what happens when routing outruns demand. If the Oakland and East Rutherford shows move slowly, promoters will quietly hold rather than add — which is exactly what a 'no new dates' resolution would look like from the outside.
