Haaland fells Brazil. England are next.

Erling Haaland scored twice as Norway beat Brazil 2-1 at MetLife Stadium, sending his country into a first-ever World Cup quarter-final. History says first-time quarter-finalists stop there.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

Haaland scored in the 11th and 90th minutes as Norway beat Brazil 2-1 in the last 16, reaching a first World Cup quarter-final in the nation's history.

Al Jazeera, 5 Jul 2026
2 · The event

The night Norway arrived

Erling Haaland headed Norway in front in the 11th minute from Andreas Schjelderup's cross, then struck again in the 90th to put the last-16 tie beyond Brazil at MetLife Stadium. Neymar, a 67th-minute substitute, pulled one back from the penalty spot in stoppage time, but it was consolation only. Norway, at their first World Cup since 1998, are in the quarter-finals for the first time in the country's history (Al Jazeera, 5 Jul 2026).

The defeat extends a grim sequence for Brazil: a sixth consecutive World Cup elimination at the hands of European opposition, and the end of a campaign built around Carlo Ancelotti's mandate to close a 24-year title drought. Goalkeeper Ørjan Nyland starred behind a disciplined Norwegian block that absorbed pressure for long stretches (ESPN, 5 Jul 2026). Neymar signalled afterwards that his international career is over (ESPN, 5 Jul 2026).

Haaland's brace took him to seven goals, level with Lionel Messi and Kylian Mbappé at the top of the tournament's scoring charts (Sky Sports, Jul 2026). Norway now face England in Miami on 11 July, a repeat of the qualifying group both sides emerged from — and an echo of 1998, when Norway also beat Brazil 2-1 in the group stage in Marseille.

3 · The pattern

First-time quarter-finalists usually stop there

Norway are the sixth nation this century to reach a maiden World Cup quarter-final. The record of the previous five is nearly uniform: the run ends in the last eight, often by the finest of margins.

2022
Morocco became the first African semi-finalist, beating Portugal 1-0 in their maiden quarter-final before losing 2-0 to France — the only first-timer this century to go further.
2014
Costa Rica held the Netherlands 0-0 through extra time in their first quarter-final, then lost the shootout 4-3.
2010
Ghana drew 1-1 with Uruguay in their first quarter-final, missed a last-minute penalty after Luis Suárez's handball, and lost the shootout 4-2.
2006
Ukraine, in their first quarter-final at a debut World Cup, were beaten 3-0 by eventual champions Italy.
2002
Senegal reached the last eight at their debut tournament and fell to a golden goal, losing 1-0 to Turkey.
1 of 5
One of the last five nations to reach a maiden World Cup quarter-final went on to the semi-finals. The ceiling is real — but Morocco in 2022 showed it can be broken.

5 · The read

The Hunch read

The weight of the pattern sits against Norway. Four of the last five first-time quarter-finalists went out in the last eight, three of them without winning the tie in ninety minutes. England bring the deeper squad, a settled Thomas Tuchel structure, and a knockout win at the Azteca that suggests resilience rather than fragility. The forecast that Norway's run ends on 11 July is the one history supports.

The counter-case is unusually strong for a first-timer. Norway have just beaten Brazil, not scraped past a seeded minnow, and Haaland is level with Messi and Mbappé on seven goals with England's defence missing the suspended Jarell Quansah. Morocco 2022 proved the ceiling breaks when a first-timer pairs elite defending with a genuine match-winner. Norway have both — and a 38 per cent call on Norway is not a fringe position.
