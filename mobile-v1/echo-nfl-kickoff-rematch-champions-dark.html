NFL04 Sep 2026

Seattle opens against the team it beat in February. Champions have lost the kickoff game five times since 2004.

The Seahawks host New England at Lumen Field on Wednesday 9 September, the first Super Bowl rematch to open a season since 2016. The reigning champion has played the kickoff game in every season but one since 2004 and lost it five times, including three of the last four before last year.

1 · Today

The Patriots visit the Seahawks at Lumen Field on Wednesday 9 September in a rematch of Super Bowl LX, which New England lost 29–13 in February; it is the third Super Bowl rematch to open a season and the first since 2016.

New England Patriots, 3 Sep 2026
2 · The event

What happened

The NFL season opens on Wednesday 9 September with the Seattle Seahawks hosting the New England Patriots at Lumen Field, a rematch of Super Bowl LX, which Seattle won 29–13 in Santa Clara on 8 February. It is only the third time a Super Bowl rematch has opened a season and the first since Denver hosted Carolina in 2016; both teams finished 14–3 last year and New England had the league's second-ranked scoring offence (New England Patriots, 3 Sep 2026).

The Super Bowl was won by Seattle's defence, which sacked Drake Maye six times and forced three turnovers, with Kenneth Walker named most valuable player (ESPN, 8 Feb 2026). The rematch is Seattle's second appearance in the kickoff game.

The opener has been a champion's privilege since 2004, and the champion has lost it five times, with one year, 2019, when the league chose a Packers-Bears centenary game instead (ESPN, 4 Sep 2025).

3 · The pattern

The pattern

The last four kickoff games, each with the defending champion at home, from the league's own match reports.

2025
September 2025: Philadelphia, the champion, beat Dallas 24–20 after a lightning delay, with Jalen Hurts rushing for two touchdowns (NFL, 5 Sep 2025).
2024
September 2024: Kansas City, the champion, beat Baltimore in the opener, ending a run of two straight kickoff defeats for defending champions (ESPN, 4 Sep 2025).
2023
September 2023: Detroit beat the champion Chiefs 21–20 at Arrowhead, David Montgomery scoring the go-ahead touchdown late (NFL, 8 Sep 2023).
2022
September 2022: Buffalo beat the champion Rams 31–10 at SoFi Stadium, sacking Matthew Stafford seven times (NFL, 9 Sep 2022).
16 of 21
In 16 of the 21 kickoff games since 2004 in which the defending champion played, the champion won. The five defeats include the Rams in 2022 and the Chiefs in 2023, before the Chiefs and Eagles won the last two. The opponent this time is the team the champion beat by 16 points seven months ago, with a quarterback who was sacked six times that night.

Two wins and two losses for the champion in the last four openers; five losses in twenty-one since 2004.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case is Seattle: a 16-in-21 base rate for champions, home field, and a defence that took the Patriots apart in February. The market sits at 60% rather than higher because the two most recent counter-examples, 2022 and 2023, came against opponents who were good enough to win the same season, and New England went 14–3.

The honest counter-case is that the Super Bowl was a one-off and the Patriots have had seven months to answer six sacks. Openers are low-information games; the Rams and Chiefs both lost theirs to teams with new pieces. The Seahawks prediction fails if Maye's protection holds; the sack market, priced below even, fails on the same thing, which is why the two are correlated.
