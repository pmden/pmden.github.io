AI Safety12 Sep 2026

AI chiefs ask to slow AI, Trump says no. UK answered 3 of 8 such calls in a month.

Anthropic's Dario Amodei asked AI firms on 12 September to slow down. Sam Altman and Elon Musk backed him. Donald Trump dismissed the risk next day.

4 min read

What happened

Dario Amodei, chief executive of Anthropic, published an essay on Saturday 12 September calling for the pace of AI capability gains to slow and setting out a three-step plan that begins with permanent third-party evaluators inside the labs; Sam Altman of OpenAI and Elon Musk of xAI endorsed the plan the same day, and on Sunday Donald Trump said in Ireland that the warnings concerned things that would not happen and that whoever wins AI wins.

(BBC News 12 Sep)

The call came from the top of the industry rather than from resigning staff. Amodei's essay, titled We Must Pace the Frontier, said capability gains must slow so that safety work can catch up, and proposed three steps: embedded outside evaluators with employee-level access, common standards among Western frontier labs, and coordination with China. Anthropic committed to the first step on its own. Altman replied that OpenAI would do the same and had more to share soon, Musk posted that Amodei was right, and Demis Hassabis said the direction was correct. Altman also told Fortune that OpenAI would not float in 2026 because of the safety moment. (Guardian 12 Sep; BBC 12 Sep; Guardian 12 Sep)

Washington refused within a day. Trump, at his golf course in Ireland on Sunday, said negative forces were raising things that would not happen and that whoever wins AI wins. David Sacks, co-chair of the president's science council, told Amodei to stop pretending he needed anyone's permission and called the demand for a regulatory framework a form of blackmail. The plan needs a federal antitrust waiver before rival labs can coordinate, which is why the refusal matters. Barack Obama, at a fundraiser last week, urged Democrats to build a public framework covering a safety slowdown, and Trump meets Xi Jinping in Washington on 24 September. (BBC 13 Sep; Guardian 13 Sep; Guardian 13 Sep)

Britain has an interest and a gap. The Department for Science, Innovation and Technology was abolished in the July reshuffle, so there is no technology secretary; the AI minister Kanishka Narayan attends cabinet and holds the AI Security Institute brief. AISI was not given pre-release access to Anthropic's Mythos 5.1 last week, and Amodei's first step is a description of the job AISI already does. The King hosts the heads of Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind and Nvidia on Thursday under the Ditchley Foundation, with a session on prudence at the frontier. No gov.uk item on the call had appeared by Sunday evening. (BBC 21 Jul; FT 9 Sep; Independent 13 Sep; Guardian 13 Sep)

Held means the UK government published a formal response within 30 days: a statement or readout naming the call or its authors, or a new review, taskforce or consultation on frontier AI safety. The Read

Each actor's need is on the record. Anthropic is expected to start marketing an IPO of more than $2tn from mid-October, and Altman put OpenAI's float off to 2027 citing safety; investors at the Goldman Sachs conference and critics quoted by the Guardian read the safety talk as a way to invite rules that shut out smaller rivals, and OpenAI itself said in June that governments, not labs, should set the pace before backing Amodei in September. Musk owns xAI and dates his own warnings to 2014. Washington needs the China race: Trump said whoever wins AI wins, and he meets Xi on 24 September. Britain needs AISI to matter, and the plan's first step is a job AISI already does. The actors with a move inside the window are Narayan and No 10 on gov.uk, the EU AI Office, and a Congress that Obama has urged to act. (Guardian 13 Sep; BBC 12 Sep; Al Jazeera 5 Jun; Guardian 12 Sep)

The weighted case is a UK statement or review inside the month, but not by much. Three of the last eight calls drew one, and all three came in eight weeks of 2023 when Sunak wanted a summit and met Altman, Amodei and Hassabis within eight days of Altman's testimony. The market opens at 42 rather than 37 because the ingredients of 2023 are partly back: a live grievance over Mythos 5.1, an AI minister in cabinet, a royal convening on Thursday, and a proposal that flatters the institute Britain built. A readout naming Amodei's plan would be enough to resolve YES. (GOV.UK 24 May 2023; GOV.UK 7 Jun 2023; BBC 21 Jul)

The honest counter-case is the last three years. Four calls since September 2023 drew nothing from London that named them, and the government's AI items this year have been adoption summits and grant rounds. Burnham's government has no technology department and has said nothing on the call. The prediction fails if gov.uk carries only adoption and funding items through 13 October; the lab market fails if the companies stop at evaluator access, as OpenAI's August slowdown stopped at two weeks of training; the EU market fails if the Commission confines itself to spokesperson remarks. (GOV.UK 19 Feb; GOV.UK 9 Jun; BBC 19 Aug)
