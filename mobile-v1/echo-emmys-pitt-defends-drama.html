Emmys04 Sep 2026

The Pitt returns with 25 nominations. Defending drama champions have repeated seven times in eleven.

The 78th Emmys are handed out on Monday 14 September with HBO Max's The Pitt, last year's drama winner, leading all series with 25 nominations. Since 2009 eleven reigning drama champions have been nominated again the following year; seven won.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

The Television Academy lists eight nominees for Outstanding Drama Series at the 78th Emmy Awards on 14 September, including the reigning winner The Pitt, alongside The Diplomat, The Gilded Age, A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms, Paradise, Pluribus, Slow Horses and Your Friends & Neighbors.

Television Academy, 4 Sep 2026
2 · The event

What happened

The 78th Primetime Emmy Awards are on Monday 14 September at the Peacock Theatre in Los Angeles. The Television Academy's nominees for Outstanding Drama Series are The Diplomat, The Gilded Age, A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms, Paradise, The Pitt, Pluribus, Slow Horses and Your Friends & Neighbors (Television Academy, 2026). The Pitt, which won the category last year, leads all programmes with 25 nominations, and Hacks leads comedy (Variety, Jul 2026).

The comedy field is Abbott Elementary, The Bear, Hacks, Margo's Got Money Troubles, Nobody Wants This, Only Murders in the Building, Shrinking and Widow's Bay (Television Academy, 2026).

The drama race is a test of a specific proposition: whether a champion that comes back with the most nominations keeps the top prize. The Academy's own archive gives eleven cases since 2009 of a reigning drama winner nominated again the next year.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

Eleven times since 2009 the reigning Outstanding Drama Series winner has been nominated again the following year. The results come from the Television Academy's records.

2023
2023: Succession, the 2022 winner, won again against Andor, Better Call Saul, The Crown, House of the Dragon, The Last of Us, The White Lotus and Yellowjackets (Television Academy, 2023).
2022
2022: The Crown, the 2021 winner, lost to Succession (Television Academy, 2022).
2019
2019: Game of Thrones, the 2018 winner, won its fourth and final drama Emmy (Television Academy, 2019).
2018
2018: The Handmaid's Tale, the 2017 winner, lost to Game of Thrones (Television Academy, 2018).
2016
2016: Game of Thrones, the 2015 winner, won again (Television Academy, 2016).
2014
2014: Breaking Bad, the 2013 winner, won for its final season (Television Academy, 2014).
2013
2013: Homeland, the 2012 winner, lost to Breaking Bad (Television Academy, 2013).
2012
2012: Mad Men, winner from 2008 to 2011, was nominated and lost to Homeland (Television Academy, 2012). Its three earlier defences, in 2009, 2010 and 2011, all succeeded (Television Academy, 2011).
7 of 11
In 7 of the 11 cases since 2009 in which the reigning Outstanding Drama Series winner was nominated the following year, it won again: Mad Men in 2009, 2010 and 2011, Breaking Bad in 2014, Game of Thrones in 2016 and 2019, and Succession in 2023. The four failures, in 2012, 2013, 2018 and 2022, each lost to a series in its first or second season. This year's field has three first-season dramas.

Seven of eleven defences succeeded; every failure came against a first-season show.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case favours the champion. Seven of eleven defences succeeded, the show comes in with the most nominations, and every one of the repeat winners in the set was, like The Pitt, the most nominated drama of its year. That puts the market at 58%, a little below the raw base rate to allow for a crowded field of eight.

The honest counter-case is the shape of the four failures: each lost to a newcomer in its first or second season. Pluribus, A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms and Paradise are all new, and the Academy has shown it will move on from a medical procedural once the novelty of its format fades. The prediction fails if voters treat the second season as more of the same, which is how Homeland and The Handmaid's Tale lost.
