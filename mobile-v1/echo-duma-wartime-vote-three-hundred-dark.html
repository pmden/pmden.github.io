Russia04 Sep 2026

Russia votes at war for the first time. United Russia has passed 300 seats in three of four.

Russia's first State Duma election since the invasion of Ukraine runs from 18 to 20 September with the only anti-war party struck from the ballot. In three of the last four Duma elections United Russia took the 300 seats that let it change the constitution alone.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

Russia's Supreme Court struck the anti-war Yabloko party from the September Duma ballot, upholding a suit from the pro-Kremlin Rodina party. Yabloko, polling around 3%, was the only registered party campaigning against the war.

Al Jazeera, 10 Aug 2026
2 · The event

What happened

Voting for the 450-seat State Duma takes place from 18 to 20 September, the first parliamentary election since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine. Putin's decree fixing the date was published in June (Kremlin, 16 Jun 2026). Half the seats are filled from party lists with a 5% threshold and half in single-mandate districts, the system that gave United Russia 88% of the district seats in 2021 on less than half the list vote (Al Jazeera, 20 Sep 2021).

On 10 August the Supreme Court annulled Yabloko's registration on a suit brought by Rodina, removing the only registered party opposing the war from the list ballot. Yabloko was polling at about 3%, below the threshold, and its leader Nikolai Rybakov said the party would appeal; about a hundred supporters gathered outside the court (Al Jazeera, 10 Aug 2026). Yabloko candidates remain in some single-mandate districts.

The number that matters is 300. Two-thirds of the Duma is the majority required to amend the constitution without other parties, and it is the line United Russia has been measured against at every election since 2007. In 2021 the party held it with 324 seats despite falling below 50% of the list vote, on a campaign the opposition called a sham (Al Jazeera, 20 Sep 2021).

3 · The pattern

The pattern

Four Duma elections since 2007 give a clean dataset: the ruling party's seat count, and whether it cleared the constitutional majority of 300.

2021
September 2021: United Russia won 324 seats, taking 88% of the single-mandate districts while its list vote fell just below 50%, from 54% in 2016 (Al Jazeera, 20 Sep 2021).
2016
September 2016: United Russia took 54% of the list vote and won 203 of 225 districts, for 343 seats and a constitutional majority (Al Jazeera, 19 Sep 2021).
2011
December 2011: United Russia fell to 238 seats on 49.3% of the vote after a campaign marked by fraud allegations, keeping a simple majority but losing the two-thirds it had held since 2007 (Al Jazeera, 30 Nov 2011).
2007
December 2007: United Russia won 64% of the vote and 315 seats, a constitutional majority that let it change the constitution without any other party (Al Jazeera, 19 Sep 2021).
3 of 4
In 3 of the 4 State Duma elections since 2007 United Russia won at least 300 of 450 seats: 315 in 2007, 343 in 2016 and 324 in 2021. The exception, 2011, came after a campaign that drew mass protests over fraud and left the party on 238 seats. The single-mandate half of the ballot, introduced for 2016, has delivered the two-thirds both times it has been used.

Three of four Duma elections since 2007 gave United Russia 300 seats or more.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case says 300 is cleared again. The district half of the ballot has produced 198 and 203 seats for United Russia at the two elections it has been used, which means the party needs only around a third of the list vote to reach the constitutional majority. With Yabloko off the list ballot and the regions reportedly instructed to secure a majority, the market sits at 80%.

The honest counter-case is 2011, the one election in the set where the party fell short, and it came when discontent was visible and the count was contested in the streets. The 2026 campaign runs against a fuel crisis, internet blackouts and strikes on Russian cities. The prediction fails if the list vote sinks far enough below 40% that even a district sweep cannot compensate, or if the authorities choose a smaller declared majority to make the result look plausible.
