Sky wants ITV. Half of this year's CMA files started the clock by day 56.

The CMA opened its file on Sky's acquisition of ITV's media and entertainment arm on 23 July and has still not started the Phase 1 clock.

4 min read

What happened

The CMA case page for Sky's anticipated acquisition of ITV M&E Holdings Limited records only the invitation to comment of 23 July to 6 August and states that Phase 1 has not been formally launched; on 2 September the CMA launched Phase 1 in E.ON's acquisition of OVO, 56 days after opening that file.

(GOV.UK, 3 Sep 2026)

The CMA opened its file on Sky UK Limited's anticipated acquisition of ITV M&E Holdings Limited on 23 July 2026 with an invitation to comment that closed on 6 August. The page states that the CMA has not yet launched its formal investigation, that the invitation was the first part of its information gathering, that it has received the information needed to begin pre-notification, and that the page will be updated when Phase 1 formally commences (GOV.UK, Sky / ITV merger inquiry). Until then no statutory deadline attaches to the deal.

That distinction is the whole of the timing question. The 40 working day Phase 1 period starts only when the CMA formally launches the inquiry by notice to the parties, once pre-notification is complete, so a merger can sit in public view for months with no clock. The day before this Echo, the CMA launched Phase 1 in E.ON's acquisition of OVO, opened on 8 July: 56 days from opening, with a decision deadline of 28 October (GOV.UK, E.ON / OVO merger inquiry). The Co-operative Group's purchase of Southern Co-operative, opened 4 June, was launched on 30 July, also 56 days (GOV.UK, Co-operative Group / Southern Co-operative).

The regulator is carrying a second large media file at the same time under a different mechanism. The Secretary of State said on 20 January 2026 that she was minded to intervene in Daily Mail and General Trust's acquisition of Telegraph Media Group, issued a public interest intervention notice on 12 February, and the CMA delivered its report on 21 April against a 10 June deadline shared with Ofcom; the case remains open (GOV.UK, DMGT / Telegraph Media Group). A notice of that kind on Sky and ITV would put the minister and Ofcom into the timetable alongside the CMA.

The Pattern

Inclusion rule, fixed before the count: every merger inquiry on the CMA case finder opened since May 2025 with an invitation to comment before the formal Phase 1 launch, where the page records the launch date or where more than 56 days had passed by 3 September 2026 without one. Cases launched on the day they opened, or opened fewer than 56 days before 3 September, are excluded because they cannot fail or pass the test. The test is the one the first market asks: was Phase 1 launched within 56 days of the case opening. Twenty-nine cases qualify; the last eight by opening date are shown.

4 of 8
Four of the last eight merger files the CMA opened with an invitation to comment had their Phase 1 clock started within 56 days, and two of those four landed on day 56 exactly. Across the 29 files since May 2025 it is 16. The interval is set partly by the parties' pre-notification and partly by the authority, and the largest transactions in the record, Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery and Getty and Shutterstock, both missed 56 by a day to four days.

4 of the last 8 started the clock within 56 days; the full record since May 2025 is 16 of 29. The misses cluster at 57 to 98 days (Seras, Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery at 57, CRH at 58, Getty at 60, nexfibre at 67, Hays at 70, Macquarie at 75, Global Payments at 77, FIS at 83, ABF at 92, Brink's at 98) and one file, AkzoNobel and Axalta opened 16 April, had no launch after 140 days. Two 2026 files launched at exactly 56.

What this pattern excludes

Sky / ITV, McCormick / Unilever, Vivisol / Air Liquide, FirstCash / Ramsdens, NRG Fleet / Specialist Fleet Services
All opened after 9 July 2026, so none had reached 56 days by 3 September; one is the subject of the market.
Aviva / Direct Line and Boeing / Spirit AeroSystems
Phase 1 was launched on the day the case opened, so there was no pre-notification wait to measure.
Healthcare Ireland / Hutchinson Homes
Opened 4 June 2026 with an initial enforcement order and an invitation to comment only on 31 July, a completed merger caught after the event; the opening date does not mark the start of a pre-notification wait.
The DMGT / Telegraph Media Group case
It runs under a public interest intervention notice with a statutory reporting deadline set by the Secretary of State; a different clock, so it belongs in the story rather than the count.

The Read

The 17 September market asks whether Sky and ITV behave like the median file or like the big ones. Half of the last eight started the clock by day 56, and two of them on day 56 itself, which is why the market sits close to evens rather than below it. What pulls it under is size. The two largest deals in the record, Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery at 57 days and Getty and Shutterstock at 60, both missed the threshold narrowly, and the one file with no launch after 140 days, AkzoNobel and Axalta, is a cross-border industrial merger with parallel reviews elsewhere. Pre-notification is in the parties' hands as much as the CMA's.

The two intervention markets are the ones that would change the shape of the case. A merger between the largest commercial broadcaster and the largest pay-television operator raises media plurality questions that competition law alone does not answer, and the government intervened in a newspaper deal this year on a minded-to letter followed by a notice 23 days later. If a notice comes, Ofcom joins the timetable, the Secretary of State takes the decision, and the Telegraph file, opened in February and still open in September, becomes the precedent for how long that takes. What would invalidate the timing pattern is a launch that arrives with a notice attached, because the 29 files above were all decided by the CMA alone.
