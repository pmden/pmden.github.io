UK health data13 Sep 2026

NHS quietly gave Palantir GP records. 2 of 6 such quiet moves got a challenge.

The Financial Times reported on 13 September that NHS England added GP patient records to Palantir's data platform with no public announcement.

4 min read

What happened

The Financial Times reported on 13 September that GP data has been added to the NHS Federated Data Platform run by Palantir without a public announcement, after NHS England's 2023 line that GP data would not be part of the national platform.

(Financial Times 13 Sep)

The Financial Times reported on Sunday 13 September that GP data has been added to the NHS Federated Data Platform, the Palantir-run system that links hospital and commissioner data across England, without a public announcement. The FT page is behind a paywall and no other whitelisted outlet had carried the story by Sunday evening. NHS England's own record shows the shift. Its December 2023 update said GP data would not be part of the national platform; its FDP questions page, last updated on 14 April 2026, says some product data may be sourced from GP records and that trusts and integrated care boards may place GP datasets they lawfully hold into their own instance of the platform. (FT 13 Sep; NHS England 7 Dec 2023; NHS England 14 Apr 2026)

The platform is already in a scrutiny cycle. In May the FT revealed that Palantir engineers could see identifiable patient data in the platform's national integration tenant; NHS England later admitted its data protection impact assessment had described supplier access wrongly, apologised and said it was correcting the privacy notice and reviewing permissions. Two select committees called in June and July for the government to use the February 2027 break clause. On 7 September the health innovation minister James Frith told the Health Committee chair Layla Moran that mistrust of Palantir might reduce people's willingness to share data, after 60,000 more patients registered a national data opt-out between mid-May and mid-July. (Guardian 11 May; NHS England response to NDG; Guardian 9 Jul; Guardian 7 Sep)

GP records are the part of NHS data with the sharpest history. The care.data scheme of 2014 and the GP data extraction of 2021 were both paused within weeks of becoming public after the BMA and the Royal College of GPs objected, and the BMA told doctors in February to explore refusing non-direct-care use of the platform. The Commons sits on 14 and 15 September and then rises for conference recess until 12 October, so Westminster's first chance to react in the chamber is short. (BBC 19 Feb 2014; BBC 8 Jun 2021; Guardian 12 Feb; UK Parliament recess dates)

The Pattern

Occasions since 2014 when an NHS body in England widened the patient data held on, or open to, a central or contractor-run platform and the public learned of it from the press or a quiet document rather than an announcement, six in all. Held means the ICO, the BMA or the Commons Health and Social Care Committee opened an inquiry, an investigation or a published written challenge within 30 days of the expansion becoming public. Two of the six quiet NHS data expansions since 2014 drew a formal ICO, BMA or Health Committee challenge within 30 days, both when GP records were involved (2014, 2021), and both were paused within 60 days. The BMA acted in both; the ICO in none.

Two of six drew a formal challenge within 30 days, both when GP records were the data at stake; in those two NHS England also paused the scheme within 60 days. The four hospital-data or contractor cases drew lawsuits, watchdog statements or debates, but nothing from the ICO, the BMA or the Health Committee inside the month.

The Read

NHS England's need is delivery. Its planning framework of October 2025 tells every acute, community and mental health provider to be on the platform from April 2026 and tells integrated care boards to run population health tools on it, while the 2023 line that GP data would stay off the national platform was softened in the April 2026 questions page to allow GP datasets in local instances. The government is deciding whether to use the February 2027 break clause, and the minister has put the opt-out rise on record. Palantir's role has grown at each step since the 2020 Covid datastore, and its UK head told MPs in April that the company has no interest in patient data. The actors with a move inside the window are the ICO, which can open an investigation and which NHS England says it is working with on governance; the BMA, which can advise practices to keep GP data out of local platform instances; and MPs, who can table an urgent question on 14 or 15 September or when the House returns on 12 October, with the Health Committee chair already in correspondence with the minister. (NHS England 27 Oct 2025; NHS England 14 Apr 2026; Guardian 7 Sep; BBC 14 Apr; NHS England response to NDG; UK Parliament recess dates)

The weighted case is a professional objection rather than a regulatory one. In both earlier GP-record cases the BMA and the Royal College of GPs objected within the month and NHS England paused the scheme: care.data was delayed six months on 18 February 2014 and the 2021 extraction was pushed back on 8 June and then shelved in July. The four hospital-data or contractor cases drew lawsuits, a National Data Guardian statement and a Commons debate, but nothing from the ICO, the BMA or the Health Committee inside 30 days. That split is why the BMA market opens at 62 and the ICO market at 20: the regulator has not moved fast in any of the six, while the doctors' body has moved fast every time GP records were the data in question. (BBC 19 Feb 2014; BBC 8 Jun 2021; GOV.UK 20 Jul 2021; Guardian 30 Nov 2023; GOV.UK 3 Jun)

The honest counter-case is that this expansion is local and lawful on NHS England's account, not a national extraction. The questions page says GP datasets enter only where a practice has a sharing agreement with its trust or board, which is the 2023 position restated rather than a new programme, and the BMA already published its advice in February, so it may see nothing new to say. The DeepMind case of 2016 shows the ICO can open an investigation into a hospital data transfer, but no whitelist page dates that opening inside a month, so it sits outside the count. The BMA prediction fails if the story stays inside the FT and the committee's break-clause campaign absorbs the response; the ICO market fails if the regulator treats the matter as one for NHS England's own governance groups; the Commons market fails if the recess swallows the window. (NHS England 14 Apr 2026; Guardian 12 Feb; BBC 22 Sep 2016; Guardian 9 Jul)
