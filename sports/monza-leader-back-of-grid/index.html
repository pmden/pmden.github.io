Formula 101 Sep 2026

The championship leader takes an engine penalty at Monza. Leaders who did that reached the podium five times in seven.

Kimi Antonelli takes a full new power unit and a back-of-grid start at Monza on 6 September, a week after Lando Norris won a second straight race at Zandvoort.

3 min read

What happened

Championship leader Kimi Antonelli will take a full new power unit and start the Italian Grand Prix towards the back of the grid, Mercedes confirmed, with Toto Wolff saying the team's calculations made Monza the best track to take the penalty; Antonelli is 59 points clear in the standings.

(Formula 1, 31 Aug 2026)

Mercedes have confirmed that Kimi Antonelli will take a complete new power unit at his home race and start towards the back of the grid on Sunday 6 September. Toto Wolff said in Zandvoort that the team's calculations made Monza the best track to take the penalty, and that the decision was about the championship rather than public relations. Antonelli arrives 59 points clear in his second season (Formula 1, 31 Aug 2026).

The form is with McLaren. Lando Norris won the Dutch Grand Prix on 23 August from Antonelli and George Russell, his second consecutive victory, in a race stopped early on after Max Verstappen crashed (FIA, 23 Aug 2026).

A leader starting at the back is rare but not new. Since 2016 the driver at the top of the standings has taken a grid penalty for new engine or gearbox parts seven times, from Hamilton's 55-place penalty at Spa in 2016 to Verstappen's 10-place penalty at the same circuit in 2024 (BBC Sport, 28 Aug 2016) (Sky Sports, 26 Jul 2024).

The Pattern

Every race since 2016 in which the championship leader started with a grid penalty for new engine or gearbox parts, where he started, and where he finished. Newest first; results from BBC Sport's race reports.

2024
July 2024: the championship leader took a 10-place penalty for exceeding his engine allowance, started 11th and finished fourth after George Russell's disqualification; the strategy models had expected him to win.
2023
July 2023: a five-place penalty for gearbox parts dropped Verstappen from pole to sixth; he led a Red Bull one-two and extended his lead to 125 points.
2022
September 2022: a five-place penalty for engine parts put Verstappen seventh on the Monza grid; he was second within five laps and won, leaving him 116 points clear.
2022
August 2022: the championship leader started 14th after an engine-parts penalty, led by lap 18 and won by a distance from Perez.
2021
September 2021: Verstappen, leading Hamilton on arrival, started from the back row after an engine penalty and came through late rain to finish second; Hamilton's win put him two points ahead.
2021
October 2021: Hamilton, leading on arrival, started 11th after a penalty for engine parts, ran third with ten laps left, and finished fifth after a late tyre stop; Verstappen retook the lead.
2016
August 2016: Hamilton started 21st after a raft of engine penalties and finished third behind Rosberg and Ricciardo, keeping the championship lead by nine points.
5 of 7
Five of the last seven championship leaders who started with a grid penalty for engine or gearbox parts finished on the podium: Hamilton from 21st at Spa in 2016, Verstappen from the back row in Russia in 2021, and Verstappen's three wins from 14th, seventh and sixth in 2022 and 2023. The two misses were Hamilton's fifth from 11th in Turkey in 2021 and Verstappen's fourth from 11th at Spa in 2024. That is the full record since 2016.

Five podiums, including three wins, in the seven races since 2016 in which the championship leader started with a component penalty.

The Read

The weighted case is a podium, not a win. Leaders who take these penalties are usually leading because they have the fastest car, and in five of seven the car carried them back into the top three. The win market opens low because Monza's grid was expected to be set by Ferrari and McLaren pace, and only Verstappen at his peak converted a penalty into victory.

The honest counter-case is the two misses. Both came when the leader started 11th and found the midfield harder to clear than the models said, at Spa in 2024 and in Turkey in 2021, and Antonelli will start behind more cars than either. The podium prediction fails if a first-lap incident or a safety car at the wrong moment traps him behind slower traffic; the Mercedes market rests on Russell, who starts wherever the car qualifies.
