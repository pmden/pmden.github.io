Forest bank £116m for Anderson. Glasner wants more before Tuesday.

Nottingham Forest sold Elliot Anderson to Manchester City for a reported £116m, one of the two largest deals of the summer. Their new head coach says he has already identified further targets, with the window closing at 11pm on Tuesday.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

Nottingham Forest head coach Oliver Glasner said he has already identified further signings he wants completed before the transfer window closes.

Sky Sports, 30 Aug 2026
2 · The event

A record fee out, and two days to spend it

Manchester City signed Elliot Anderson from Nottingham Forest for a reported £116m, second only to Chelsea's £117m purchase of Morgan Rogers from Aston Villa among this summer's Premier League deals (Sky Sports, Aug 2026; ESPN, Aug 2026).

Forest also start the season under a new head coach. Oliver Glasner is one of a record nine Premier League managers taking charge of a first league game for their club this month (Sky Sports, 20 Aug 2026), and he has said publicly that further targets are already identified (Sky Sports, 30 Aug 2026).

The window opened on 15 June and closes at 23:00 BST on Tuesday 1 September (Premier League, 2026). Forest have form for volume at speed: they made 21 signings in the summer of 2022, and 30 in the year after promotion (ESPN, Sep 2022).

3 · The pattern

What selling clubs do with the fee

Inclusion rule, fixed before the outcome: Premier League clubs that lost one or more first-choice starters for a fee during a summer window since 2023, with at least a fortnight of that window still to run. January windows are out, because registration constraints differ. Deals completed on deadline day itself are out, because replacement is arithmetically impossible. The measured outcome is whether a senior replacement in the same area of the pitch was signed before the same window shut.

2023
Liverpool lost Jordan Henderson and Fabinho from central midfield in July and signed Wataru Endō from Stuttgart on 18 August, on a four-year deal (Sky Sports, Aug 2023). Replaced.
2023
West Ham lost captain Declan Rice from midfield to Arsenal for £105m on 15 July and signed Edson Álvarez from Ajax on 10 August (Premier League, Aug 2023). Replaced.

2026
Aston Villa sold Morgan Rogers to Chelsea for £117m and Ezri Konsa to Arsenal for £51m, and brought in Nicolas Jackson for £65m and Johan Manzambi for a reported £59.5m in the same window (ESPN, Aug 2026). Replaced.
4 of 4
All four qualifying cases since 2023 ended with a senior replacement signed inside the same window. Four clears the floor for publishing a number at all, but a unanimous four still carries a wide margin of error: the honest reading is that the direction is well established and the strength is not measurable from this sample. The mechanism behind it is straightforward. A club that sells in August cannot spend the money again until January, faces its own supporters immediately, and has a head coach who has just lost a starter. Forest sit squarely inside that description, with a new coach saying in public that targets exist.

4 of the 4 qualifying cases since 2023 saw the selling club sign a senior replacement before the same window closed. The sample is small and unanimous, which is a reason to treat the direction as informative and the strength as not.

What this pattern excludes

Marc Guéhi, Crystal Palace, 2025
The sale collapsed at the last hour, so there was no completed departure for a replacement to answer; the case belongs to the deal-completion pattern, not this one.
Deadline-day departures with no window left
Where the sale completes in the final hours, a replacement is arithmetically impossible, so including them would load the count towards a foregone answer.
January-window sales
Mid-season squad registration and a smaller market change the club's options, so the mechanism is not the same one.
Squad and academy sales
Selling a player who was not a first-choice starter creates no hole to fill, so the decision under test does not arise.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case is more business. Forest have removed their most valuable midfielder from a squad that has a new head coach, and the coach has said on camera that the targets exist. The club's own recent history is the most aggressive in the league on volume, and the money from the Anderson sale cannot be redeployed for four months if it is not spent by Tuesday night. Every qualifying comparison since 2023 went the same way, including two clubs that had just set records on the way out.

The counter is price and time. A seller with £116m in the building on 31 August is the most expensive kind of buyer in football, and Forest's negotiating position weakens with every hour. There is also a coherent alternative strategy, which is to hold the money, promote from within for three months and buy properly in January against a settled league table. That is the shape the prediction fails in: not a dramatic collapse, but a quiet Tuesday with one loan and a statement about patience.
