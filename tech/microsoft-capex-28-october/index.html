AI shares fell before Microsoft reports on 28 October. Will it plan to spend more?

Microsoft reports its July to September results on 28 October. Tech shares have fallen on fears that the AI building boom will not pay off.

5 min read

What happened

The Nasdaq 100 fell 5.5% last week, and the IMF put the bursting of the AI investment bubble at the top of three risks to the world economy.

The Nasdaq 100 index of big US tech firms fell 5.5% in the week to 16 October. Some analysts blamed the failed stock market debut of Firmus, an Australian data centre company. The International Monetary Fund (IMF) had just named the bursting of an AI investment bubble as the first of three risks to the world economy.

Big tech firms borrow heavily to build data centres. Their bonds now make up nearly 10% of the world market for top-rated company debt, up from 0.5% at the end of 2024, the IMF says. On 15 October AI shares fell further after US inflation rose to 3.7% in September, and traders began to expect higher interest rates.

Microsoft reports results for July to September on 28 October, with chief executive Satya Nadella and finance chief Amy Hood. On 18 October it pulled out of a planned AI data centre in Essex, England, after the local power network said it could not supply enough electricity until the 2030s. It stays in another project of the same builder, Nscale, in Norway.

In July Hood forecast over $50bn of spending on buildings and equipment, mostly chips and data centres, for July to September, up from $41bn in the quarter before. She said demand exceeds available supply. She put spending for calendar 2026 at about $175bn, after an accounting change moved some data centre leases out of the figure.

We expect CapEx spend will be over $50 billion including the lease reclassification impact from the useful life update.

The Pattern

We looked for cases where tech shares fell on fears that the AI boom would not pay off, before Microsoft reported its quarterly results. We found nine such reports since April 2024. All nine come from one company, with the same finance chief, Amy Hood.

The Read

Microsoft has kept promising more. In seven of nine reports after a market scare since 2024, it forecast higher spending for the next quarter. The two exceptions came in January 2025, after DeepSeek, when it said spending would stay level, and in January 2026, when it said spending would dip because of the timing of leases.

There are reasons for doubt. Hood’s estimate of about $175bn for 2026 leaves about $102bn for July to December, after $31.9bn and $41bn in the first half. If July to September comes in above about $51bn, that plan points to a lower figure for October to December. All nine reports come from one company in one boom, and Hood said in July that if demand changes, you just slow down.

The second market asks whether July to September spending tops the $50bn forecast. Twice before Microsoft gave a number with over in it, and both times it spent more: $34.9bn against over $30bn in October 2025, and $41bn against over $40bn in July 2026.
