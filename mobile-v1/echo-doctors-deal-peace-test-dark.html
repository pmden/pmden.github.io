The doctors voted yes. Now the peace gets tested.

Resident doctors in England have accepted the government's pay and jobs offer, ending a dispute that produced 21 strike days since July 2025.

4 min read

A 53% yes on a 57% turnout

Resident doctors in England vote to accept the government's offer, ending 21 days of strikes since July 2025.

(GOV.UK, 29 Jun 2026)

The BMA's resident doctors committee announced on 29 June that its members had voted to accept the government's offer on pay and jobs, formally ending the dispute in England. The margin was narrow: 53% of eligible members voted in favour, on a 57% turnout, with 32,932 doctors casting a vote (BMA, 29 Jun 2026). The dispute had produced 15 rounds of industrial action since 2023 and 21 strike days since July 2025 alone, each costing the NHS roughly £50 million.

The package delivers an average 6.6% uplift to be fully implemented by April 2027, taking resident doctor pay 35.2% higher on average than four years ago. It adds up to 4,500 extra specialty training places over three years, reimbursement of mandatory Royal College exam and portfolio fees, and standard 2016 contract terms for locally employed doctors (GOV.UK, 29 Jun 2026). Health and Social Care Secretary James Murray called it a chance to "draw a line under the disruption of previous months".

The BMA committee's own framing was less final. Its leadership said the vote was "by no means the end of the road for pay restoration", noting that resident doctors remain nearly a fifth behind 2008 pay levels in real terms (BMA, 29 Jun 2026). Nurses, meanwhile, remain in open disagreement with the government over the 3.3% award for 2026/27, keeping the wider NHS pay question live through the summer.

The Pattern

Every major NHS pay dispute of the past decade has ended with a settlement or an imposition. What varies is how long the quiet lasts afterwards. The record shows deals reliably close the immediate dispute, but the interval before the next confrontation ranges from six years to under twelve months.

2024
Resident (junior) doctors accepted a 22.3% two-year deal in September 2024, ending 44 strike days. A fresh dispute and new strikes began within ten months, in July 2025.
2023
Agenda for Change staff took a 5% rise plus a lump sum in May 2023. RCN members voted against it, but the union's re-ballot for strikes missed the legal 50% turnout threshold and nurse strikes ended anyway.
2019
Junior doctors accepted an 8.2% four-year contract deal in 2019, formally closing the 2016 dispute. No further doctors' strikes until the 2023 wave.
2018
The three-year Agenda for Change deal worth 6.5% held for its full term; no national NHS strike action followed before the pandemic.
2016
Jeremy Hunt imposed the junior doctors' contract after the first all-out strikes in NHS history. The imposition ended the strikes but the grievance resurfaced as the 2023–26 pay restoration campaign.
1 of 5
1 of the last 5 major NHS pay settlements was followed by new strike dates within twelve months. The caveat: that one was the most recent, in 2024, involving these same doctors.

NHS pay deals always end the immediate strikes; only one of the last five settlements saw strike dates return within a year.

The Read

The case for quiet is strong in the near term. The deal is broader than a headline pay number: structural pay reform, training places, and fee reimbursement address the committee's stated grievances, and members have just voted to stand down. A union that accepted an offer on Monday does not ballot for strikes in July. The 6% forecast on new BMA strike dates before 10 August reflects that mechanical reality, and the 74% on 'None' in the cross-union market reflects the same summer logic: the RCN has escalation language but no live mandate, and a lawful ballot takes weeks to run.

The honest counter-case sits in the vote itself. A 53% yes on a 57% turnout means roughly three in ten eligible doctors actively endorsed this deal, and the committee immediately reframed acceptance as a staging post toward full pay restoration. The 2024 precedent shows a signed deal is compatible with new strike dates ten months later. And the nurses' dispute over the 3.3% award for 2026/27 is unresolved, with the RCN having warned of formal escalation if summer talks fail. The doctors' war is over; the NHS pay question is not.
