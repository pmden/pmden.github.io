Mungiu joins the double club. Neon's streak hits seven.

Cristian Mungiu's Fjord won the Palme d'Or at the 79th Cannes Film Festival, making the Romanian director only the tenth filmmaker to take the prize twice. Its US distributor Neon has now handled seven consecutive Palme winners.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

Cristian Mungiu's Fjord won the Palme d'Or at the 79th Cannes Film Festival, his second after 2007.

Variety, 23 May 2026
2 · The event

What happened

Fjord, Cristian Mungiu's drama about a Romanian family of Evangelical Christians caught in a child-abuse case inside the Norwegian social system, won the Palme d'Or at the close of the 79th Cannes Film Festival on 23 May (Deadline, May 2026). The film stars Sebastian Stan and Renate Reinsve. Mungiu became the tenth director to win the prize twice, 19 years after 4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days took it in 2007 (Variety, 23 May 2026).

The win extended a remarkable commercial pattern: Neon, the US independent distributor, has now handled seven consecutive Palme d'Or winners, a run that began with Parasite in 2019 and ran through Titane, Triangle of Sadness, Anatomy of a Fall, Anora and It Was Just an Accident (Variety, 23 May 2026). Two of those — Parasite and Anora — went on to win the Academy Award for Best Picture.

The full jury slate gave prizes to Minotaur, The Black Ball and Fatherland (Festival de Cannes, 23 May 2026). For Fjord, attention now shifts to the autumn machine: Neon's Palme winners typically get a September–October festival lap and a US theatrical date announced over the summer, and the first lineup announcements from the fall festivals land in late July.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

Two-time Palme d'Or winners are one of world cinema's smallest clubs — ten members in 79 editions. The gaps between wins tell their own story: the prize tends to return to laureates late, not soon.

Ten directors have won twice in 79 editions of the festival — a 13% incidence across its whole history. But the club is compounding: three of the ten doubles landed in the last 15 years, as juries increasingly return to established auteurs rather than crown new ones.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case follows Neon's playbook, which has barely changed since Parasite: a fall-festival lap for awards positioning, then a late-September or October platform release. Anatomy of a Fall and Anora both opened in US cinemas in October of their Cannes year, with dates set over the summer. TIFF announces its main slate in late July and NYFF follows in early August, so the machinery for a YES on the festival market and a September–October answer on the window market is scheduled to move inside the resolution period. That is why the two live markets lean the way they do.

The honest counter-case is timing risk on both. Palme winners sometimes skip the announced-early circuit: Telluride, whose lineup stays secret until late August, has become Neon's preferred first US stop, and a Telluride-first strategy resolves the festival market NO even if Fjord plays every autumn festival that matters. On the release window, Neon dated some titles late — Titane's US date firmed up closer to release — and a crowded awards autumn could push Fjord toward November or even a slow 2027 international rollout, given its Romanian-Norwegian setting and subtitled, two-nation subject matter. The markets are really forecasting Neon's press-release calendar, not the film's quality.
