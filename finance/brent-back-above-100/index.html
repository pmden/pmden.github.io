Oil10 Sep 2026

Brent is back above $100. Five of the last eight crossings unwound.

Brent crude topped $101 on Wednesday after US and Houthi strikes on tankers and Saudi oil sites, and touched $102.17 on Thursday morning.

3 min read

What happened

Brent crude rose by more than 3% on Wednesday 9 September to top $101 a barrel, the first time above $100 since July, after the US said it had destroyed several Iranian tankers and Iran-backed Houthis attacked four cities in Saudi Arabia.

(The Guardian, 9 Sep 2026)

Brent crude, the international oil benchmark, rose by more than 3% on Wednesday to top $101 a barrel, its first move above $100 since July, after the US said it had destroyed multiple Iranian tankers and Houthi forces set oil installations in four Saudi cities ablaze (Guardian, 9 Sep 2026). The BBC put the price at $100.70 on Wednesday afternoon, up 2.83% on the day and the highest since late July (BBC, 9 Sep 2026). It went further on Thursday morning, touching $102.17 (Guardian, 10 Sep 2026).

The move has already reached British homes. The AA put the average price of petrol at 166.2p a litre on Tuesday, a four-year high and 4.4p up on the August bank holiday, with diesel at 187.7p; the British wholesale gas contract passed 200p a therm on Thursday, its highest since December 2022, and Ofgem's cap rises by 4% in October (Guardian, 10 Sep 2026). Andrew Bailey told MPs on Tuesday that the oil price was pushing the risks to inflation and interest rates to the upside.

The number itself is what markets trade around. Brent is up more than 60% this year, and the Guardian counts three separate spells above $100 in 2026 (Guardian, 9 Sep 2026); counted crossing by crossing, the benchmark has moved back over the line five times since Iran's block on the strait of Hormuz began in late February. The open question is not whether the war continues but whether the line holds.

The Pattern

Every occasion since 2011 on which a whitelisted publication reported Brent moving back above $100 a barrel from below. Held means the benchmark was reported below $100 again within 30 days. Newest first.

2026
Brent broke above $100 on 23 July, up from $95 the day before, on Houthi attacks on Saudi tankers, and was trading at $83.47 by 3 August after Trump called off strikes on Iran.
2026
Brent rose back above $100 on 26 May after fresh US strikes on Iranian missile sites, and was at about $92 by 29 May as Trump circulated a draft peace agreement.
2026
Brent settled above $100 on 22 April for the first time in more than two weeks as Iran seized two container ships, and did not fall below the line again until 25 May.
2026
Brent rose 6.9% to $101.70 on 13 April on news of the US blockade of the strait of Hormuz, then eased to a little over $99 the same afternoon after Trump said Iran had made contact.
2026
Brent climbed as high as $119.50 on 9 March, its first move above $100 since 2022, and was down to $85 by the time US stock markets closed the same day.
2022
Oil reached $105 a barrel on 24 February 2022 as Russia invaded Ukraine, and slid back to $99 on 15 March amid ceasefire talks and rising Covid infections in China.
2011
Brent rose to nearly $109 on 10 October 2011, a week after dropping below $100 in the eurozone sell-off, and was still cruising near $110 on 3 November.
2011
Brent went through $100 to $101.08 on 31 January 2011 on fears the Egyptian uprising would close the Suez canal, and reached $116.20 on 4 March as fighting in Libya intensified.
5 of 8
Five of the last eight moves back above $100 were reversed inside 30 days: July 2026 (11 days), May 2026 (three days), April 2026 (the same afternoon), March 2026 (the same day) and February 2022 (19 days). Three held: January 2011, which ran to $116 within five weeks on the Libyan uprising, October 2011, which was still near $110 a month later, and 22 April 2026, which stayed above the line for 33 days. The split is not random. The two crossings that held before this year came on supply losses with no diplomatic off-ramp; four of the five reversals came inside a war in which a single ceasefire headline has repeatedly taken $10 to $20 off the price in a session.

Five of the eight crossings were given back within 30 days; the three that stuck were January 2011, October 2011 and April this year, the last of them by three days.

What this pattern excludes

February 2008, Brent's first crossing of $100
The Guardian archive pages for the crossing days no longer resolve and no other whitelisted source pins the date, so the case cannot be dated to the standard.
Summer 2012 and mid-2013, when Brent drifted back across $100
No whitelisted publication marked either crossing as an event, so neither has a datable trigger; both were quiet drift rather than a reported supply shock.

The Read

The weighted case is that the line goes again. Five of the eight crossings reversed, four of them inside this year's war, and the reversals have been quick rather than gradual: the same afternoon in April, three days in May, eleven in July. The mechanism is diplomatic rather than physical. Trump has called off strikes, extended ceasefires and announced talks four times since February, and each time the price has dropped $10 to $20 in a session. Thirty days is a long window and $102 is a thin cushion above the line.

The honest counter-case is that the one crossing that held this year, 22 April, held because the blockade was physical and open-ended, and the supply picture now is worse than it was then. The Houthis have opened a second front against Saudi export infrastructure, Iran's Revolutionary Guards have declared a prohibited shipping zone beyond Hormuz, EU gas stores are 67% full against a five-year average of 84%, and Chinese crude buying has recovered towards 9m barrels a day. If Saudi barrels are physically lost rather than threatened, $100 becomes a floor and this market resolves NO. The pump market fails on a different mechanism: retailers can hold margin for weeks, so 170p may arrive after the resolution date rather than before it.
