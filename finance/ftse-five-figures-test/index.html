The FTSE crosses 10,000. The record says look down.

The FTSE 100 traded through 10,000 for the first time on the opening session of 2026, then closed just under the line at 9,951.

4 min read

What happened

The FTSE 100 crossed the 10,000 mark for the first time in its history on 2 January, the opening session of 2026.

(Reuters, 2 Jan 2026)

The FTSE 100 touched 10,000 for the first time on Friday, the opening session of 2026 (Reuters, 2 Jan 2026). The close was 9,951, just under the line, after the index ended 2025 at 9,931. The milestone has been reached in intraday trade; it has not yet been held into a closing bell.

The pace of the ascent is the story. The index first held 9,000 in July 2025, a level London had spent years failing to clear (The Times, 15 Jul 2025). Falling gilt yields, a weaker pound flattering overseas earnings and a rotation into cheap UK large caps have all pulled in the same direction, and a thousand points in under six months is a rate of climb London has not seen since the late 1990s.

Breadth is not, for once, the obvious objection. The mid-cap FTSE 250 closed 2025 at 22,470, near its own highs, so the domestic side of the market is moving with the blue chips rather than against them. What waits is the round number itself, a Bank of England decision and a full earnings calendar in the weeks ahead.

The Pattern

Action type: the FTSE 100 crosses a thousand-point milestone for the first time. Held = the index closed back below that milestone within a year of first crossing it, the test the first market applies to 10,000. Round numbers exert a documented pull on markets: they attract profit-taking, anchor headlines and mark psychological resistance.

2025
First close above 9,000 in July. The level held — the index kept climbing into 2026 without falling back below.
2023
First close above 8,000 in February. Within a month the US regional-banking crisis had dragged it back below 7,400.
2015
First crossed 7,000 in March. The level did not hold durably until October 2016, more than 18 months later.
1998
Passed 6,000 in the spring; the autumn emerging-markets and LTCM selloff pulled it back below before year-end.
3 of 4
Three of the four thousand-point milestones since 1998 were followed by a retreat below the line within a year: 6,000 in 1998, 7,000 in 2015 and 8,000 in 2023. Four cases on the page, four in the record. The one that held is the most recent, 9,000 in July 2025, which is also the closest in character to this crossing, so the longer record and the latest case point opposite ways.

Of the 4 thousand-point milestones before 10,000, 3 were followed by a fall back below the line within a year.

The Read

The weighted case is for a retreat. Round numbers do work on markets: they concentrate profit-taking, they anchor the headlines that bring in the last buyers, and three of the four times the FTSE 100 has cleared one it was back below the line inside a year. This crossing arrives after a thousand points in under six months, which is the kind of run that leaves little cushion under the level when the next shock lands.

The honest counter-case is that the one milestone that held is the most recent one, and the conditions that carried the index from 9,000 to 10,000 have not gone away: falling gilt yields, valuations cheap against global peers, and heavyweight earners paid in dollars. Breadth is confirming rather than contradicting, with the FTSE 250 closing the year near its own highs. The FTSE 100 is not the UK economy; it is a global earnings machine listed in London, and it will follow global earnings rather than the round number.
