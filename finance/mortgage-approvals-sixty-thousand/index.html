Housing30 Jul 2026

Approvals bounced in June after May's slump. The record says the bounce carries.

Mortgage approvals for house purchase rose to 58,200 in June from about 56,400 in May, after a 13 per cent fall that was the sharpest since November 2022.

4 min read

What happened

Net mortgage approvals for house purchase increased to 58,200 in June 2026, below an average of around 61,400 over the previous six months; net mortgage borrowing rose to £7.7 billion from £3.3 billion in May.

(Bank of England, 29 Jul 2026)

The Bank of England's Money and Credit release for June put approvals for house purchase at 58,200, up from May but below the six-month average of around 61,400; net borrowing of mortgage debt rose to £7.7 billion from £3.3 billion (Bank of England, 29 Jul 2026). In the seasonally adjusted series the shape is a drop from 65,044 in April to 56,396 in May, a fall of 13.3 per cent, then a rise to 58,215 in June (Bank of England, series LPMVTVX). The May fall was the largest single-month drop since November 2022, in the aftermath of the mini-Budget.

The rate backdrop has not moved. The Monetary Policy Committee held Bank Rate at 3.75 per cent on 17 June (Bank of England, June 2026), and the ONS reported CPI inflation of 2.6 per cent in the year to June, down from 2.8 per cent in May (ONS, 21 Jul 2026). Approvals lead completions by roughly three months, so June's figure reflects decisions taken with the rate on hold and quoted mortgage pricing little changed.

The next Money and Credit release covers July and is published on 1 September. It answers a narrow question: whether June's bounce was the start of a recovery or a pause in a slide. May and June were the first consecutive readings below 60,000 since January and February 2024, which is why the 60,000 line is watched, but the test the record can speak to is simpler, whether July prints above June.

The Pattern

Inclusion rule, fixed before the count: the Bank of England's seasonally adjusted series for approvals for house purchase (LPMVTVX) from April 1993, taking every month in which approvals fell by 10 per cent or more from the previous month and then rose in the following month, the shape of May and June 2026. The test is single: did the month after the rebound print higher than the rebound month. Six prior cases meet the rule; all are shown. The May to June 2026 sequence is the event under test and is not counted.

2020
Approvals fell 41.1 per cent from 15,888 in Apr 2020 to 9,360 in May 2020, then rose to 40,375 in Jun 2020; Jul 2020 printed 66,660, higher than the rebound month (Bank of England, series LPMVTVX).
2010
Approvals fell 19.8 per cent from 58,018 in Dec 2009 to 46,522 in Jan 2010, then rose to 47,791 in Feb 2010; Mar 2010 printed 49,101, higher than the rebound month (Bank of England, series LPMVTVX).
2008
Approvals fell 16.5 per cent from 31,600 in Oct 2008 to 26,371 in Nov 2008, then rose to 31,637 in Dec 2008; Jan 2009 printed 32,389, higher than the rebound month (Bank of England, series LPMVTVX).
2004
Approvals fell 14.7 per cent from 86,352 in Oct 2004 to 73,621 in Nov 2004, then rose to 83,429 in Dec 2004; Jan 2005 printed 81,633, lower than the rebound month (Bank of England, series LPMVTVX).
2000
Approvals fell 10.0 per cent from 91,537 in Dec 1999 to 82,381 in Jan 2000, then rose to 95,873 in Feb 2000; Mar 2000 printed 96,752, higher than the rebound month (Bank of England, series LPMVTVX).
1997
Approvals fell 10.1 per cent from 106,088 in Jan 1997 to 95,395 in Feb 1997, then rose to 98,463 in Mar 1997; Apr 1997 printed 102,007, higher than the rebound month (Bank of England, series LPMVTVX).
5 of 6
Five of the six rebounds since 1993 carried: March 1997, February 2000, December 2008, February 2010 and June 2020 were each followed by a higher month. The one that did not was December 2004, when the series fell back in January 2005. The window and the full record are the same six cases, because a fall of 10 per cent in a single month is rare outside a crisis; three of the six sit in 2008, 2010 and 2020, and the size of the bounce in those years was larger than June 2026's 3.2 per cent.

Five of the six rebounds after a fall of 10 per cent or more carried into a higher print the following month. The exception was December 2004, when January 2005 fell back.

What this pattern excludes

The June-to-July rule used in the earlier version of this Echo
It asked what a July does to a June figure in any year, a calendar question. The action in the news is a rebound after a sharp fall, so the cases are rebounds, whatever the month.
The 60,000 line as the held test
A fixed level cannot be carried across cases whose levels ran from 26,000 to 106,000. The test the cases can answer is whether the rebound continued; 60,000 is carried as the adjacent market.
Falls of 10 per cent or more not followed by a rise
Thirteen such months exist in the series, most in 2008 and 2020. They describe a different shape from June 2026, which rose, and are left out under the rule.
Approvals for remortgaging and other secured lending
The markets resolve on approvals for house purchase, series LPMVTVX. Remortgaging has a different seasonal profile and a different link to transactions.

The Read

The market opens above even because the record favours the bounce. Five of the six rebounds after a fall of 10 per cent or more were followed by a higher month, and the one exception came in a winter when the series was already sliding. June's rise was small, 3.2 per cent against double-digit bounces in 2000, 2004, 2008 and 2020, which is the reason the figure is not higher still: a small rebound leaves less evidence that buyers have returned.

What the pattern cannot see is that the rate is not moving. The three crisis-era cases in the sample were followed by cuts or by a market reopening; this one has Bank Rate held at 3.75 per cent and inflation at 2.6 per cent, a setting in which lenders have little reason to reprice. A July below June would be the first sign that May was a turn rather than a stumble, and it would put the 60,000 line, 1,800 above June, out of reach for a third month.
