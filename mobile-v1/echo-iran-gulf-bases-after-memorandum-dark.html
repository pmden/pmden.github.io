Iran04 Sep 2026

The memorandum ran out on 17 August. Two weeks later the Gulf bases are targets again.

US strikes along Iran's southern coast and Iranian missiles at bases in Bahrain, Jordan, Kuwait and Iraq made 2 September the heaviest exchange in more than a month. In four earlier Gulf confrontations both sides stood down within days; the 2026 war is the case that broke the rule.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

US strikes hit dozens of targets along Iran's southern coast, killing at least 18, and Iran fired missiles and drones at US bases in Bahrain, Jordan, Kuwait and Iraq. Trump said Iran would be hit 'much harder' if it responded again.

Al Jazeera, 2 Sep 2026
2 · The event

What happened

In the early hours of Wednesday 2 September the United States struck dozens of military targets along Iran's southern coast, including Konarak, Bandar Abbas, Jask, Asaluyeh and Qeshm Island, and sites near Ahvaz in Khuzestan. Iranian officials said at least 18 people were killed, among them guests at a wedding in Kuhestak, where more than 60 were wounded (Al Jazeera, 2 Sep 2026). Central Command said it acted after the IRGC attempted attacks on US troops and commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz (Al Jazeera, 1 Sep 2026).

Iran's response reached four countries. The IRGC said it hit the Sheikh Isa airbase in Bahrain, the Prince Hassan airbase and a Marine base in Jordan, the headquarters of the US commander at Ali Al Salem in Kuwait and US facilities in Erbil. Jordan said it intercepted 10 of 13 ballistic missiles; Kuwait reported intercepting drones and missiles with no casualties (Al Jazeera, 2 Sep 2026). By Thursday Kuwait's military described 'ongoing Iranian aggression' as its air defences engaged again (Al Jazeera, 3 Sep 2026).

The diplomatic frame has gone. The 14-point memorandum signed on 17 June by Trump at Versailles and by Pezeshkian in Tehran (Al Jazeera, 17 Jun 2026) set a 60-day window to agree final terms; that window closed on 17 August with the Strait of Hormuz unresolved. Trump said he was not trying to force Iran to the table and preferred the present position, 'with almost total control of the Hormuz Strait' (Al Jazeera, 2 Sep 2026).

3 · The pattern

The pattern

Before 2026, every direct US-Iran confrontation in the Gulf ended the same way: a sharp exchange, then both sides stood down within days or weeks. The war that began in February is the exception that already happened.

2024
January 2024: after Iran-backed Houthi attacks on more than a hundred merchant ships, the US and UK launched sustained strikes on Yemen. The campaign ground on for months without direct US-Iran fighting (Al Jazeera, 4 Feb 2024).
2020
January 2020: five days after the US killed Qassem Soleimani, Iran fired ballistic missiles at the Ain al-Asad base in Iraq. Both sides publicly stood down within a week (Al Jazeera, 8 Jan 2020).
2019
June 2019: the Revolutionary Guard shot down a US Global Hawk drone on 20 June; Trump approved retaliatory strikes and called them off minutes before launch (Al Jazeera, 20 Jun 2019).
1988
April 1988: four days after the USS Samuel B. Roberts struck a mine, Operation Praying Mantis destroyed a large part of Iran's navy in a single day, on 18 April. Iran accepted the UN ceasefire ending the Iran-Iraq war three months later (Naval History and Heritage Command).
4 of 4
In 4 of 4 US-Iran confrontations in the Gulf before 2026, the exchange stopped within days or weeks and no war followed: 1988, 2019, 2020 and 2024. The 2026 war is the exception that has already happened, and it changes what the base rate means. The old pattern says the off-ramp gets taken; the new fact is that it was not taken in February, and a memorandum meant to restore it has lapsed.

Four confrontations before 2026, four stand-downs; the fifth became a war.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case still leans on the old pattern: four stand-downs in four, and a president who said on 2 September that the renewed fighting would not last long. That is what keeps the halt market above 40%. But the memorandum expired on 17 August and neither side has offered a replacement, and Iran has already struck four countries in two days, so the market on another Gulf strike sits higher than the market on a halt.

The counter-case is that 2026 is not a confrontation but a war with a pause inside it. Trump said he was not trying to force Tehran to the table and preferred the current position in the Strait; Tehran has rejected mediation before. The halt prediction fails if either side treats the Strait of Hormuz as non-negotiable, and the talks market fails if Washington keeps to the line that it does not need a bargaining table at all.
