Brussels blinked on high-risk AI. The August deadline still stands.

The EU has formally pushed its toughest AI Act obligations back by up to two years. What survived the retreat matters more: transparency rules still bite on 2 August.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

The Council of the EU gave its final green light to the AI Act simplification package, following the European Parliament's endorsement on 16 June; publication in the Official Journal is expected shortly.

Council of the EU, 29 Jun 2026
2 · The event

What happened

On 29 June the Council of the EU gave final approval to the Digital Omnibus on AI, the first amendment to the AI Act since its adoption in 2024, after the European Parliament's formal endorsement on 16 June (Council of the EU, 29 Jun 2026). The political deal had been struck between the Council, Parliament and Commission on 7 May (Council of the EU, 7 May 2026).

The headline change is timing. Obligations for stand-alone high-risk AI systems under Annex III move from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027, a 16-month deferral; obligations for AI embedded in regulated products under Annex I move a full year to 2 August 2028 (European Parliament, 7 May 2026). The package also adds new prohibited practices, including AI generation of non-consensual intimate imagery, effective December 2026.

Crucially, one date did not move. Article 50 transparency obligations, requiring users to be told when they interact with AI systems and synthetic content to be labelled, still take effect on 2 August 2026 (European Commission, 2026). Whatever the retreat on high-risk systems, the EU's first broad AI enforcement date arrives in five weeks.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

The EU's record on holding its own digital deadlines is genuinely split. Flagship regimes have taken effect on the day promised; the ambitious follow-ons have slipped, stalled or died. The AI Act is now doing both at once.

2024
NIS2's transposition deadline of 17 October 2024 collapsed: the Commission opened infringement proceedings against 23 member states for missing it.
2024
The DMA's gatekeeper compliance deadline of 7 March 2024 held, and the Commission opened non-compliance investigations into Apple, Alphabet and Meta within 3 weeks.
2023
The DSA held its schedule: obligations bit for very large platforms in August 2023 and for all platforms on 17 February 2024, on the dates written in the law.
2018
GDPR took effect on 25 May 2018 exactly as legislated, after a 2-year transition, despite a sustained industry campaign for delay.
2017
The ePrivacy Regulation, proposed to take effect alongside GDPR in 2018, never made it: it stalled for eight years and was formally withdrawn in 2025.
3 of 5
Of five major EU digital-law deadlines since 2018, three held exactly as legislated and two slipped or died. The pattern is honestly split, and it splits on a line: dates already in force held; dates still ahead of industry readiness moved. Article 50 is about to test which side it sits on.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case is that both live dates hold. Final approval is done in both institutions, so Official Journal publication is administrative sequencing, not politics, and five weeks is ample by normal practice. On Article 50, the legislator has just finished choosing what to defer, and it deliberately left transparency in place; reversing that within weeks would require a new legislative act nobody has proposed. The 88% call reflects a date that is now the path of least resistance.

The counter-case runs through the calendar and the lobby. August publication windows have slipped before when legal-linguistic revision runs long, and a late-July publication leaves no buffer. On substance, the omnibus proved the AI Act's dates are negotiable under pressure, and the same industry coalition that won a 16-month deferral on high-risk systems has every incentive to argue that labelling standards for synthetic content are not ready either. The pattern's lesson cuts both ways: deadlines the EU has already blinked on once are easier to blink on twice.
