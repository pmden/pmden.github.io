Meta counts UK revenue. Ofcom counts the world.

Meta has taken Ofcom to the High Court over how the regulator calculates Online Safety Act fees and fines, challenging a methodology that reaches into the company's global income. A full hearing is set for October; the first invoices land before it.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

Meta is fighting Ofcom in the High Court over how many billions count as billions, seeking judicial review of the Online Safety Act fees and fines regime.

BBC News, 7 May 2026
2 · The event

The first structural challenge to the Act

Meta has filed for judicial review of the way Ofcom calculates fees and penalties under the Online Safety Act, the High Court was told in early May. The company's three complaints: fees should reflect UK revenue from regulated services rather than global income; grouping multiple services under one corporate umbrella wrongly exposes the wider organisation to joint liability; and Ofcom aggregates revenue across services instead of assessing each individually (BBC News, 7 May 2026).

The ceiling is set by the statute. Ofcom can fine a company up to 10 per cent of qualifying worldwide revenue or £18 million, whichever is higher, and it funds its online-safety regime through fees levied on the largest platforms on the same worldwide basis. Ofcom's counsel told the court the first round of invoices is expected in the third quarter, most likely September, with the substantive hearing listed for 13 and 14 October. Epic Games and the CCIA, a US trade body, are expected to join the case as third parties (BBC News, 7 May 2026).

The challenge arrives with Ofcom's enforcement machine running at full speed.

3 · The pattern

How tech challenges to UK regulators have fared

Judicial review is a narrow weapon: courts test the lawfulness of the regulator's process, not the wisdom of its policy. Recent attempts by technology companies to overturn UK regulators show how rarely the challenger wins outright.

2025
The Wikimedia Foundation challenged the Online Safety Act's categorisation regulations; the High Court dismissed the challenge in August 2025, the first major judicial test of the Act.
2023
Apple beat the CMA at the Competition Appeal Tribunal over its mobile-browser market investigation, but the Court of Appeal reinstated the CMA's probe in November 2023. The regulator ultimately kept its powers.
2022
Meta challenged the CMA's order to sell Giphy at the Competition Appeal Tribunal; the tribunal upheld the regulator on five of six grounds in June 2022, and Meta sold Giphy the following year (GOV.UK, 2023).
2020
Uber won back its London licence at Westminster Magistrates' Court in September 2020, ten months after TfL refused renewal, a rare outright win for the platform, though on an 18-month probationary licence.
1 of 4
1 of 4 prominent legal challenges by tech companies against UK regulators since 2020 ended in an outright company win. The sample is small, and the one case the company did win was against a licensing decision rather than a regulator's substantive finding, but the procedural deck favours the regulator.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case favours Ofcom's momentum continuing through the summer. The regulator issued 16 fines in its first year of enforcement and has publicly committed to escalating action on age assurance and illegal-content duties, so another six-figure penalty inside five weeks is more likely than not. On the joinder question, Epic and the CCIA were named in open court as expected third parties, and interveners in a case with an October hearing date must be joined well in advance, which compresses the timeline in favour of formal joinder by early August.

The honest counter is that judicial review changes the regulator's behaviour even before any judgment. Ofcom knows every enforcement decision it issues between now and October will be read by the judges hearing Meta's claim, which argues for caution in exactly the window this market covers: fewer aggressive penalties, more process. And on the wider fight, Meta's complaint targets the one part of the regime, revenue methodology, where the Act's drafting is genuinely contested; a company does not need to win a judicial review outright to force Ofcom into a narrower fee basis.
