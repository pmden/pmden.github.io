Canada politics02 Oct 2026

Quebec votes on 5 October. The party that wants independence leads the polls.

The Parti Québécois, which wants Quebec to leave Canada, is first in the last polls before Monday's vote, while the votes against it are split.

5 min read

What happened

Leger’s last poll before Quebec’s 5 October election, taken on 30 September and 1 October, puts the Parti Québécois at 29%, the Liberals at 25% and the Conservatives at 20%.

Quebec elects all 127 members of its National Assembly on Monday 5 October. The Parti Québécois (PQ), which wants Quebec to become an independent country, has led the polls through the campaign. In Leger’s last poll, taken on 30 September and 1 October among 1,000 voters, the PQ has 29%, the Liberals 25% and the Conservatives 20%.

The party now in power, the Coalition Avenir Québec (CAQ), has fallen behind. A Leger poll of 25 to 27 September put it fourth with 16%, behind the Conservatives at 18%. One seat projection site said the CAQ could win no seats at all. Liberal leader Charles Milliard said the CAQ had “disqualified itself”, and Conservative leader Éric Duhaime said his party was “the only ones who can stop the Parti Québécois”.

Most voters want a new government, but not independence. In the last Leger poll 77% want a change of government. Other polls put opposition to independence at 69%. PQ leader Paul St-Pierre Plamondon has said he would not hold a referendum on leaving Canada while Donald Trump is in the White House, and talks of 2029.

Votes are counted on the night. Élections Québec says the deadline to ask for a judicial recount is 13 October in most areas, and most winners are formally declared on 14 October.

Sources: Leger (2), Global News, Policy Magazine, Élections Québec

The Pattern

We looked for cases where, in a Canadian federal or provincial election, one party was first in the last polls with less than half the vote, while at least two rivals each had 15% or more, and the party in power was not first. We found eight, from 2006 to 2018. In all eight the leading party formed the government.

8 of 8
In 8 of 8 Canadian elections where a party led the last polls with under half the vote and its rivals were split, that party formed the government.
2018 Quebec 2018, CAQheld
October 2018: the CAQ led the last polls with about 32%, the governing Liberals 29.5%, the PQ and Québec solidaire 19% and 16.5%. It took 37.4% of the vote and 74 of 125 seats, a majority.
2018 Ontario 2018, Conservativesheld
June 2018: Ipsos had the Progressive Conservatives at 39%, the NDP 36% and the governing Liberals 19%. The Conservatives took about 40% of the vote and 76 of 124 seats, a majority.

2015 Canada 2015, Liberalsheld
October 2015: Nanos had the Liberals at 39.1%, the governing Conservatives 30.5% and the NDP 19.7%. The Liberals took 39.5% of the vote and 184 of 338 seats, a majority.
2015 Alberta 2015, NDPheld
May 2015: Angus Reid’s final poll had the NDP at 41%, Wildrose 28% and the governing Progressive Conservatives 22%. The NDP took 40.6% of the vote and 54 of 87 seats, a majority.
2013 Nova Scotia 2013, Liberalsheld
October 2013: Forum had the Liberals at 48%, the governing NDP 26% and the Progressive Conservatives 23%. The Liberals took about 45.5% of the vote and 33 of 51 seats, a majority.
2012 Quebec 2012, PQheld
September 2012: the last poll had the PQ at 33%, the CAQ 28% and the governing Liberals 26%. The PQ took 32% of the vote and 54 of 125 seats. It formed a minority government.
2009 Nova Scotia 2009, NDPheld
June 2009: CRA had the NDP at 44%, the Liberals 28% and the governing Progressive Conservatives 26%. The NDP took 45.3% of the vote and 31 of 52 seats, a majority.
2006 Canada 2006, Conservativesheld
January 2006: Ipsos had the Conservatives at 38%, the governing Liberals 27%, the NDP 19% and the Bloc 12%. The Conservatives took 36.3% and 124 of 308 seats, and formed a minority government.

The Read

History points to a PQ government. In all eight elections the party leading the last polls, with its rivals split, formed the government. In six it won a majority of seats. Its vote share always stayed within 2.5 points of its last poll, and the split turned votes into a larger share of seats.

The record is thin and the PQ is weaker than any of the eight. Its 29% to 30% is below every leader in the cases, where the lowest was 32%. The cases only run from 2006 to 2018, and only two are from Quebec. One of those two, the PQ in 2012, was the leader on 33% and ended with a minority, 54 of 125 seats.

The majority is the open question. The two minorities came with 33% and 38% in the last poll, yet the CAQ won a majority in 2018 from 32%. This time the vote is split five ways, with the Conservatives rising and the CAQ falling, and seat projections put the PQ near a majority.
