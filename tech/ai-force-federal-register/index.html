Donald Trump announced an AI Force with no order signed. 3 of 7 later got one.

Donald Trump said on Saturday that he is forming an AI Force and will name an AI czar. No order, memo or appointment had appeared by Sunday.

5 min read

What happened

Donald Trump said in a social media post on Saturday 19 September that he is forming an AI Force, comparing it to the Space Force he set up in his first term, and that he will name an AI czar in the near future. He gave no powers, no budget and no date.

(The Guardian 19 Sep)

Donald Trump announced the AI Force in a Truth Social post on Saturday 19 September 2026. There was no press conference, no signing table and no document. He wrote that he was forming the body much as he had formed the Space Force in his first term, and that an AI czar would be named in the near future. (The Guardian, 19 Sep 2026)

No powers were named. The post did not say whether the AI Force would be a branch of the military, an office inside the White House or a committee, and it set no budget, no staff and no date. Reuters reported the announcement under the plain description that he had given no details. (Reuters, 19 Sep 2026) The Financial Times reported it the same evening as a response to the alarm spreading through the industry. (Financial Times, 19 Sep 2026)

The post also repeated his position that the danger of artificial intelligence is a hoax and that his administration will not slow the industry down. He said bad actors can be handled by the existing criminal and civil courts. (BBC News, 19 Sep 2026) The job of AI czar is not new. David Sacks held it, resigned earlier this year and now advises Donald Trump from outside the building while chairing the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology. (Al Jazeera, 19 Sep 2026)

As of Sunday 20 September nothing had been signed. The White House list of presidential actions stops at 18 September, and the Federal Register has published no presidential document naming an AI Force. (White House presidential actions, read 20 Sep 2026) Two AI orders already exist from this administration: one on a national policy framework signed on 11 December 2025, and one on innovation and security signed on 2 June 2026. (Federal Register, Executive Order 14409)

The Pattern

New federal bodies, working groups and posts that the US president announced in public in his own words since January 2025 without signing a document that day, and whether an executive order, a memorandum or an official appointment then followed. Days are counted from the spoken or posted announcement to the document. Announcements made at a signing, where the paper was already on the desk, are not in this class. One man decides in every case, so these are seven separate decisions by the same person rather than seven independent draws, and that is a real limit on the base. Each case comes from its own announcement and its own document, so nothing here is a package counted twice. Newest first.

3 of 7
Three of these seven announcements produced an order or an appointment within 45 days and four within 105. The Gold Card visa took 206 days. The shipbuilding office and the External Revenue Service never arrived.

Three of the seven bodies or posts announced this way drew an order or an official appointment within 45 days, and those three were quick: 4, 11 and 20 days. Then the record breaks. The next one took 58 days, the one after 206, and two have produced nothing at all.

The Read

The announcement carried no powers, no money and no date, which is the normal starting state for this class rather than a sign that nothing will follow. Since January 2025 Donald Trump has announced seven federal bodies or posts in his own words with nothing signed on the day. Three of the seven had an order or an appointment inside 45 days, and those three were quick: 4, 11 and 20 days.

The record then thins out sharply. The Golden Dome manager took 58 days because the Senate had to confirm him. The Gold Card visa took 206 because it needed rules and a price. Two others never arrived at all: the shipbuilding office he promised Congress in March 2025 exists only as staff moved between White House offices, and the External Revenue Service would need an act of Congress. Things the president can do with a pen happen in weeks; things that need law, money or other people take months or never come.

The AI Force sits on both sides of that line at once. Naming an AI czar is a pen stroke, and the job has been vacant since David Sacks resigned, which is why the first market sits above its base. Building a service modelled on the Space Force is the other kind of task: the Space Force was announced in March 2018 and only written into law in December 2019. Watch which half of the promise moves first, because a named czar with no force behind him resolves these markets YES while changing almost nothing.
