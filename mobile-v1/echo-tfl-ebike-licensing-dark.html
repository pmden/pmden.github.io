Fifty thousand e-bikes. One licence to write.

The English Devolution and Community Empowerment Act has handed TfL sweeping powers to license London's dockless e-bike operators. Now Labour Assembly Members want the rules written — fast.

4 min readEcho #116LONDON
1 · Today

Labour Assembly Members urged TfL to use its new licensing powers to curb nuisance e-bike parking. TfL said it is developing a London-wide scheme and will set out details "in due course".

Evening Standard, 8 Jun 2026
2 · The event

What happened

Labour members of the London Assembly publicly called on Transport for London to deploy its new legal powers against "nuisance" e-bike parking, arguing that pavements blocked by hire bikes have become a daily accessibility problem. Around 10% of London's 1.5 million daily cycling trips are now made on dockless e-bikes, with roughly 50,000 available for hire at any one time (Evening Standard, 9 June 2026).

The powers are new. The English Devolution and Community Empowerment Act, which became law on 15 May, lets TfL license dockless operators such as Lime, Forest and Voi under a single city-wide framework — including the ability to cap fleet sizes, dictate where bikes can be parked, and fine operators who fail parking or safety standards (Evening Standard, 15 May 2026).

TfL welcomed the Act, saying it would replace the existing borough-by-borough patchwork with a consistent London-wide scheme, and that it is working with boroughs on proposals it will set out "in due course" (Evening Standard, June 2026). No timetable has been published. The open question is speed: whether TfL puts proposals or a consultation in front of Londoners this summer, or lets the scheme slip into the autumn and beyond.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

London has been here before: a new micromobility power or product arrives, and the machinery of scheme design grinds through consultation, borough negotiation and procurement. The record on speed is not encouraging.

2023
Paris banned rental e-scooters after an April 2023 referendum — 89% voted for a ban and the wheels were off the streets by 1 September. Five months from vote to enforcement, but a ban is far simpler to execute than a licensing regime.
2021
London's e-scooter rental trial launched in June 2021 — 11 months after the government legalised rentals in July 2020, and even then only in a minority of participating boroughs.
2019
Dockless pioneer ofo quit London entirely in January 2019, after struggling under a borough-by-borough patchwork of separate agreements — the exact regulatory gap the new Act is meant to close.
2010
The cycle hire scheme took roughly two years from mayoral commitment to its July 2010 launch — and that was TfL building its own docked network, not writing rules for private fleets.
0 of 3
None of the three previous London micromobility schemes moved from legal power to a live framework in under 11 months — and none published detailed proposals within three months of gaining the power. Paris's five-month e-scooter ban is the speed outlier, and it was a ban, not a licence.

No London micromobility scheme has gone from legal power to published rules in under 11 months.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case sits with the base rate: nothing in TfL's history suggests a licensing framework lands in three months. The Act is barely eight weeks old, the scheme requires negotiation with 32 boroughs plus the City, and TfL's own language — "in due course" — is the vocabulary of an organisation that has not fixed a date. The e-scooter trial, a far simpler undertaking, took 11 months from legal power to launch. A 27% price on a summer publication may still be generous.

The honest counter-case is political heat. Assembly Members are applying public pressure in local papers across the capital, pavement clutter is a complaint that cuts across party lines, and TfL has been preparing for these powers since the Bill was drafted — the thinking may be further along than the public record shows. A short scoping consultation is cheap to publish and would let TfL show motion without binding it to detail. If TfL wants the pressure gone before the autumn, a summer document is the easiest way to buy quiet.
