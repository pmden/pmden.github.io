The carve-outs keep coming. October still holds.

Downing Street has barred another set of offences from the early release changes due on 1 October, four weeks after the first exclusions were announced.

4 min read

Two rounds of exclusions in four weeks

The Prime Minister blocks further offence categories from the sentencing changes, including manslaughter, death by dangerous driving and historic child sex offences.

(GOV.UK, 30 Aug 2026)

The Ministry of Justice announced on 30 August 2026 that a further set of offences would be barred from the release changes taking effect this autumn, covering all forms of manslaughter, death by dangerous or drunk driving, historic child sex offences, the unlawful killing of emergency workers, and causing or allowing the death of a child (GOV.UK, 30 Aug 2026). It is the second such announcement of the month. On 4 August the government excluded rape, serious child sexual offences and grooming offences, and simultaneously moved the commencement date from September to 1 October 2026 (GOV.UK, 4 Aug 2026).

The underlying measure is the progression model created by the Sentencing Act 2026. From 1 October most adults on standard determinate sentences reach their automatic release point earlier: a first group after a third of the sentence rather than 40 or 50 per cent, a second group after half rather than two thirds (House of Commons Library, 2026). The August exclusions do not change the release fractions. They change who is inside the gate, and the government puts the number of offenders now placed outside it at more than 18,000.

The driver is capacity rather than penal philosophy. The adult male estate is running at 98 per cent occupancy, with roughly 3,200 places added since 2024 and 14,000 more promised by 2031, a build programme that lands long after the pressure does (GOV.UK, 30 Aug 2026). That is the structural point: the exclusion list is the only variable ministers can move quickly, because moving the start date costs prison places they do not have. The political pressure has been running since early August, when the Prime Minister said he was increasingly confident that the killers of PC Andrew Harper would not be released under the changes (PoliticsHome, 11 Aug 2026).

The Pattern

Inclusion rule, fixed before the event resolves: this pattern counts every measure in England and Wales since 2007 that brought forward the automatic release point of serving prisoners, was introduced primarily to relieve prison capacity, carried a published list of excluded offences, and had a commencement date announced by ministers in advance. For each, the test is whether it took effect on the date first announced. Temporary release schemes that leave the automatic release point untouched are out, and so are changes to discretionary licence windows.

2007
The End of Custody Licence was announced to Parliament on 19 June and began operating on 29 June, releasing eligible prisoners up to 18 days early with excluded offence categories from the outset.
2023
The End of Custody Supervised Licence was announced on 16 October and was operating from 17 October, initially at 18 days, later extended in stages during 2024.
2024
SDS40 was announced on 12 July for a September start; the enabling order passed on 29 July and the scheme took effect on 10 September for shorter sentences and 22 October for longer ones, both as scheduled.
2025
The Fixed Term Recall order was made on 8 July 2025 with a commencement date of 2 September, and took effect that day. It mandated 28-day automatic release after recall for sentences under four years, with a published schedule of excluded offences (legislation.gov.uk, July 2025).
4 of 4
4 of 4 since 2007. Four cases still leaves a wide interval, and the fourth sits on a different limb of the sentence: it moved the automatic release point after recall rather than the first release date. The current measure is not in the base, and it has already broken the pattern once by slipping from September to 1 October.

4 of the 4 qualifying capacity-driven release measures since 2007 took effect on the date ministers first announced.

What this pattern excludes

End of Custody Temporary Release, 2020
Released prisoners temporarily on licence with a return to custody built in, so it did not bring forward the automatic release point the rule is built around.
Home Detention Curfew maximum period extended, 2024
Widened eligibility for a discretionary licence already on the statute book rather than moving the automatic release point, so it falls outside the rule.
Scottish emergency release tranches, 2024 and 2025
Authorised as one-off tranches by Scottish Ministers under separate statute, with no continuing change to the automatic release point, so the decision mechanism is not the same.

The Read

The weighted case is that 1 October holds. The record since 2007 is that capacity-driven release measures start when they are announced, because the alternative is a prison estate with no places, and the alternative has no political constituency either. Narrowing the exclusion list is cheap and reversible; moving the start date is neither. The August pattern reinforces this reading: on both occasions ministers gave ground on who is released rather than on when releases begin.

The counter-case is that this scheme has already slipped once, from September to 1 October, which no measure in the base did. Two rounds of exclusions in four weeks suggests the offence list is being managed against news pressure rather than settled policy, and each carve-out reduces the number of places the model frees, weakening the argument that the date is unmovable. A third round before commencement would be the signal that the government is still short of a defensible line, and the case for the date holding would weaken with it.
