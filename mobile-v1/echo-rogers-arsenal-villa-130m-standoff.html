Arsenal have Rogers' yes. Villa's answer starts at £130m.

Morgan Rogers has agreed his side of a move to Arsenal, with personal terms and contract details advanced over the past month. Aston Villa, holding a contract that runs to 2031, value him at £130m — a fee that would make him the most expensive signing in Premier League history.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

Arsenal have made Morgan Rogers their top forward target and an approach is expected — but Aston Villa's British-record £130m valuation still stands between them.

Sky Sports, 19 Jul 2026
2 · The event

What happened

Arsenal have made Morgan Rogers the top name on their summer shortlist, and the player's side of the deal is largely done. Player agreement is the easier half of any transfer; the harder half has not started in earnest.

Aston Villa hold almost all of the remaining leverage. Rogers signed a new contract to 2031 in November 2025, and the club has set its valuation at £130m — a figure Villa can justify by pointing to Elliot Anderson's £116m move from Nottingham Forest to Manchester City earlier this window (Sky Sports, 19 Jul 2026; Sky Sports, 6 Jul 2026). Villa are already banking £35m from Youri Tielemans' release-clause move to Manchester United, which eases any pressure to cash in a second star (Sky Sports, 15 Jul 2026). A club that does not need to sell can simply wait.

The next move is Arsenal's, and it is expected within days. At the full asking price Rogers would surpass Alexander Isak's £125m as the most expensive signing in Premier League history (Sky Sports, 2 Sep 2025). With the World Cup running to 19 July, the negotiating calendar is compressed: serious club-to-club talks and a medical can realistically only begin once Rogers is released from England duty.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

Rogers would be the seventh £100m-plus deal involving a Premier League buying club. The previous six show how rarely these sagas close in July — and how often they drift deep into August, or collapse entirely.

2021
Manchester City triggered Jack Grealish's £100m release clause at Aston Villa — the first English £100m deal — but it was only announced on 5 August, after a pursuit that ran all July.
2021
City's parallel pursuit of Harry Kane, valued above £100m, ran the whole summer. Tottenham never accepted a bid and Kane announced he was staying on 25 August. The saga produced no deal at all.
2023
Arsenal's move for Declan Rice was the exception: West Ham accepted a £105m package on 6 July and Arsenal announced him on 15 July — done before the pre-season tours left.
2023
Chelsea's £115m deal for Moises Caicedo, live from early July, was only announced on 14 August after Liverpool hijacked and lost a month-long three-way standoff with Brighton.
2025
Liverpool wrapped Florian Wirtz's £116m move from Bayer Leverkusen by 20 June — proof that record deals can close early when the selling club engages.
2025
Liverpool's pursuit of Alexander Isak began with a rejected £110m bid in early August; Newcastle held out and the £125m British record was signed only on deadline day, 1 September.
2 of 6
2 of the last 6 £100m-plus transfer sagas involving Premier League buyers were completed before 1 August.

Two of the six closed before August; three dragged into August or September; one never happened. When the selling club holds a long contract and says the price out loud — Villa's exact position — the deal has tended to land late or not at all.

5 · The read

The Hunch read

The case for completion is real: the player's side is done, Arsenal have institutional memory of closing a nine-figure deal in mid-July (Rice, 2023), and Villa are already selling this summer — Tielemans' £35m exit shows the squad is not sealed. If Arsenal's opening bid lands near £100m and Villa's true reserve is below their public £130m, this can move fast once the World Cup releases the player.

The honest counter is stronger on the calendar. Villa hold a contract to 2031, have publicly named their price, and face no financial pressure to blink. The pattern says £100m sagas against an unwilling seller resolve in August or September, not July — Grealish, Caicedo and Isak all slipped past 1 August, and Kane never moved at all. A 30-ish per cent chance of an announcement inside 18 days respects both the momentum and the history.
