Trump tariffs14 Sep 2026

Donald Trump vowed no tariff on Irish whiskey. 4 of 8 vows were signed in 30 days.

Donald Trump told a golf crowd in Ireland on 13 September that the United States will stop taxing Irish whiskey. Nothing has been signed yet.

4 min read

What happened

Donald Trump announced at the Irish Open trophy ceremony at his Doonbeg resort on 13 September, closing a two-day visit to Ireland, that he will remove all US tariffs on Irish whiskey, with no instrument signed and no date given.

(BBC News NI 13 Sep)

Donald Trump closed his two-day visit to Ireland on 13 September with a promise from the stage at Doonbeg. Before handing the Irish Open trophy to Shane Lowry, he told the crowd that Taoiseach Micheál Martin and Lowry had both raised whiskey with him and that the United States would take the tariff off Irish whiskey. No document was signed and no date was given. Whiskey distilled in the Republic pays the US rate on European Union goods, which BBC News NI puts at 15 per cent and PA and Al Jazeera put at 10 per cent after a change in July. Whiskey made in Northern Ireland or elsewhere in the UK pays nothing. (BBC News NI 13 Sep 2026; Al Jazeera 13 Sep 2026; Independent 14 Sep 2026)

Martin said on 14 September that he had planted a seed. He had told Trump on the way into their Dublin meeting that the Scotch decision left the two halves of the island on different rates, and Trump raised it again at lunch the next day. The Irish Whiskey Association welcomed the announcement and said it would keep working with the EU and the US for a return to zero tariffs on all drinks. The visit itself drew a march of more than 10,000 people in Dublin. (Independent 14 Sep 2026; BBC News NI 13 Sep 2026)

The UK has seen this film. On 30 April Trump posted that he would remove whisky tariffs in honour of the King and Queen, and the UK government said that day it covered Irish whiskey too. The next day the US Trade Representative narrowed it to whiskey produced in the UK, and the zero rate took effect on 24 July, 85 days after the post. That gave Bushmills, the largest Northern Ireland distillery with an estimated 5 to 10 per cent of Irish whiskey sales in the US, an edge over Jameson and other Republic brands. Newer Northern Ireland distilleries bottling spirit from the Great Northern Distillery in Dundalk were classed as EU producers and got nothing. A zero rate for the Republic would end the Bushmills edge and the Scotch head start against Irish whiskey on US shelves, and would bring the Dundalk-supplied distilleries in. (BBC 30 Apr 2026; BBC News NI 1 May 2026; GOV.UK 24 Jul 2026)

The Pattern

Every occasion in Donald Trump's second term when he promised tariff relief for a named product or partner out loud, on Truth Social, at a podium or to reporters, with no signed instrument on the day. Nine cases since March 2025; the last eight shown. Held means an executive order, proclamation or Federal Register notice giving that relief legal effect was signed or published within 30 days of the promise. Newest first.

Four of Trump's last eight spoken tariff promises were signed into an order within 30 days: coffee and beef in 3, China's fentanyl cut in 5, gold in 25, Vietnam in 29. Scotch, EU cars, Japan cars, UK deal: 85, 60, 44, 39 days.

Four of the last eight verbal tariff promises were signed into an order within 30 days; the four that were not took 39, 44, 60 and 85 days. The ninth case, the one-month reprieve for carmakers announced on 5 March 2025 and signed the next day, makes it five of nine.

The Read

Trump's need on 13 September was a cheap gift at the end of a visit that had gone sour over his united Ireland remarks. The whiskey line cost him nothing on the day and answered, in his words, everyone who had been bugging him. Martin needed a trophy from a visit that 10,000 people marched against and had prepared the ground with a distillery visit in Lowry's home county. The players with a move before 14 October are USTR and Commerce, who draft or stall the instrument as they did with Scotch, the Commission, which can fold the cut into a spirits deal it would claim, and the UK government, whose Scotch precedent is the template Dublin cited. (Independent 14 Sep 2026; BBC News NI 13 Sep 2026; BBC News NI 1 May 2026)

The weighted case is that paper follows, but not inside a month. Four of the last eight verbal promises were signed within 30 days, and all four were single-product or single-partner cuts made in Washington. The four that ran long were deals announced abroad or as gifts to a visitor, and the Scotch vow, the closest match in product, actor and setting, took 85 days and came back narrower than the UK government first said. Irish whiskey also sits inside the EU rate rather than a UK deal, so the instrument has to carve one member state's product out of a bloc-wide tariff. The first market opens at 30 and the result market at 35. (BBC 30 Apr 2026; BBC News NI 1 May 2026; GOV.UK 24 Jul 2026)

The honest counter-case is that gold shows how fast a single-line exemption can move when the White House wants it: 25 days from post to signed annex. A one-line order adding Irish whiskey to the exempt list is the same mechanic, and Trump has a midterm campaign and an Irish-American audience to please before 3 November. The prediction fails on the upside if such an order appears before 14 October, and on the downside if USTR treats the promise as an EU matter and parks it in the spirits talks, in which case the Bushmills edge and the Scotch head start survive into 2027. (Guardian 12 Aug 2025; Independent 14 Sep 2026)
