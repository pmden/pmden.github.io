UK interest rates04 Oct 2026

Markets expect a Bank of England rate rise on 5 November. They were usually right.

Investors put the chance of a rise from 3.75% to 4% at 92%. In September three of the Bank's nine rate setters voted for it.

5 min read

What happened

On 1 October 2026 investors put a 92% chance on the Bank of England raising interest rates from 3.75% to 4% at its next meeting on 5 November.

Investors put the chance of a rate rise on 5 November at 92%. The rise would take the Bank of England’s main interest rate, Bank Rate, from 3.75% to 4%. Markets also expect at least three more rises in 2027, to 4.75% by the end of next year.

At the last meeting, on 17 September, the Bank’s Monetary Policy Committee voted 6 to 3 to keep the rate at 3.75%. Megan Greene, Catherine Mann and Huw Pill voted for a rise to 4%. Pill wrote that a rise now sends a clear signal of the MPC’s commitment to keeping prices stable.

Inflation was 3.1% in August, against a target of 2%. The Bank expects it to reach slightly above 4% early in 2027, mostly because of higher oil and gas prices since the conflict in the Middle East. Mann wrote that the markets’ earlier view of a long pause was hard to square with achieving the 2% target.

Borrowing has already become more expensive. The average five-year fixed mortgage rate reached 5.95% on 1 October, the highest in three years, and lenders withdrew more than 1,000 deals below 5% in a month. The same day the interest rate on 30-year UK government debt hit 6% for the first time since 1998.

The Chancellor, John Healey, presents his first Budget on 28 October, a week before the vote. Higher borrowing costs since the spring are expected to have used up at least half of the £24bn margin the government had kept under its own borrowing rules.

Sources: The Guardian (2), Bank of England, The Independent

The Pattern

We looked for cases where the Bank of England kept its rate the same while some members voted for a rise, inflation was above target, and before the next meeting markets put the chance of a rise at 80% or more. We found six since 1997. In five the Bank raised its rate at the next meeting. All six were decided by the same committee, so this is the record of one institution.

5 of 6
In 5 of 6 cases the Bank raised its rate at the next meeting, each time by a quarter point. In May 2018 it waited after weak growth figures, and raised in August.

5 April 2007: the Bank kept its rate at 5.25% by 7 votes to 2, with inflation at 3.1%. Markets fully expected a rise in May. On 10 May it raised the rate to 5.5%, by 9 votes to 0.
2006 Bank of England, Oct 2006held
5 October 2006: the Bank kept its rate at 4.75% by 7 votes to 2, with inflation at 2.4%. Markets expected a rise, and every economist in a Reuters survey did too. On 9 November it raised the rate to 5%, by 7 votes to 2.

The Read

History points to a rise. In five of the six times the Bank held its rate while some members voted for a rise and markets expected one, it raised at the next meeting, always by a quarter point. This time the split is 6 to 3, as in June 2018 before the August rise, and investors put the chance at 92%. The Bank’s own forecast also shows inflation rising, as it did in only two of the earlier cases, 2006 and 2017, and both ended in a rise.

The one exception is the warning. In spring 2018 markets put a May rise at about 90%, then weak growth figures cut that to about 10% within three weeks, and the Bank waited until August. All six cases are the same committee, and none had a Budget a week before the vote. A calm Budget and steady oil prices would keep the rise on track. A shock in the debt market after 28 October, or a sharp fall in oil, would look more like 2018.

The rate market resolves on the Bank of England's Monetary Policy Summary of 5 November 2026, published at 12 noon London time on bankofengland.co.uk: YES if Bank Rate is set at 4% or higher, and a hold at 3.75% or a cut resolves NO.
