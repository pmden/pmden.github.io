The CMA closes the Apple file. A steering notice comes next.

The competition regulator has published the responses to its consultation on forcing Apple to let UK app developers point customers to payment outside the App Store. The consultation shut on 28 July. The decision now sits with the CMA alone.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

The CMA publishes responses to its consultation on a proposed steering conduct requirement for Apple's mobile platform; the consultation closed on 28 July 2026.

CMA, 14 Aug 2026
2 · The event

One regulator, one notice, no vote

On 30 June 2026 the Competition and Markets Authority opened a consultation on a proposed steering conduct requirement for Apple's mobile platform, drafted under section 19 of the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 (CMA, 30 Jun 2026). The rule would oblige Apple to let developers tell UK users about cheaper terms elsewhere and to carry links or buttons that send those users to an external website to pay. It would also bar Apple from retaliating through app review, search ranking or access to interoperability features. A parallel consultation covers Google's mobile platform (CMA, 30 Jun 2026).

The consultation closed on 28 July and the non-confidential responses were published on 14 August, the last recorded step on the case file (CMA, 14 Aug 2026). Apple has been subject to the regime since 22 October 2025, when the CMA confirmed its strategic market status designation, and in April 2026 the regulator accepted separate commitments from the company on app review, ranking, data use and interoperable access.

The decision structure is unusually narrow. No minister signs it, no committee votes on it and no default applies if the CMA does nothing. The regulator issues a notice or it does not, and Apple's only route out is an appeal to the Competition Appeal Tribunal on judicial review grounds. That makes the question purely one of institutional habit: how this regulator has behaved after closing a consultation of exactly this type.

3 · The pattern

What happens after the CMA consults on a conduct requirement

Inclusion rule, fixed before the outcome: every conduct requirement the CMA has formally consulted on under section 19 of the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, from the start of the regime in October 2025 to 31 August 2026. Cases are counted one per proposed requirement, not one per firm. The outcome scored is simply whether the CMA had imposed that requirement by 31 August 2026. Designations, voluntary commitments and decisions by other authorities are outside the rule and are listed in the exclusions.

What this pattern excludes

Strategic market status designations for Google search and for Apple and Google mobile platforms, October 2025
A designation decision, not a conduct requirement; different section of the Act, different legal test, and no compliance obligation attached at the point of decision.
Apple's commitments on app review, app ranking, data use and interoperable access, accepted 1 April 2026
Voluntary commitments offered by the firm and accepted in place of a rule, so there is no consulted-upon section 19 notice to score.
CMA strategic market status investigation into Microsoft's business software ecosystem, opened 2026
No conduct requirement has been proposed in that case, so it has no outcome that the inclusion rule can measure.
European Commission steering decisions against Apple under the Digital Markets Act
Different decision-maker, different statute and a different appeal route; the mechanism scores too low against the current case to belong in the same base.

5 · The read

The read

The weighted case is a notice this autumn. The mechanism is the same one that produced the Google search requirements: one decision-maker, no veto points, a consultation that has already closed, and a regulator that has published a roadmap it is being judged against. Three of the four requirements it has consulted on became binding within four to five months of the consultation opening, and the steering consultation opened on 30 June. On that clock the equivalent date falls between late October and the end of November, which is where the 66% sits. Apple's April commitments cut the other way only in part: they covered review, ranking and interoperability, and left payments untouched.

The counter-case is the user choice requirement. The CMA consulted on four rules in January and delivered three, without publishing a decision explaining the fourth. Steering is the most contested of the mobile proposals, it goes directly at App Store commission revenue, and the responses published on 14 August include detailed objections. A regulator that has already shown it will drop a consulted requirement can drop this one, split it, or hold it while it works through the separate call for evidence on NFC access. That is the 9% on abandonment and most of the weight in the post-November bands.
