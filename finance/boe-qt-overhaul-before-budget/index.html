Bank of England15 Sep 2026

Economists tell the Bank of England to slow bond sales. 5 of 8 such calls worked.

The Bank of England is selling back the government bonds it bought after 2008. Economists want it to slow down. It decides on Thursday 17 September.

4 min read

What happened

On 15 September economists including former Bank of England deputy governor Charlie Bean urged Chancellor John Healey to press the Bank to slow or halt its sale of government bonds before its decision on Thursday 17 September, as the Telegraph reported that officials had drawn up plans with the Treasury and the Debt Management Office to stop selling 20- and 30-year bonds.

(The Guardian 15 Sep)

The Bank of England is selling back the government bonds it bought after the 2008 crash and during the pandemic, a process it calls quantitative tightening. On 15 September a group of economists, among them former deputy governor Charlie Bean, former OECD chief economist John Llewellyn and fund manager Christopher Mahon, urged Chancellor John Healey to press the Bank to slow or stop those sales. The same morning the Telegraph reported that Bank officials had drawn up a plan with the Treasury and the Debt Management Office to stop selling 20- and 30-year bonds, with an announcement expected on Thursday. Reuters said it could not confirm the report and that the final decision rests with the Bank. (Guardian 15 Sep; Telegraph 15 Sep; Reuters 15 Sep)

The pressure has a price tag. The Bank estimated in August that the programme could cost the Treasury £120bn over its life if interest rates follow the path markets expect, and on Monday 14 September the yield on the 10-year bond passed 5.4%, its highest since July 2007, while the 30-year reached 5.93%, its highest since March 1998. The Bank's holdings have fallen from a peak of £875bn to under £490bn. A year ago the Bank cut its annual sales target from £100bn to £70bn, with long-dated bonds reduced to a fifth of sales, and its schedule for July to September 2026 contained no long-dated auctions at all. The consensus is a further cut to £50bn this week. (Guardian 15 Sep; Bank of England 18 Sep 2025; Bank of England 19 Jun 2026; Reuters 14 Sep)

The decision comes with Thursday's noon rate announcement and six weeks before John Healey's first Budget on 28 October, when he also writes the Bank's remit. Governor Andrew Bailey told MPs earlier this year that limiting the cost to government is not the Monetary Policy Committee's job, and in June said the Bank would not stop bond sales. Louise Haigh, now in the Cabinet Office, has pledged to stop him pursuing policies that damage the government's balance sheet; Healey has so far rejected cabinet calls for a hard line, preferring assurances that the Bank will be mindful of Treasury losses. (Bank of England, September 2026 summary; GOV.UK 31 Jul 2026; The Times 2 Jun 2026; Guardian 15 Sep)

The Pattern

Scheduled decisions on the pace of bond sales or run-off by the Bank of England, the US Federal Reserve and the Bank of Canada since 2019, taken while public calls to slow from markets, government or economists were on the record. Held means the bank cut the annual pace, dropped a maturity bucket or ended active reduction at that decision, within 31 days of the calls. Held at 90 days means the 10-year government bond yield was lower 90 days after the decision than on the day, from the Bank of England database, the US Treasury and the Bank of Canada. Newest first.

Five of the last eight decisions under calls to slow ended in a cut, a dropped bucket or a halt: the Bank of England in 2022 and 2025, the Fed in 2024 and 2025, Canada in 2025. The Bank held in 2023 and 2024, the Fed in 2019.

Five of the eight decisions cut the pace, dropped a maturity bucket or ended active reduction; the Bank of England held in 2023 and 2024 and the Fed held in January 2019. In six of eight the 10-year yield was lower 90 days later.

The Read

Each side has a need and a move. The Bank's line, given by Andrew Bailey to the Treasury committee, is that the cost to government is not the MPC's job; but a £120bn loss estimate of its own and a 10-year yield at a 19-year high six weeks before a Labour chancellor's first Budget put the Bank in the Budget story, and the Telegraph's account of a plan agreed with the Treasury and the DMO is the Bank choosing its own ground before Thursday. John Healey's need is a cheaper Budget without the charge of leaning on the Bank; his move is the remit letter he writes on 28 October, and he has already turned down cabinet calls for a hard line. Louise Haigh's move is the public fight with Bailey she promised. The gilt market moves at noon on Thursday. (Guardian 15 Sep; Telegraph 15 Sep; GOV.UK 31 Jul 2026)

The weighted case is a cut to £50bn with long-dated bonds cut back further, and the first market sits at 70, the second at 55. Five of the eight banks in the record cut at the scheduled decision, the Bank itself cut a year ago four days after the same kind of call, £50bn is the figure the Guardian and Reuters report as expected, and the Bank has already scheduled a quarter without long-dated auctions. The third market sits at 45 against a history of six in eight because the two misses, the UK in 2024 and the Fed in 2025, are the cases where yields were being driven from outside the bank's own sales, and this month's driver is oil above $100 and a US 10-year yield at 5%, with the Budget itself bringing new gilt supply. (Guardian 15 Sep; Reuters 14 Sep; Bank of England 19 Jun 2026)

The honest counter-case is that the Bank has never halted active sales and Bailey said in June it would not; a cut to £55bn or £60bn, or a smaller long share rather than none, resolves the first two markets NO, and Reuters could not confirm the Telegraph's plan. The Bank's own August report puts the combined lifetime effect of the programme, once cheaper past borrowing is counted, anywhere between a £70bn cost and a £65bn gain, which is the case for standing firm. The long market fails if oil and US yields keep rising into October or if the Budget raises the year's gilt sales, as the DMO's revised remit on the same day could. (The Times 2 Jun 2026; Bank of England 4 Aug 2026; Reuters 15 Sep)
