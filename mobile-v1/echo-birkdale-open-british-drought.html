Golf15 Jul 2026

Ten Opens at Birkdale, no British winner. The eleventh starts Thursday.

The 154th Open begins at Royal Birkdale on 16 July with Scottie Scheffler defending and Rory McIlroy chasing. The Southport links has staged ten Opens since 1954 and never crowned a British champion, though a home player has finished in the top five at each of the last three.

1 · Today

The PGA Tour's power rankings for the 154th Open put Scottie Scheffler at the head of the field at Royal Birkdale, with Rory McIlroy his closest challenger and Southport local Tommy Fleetwood leading the home contingent.

PGA Tour, 13 Jul 2026
2 · The event

Birkdale again, nine years on

The 154th Open runs 16-19 July at Royal Birkdale, the course's first Championship since Jordan Spieth's win in 2017 (The Open, 2026). Scheffler arrives as defending champion after winning by four strokes at Royal Portrush last summer (The Open, 15 Jul 2026).

Scheffler and McIlroy sit first and second in pre-tournament projections, with Fleetwood, Matt Fitzpatrick and Jon Rahm in the tier behind (ESPN, Jul 2026). The two headliners spent practice week warning that a sun-baked, running Birkdale will produce 'weird stuff', with players describing the firm conditions as unprecedented for the venue (ESPN, Jul 2026).

3 · The pattern

Overseas champions, British near-misses

Royal Birkdale has hosted ten Opens since 1954. Every winner came from outside Britain, but the last three editions all put a home player inside the top five.

1991
Ian Baker-Finch of Australia won Birkdale's seventh Open, extending a run of champions from Australia and the United States stretching back to Peter Thomson in 1954.
1998
Mark O'Meara, at 41 the oldest first-time Champion Golfer of the modern era, took the Jug while 17-year-old English amateur Justin Rose finished tied fourth.
2008
Padraig Harrington of Ireland defended his title despite a wrist injury, finishing four clear of England's Ian Poulter in second; 53-year-old Greg Norman tied third.
2017
Jordan Spieth closed at 268 (-12), three ahead of Matt Kuchar; Rory McIlroy of Northern Ireland shared fourth at -5.
2025
At Portrush, Scheffler showed favourites can convert at an Open, winning by four strokes as world No.1 and pre-tournament pick.
0 of 10
0 of the 10 Opens played at Royal Birkdale have been won by a British player.

Ten Birkdale Opens, zero British champions. Yet Rose in 1998, Poulter in 2008 and McIlroy in 2017 mean a British top-five finish has arrived at each of the last three Birkdale Championships. The trophy has stayed foreign; the leaderboard has not.

5 · The read

The Hunch read

The case for the favourites: Scheffler is the defending champion with the most complete game in the field, and last year at Portrush he showed the top of the market can convert at an Open, winning by four as world No.1. McIlroy finished fourth here in 2017 and has already won a major this season. On a firm, fast Birkdale that punishes error, class tends to separate.

The counter sits in the venue's history. Ten Opens at Birkdale have produced ten overseas champions and a string of British near-misses: Rose's tied fourth as an amateur, Poulter's second, McIlroy's fourth. That 0-from-10 base rate argues against paying up for a home winner, but the three straight British top-fives say 58 per cent on a top-five finish is grounded in pattern, not patriotism.
