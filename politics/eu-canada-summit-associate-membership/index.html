EU and Canada22 Oct 2026

Germany wants 'associate membership' out of the EU text with Canada. Will it stay?

EU and Canadian leaders meet in Montreal on 29 and 30 October. At least eight EU countries want one phrase out of their joint text.

4 min read

What happened

Germany, France and at least six other EU countries are pushing against ‘associate membership’ as the name for closer EU ties with Canada, six diplomats said.

Germany, France and at least six other EU countries want the words ‘associate membership’ out of a joint text with Canada. Six diplomats told Politico the label is one of the main open points before the EU-Canada summit in Montreal on 29 and 30 October. They object to the name, the diplomats said, and not to closer ties. Germany is pushing hardest.

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen made the offer on 16 September, in her yearly State of the Union speech. Canada cannot become a full member, because EU treaties limit membership to countries in Europe. The capitals that object say the EU gave the relationship a name before agreeing what it means, and some are annoyed they were not asked first.

I would like to work with you on opening the door for Canada to be the first associate member of the EU.

Ursula von der Leyen to Mark Carney, 16 September 2026

Canada’s Prime Minister Mark Carney said in the European Parliament the next day: ‘We welcome this ambition.’ He spoke of ‘an alliance for the future’ that both sides would define together. US President Donald Trump threatened ‘serious tariffs’ if Canada moved closer to the EU.

EU ambassadors were due to discuss the draft again on Monday 26 October. One EU official said several parts of the text still had open comments, and Austria also objects to a line on nuclear energy.

Sources: POLITICO (2), Al Jazeera

The Pattern

We looked for cases where the EU met a partner at a summit after a name for the partner’s special status had been proposed in public, and both sides then signed a joint text. We found seven such summits from 2004 to 2023. In six of them the European Commission proposed the name. Each time we checked whether the name appeared in the joint text word for word.

5 of 7
In five of seven EU summits since 2004 where a name for a partner's status was proposed first, the joint text used it word for word. Twice it was changed: the Mediterranean plan in 2008 and Tunisia in 2023.
2023 EU and Tunisianot held
In June 2023 EU leaders and the Commission offered Tunisia a ‘comprehensive partnership package’. The paper signed on 16 July said ‘strategic and global partnership’. EU states later said the Commission acted alone.
2010 EU and Mexicoheld
The Commission proposed a ‘Strategic Partnership’ with Mexico in July 2008. At the summit in Comillas on 16 May 2010 both sides adopted a ‘Joint Executive Plan of the Strategic Partnership’.
Show the other 5 cases
2009 EU and six eastern neighboursheld
The Commission proposed an ‘Eastern Partnership’ in December 2008. The Prague summit of 7 May 2009 said: ‘The Eastern Partnership is launched.’ A word in the draft, ‘Europeans’, became ‘Eastern European Partners’.
2008 EU and South Africaheld
The Commission proposed a ‘Strategic Partnership’ with South Africa in June 2006. It was set up in May 2007, and the first summit in Bordeaux on 25 July 2008 named the ‘SA-EU Strategic Partnership’.
2008 EU and the Mediterraneannot held
France’s Nicolas Sarkozy wanted a ‘Mediterranean union’ in May 2007. Angela Merkel objected, and in March 2008 EU leaders cut it back. The Paris summit text said ‘Barcelona Process: Union for the Mediterranean’.
2007 EU and Brazilheld
The Commission proposed a ‘Strategic Partnership’ with Brazil on 30 May 2007, before the first summit. In Lisbon on 4 July 2007 both sides ‘decided to establish a comprehensive strategic partnership’.
2004 EU and Indiaheld
The Commission proposed a ‘strategic partnership’ with India in June 2004. At the summit in The Hague on 8 November 2004 both sides agreed an ‘EU-India Action Plan for a Strategic Partnership’.

The Read

The one time a big EU country objected in public, the name changed. In 2008 Nicolas Sarkozy wanted a ‘Mediterranean union’. Angela Merkel objected, and EU leaders folded it into an older plan, so the summit text said ‘Barcelona Process: Union for the Mediterranean’. This time Germany leads the objection.

The European Commission is already looking for other words. An EU official said what the two sides do together matters more than the name. A diplomat described a review of progress a year after the summit, after which Canada could become the first associate member, so the phrase could also stay in as a goal for later.

There are reasons for doubt. Four of the five names that went in word for word were ‘strategic partnership’, a label the EU gives many countries, and no EU country objected to them in public. None of the seven names used the word ‘membership’ for a country that cannot join. Five of the seven summits came before the Lisbon Treaty took effect in December 2009 and gave the EU its first permanent president of the European Council.

Sources: The Guardian, Council of the EU (2, 3, 4), POLITICO, BBC

How this resolves

The associate member market resolves on the joint declaration or statement of the EU-Canada summit, published on the websites of the Council of the EU and the Canadian prime minister: YES if the text signed by both sides uses the words 'associate member' or 'associate membership' for Canada or for EU-Canada ties, even as a future goal, and NO if it does not. Politico Europe, BBC News and The Guardian reports settle it if the official pages do not open, and if the summit is postponed or no joint text is out by 2 November the market is void.
