Forgotten Island opened weakly, but families gave it an A. Will they keep coming?

Forgotten Island took $13m in US cinemas in its first weekend. It cost $80m to make, and audiences gave it an A grade.

4 min read

What happened

Forgotten Island, an original animated film from Universal and DreamWorks Animation, opened fifth in US cinemas on 25 to 27 September 2026 with about $13m, against an $80m budget, and an A grade from audiences. (Variety)

It took $13,007,005 in 3,548 US cinemas from 25 to 27 September, and $7.8m in 34 other countries. (Box Office Mojo; Variety)

The film cost $80m to make. It is an original story, not a sequel. Audiences leaving the cinema gave it an A in the CinemaScore poll, and critics liked it too. Variety wrote that Universal now needs the film to keep selling tickets for weeks. (Variety)

Universal’s head of US distribution, Jim Orr, said he hoped the reaction of audiences would portend a long domestic run. (Variety)

No new family film opens wide on 2 October. The new wide releases that weekend are Verity, Digger and The Scout, all for older audiences. On 9 October two animated family films arrive in many cinemas: Paramount’s Avatar Aang: The Last Airbender and Treasure Trekkers. (Box Office Mojo release calendar)

The Pattern

We looked for cases where a big studio’s original animated film cost at least $50m, opened in US cinemas to less than a third of its budget and still got an A from audiences, with no holiday in its first two weekends. We found six since 2013. Three of them were Pixar films.

4 of 6
In 4 of 6 films the second weekend fell by less than 40%: Kubo, Elemental, Turbo and Hoppers. Abominable fell 42.2% and Elio 50%. None lost more than half.

The Read

History points to a steady second weekend. Four of the six films lost less than 40% of their takings, and none lost more than half. Forgotten Island also has no new family film against it on 2 October, and neither did any of the six. Over the whole run, five of six went on to take at least three and a half times their first weekend.

The record is small, and three of the six are Pixar films. A good grade did not mean the money came back: only Hoppers earned its budget in US cinemas, and it had the strongest start. The third weekend depends on rivals. Forgotten Island meets two new animated films on 9 October, and the two films that met one in that weekend lost around half their takings.
