Revolut is finally a bank. Now it has to act like one.

After a five-year application and a 20-month mobilisation, the Prudential Regulation Authority lifted Revolut's restrictions. It is now a fully licensed bank.

4 min read

What happened

The PRA approves Revolut Bank UK to launch without restrictions, ending a mobilisation phase that ran nearly twice the standard length.

(Revolut, 11 Mar 2026)

On 11 March 2026 the Prudential Regulation Authority lifted the restrictions on Revolut Bank UK, granting the company full banking status in its home market. The application began in 2021; a restricted licence arrived in July 2024 with deposits capped at £50,000 while the bank completed its build-out. That mobilisation phase, designed to run around twelve months, ran nearly two years Revolut, 11 Mar 2026.

UK chief executive Francesca Carlesi called the approval "a defining moment in our journey". Current accounts open to new customers within days, starting with a small cohort, and existing UK customers migrate to the bank in phased batches over several months. Eligible deposits now carry Financial Services Compensation Scheme protection Revolut, 11 Mar 2026.

The licence unlocks the products Revolut could not offer as an e-money institution: consumer lending, credit cards and, eventually, mortgages. It also sharpens the question hanging over the company since its last funding round, which is where a business of this size lists. Revolut has filed for a US national bank charter and has repeatedly declined to commit to London Revolut, Mar 2026.

The Pattern

A UK banking licence is a beginning, not an arrival. Every app-based bank licensed before Revolut discovered that the distance between authorisation and a profitable, full-service bank is measured in years. The record is real but split: most took six years or more to turn a licence into an annual profit, while one did it in three.

2024
Monzo reports its first annual profit, £15.4m pre-tax, seven years after receiving its full licence in April 2017.
2023
Zopa posts its first full year of profitability just three years after its June 2020 banking licence, the fastest of the cohort.
2022
Starling delivers its first annual profit, £32.1m pre-tax, six years after its July 2016 licence.
2015
Atom Bank becomes the UK's first app-only licensed bank; its first annual operating profit does not arrive until 2024, nine years later.
3 of 4
Three of the four UK app-based banks licensed before Revolut took six or more years from licence to first annual profit. The pattern is split: Zopa managed it in three. Revolut arrives already profitable at group level, which no predecessor could claim.

3 of 4 UK neobanks took six-plus years from licence to first annual profit.

The Read

The weighted case: Revolut is not a startup learning to bank. It enters full UK licensing with more than 60 million global customers and a group that already reports billions in revenue and a substantial pre-tax profit. The predecessors in the pattern spent their post-licence years searching for a business model; Revolut has one and is bolting a UK balance sheet onto it. Expect lending products faster than the historical cadence suggests, because the commercial pressure to monetise the UK base is immediate.

The honest counter-case: the licence saga itself is the warning. A mobilisation phase that ran nearly twice the standard length signals a regulator that took convincing, and full status means full supervisory intensity, on capital, on financial crime controls, on complaint handling, at a scale no UK neobank has faced on day one. Monzo's £21m FCA fine in 2025 showed what happens when growth outruns controls. Migration of millions of accounts is also precisely the kind of operation that generates headlines when it goes wrong.
