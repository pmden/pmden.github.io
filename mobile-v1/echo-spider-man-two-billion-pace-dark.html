Spider-Man hit $2bn in 17 days. The speed has always named the ceiling.

Spider-Man: Brand New Day has crossed $2bn worldwide faster than every film except Avengers: Endgame, and Tom Holland's pay is now part of the story.

4 min read

What happened

Tom Holland is projected to earn at least $100m from Spider-Man: Brand New Day — a $20m fee plus roughly $80m in box office bonuses first reported by The Wrap — after the film passed $2bn worldwide in its third week, the second-fastest crossing after Avengers: Endgame, to rank eighth on the all-time chart.

(The Guardian, 19 Aug 2026)

At least $100m is what Tom Holland now stands to make from Brand New Day: a $20m upfront fee plus roughly $80m in bonuses tied to box office performance, according to figures first reported by The Wrap — ten times the $10m he was paid for No Way Home in 2021 (The Guardian, 19 Aug 2026). The backend structure means his payday and the film's final gross now move together.

The film crossed $2bn worldwide in its third week of release, the eighth film ever to reach the mark and the second-fastest behind 2019's Avengers: Endgame (The Guardian, 19 Aug 2026). Trade reporting puts the crossing at day 17, against Endgame's 11, off a record $360m domestic and $932m global opening weekend and a run to $1bn in six days, with the total at $2.022bn — $785m domestic, $1.23bn overseas — at the milestone (Variety, Aug 2026).

The run lands after superhero films spent 2023 to 2025 in commercial decline, which is why the trade press is treating Brand New Day as a genre revival rather than a routine hit (The Guardian, 19 Aug 2026). It also caps an unusual year for Holland, whose other 2026 release, Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey, has taken $1.3bn (The Guardian, 19 Aug 2026).

The Pattern

Four films crossed $2bn on a first theatrical run between 2015 and 2023. In every case, the pace of arrival at the line predicted what came after it.

2016
53 days: Star Wars: The Force Awakens became only the third film past $2bn — after Avatar and Titanic, which itself only crossed thanks to a 3D re-release — sitting at $2.01bn on crossing day. It barely moved again: seven years later it still ranked just above the line (The Guardian, 8 Feb 2016) (The Guardian, 23 Jan 2023).
2018
4th member Avengers: Infinity War followed the same shape: over the line mid-run, then parked just beyond it, still listed immediately above the club's newest entrant in January 2023 (The Guardian, 23 Jan 2023).
2019
$1.2bn in its opening weekend — the first billion-dollar debut — set up Avengers: Endgame as the fastest film ever to $2bn. It overtook Avatar's $2.79bn lifetime total within three months and finished at $2.8bn (The Guardian, 28 Apr 2019) (The Guardian, 21 Jul 2019).
2023
38 days: Avatar: The Way of Water reached $2.024bn to become the sixth member, with analysts projecting at least $2.5bn; by March it stood at $2.2bn and counting (The Guardian, 23 Jan 2023) (The Guardian, 1 Mar 2023).
2 of 4
Four films crossed $2bn on a first run between 2015 and 2023. The Force Awakens took 53 days and stalled at the line; Infinity War likewise never travelled far past it. Endgame, still the fastest to cross, finished at $2.8bn; The Way of Water crossed on day 38 and climbed past $2.2bn. Brand New Day's 17-day crossing is quicker than all of them except Endgame.

Slow crossers (53 days and mid-run) stalled at the line. Fast crossers (11 and 38 days) added between $200m and $800m. Brand New Day crossed on day 17.

The Read

The 72% on $2.2bn is a pace argument. Brand New Day reached the line roughly $200m short of that target with a crossing speed only Endgame has beaten, and every fast crosser in the dataset kept earning for weeks after the milestone: Endgame added roughly $800m beyond the line, The Way of Water at least $200m within six weeks. Late August is also the year's thinnest release corridor, which supports the weekend market: the film's challengers are holdovers, not new tentpoles. The top-three anchor asks more — roughly $330m beyond the crossing figure — which is why it sits barely above a coin flip.

The honest counter-case is front-loading. A record $360m opening means an unusually large share of demand was spent in week one, and No Way Home showed that even a hysteria-level Spider-Man opening can fade before the line — it stopped short of $2bn entirely. Endgame, the only faster crosser, was the finale of a 22-film saga with a tail no ordinary sequel has matched. The prediction fails if weekend holds steepen sharply from here, if September's new releases take screens faster than the club's history suggests, or if the overseas run — $1.23bn of the total so far — decays quicker than the domestic one; any of those leaves $2.2bn arriving after 11 September and the top three out of reach.
