Wales03 Sep 2026

The last Senedd passed eleven Acts before it died. The new one has passed none.

Rhun ap Iorwerth's minority government has finished its first hundred days, says next year's budget will be lower in real terms, and has not yet introduced a Bill. Wales has been through this exact point in the cycle three times, and the year after an election is where the statute book goes quiet.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

The First Minister says next year's Welsh Government budget will be lower in real terms than this year's, and that financial pressures will not dim the new administration's ambition.

Welsh Government, 1 Sep 2026
2 · The event

A hundred days done, a budget shrinking, a legislative programme unwritten

The First Minister, Rhun ap Iorwerth, marked the hundred-day point by confirming that the Welsh Government's budget for next year will be lower in real terms than this year's, and saying that financial pressures will not dim this new administration's ambition to improve outcomes for the people of Wales. His government reports having completed initial planned activity on all 96 commitments in its First 100 Days Progress Report, and he intends to raise fairer funding from Westminster directly with the Prime Minister at a meeting in October (Welsh Government, 1 Sep 2026).

The arithmetic behind him is new. The election of 7 May 2026 returned 96 Members under the new closed-list system, with Plaid Cymru on 43 seats from 35.4 per cent of the vote, Reform UK on 34 from 29.3 per cent, Welsh Labour on 9, the Welsh Conservatives on 7, the Wales Green Party on 2 and the Welsh Liberal Democrats on 1. Turnout was 51.7 per cent against 46.6 per cent in 2021, the highest since the Senedd was established and the first time it has passed half. Two thirds of the chamber, 66 Members of 96, had never sat before.

A government on 43 of 96 needs six more votes for a majority on anything contested, and the second largest group is the one least likely to lend them. That is the constraint the legislative programme has to be written around, and it is why the interesting question this autumn is not what Wales wants to legislate on but how much of it reaches the statute book.

3 · The pattern

What Welsh legislative output does after an election

Inclusion rule, fixed before the count: this pattern counts Acts of the National Assembly for Wales and Acts of Senedd Cymru by the calendar year in which they appear on the statute book, from 2012 to 2026, as published on legislation.gov.uk. The test is applied to each of the three completed cycles and is stated in advance: was the first full calendar year after an election the lowest-output year of that term. Measures of the National Assembly, the instrument used before 2012, are excluded, because they were made under a different competence and are not comparable to Acts.

2012
First full year of the Fourth Assembly, elected May 2011: 2 Acts. The rest of that term ran 7, 7, 6 and 6. The lowest year of the term, by a distance.
2017
First full year of the Fifth Assembly, elected May 2016: 4 Acts. The term ran 4, 5, 4, 5 and 4, so the first year was joint-lowest rather than lowest. The test fails here, and the term as a whole was unusually flat.
2022
First full year of the Sixth Senedd, elected May 2021: 2 Acts, against 4, 6 and 5 in the years that followed. The lowest year of the term.
2026
The final months of the Sixth Senedd produced 11 Acts, among them Planning (Wales), Building Safety (Wales), Bus Services (Wales), Homelessness and Social Housing Allocation (Wales), British Sign Language (Wales) and Prohibition of Greyhound Racing (Wales). A third of the term's output arrived in its last five months.
2 of 3
2 of 3, and three cycles is the bare floor for a base rate. Two further caveats matter here. Each cycle is one observation of a whole parliament, so the sample can never grow quickly. And every previous cycle was run by a Welsh Labour administration, twice with a working arrangement to lean on; this is the first Senedd in which the largest party is not Labour and the opposition's largest bloc is Reform UK, so the mechanism producing the old numbers is not obviously the mechanism now.

2 of the 3 completed cycles had their lowest legislative output in the first full year after an election. The exception, 2017, was joint-lowest in a term that never rose above five Acts in any year.

What this pattern excludes

Measures of the National Assembly for Wales, 2008 to 2011
They were made under legislative competence orders rather than the general power the Senedd has held since 2011, so counting the seven Measures of 2011 alongside Acts would compare two different instruments and flatter the pre-2012 years.
The election year itself
An election year is split between two terms and its output belongs almost entirely to the outgoing one, as 2026 shows. Scoring it would credit a new government with the legislation of the government it replaced.
Subordinate legislation
Welsh ministers make hundreds of statutory instruments a year regardless of the parliamentary arithmetic, so including them would drown the signal the count is trying to isolate, which is what a government can get through the chamber.

5 · The read

The read

The Royal Assent market is the one the record speaks to most clearly and it sits low. A Bill introduced in the autumn of a new term does not reach Royal Assent in the same calendar year in any of the three previous cycles, because the Senedd's stages take months and the Christmas recess sits in the middle of them. The pattern above is really a statement about that timetable rather than about political will.

The two nearer markets are about a government's choices rather than about procedure, and they pull against each other. A minority administration on 43 of 96 has an incentive to publish its programme early, while it still has the authority of a fresh election and while the opposition is two thirds new and unorganised. It has the opposite incentive on Bills: every Bill introduced is a vote it has to find six extra Members for, and the budget is the one vote it cannot avoid. The precedent that should worry Cardiff is 2022, when a Labour government with a cooperation agreement in place still managed only two Acts in its first full year. This government has no such agreement, and it has already said the money is shrinking.
