Ofcom31 Aug 2026

Ofcom has fined the small sites. TikTok is the test.

The regulator updated two of its open child-safety investigations on 20 August, keeping a caseload that now runs to dozens of services. Every age-assurance penalty it has issued so far has landed on an adult site or a fringe forum.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

Ofcom updates its open Online Safety Act investigations into the providers of Chat-Avenue and Teen-Chat, part of an enforcement caseload covering dozens of sites and apps.

Ofcom, 20 Aug 2026
2 · The event

Six penalties, none against a household name

Ofcom's enforcement pages were updated on 20 August 2026 with the current position on its investigations into the providers of Chat-Avenue and Teen-Chat, two chat services being examined over grooming risk and children's access to pornography (Ofcom, 20 Aug 2026). Both investigations were opened in April 2026 and neither has produced a decision.

The regulator's published record on age assurance now runs to six penalty decisions, beginning with Itai Tech in November 2025 and most recently the operator of fapello.com, fined £630,000 in July 2026 (Ofcom, Jul 2026). Every one of the six services involved is an adult site or a fringe forum, and several responded by geoblocking the UK rather than installing age checks.

The open question is scale. In July 2026 Ofcom opened an investigation into TikTok's age-assurance measures, the first such case against a platform with a mass UK audience. The decision mechanics are the same in both classes of case: Ofcom decides alone under Part 3 of the Online Safety Act 2023, there is no ministerial sign-off and no default outcome, and the only route out for the company is an appeal. What differs is the size of the legal budget on the other side and the ceiling on the fine, which is 10% of qualifying worldwide revenue.

3 · The pattern

What an Ofcom age-check penalty looks like

Inclusion rule, fixed before the outcome: every penalty decision Ofcom has published for breach of the Online Safety Act's age-assurance duties, from 25 July 2025, when those duties took effect, to 31 August 2026. One case per penalised provider. The outcome scored is whether Ofcom attached a separate penalty for failing to answer a statutory information request alongside the age-assurance penalty. Penalties under other duties, under other statutes, and open investigations without a decision are outside the rule and are listed in the exclusions.

2026

2026
Fapello, July 2026. £600,000 covering July to November 2025, plus £30,000 for a late reply to a legally binding information request.
5 of 6
5 of the 6 published age-assurance penalties carried an extra fine for not answering Ofcom's information requests. That is a finding about the kind of operator penalised so far, all of them small, offshore or anonymous, rather than a prediction about a company with a UK legal team on retainer.

In 5 of the 6 published age-assurance penalties, Ofcom added a separate fine for failing to answer its information requests. The sixth, 4chan, was stacked differently, with the add-ons covering risk assessment and terms of service instead.

What this pattern excludes

Fenix International, the owner of OnlyFans, fined more than £1 million
The penalty was for giving Ofcom inaccurate information about its age-assurance settings, an information duty breach, not a finding that the age checks themselves failed.
Ofcom's £28 million penalty on Virgin Media O2, July 2026
Imposed under the Communications Act consumer contract rules over cancellation handling; a different statute, a different duty and no child-safety element.
The ICO's fine on Reddit over age verification and data protection impact assessments, February 2026
A different regulator applying UK data protection law; the decision-maker, the test and the appeal route all differ from an Ofcom Online Safety Act penalty.
Open investigations into TikTok, Chat-Avenue, Teen-Chat, Eporner and Kemono
No penalty decision has been published, so there is no outcome the inclusion rule can score. Counting them either way would prejudge the very question this Echo asks.

5 · The read

The read

The weighted case is more small penalties soon and nothing on TikTok this side of spring. Ofcom has issued six age-assurance decisions in nine months and has a queue of open investigations, several of them against operators that have already refused to engage, which is the fact pattern that produces the fastest decisions. The information-request add-on appears in five of six cases because the same operators that skip age checks also skip the regulator's letters. None of that transfers to a company that answers correspondence, files evidence and can litigate, which is why the TikTok branch sits at 34%.

The counter-case is that Ofcom has an incentive to make an example of a large service, and the political weather supports it. The regime's credibility rests on whether it can reach the platforms children actually use, and the regulator has already shown with the £28 million Virgin Media O2 penalty that it will price a fine against a major UK-facing business at a level that hurts. If a TikTok decision does land, the £5m-plus band stops looking like an outlier, since the statutory ceiling is a share of global revenue rather than a fixed cap. That is the case for the 11%.
