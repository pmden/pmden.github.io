Approvals slipped under 60,000. July rarely rescues June.

Mortgage approvals for house purchase fell to 56,565 in May and recovered only to 58,200 in June, the first back-to-back readings below 60,000 since early 2024. The July figure arrives in the Bank of England's next Money and Credit release.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

Approvals for house purchase stand at 58,200 for June 2026 against 65,206 in April, with May and June the first consecutive months below 60,000 since January and February 2024.

Bank of England, 31 Aug 2026
2 · The event

Two months below the line

Approvals for house purchase, the standard forward indicator for housing transactions, ran at 65,206 in April 2026, dropped to 56,565 in May and edged back to 58,200 in June (Bank of England, series LPMVTVX). The May fall of 8,641 in one month is the largest outside the 2020 suspension of the housing market since November 2022, in the aftermath of the mini-Budget. May and June are the first consecutive sub-60,000 readings since January and February 2024.

The rate backdrop is not helping. The Monetary Policy Committee held Bank Rate at 3.75% in July, with three of nine members voting to raise it by a quarter point (Bank of England, July 2026). Consumer price inflation rose to 2.9% in the year to July, up from 2.6% in June, driven mainly by a 14.7% annual rise in gas prices following the change to Ofgem's cap (ONS, 19 Aug 2026). Quoted mortgage rates have not fallen materially since spring.

The next Money and Credit release covers July and is the first hard reading on whether the spring drop was a pause or a turn. It lands weeks before a Budget in which property taxation has been among the most persistent subjects of speculation, which gives approvals an additional reading: buyers who expect a change to how transactions are taxed have an incentive to wait.

3 · The pattern

What July does to June's approvals

This is a threshold event. Nobody decides the approvals count; a continuous series either travels the 1,800 approvals now standing between June's 58,200 and the 60,000 line or it does not. Counting comparable episodes would be the wrong tool, so the base rate comes from the series. Inclusion rule, fixed before the count: the Bank of England's seasonally adjusted approvals for house purchase in every June and the following July, for the ten consecutive years 2016 to 2025, no gaps, no extension of the window. The test is whether July exceeded June by 1,800 approvals or more.

Two of the last ten Julys cleared June by 1,800 approvals or more, and one of those two was the month the housing market reopened after lockdown. The honest reading of that is that a 60,000 print requires an unusually strong July rather than an ordinary one. Two features argue the other way. June 2026 was itself a recovery month, up 1,635 on May, so the series has already turned once. And the two most recent Julys, 2024 and 2025, both rose, one of them missing the 1,800 mark by 193 approvals.

In 2 of the last 10 years July approvals exceeded June's by 1,800 or more, the distance now separating the series from 60,000.

What this pattern excludes

Every month pair other than June to July
Approvals carry a seasonal shape and the market resolves on a July print. A rolling two-month window across all months would answer a different question with a different distribution.
Years before 2016
The window was fixed at ten consecutive pairs before the count was run. Extending it backwards reaches into the post-crisis recovery, when the level of approvals and the mortgage guarantee schemes supporting them were both materially different.
July 2020, a rise of 26,241
Considered for removal as the reopening of a market that had been closed by law and retained. It is one of only two affirmative observations, so dropping it would cut the fraction to 1 of 9, and no rule for its removal survives except its effect on the answer.
Approvals for remortgaging and for other secured lending
The markets resolve on approvals for house purchase, Bank of England series LPMVTVX. Remortgaging approvals have a different seasonal profile and a different relationship to transactions.

5 · The read

The read

The weighted case is a print in the fifties. Approvals lead completions by roughly three months, so July reflects decisions taken when Bank Rate had been on hold for a quarter, three committee members were voting for a rise and quoted mortgage rates had stopped falling. The record says an ordinary July adds between one and two thousand approvals to June, not the near two thousand needed here, and the eight Julys in the sample that fell short include every year since 2021 except the two most recent. That combination is why the market sits in the high twenties on 60,000 while pricing the simple direction question close to a coin toss.

The counter-case is the Budget. Reports through August have kept a property tax overhaul in circulation, and transactions tend to pull forward when buyers expect a levy to change, as they did before the stamp duty threshold changes of 2021 and 2025. If that pull-forward has begun, it shows up in approvals first and would push July toward the top of the range. The risk runs the other way too: a buyer who expects stamp duty to be cut has every reason to wait, and a July figure in the low fifties would be the first sign that the market has decided to sit out the autumn.
