Berlin02 Jul 2026

Merz got his 34 measures. The AfD sets his deadline.

After seven hours of coalition talks, CDU, CSU and SPD agreed a sweeping package of tax cuts, pension reform and tougher sick-leave rules. With the far right leading national polls ahead of September's eastern state elections, the package now has to become law faster than German coalitions usually manage.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

Merz's coalition unveils 34 reform measures — tax cuts for low and middle earners, a pension overhaul and new sick leave rules — in a bid to revive the economy and counter the AfD surge.

Al Jazeera, 2 Jul 2026
2 · The event

What happened

Chancellor Friedrich Merz's CDU/CSU-SPD coalition agreed a 34-measure reform package on 2 July after seven hours of coalition committee talks, ending months of internal deadlock. The 'Programme for Revival and Employment' delivers roughly €10bn a year in income tax relief aimed at lower and middle incomes from January 2027, keeps the 42% top rate but restructures the upper bands — 45% above €250,000 and 47% above €280,000 (Politico, 2 Jul 2026).

The harder-edged measures target Germany's structural problems: the retirement age, currently 65–67, would rise gradually in line with life expectancy, and the pandemic-era telephone sick note is abolished — employees will need a doctor's certificate from the first day of illness rather than the fourth (Al Jazeera, 2 Jul 2026).

The political context is the deadline. The CDU/CSU trails the far-right AfD in national polling, and critical eastern state elections arrive in September. The package is explicitly framed as the coalition's answer to that surge — which means the market question is not whether the measures were announced, but how quickly any of them reach cabinet-approved draft law.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

German governments announce grand reform programmes with some regularity. What separates the ones that changed the country from the ones that became footnotes is whether draft legislation moved before the political window closed.

2025
Merz loosened the constitutional debt brake and created a €500bn infrastructure fund — pushed through the outgoing Bundestag in March, two weeks before the new parliament sat. Announced and enacted inside a month.
2024
Scholz's three-party coalition unveiled a 49-measure 'growth initiative' in July. The coalition collapsed in November; most measures never became law.
2007
Merkel's grand coalition raised the retirement age to 67. The Bundestag passed it in March 2007, over union protests, and the reform stuck.
2003
Schröder announced Agenda 2010 in March; the Hartz labour reforms passed and reshaped the German economy — at the cost of an SPD revolt and Schröder's chancellorship by 2005.
3 of 4
3 of 4 major announced reform packages since 2003 were substantially enacted. The base rate favours passage — but the failed case is the most recent one, and it failed for the same reason this coalition nearly deadlocked: partners who agree on headlines and disagree on laws.

3 of 4 headline German reform packages since 2003 became law. The one that died — 2024's growth initiative — took the government down with it.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case: this coalition has already shown it can move fast when cornered — the €500bn fund went from announcement to constitutional amendment in under a month in 2025. The tax relief measures are fiscally settled and politically painless, exactly the kind of law a chancellery fast-tracks before an election-season autumn. A 58% call on cabinet-approved drafts by 10 August reflects a government that knows a package with no laws attached reads as another broken promise, with September polls watching.

The honest counter-case: the German legislative summer is real — cabinet business thins sharply from mid-July — and the package's hardest measures, the retirement age and sick-leave rules, are precisely the ones the SPD base will fight through every drafting stage. The 2024 precedent is uncomfortable: 49 measures announced in July by a coalition under far-right pressure, and by November there was no coalition. Announcement euphoria is not a legislative calendar.
