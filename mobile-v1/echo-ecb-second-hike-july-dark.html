ECB11 Jun 2026

One hike is a signal. Two is a cycle.

The European Central Bank raised rates on 11 June for the first time in three years, citing war-driven energy inflation. On 23 July the Governing Council decides whether that was a correction or the start of a tightening cycle.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

The ECB raised all three key rates by 25 basis points, its first hike in three years, as the war in the Middle East pushed eurozone inflation to 3.2%.

Bloomberg, 11 Jun 2026
2 · The event

What happened

The Governing Council raised the deposit facility, main refinancing and marginal lending rates by 25 basis points on 11 June, to 2.25%, 2.40% and 2.65% respectively, effective 17 June. It was the ECB's first increase since the tightening cycle ended in September 2023, and a sharp reversal after eight consecutive cuts delivered between June 2024 and June 2025 (ECB, 11 June 2026).

The trigger was energy. Flash eurozone inflation for May came in at 3.2% year on year, up from 3.0% in April and the highest reading since September 2023, as the war with Iran and disruption in the Strait of Hormuz fed through to fuel and transport costs (Bloomberg, 11 June 2026). New staff projections put headline inflation at 3.0% in 2026, 2.3% in 2027 and 2.0% in 2028.

The next decision lands on 23 July at 13:45 CET. It is a non-projection meeting, so the Council will act on incoming data rather than fresh forecasts. Since the June meeting, a US-Iran de-escalation has pulled Brent crude from above $120 back to around $72 a barrel, easing the energy impulse that justified the hike. Hawks from Germany, the Netherlands and Austria will argue second-round wage effects are already loose; doves will point at the oil chart.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

The ECB has opened four hiking cycles in its history. The question each time was the same: does the second hike come at the very next meeting, or does the Council pause to watch the data? The record leans heavily towards the pause.

2022
First hike in July 2022, a 50-point move ending eight years of negative rates. The Council followed with 75 points at the very next meeting in September and hiked ten times in a row, taking the deposit rate from -0.5% to 4.0% in fourteen months. The only cycle with an immediate follow-up.
2011
Trichet opened with 25 points in April 2011, then held at the next two meetings before a second hike in July. Both moves were reversed by November as the sovereign debt crisis forced Draghi into cuts.
2005
First hike in December 2005, to 2.25%. The Council paused for two meetings and delivered the second hike in March 2006, settling into a quarterly rhythm that ran to 4.0% by mid-2007.
1999
The young ECB hiked 50 points in November 1999, then waited three months before the February 2000 follow-up, eventually reaching 4.75% by October 2000.
1 of 4
In 1 of 4 ECB hiking cycles the second hike came at the very next meeting. The other three times the Council paused for at least two meetings. Only the 2022 energy-shock cycle moved back to back, and July 2026 is also an energy-shock cycle, so the split cuts both ways.

In four ECB hiking cycles, only one (2022) saw a second hike at the immediately following meeting.

5 · The read

How to read it

The case for a hold is the oil chart and the history. Brent has surrendered its entire war premium since the June decision, the direct energy impulse behind the 3.2% inflation print is fading, and three of four past ECB cycles paused after the opening move. July is a non-projection meeting, and this Council has repeatedly preferred to move when new staff forecasts give it cover, which next comes in September.

The counter-case is that the one cycle that did move back to back, 2022, is the one that looks most like today: an energy shock leaking into core prices. May core inflation at 2.5% is above target, wage rounds in Germany and the Netherlands are settling higher, and a Council that waited too long once before may prefer to overtighten early rather than repeat 2021's patience. If June's flash core reading, due mid-July, surprises above 2.5%, the 31% priced for a hike will look cheap.
