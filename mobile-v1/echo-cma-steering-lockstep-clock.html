Regulation03 Sep 2026

Ten months after designating Apple and Google, no rule imposed. Both files move in lockstep.

The CMA published responses to its steering consultation on 14 August. Neither Apple nor Google has yet been given a conduct requirement.

4 min read

Two files, one calendar

The CMA publishes the responses to its consultation on a proposed steering conduct requirement for Apple's and Google's mobile platforms, with no decision yet issued.

(GOV.UK, 14 Aug 2026)

The CMA opened strategic market status investigations into Apple's and Google's mobile platforms on 23 January 2025 under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, published proposed designation decisions and an initial intervention roadmap on 23 July 2025, closed that consultation on 20 August 2025 and issued final decision notices designating both firms on 22 October 2025 (GOV.UK, Apple case; GOV.UK, Google case).

The conduct stage has run on the same calendar. On 10 February 2026 the authority sought views on commitments covering app review, ranking and data use, and published final commitments on 1 April 2026. On 30 June 2026 it launched a consultation on a proposed steering conduct requirement, the rule that would let developers point users to payment options outside an app store, and on 14 August 2026 it published the responses. Neither case page records a conduct requirement having been formally imposed.

The roadmap published alongside the proposed designations said the CMA expected to begin consulting on initial conduct requirements from autumn 2025. The first consultation on commitments came in February 2026 and the first on a conduct requirement in June 2026. Ten months after designation, the regime has produced commitments accepted from the firms and no rule imposed on them.

The Pattern

Inclusion rule, fixed before the count: this pattern counts every completed interval in the two mobile platform cases between the CMA closing a consultation or seeking views and publishing the resulting decision or document, as recorded on the two case pages on 3 September 2026. The test is whether the interval was 70 days or fewer. The step from opening an investigation to proposing a designation is excluded, because it is a different act done under a statutory nine-month clock rather than a discretionary one. Intervals still running are excluded, which is the one this Echo is about.

2025
Apple. Consultation on the proposed designation closed 20 August 2025; the final decision notice designating the firm followed on 22 October 2025. 63 days.
2025
Google. The same consultation closed on the same day and the same final decision notice followed on the same day. 63 days, and the identical figure is the point rather than a coincidence.
2026
Apple and Google, commitments. Views sought 10 February 2026, final commitments published 1 April 2026. 50 days in both cases, again on the same dates.
2026
The interval now running. Responses to the steering consultation were published on 14 August 2026. At 3 September that is 20 days old, and 70 days would fall on 23 October.
4 of 4
4 of 4 within 70 days, and the denominator is soft. Two decisions taken twice is closer to a sample of two than of four, both came from the same team in the same year, and the regime is barely eighteen months old. A perfect record over that little evidence should be read as a working practice that has not yet been tested by a contested decision, which is exactly what a steering rule opposed by both firms would be.

4 of the 4 completed intervals were 70 days or fewer, at 63, 63, 50 and 50 days. The four are two decisions taken twice, because the Apple and Google files have not diverged by a single day.

What this pattern excludes

The investigation to proposed decision step, 23 January to 23 July 2025
It ran 181 days under the statutory nine-month designation clock, which is a legal deadline rather than a discretionary pace. Mixing a statutory period with discretionary ones would make the base rate describe two different constraints at once.
Treating Apple and Google as independent observations
They are not, and the count says so. Every published date in the two cases is identical, so the effective sample is two decisions, not four. The four are shown because the pairing is itself the evidence for the lockstep, but a reader should discount the denominator by half.
CMA merger and markets cases
They run under the Enterprise Act with different statutory deadlines. The digital markets regime is new, and borrowing timings from the older one would import a pace set by different rules.

The Read

The two date markets are a straight read of the regime's only established habit. Fifty days from 14 August is 3 October and sixty-three days is 16 October, so the precedent brackets sit either side of the end of September and comfortably inside the end of October. That is why the first market is below evens and the second is above it. What argues for a longer wait is the nature of the decision. Designation and commitments were both, in the end, agreed outcomes; a steering rule is the first thing the CMA would be imposing on firms that do not want it, and imposed rules attract legal scrutiny that agreed ones do not.

The same-day market is the most informative of the three and it is the one a reader should look at first. If the two decisions land together, the CMA is running a single mobile-platform policy with two addressees, and every future date in one case predicts the other. If they split, it means something in one file has become genuinely contested, and the base rate above stops describing anything. On the record so far the two have not diverged by a day in nineteen months, which is why this market sits high, but a divergence would be worth more than any of the timing above.
