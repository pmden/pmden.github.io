Sinatra opens at the Aldwych. Tina set the bar at seven years.

Sinatra The Musical held its official opening night at the Aldwych Theatre on 24 June, moving into the house Tina Turner's bio-musical occupied for seven years and booking through 10 April 2027.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

First reviews landed for Sinatra The Musical at the Aldwych after its 24 June opening night, the West End's biggest bio-musical launch of 2026.

The Guardian, 25 Jun 2026
2 · The event

A new voice in Tina's old house

Sinatra The Musical began previews at the Aldwych on 3 June and held its official opening night on 24 June, with first reviews landing on 25 June (The Guardian). Joel Harper-Jackson plays Frank Sinatra, alongside Ana Villafañe as Ava Gardner, in a production written by two-time Tony winner Joe DiPietro and directed and choreographed by Kathleen Marshall. The West End transfer was announced earlier this year after the show's 2023 premiere at Birmingham Rep.

The address carries its own expectations: the Aldwych has been the West End's bio-musical house since Tina: The Tina Turner Musical's long residency there. Sinatra arrives with a catalogue of more than twenty standards and the theatre booked until 10 April 2027 (The Guardian, 25 June 2026).

The commercial question is the one every jukebox biography faces: whether the songbook converts into the sustained advance sales that carry a show past its first booking period. The first eight weeks of summer trading, and any extension announcement, are the earliest hard evidence.

3 · The pattern

The bio-musical split: decade runs and fast collapses

The biographical jukebox musical is the West End's most binary format. When the book holds, brand recognition powers runs measured in years; when it does not, even the most famous catalogue in pop cannot stop a closure inside twelve months. The history splits hard.

2018
Tina opened at this same Aldwych Theatre in April 2018 and ran seven years to September 2025, surviving the pandemic shutdown.
2012
Viva Forever!, built on the Spice Girls catalogue, closed after 7 months with reported losses around £5 million — proof the songbook alone saves nothing.
2008
Jersey Boys, the Frankie Valli biography and Sinatra's closest stylistic cousin, ran 9 years at the Prince Edward and Piccadilly before returning again in 2021.
2002
We Will Rock You opened to scathing reviews and ran 12 years at the Dominion regardless — critics have never been the resolution mechanism for this genre.
3 of 4
3 of these 4 landmark jukebox musicals ran for seven years or more; 1 collapsed inside a year. The split is real: survival past the first booking period has historically meant a multi-year run, but the failure mode is fast and total.

Bio-jukebox musicals either run for many years or die within one — the middle outcome barely exists.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case says nothing moves by 10 August. West End producers extend booking periods when the advance justifies it, and the standard rhythm is to announce extensions in early autumn for the spring, once summer tourist trading is banked. Sinatra already holds a ten-month booking, long by launch standards, so there is no operational pressure to extend in the first eight weeks. The status quo option carries the weight, and the seven-in-ten pricing on an unchanged booking looks about right.

The counter-case runs through the box office. A strong opening — Sinatra's brand skews to exactly the higher-spending, older audience that books premium seats months ahead — can front-load the advance enough that producers extend early to capture Christmas-gift buying. Tina in this same house extended repeatedly and early in its life. If the first reviews convert into full houses through July, an August extension announcement is the cheap, loud way to declare a hit, and the one in five priced on it is not a rounding error.
