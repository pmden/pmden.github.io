The Fringe shuts after 25 days. The ticket number lands next.

The Edinburgh Festival Fringe closes today after running from 7 August. The Fringe Society publishes ticket and audience data at the end of each festival.

4 min read

Twenty-five days, and then a number

The 2026 Edinburgh Festival Fringe closes after 25 days; the Fringe Society publishes ticket and audience data as each festival ends.

(Edinburgh Festival Fringe, 31 Aug 2026)

The 2026 Fringe ran from 7 to 31 August. The Fringe Society launched the programme on 4 June with 3,649 shows across 258 venues and artists from 71 countries, with registrations remaining open through the summer (Edinburgh Festival Fringe, 2026). The festival's scale is set by artists paying to register rather than by a curator, which is why the final show count is not known until late.

The figure that matters commercially is tickets issued. In 2025 the Fringe Society reported 2,604,404 tickets across 3,893 shows at 301 venues, with 53,942 performances (Edinburgh Festival Fringe, 25 Aug 2025). The Society publishes that number in an end-of-festival release, and it excludes free unticketed work, so it understates attendance while remaining the only consistent year-on-year series.

The 2026 edition carried fewer venues at launch than the 2025 festival finished with, which is the variable most closely tied to ticket capacity. Accommodation costs, artist registration fees and the economics of running a Fringe venue have been the running argument of the last four festivals, and the ticket total is the number both sides cite.

The Pattern

Inclusion rule, fixed before the outcome: every Edinburgh Festival Fringe since the festival returned at full scale in 2022, using the tickets-issued figure published by the Fringe Society in its end-of-festival release. The run is complete and no year is dropped. The outcome scored is whether the published figure exceeded 2.5 million. Cancelled and reduced editions, and pre-pandemic festivals, are outside the rule and are listed in the exclusions.

2022
The first full-scale return. About 2.2 million tickets issued, roughly three-quarters of the 2019 level. Below 2.5 million.
2023
Recovery continued to about 2.44 million tickets, up on 2022 but still short of the threshold. Below 2.5 million.
2024
The count reached about 2.6 million, the first post-pandemic festival to clear the line. Above 2.5 million.
2025
2,604,404 tickets across 3,893 shows and 301 venues, effectively flat on 2024. Above 2.5 million.
2 of 4
2 of the 4 Fringes since 2022 cleared 2.5 million tickets, and both were the last two. A four-year run is a thin series for a continuous number, so the recent clustering around 2.6 million carries more information than the raw fraction does.

2 of the 4 Fringes since 2022 issued more than 2.5 million tickets, and both of those were the most recent two, which finished within a few thousand tickets of each other.

What this pattern excludes

The 2020 and 2021 festivals
One was cancelled and the other ran as a reduced hybrid edition, so neither produced a ticket figure on a basis comparable with a full-scale Fringe.
The 2019 festival and earlier, including the 3,012,490 peak
Venue numbers, registration costs and accommodation prices have all shifted since; the series is not stationary, and importing the old peak would set a threshold the current festival has never been close to.
Total attendance estimates that include free unticketed shows
A different measure on a different basis, produced irregularly, and not the number the Fringe Society reports at the end of each festival.
Edinburgh International Festival, Book Festival and Art Festival ticket figures
Separate organisations with curated programmes, separate box offices and separate releases; the mechanism that sets their scale is not the Fringe's open registration model.

The Read

The weighted case is another number close to 2.6 million. The last two festivals landed within a few thousand tickets of each other, which is what a mature market looks like when capacity, not demand, is the binding constraint: the venues are full at the hours people want, and the ceiling moves only when rooms are added. That stability is why the 2.5 million market sits at 73% despite the raw base rate being 2 of 4, and why the central band carries the most weight.

The counter-case is capacity going the other way. The 2026 programme launched with 258 venues against the 301 the 2025 festival finished with, and venue count is the closest proxy the series has for how many seats exist to be sold. Registration and accommodation costs have pushed companies towards shorter runs and smaller rooms for several years, and a festival that is a fraction smaller mechanically issues fewer tickets whatever the reviews say. That is the case for the sub-2.5 million bands and for the third market sitting below even money.
