England stumble at Lord's. Pakistan have owned this ground.

England closed the first day of the second Test on 248 for 9 after Mohammad Abbas took four wickets. Pakistan lost four of their own in reply on Friday morning, in the ground where they have won more Tests in England than anywhere else.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

Mohammad Abbas took four wickets on the opening day of the second Test at Lord's, three of them before lunch, as England closed on 248 for 9 after slipping to 80 for 4.

Al Jazeera, 27 Aug 2026
2 · The event

What happened

England batted first at Lord's on 27 August and were 80 for 4 at lunch, with Mohammad Abbas taking three for 34 in the morning session and a fourth wicket later. Jordan Cox made 55 and Jamie Smith 61 to steady the innings, and Gus Atkinson was 34 not out when bad light ended play with England on 248 for 9 (Al Jazeera, 27 Aug 2026).

Pakistan lost four wickets of their own in reply, reaching 63 for 4 inside 23 overs on the second morning with Azan Awais unbeaten on 26 (ESPNcricinfo, Lord's records). The match is scheduled to finish on 31 August.

The series context favours England. They won the first Test at Headingley by an innings and 103 runs (Al Jazeera, 27 Aug 2026) and lead 1-0 with the third and final Test at Edgbaston starting on 9 September. The tour has also been disrupted off the field, with police investigating an incident involving England bowler Brydon Carse (Al Jazeera, 27 Aug 2026).

3 · The pattern

The pattern

Lord's is supposed to be a fortress. Against Pakistan it has been the opposite. Since 1982 the two sides have met nine times at the ground and the visitors have taken more of them than the hosts, in eras with entirely different squads, pitches and balls.

1982
Pakistan won by 10 wickets, ending a 28-year wait for a Test win in England and opening Imran Khan's captaincy (ESPNcricinfo, Lord's records).
1992
Pakistan won by 2 wickets, one of the tightest finishes of the Wasim and Waqar era (ESPNcricinfo, Lord's records).
1996
Pakistan won by 164 runs on the way to taking the series (ESPNcricinfo, Lord's records).
2016
Pakistan won by 75 runs, with Misbah-ul-Haq's first-day hundred and Yasir Shah's ten wickets in the match (ESPNcricinfo, Jul 2016).
2018
Pakistan won by 9 wickets inside four days. England's two Lord's wins in the same period, in 2001 and 2010, both came by an innings (ESPNcricinfo, Lord's records).
5 of 9
Since 1982 the sides have played nine Tests at Lord's: Pakistan have won five, England two and two were drawn. That is a higher win rate for the touring side than at any comparable English venue, and only two matches in more than forty years have ended without a result. The caveat is that England's two wins were both by an innings, so when the hosts have been on top at Lord's they have been overwhelmingly on top.

5 of the 9 England-Pakistan Tests at Lord's since 1982 have gone to Pakistan, against 2 for England and 2 draws.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case is still England. They arrived on the back of an innings victory at Headingley, they have the deeper attack, and 248 on a Lord's first day against a bowler of Abbas's control is a par score rather than a collapse. Pakistan at 63 for 4 in reply have already conceded the initiative once, and England's two wins at this ground in the modern record both came by an innings, which is what happens when their seamers get a surface that offers something. Two of the last nine Tests here have been drawn, so the market prices a result heavily.

The honest counter is the ground itself. Pakistan have won more Tests at Lord's than at any other venue in England, and the wins have come in every era and with every generation of bowler, which argues for conditions rather than personnel. Abbas has already shown the ball moves. If England's tail cannot push past 260 and Pakistan's middle order absorbs the new ball, a first-innings deficit of any size puts the fourth innings on a fifth-day pitch. The prediction fails if rain takes a full day out of the match, which would turn the result market into a draw and leave the series where it started.
