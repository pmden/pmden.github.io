Chips14 May 2026

Washington opened the door. Beijing won't walk through it.

The US cleared ten Chinese firms to buy Nvidia's H200 in May. Seven weeks on, not a single chip has shipped, and the blockage is now on the Chinese side.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

Seven weeks after Washington cleared H200 sales to ten Chinese firms, deliveries remain stuck at zero — and Nvidia's China problem has become the test case for the whole export regime.

Reuters, 2 Jul 2026
2 · The event

What happened

On 14 May the US Commerce Department cleared roughly ten Chinese companies, including Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance and JD.com, to purchase Nvidia's H200 accelerator, the first sanctioned route back into China for a near-frontier American AI chip since 2023 (Reuters, 14 May 2026). Licensing terms cap each approved buyer at 75,000 units, a ceiling Washington had been weighing since the spring (Bloomberg, 2 Mar 2026).

The clearance has produced no deliveries. Chinese buyers pulled back after guidance from Beijing, which is pressing its platform companies to build on Huawei's Ascend line instead. Jensen Huang has said Nvidia received purchase orders and restarted H200 manufacturing for the Chinese market, yet the US Commerce Secretary has confirmed that no H200s have actually been sold into China, blaming import blocking by the Chinese government.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

This is the fourth round of the same cycle since 2019: Washington restricts, Nvidia engineers a compliant product or a licence path opens, and then one of the two governments closes the window. What has changed is which government does the closing.

2025
The H20 cycle ran the full loop in five months. An April licence requirement forced Nvidia to take a $4.5 billion charge; July approvals resumed under a 15% revenue-share arrangement with Washington; by September Beijing had told major platform companies to halt H20 orders.
2023
The A800 and H800, designed specifically to comply with the 2022 rules, were banned by new October 2023 controls after barely 12 months on the market.
2022
October 2022 export controls cut off the A100 and H100. Nvidia held an estimated 95% of China's AI accelerator market at the time; Huang later said that share collapsed toward 50% and kept falling.
2019
Huawei's placement on the Entity List in May 2019 was meant to cripple it. Instead it forced China's flagship onto domestic silicon; the Ascend line it accelerated is now Beijing's preferred substitute for Nvidia.
1 of 3
Of three rounds of China-compliant Nvidia chips since 2022, only one (A800/H800) achieved sustained sales, and Washington killed it after a year. The pattern is split on who closes the window, US or China, but not on the outcome: 1 of 3 rounds lasted.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case is that nothing ships by early August. Beijing's blocking is not a negotiating pause but industrial policy: every quarter Chinese platforms spend on Ascend instead of H200 deepens the domestic ecosystem, which is precisely the point. The September 2025 halt order on H20 purchases was never reversed, and the H200 approval changes American policy, not Chinese policy. On the historical pattern, compliant-chip windows close; this one arguably arrived pre-closed.

The counter-case is that the H200 is a much bigger prize than the H20, and Chinese labs racing frontier models know Ascend supply and software maturity cannot yet match it. Purchase orders exist; Huang says manufacturing has restarted. If even one approved buyer takes delivery, quietly and with Beijing's tolerance, the market flips fast, and a US-China trade accommodation over the summer could make chips the bargaining chip. A 22% call on delivery is low, but it is not a rounding error.
