Pogacar leads by 3:36. The Tour kept Alpe d'Huez twice for last.

Tadej Pogacar reached the first rest day of the 2026 Tour de France in yellow and then extended his lead with a Bastille Day solo at Le Lioran.

4 min read

Yellow at the rest day, and a widening gap

Tadej Pogacar attacked alone to win stage 10 into Le Lioran on Bastille Day, extending his overall lead as Jonas Vingegaard lost contact in the final metres.

(ESPN, 14 Jul 2026)

Vingegaard wore yellow first after the time-trial opener in Barcelona on 4 July (ESPN, 5 Jul 2026), but Pogacar took the jersey by dropping the Dane on the final ramps of Les Angles to win stage 3 (ESPN, 9 Jul 2026).

The sprinters get Wednesday's flat run from Vichy to Nevers (ESPN, 15 Jul 2026), but the race's centre of gravity sits in the final weekend: the first back-to-back Alpe d'Huez finishes in Grand Tour history, a 128km dash from Gap on 24 July and then a 171km queen stage with 5,600m of climbing over the Croix de Fer, Telegraphe, Galibier and Col de Sarenne on 25 July, before Paris and three ascents of Montmartre on 26 July (ESPN, 24 Jul 2026).

The Pattern

Leading the Tour at the first rest day sounds decisive. Over the last ten editions it has been a coin flip, and the exceptions include Pogacar himself.

2025
Breakaway rider Ben Healy wore yellow at the first rest day, then lost 13 minutes in the Pyrenees on stage 12. Pogacar won in Paris.
2024
Pogacar led at the first rest day and won the Tour, closing out in Nice.
2023
Vingegaard reached the first rest day in yellow and defended it to Paris.
2022
Pogacar led at the rest day, then cracked on the Col du Granon on stage 11 as Vingegaard took yellow for good.
2020
Primoz Roglic held yellow from the first rest day until the penultimate-day time trial, where Pogacar overturned the race at La Planche des Belles Filles.
2019
Julian Alaphilippe carried yellow deep into the third week from the first rest day but faded in the Alps; Egan Bernal won.
5 of 10
5 of the last 10 Tours were won by the rider wearing yellow at the first rest day.

Add Chris Froome's conversions in 2016 and 2017 and Pogacar's in 2021, and the rider in yellow at the first rest day won 5 of the last 10 Tours. Narrow it to genuine GC favourites in yellow, rather than breakaway leaders like Healy or Alaphilippe, and the record improves to 5 of 7.

The Read

The case for Pogacar at 79 per cent: he has won every summit test so far, took time on Vingegaard at Les Angles and Le Lioran, and 3:36 is a bigger rest-day cushion than he held in either of his last two winning Tours. Among GC favourites who led at the first rest day, 5 of 7 went on to win.

The honest counter is that the raw base rate is 5 of 10, and both of Pogacar's own failures came after he led at the first rest day, in 2020's final time trial and on the Granon in 2022. This route is built for a late ambush: no Tour has ever stacked Alpe d'Huez on consecutive days, and the 5,600m queen stage falls less than 48 hours before Paris. A 3:36 lead has evaporated on a single climb before, on this rival's wheel.
