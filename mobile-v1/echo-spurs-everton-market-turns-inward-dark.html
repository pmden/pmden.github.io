Spurs and Everton trade twice. The market has turned inward.

Tottenham and Everton are in advanced talks over two separate deals: Iliman Ndiaye to north London for a fee discussed at around £60m, and Richarlison back to Merseyside for about £35m. The window closes at 11pm on Tuesday.

2 · The event

Two deals, one pair of clubs, forty-eight hours

Tottenham and Everton are in advanced talks over separate deals rather than a straight exchange, with a potential fee of around £60m under discussion for Iliman Ndiaye and around £35m for Richarlison to return to the club he left for Tottenham in 2022 (Sky Sports, 30 Aug 2026). Neither is complete.

They would join a summer already dominated by domestic trade. Chelsea paid Aston Villa £117m for Morgan Rogers and Manchester City paid Nottingham Forest £116m for Elliot Anderson, the two largest deals of the window. Tottenham took Sandro Tonali from Newcastle for £100m and Mateus Fernandes from West Ham for £85m; Arsenal took Bruno Guimarães from Newcastle for £75m and Ezri Konsa from Villa for £51m (Sky Sports, Aug 2026; ESPN, Aug 2026).

The deadline is fixed. The window opened on 15 June and closes at 23:00 BST on Tuesday 1 September, with a correctly submitted deal sheet buying time for paperwork only (Premier League, 2026).

3 · The pattern

Where the biggest fee of a summer comes from

Inclusion rule, fixed before the outcome: the largest reported fee paid by a Premier League club in a summer window, for the four windows since 2019 whose largest fee is documented in a primary report cited below. The measured outcome is single: was the selling club also a Premier League club? Windows we could not verify against a primary report are named in the exclusions rather than quietly dropped; a reader who can source a missing window is invited to send it and it goes in.

2019
Manchester United paid Leicester £80m for Harry Maguire, then a world record for a defender and the summer's largest Premier League fee (Sky Sports, Aug 2019). Domestic.
2021
Manchester City triggered a £100m release clause for Jack Grealish at Aston Villa, a British record (Sky Sports, Aug 2021). Domestic.
2022
Manchester United paid Ajax £86m for Antony on deadline day, then the largest deadline-day fee on record (Sky Sports, Sep 2022). Overseas.
2025
Liverpool paid Newcastle a British record £125m for Alexander Isak on deadline day (Premier League, Sep 2025). Domestic.
3 of 4
Three of the four verified windows had the summer's biggest Premier League fee flowing to another Premier League club, and the exception was an overseas deadline-day deal. The mechanism is not sentiment about English football. Profitability and sustainability rules reward selling academy and domestically registered players, buyers pay a premium for proven league experience, and clubs facing an accounting deadline find it easier to agree terms with a club under the same rules. Four windows is a thin sample and the exclusions list says which ones we could not verify.

3 of the 4 windows checked had their largest Premier League fee paid to another Premier League club. The one exception, 2022, was a deadline-day purchase from the Netherlands.

What this pattern excludes

Summer windows 2020, 2023 and 2024
We could not confirm the single largest Premier League fee of those windows in a primary report from our source list before publication, so they are named here rather than counted either way.
The 2026 window itself
It is still open. Counting a case whose outcome the market is being asked to price would be circular.
January windows
Mid-season buying is driven by injury and league position rather than squad construction, so the decision under test is a different one.
Free transfers and loan fees
No transfer fee is paid, so the case cannot be the largest fee of a window by definition.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case is that the domestic market keeps clearing. Both of this summer's nine-figure deals were Premier League to Premier League, both of Tottenham's largest purchases were too, and the two clubs talking on Sunday each want something the other has: Everton have a forward who has turned down contract offers, Tottenham have one who wants to leave. Deals of that shape are the easiest kind to complete in forty-eight hours because valuation is the only open question and both sides work under the same accounting rules.

The counter is that two-club, two-way business is fragile precisely because each side can hold the other hostage on the second leg. Last September's collapse of Marc Guéhi's move to Liverpool showed how quickly a deal with an agreed fee dies when the selling club's own arithmetic fails at the last hour. The prediction on the pair of deals fails if either club decides the other is dependent, and starts moving the price on Tuesday afternoon.
