The FTSE holds five figures. History says look down.

The FTSE 100 ended June near 10,508, within sight of its all-time high and six months after closing above 10,000 for the first time. The FTSE 250 is quietly going the other way.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

The FTSE 100 crossed 10,000 for the first time on 2 January and has held five figures into the summer — the question now is whether the index closes July above the line.

Reuters, 2 Jan 2026
2 · The event

What happened

The rally caps a remarkable half-year: the index closed above 10,000 points for the first time in its history on 2 January, the opening session of 2026 Reuters, 2 Jan 2026.

That milestone came barely six months after the index first held 9,000 in July 2025, a pace of ascent London has not seen since the late 1990s The Times, 15 Jul 2025. Falling gilt yields, a weaker pound flattering overseas earnings and a rotation into cheap UK large caps have all pulled in the same direction.

Beneath the surface the picture is narrower. A large-cap rally without mid-cap confirmation is the classic signature of a top-heavy market, and July brings a Bank of England rate decision and a heavy earnings calendar to test it.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

The FTSE 100 has crossed a thousand-point milestone five times. Round numbers exert a documented pull on markets — they attract profit-taking, anchor headlines and mark psychological resistance. The record after each first crossing is mostly a story of retreats.

2026
The index closed above 10,000 on 2 January, its first trading day of the year, and traded near 10,508 by late June.
2025
First close above 9,000 in July. The level held — the index kept climbing into 2026 without falling back below.
2023
First close above 8,000 in February. Within a month the US regional-banking crisis had dragged it back below 7,400.
2015
First crossed 7,000 in March. The level did not hold durably until October 2016, more than 18 months later.
1998
Passed 6,000 in the spring; the autumn emerging-markets and LTCM selloff pulled it back below before year-end.
1 of 4
Of the four previous thousand-point milestones, only one — 9,000 in 2025 — held without the index falling back below within a year. Three of four first crossings were followed by a retreat. The most recent case broke the pattern; the longer record supports it.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case for caution: the historical record at round numbers is 1 for 4, the rally is being carried by a narrow set of blue chips, and the FTSE 250's slide means the domestic economy is not confirming the move. Markets that make highs on shrinking breadth have a habit of giving the last few hundred points back, and 10,600 requires the index to push through record territory in a holiday-thinned month with a rate decision in the middle of it.

The honest counter-case: the one milestone that held is the most recent one, and the conditions that carried the index from 9,000 to 10,500 — falling gilt yields, cheap valuations relative to global peers, heavyweight earners priced in dollars — have not gone away. The FTSE 100 is not the UK economy; it is a global earnings machine listed in London. If the Bank of England holds and July earnings deliver, another one per cent to 10,600 is the market's path of least resistance, not a stretch.
