Nations Championship04 Jul 2026

A mauling in Johannesburg. A must-win in Liverpool.

South Africa ran seven tries past England in a 45-21 rout at Ellis Park to open the inaugural Nations Championship. England have seven days to find an answer before Fiji arrive in Liverpool.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

South Africa beat England 45-21 at Ellis Park, running in seven tries in their Nations Championship opener in front of nearly 53,000 in Johannesburg.

Sky Sports, 4 Jul 2026
2 · The event

What happened at Ellis Park

South Africa scored through Thomas du Toit, Cheslin Kolbe and Kurt-Lee Arendse to lead by 17 points inside twelve minutes, before England clawed the deficit back to three by half-time through solo finishes from Ellis Genge and George Martin (Sky Sports, 4 July 2026). The recovery did not survive the restart: Grant Williams and Jesse Kriel scored within sixteen minutes of the second half, and late tries from Malcolm Marx and Ben-Jason Dixon turned a contest into a rout.

England finished the match with thirteen men on the field after yellow cards for Tommy Freeman and Guy Pepper, and the 45-21 final score stood as a statement opening to the inaugural Nations Championship (ESPN, 4 July 2026). Damian Willemse and Kolbe, both winning their 50th caps, were among the Springboks' standout performers.

England, captained by Jamie George with Maro Itoje rested for the summer window, now return home for the second round of the competition: Fiji at the Hill Dickinson Stadium in Liverpool on 11 July, followed by Argentina in Santiago del Estero on 18 July (RFU, 2026).

3 · The pattern

England against Fiji: near-total dominance, one fresh scar

England's record against Fiji is one of the most lopsided in their fixture list, but the most recent home meeting produced the upset that reshaped how the fixture is priced. The pattern is dominance with a live, recent exception.

2023

Twickenham: England won 58-15, running in eight tries.

Twickenham: England won 54-12 in the autumn internationals.

8 of 9
England have won eight of nine meetings with Fiji. But the single defeat is recent — August 2023, at home — and Fiji arrive in Liverpool against an England side that has just conceded 45 points. The base rate favours England; the trajectory does not.

5 · The read

How to read it

The case for England is structural. Fiji have beaten England once in nine attempts, England are at home, and the 2023 Twickenham defeat came from a weakened warm-up selection rather than a full-strength Test side. Ellis Park is among the hardest away fixtures in world rugby; losing there by 24 says less about the Fiji game than the raw score suggests. England also recovered from 17-0 down to within three points by half-time, which points to a side that malfunctioned in bursts rather than for eighty minutes.

The counter-case is that the bursts are the problem. England conceded three tries in twelve minutes, lost the second half 28-6, and finished with thirteen men — indiscipline and defensive lapses are exactly what Fiji's transition game punishes, as Twickenham 2023 demonstrated. Fiji at home in name only, in a new stadium in Liverpool rather than a fortress, against a team whose one recent meeting with them at home ended in defeat: the 71 per cent the market gives England is dominance priced with a visible crack.
