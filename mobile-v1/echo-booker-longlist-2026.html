Taiwan took the International. Now the Booker Dozen looms.

The Booker Prize 2026 longlist lands on 28 July, after Taiwan Travelogue made Yáng Shuāng-zǐ the first Mandarin-language winner of the International prize.

4 min read

One prize decided, one about to open

Taiwan Travelogue by Yáng Shuāng-zǐ, translated by Lin King, wins the International Booker Prize 2026 at Tate Modern — the first book written in Mandarin, and the first by a Taiwanese author, to take the prize.

(The Booker Prizes, 19 May 2026)

The International Booker Prize 2026 was settled on 19 May at Tate Modern, where the £50,000 award — split equally between author and translator — went to Taiwan Travelogue by Yáng Shuāng-zǐ, translated from Mandarin by Lin King. The panel, chaired by Natasha Brown, chose it from a longlist of 13 books drawn from 128 submissions, having announced the shortlist on 31 March. It is the first Mandarin-language book, and the first by a Taiwanese author, to win.

Attention now shifts to the English-language prize. The Booker Prize Foundation has confirmed the 2026 calendar: the longlist of 12 or 13 books — the 'Booker Dozen' — arrives on Tuesday 28 July, the shortlist of six on 22 September, and the winner on 9 November.

Longlist day has become the trade's sharpest single signal. Publishers time paperback runs to it, and the composition of the Dozen — how many debuts, how many small presses, whether any past winner returns — sets the narrative for the autumn. Last year's list put Kiran Desai back in the race 19 years after The Inheritance of Loss and carried her to the shortlist, though the 2025 prize ultimately went to David Szalay's Flesh.

The Pattern

Judging panels change every year, and each new panel faces the same charged choice: whether to re-admit a past champion. The record since 2019 cuts both ways — some panels welcome the returning heavyweight, others build the list entirely from new blood.

2025
Kiran Desai, winner in 2006, is longlisted and then shortlisted for The Loneliness of Sonia and Sunny — her first novel in 19 years. She does not win; David Szalay does.
2024
No former winner on the 13-book longlist. Samantha Harvey's Orbital wins from a list built around first-time nominees.
2022
The second of three consecutive longlists (2022–2024) with no former winner anywhere on the list.
2021
Kazuo Ishiguro, winner in 1989, is longlisted for Klara and the Sun — 32 years after The Remains of the Day — but goes no further than the shortlist.
2019
Two former winners — Margaret Atwood and Salman Rushdie — reach the shortlist together, and Atwood shares the prize with Bernardine Evaristo, 19 years after her first win.
3 of 7
Three of the last seven Booker longlists (2019–2025) included at least one former winner of the prize. The pattern is split — and the misses came in a consecutive block, so panel temperament matters more than trend.

3 of the last 7 longlists carried at least one former winner — a genuine split.

The Read

The weighted case leans slightly against a returning winner. Four of the last seven lists had none, and the three that did each required an unusually big name with an unusually long absence — Atwood, Ishiguro, Desai. The 2026 submission pool of 128 books is not known to contain an obvious equivalent, and panels that follow a former-winner year often swing deliberately towards discovery. The debut count is steadier ground: every recent longlist has carried at least two first novels, and 2–3 is the modal outcome.

The honest counter-case: this is a market about information nobody outside the judging room holds. Submission lists are confidential, and a single high-profile autumn novel from a past laureate would flip the former-winner question instantly. A 42 per cent price on YES is not a low-probability call mispriced — it is roughly the honest base rate, which means the edge here is small and the resolution date is the real attraction.
