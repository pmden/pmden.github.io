The US offers 40m barrels of reserve oil again. In June, companies took almost none

On 29 September the US Energy Department offered to lend companies up to 40m barrels from its emergency oil reserve. Bids are due on 6 October.

4 min read

What happened

On 29 September 2026 the US Energy Department asked companies to bid by 6 October to borrow up to 40 million barrels of crude from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve. (US Department of Energy)

The US Energy Department wants to lend up to 40 million barrels of crude from its emergency reserve. Companies must send bids by 11:00 Central Time on 6 October. The oil would leave the reserve in November and December. Companies that borrow it must later give back more oil than they took. (US Department of Energy)

This is the last part of the 172 million barrels the US promised in March. That was its share of a joint release agreed by about 30 countries in the International Energy Agency. (US Department of Energy; Reuters)

The same 40 million barrels were offered in June. Later that month the government said companies had agreed to borrow only about 500,000 barrels. (Reuters)

The department says its five earlier offers this year gave out more than 133 million barrels in four loans. Companies return the oil with extra barrels, 25% extra in the past loans, which it says saves taxpayers more than $3 billion. (US Department of Energy)

Energy Secretary Chris Wright also criticised allies: “While the United States and Japan are delivering on their commitments, several European member countries have released only a fraction of the crude oil and petroleum products they pledged.” (US Department of Energy)

The reserve holds less than 284 million barrels, the lowest since 1982. US diesel costs about $6.50 a gallon and petrol about $4.45. (Reuters)

The Pattern

We looked for cases where the US government offered to lend a set amount of oil from its emergency reserve, set a date for bids, and let the companies’ bids decide how much went out. We counted only offers made together with other countries. We found six, one in 2021 and five this year. Companies took more than half of what was offered in four. They never took all of it.

4 of 6
In four of six offers companies took more than half of the oil, and the government said so within a week of the bid date. In the other two they took 15% and about 1%.

The Read

History says companies usually take most of the oil. In four of six offers they took more than half, and the government said so within a week of the bid date. In five of six it announced the result within a week.

The record is thin. Five of the six cases come from one programme this year, under one agreement and one decider, so they are less independent than six cases look. We also did not record oil prices at the time of each bid, and that is what makes borrowing worth it for a company. In 2021 the White House said such loans work when markets expect oil to be cheaper later than it is today.

The closest case points the other way. In June the government offered the same 40 million barrels and companies took about 500,000, about 1%. The department put out no statement on that result. The 2021 offer also started weak, at 15%, and later requests took it to 92%. A statement on the department’s news page between 6 and 13 October settles both questions.
