US energy23 Sep 2026

Trump backs a US diesel export ban. Such threats usually worked within two months.

Donald Trump said on 22 September that he supports a ban on US diesel exports. His Treasury Secretary said a full or partial ban is being studied.

5 min read

What happened

Donald Trump said on 22 September 2026 that he supports a ban on US diesel exports, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said officials were examining whether a full or partial ban would work. (The Guardian)

US diesel cost $6.53 a gallon on 22 September, a record, and more than 75% above a year earlier. That day Donald Trump told reporters: “I’ve said let’s not send out the diesel. We make a lot of diesel … I’ve called for it within my people.” No order has been signed. (The Guardian; BBC News)

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said officials were “examining whether it’s feasible in terms of the overall refining capacity and whether a full or partial ban would work”. (The Guardian; BBC News)

This is the first time the president has backed the idea in public. His own team turned down an export ban more than once this year. In March the government said it was not considering an oil export ban. In May Interior Secretary Doug Burgum and Energy Secretary Chris Wright both said the White House was not considering one. On 14 September Burgum said an oil export ban was unlikely to lower prices. (Financial Times; The Independent; Reuters)

Republicans in close races before the 3 November midterm elections are pushing for a ban. Senator Dan Sullivan of Alaska asked for a “temporary pause” of diesel exports. (BBC News; The Guardian)

The oil industry is against it. The refiners’ trade group, the American Fuel and Petrochemical Manufacturers, said a ban would “backfire”. The American Petroleum Institute said it “would wreak havoc at home and abroad”. (BBC News; American Petroleum Institute)

The US exports about 1.3 million barrels of diesel a day, nearly a quarter of what its refineries make. The UK and the Netherlands rely on it. (BBC News; The Telegraph)

The Pattern

We looked for times when a leader, or ministers speaking for him, publicly threatened a tough step against his own country’s industry to bring a price down at home. In each case no decision had yet been taken, he could act without parliament, and the industry openly opposed him. We counted a result if, within 69 days, the government acted or the industry gave way. We found eight cases since 1962. Only one, Mexico in 2021, is outside the United States, and two are the same president against the same drug makers.

7 of 8
In 7 of 8 cases a rule or the industry giving way followed within 61 days. The one miss, in 2022, was a warning that led nowhere.
2025 US, Trump and Pfizerheld
31 July 2025: Donald Trump wrote to 17 drug makers that the government would “deploy every tool in our arsenal” if they did not cut prices by 29 September. Pfizer agreed a price deal on 30 September.
2022 US, Granholm and refinersnot held
August 2022: Energy Secretary Jennifer Granholm wrote to refiners that the government might need “additional federal requirements”. Refiners objected in writing on 4 October. No rule and no pledge followed.

2021 Mexico, López Obradorheld
26 July 2021: Mexico’s President Andrés Manuel López Obrador said there would be a maximum price for bottled gas. The energy regulator set price caps for six months on 29 July.
2020 US, Trump and drug makersheld
24 July 2020: Donald Trump signed a drug price order and held it back for a month so drug makers could offer a deal. No deal came, and he signed a new order on 13 September.
1973 US, Nixon and soyaheld
13 June 1973: President Richard Nixon said a new system of export controls on food was needed. On 27 June soybean and cottonseed exports were stopped; on 2 July the ban was replaced by export controls.
1966 US, Johnson and steelheld
January 1966: President Lyndon Johnson called Bethlehem Steel’s $5 a ton rise “unwarranted”, and agencies were told to buy only from firms that held prices. U.S. Steel raised by $2.75 instead, and others followed.
1965 US, Johnson and aluminiumheld
November 1965: on President Lyndon Johnson’s order, his ministers said the government would sell 200,000 tons of stored aluminium. About four days later Alcoa and the other makers cancelled their price rise.
1962 US, Kennedy and steelheld
11 April 1962: President John F. Kennedy called the steel price rise “wholly unjustifiable” and named checks by the Justice Department. Bethlehem Steel and U.S. Steel withdrew the rise on 13 April.

The Read

History points to a result within weeks. In all six cases where the leader made the threat himself, the government acted or the industry gave way within 61 days, and four of the six came within two weeks. The one case with no result, in 2022, was a letter from an energy minister to refiners. It is also the closest in subject, since it was about fuel exports and the same industry. This time the president has spoken himself, Senator Dan Sullivan and other Republicans want a pause before the 3 November elections, and his Treasury Secretary says the idea is being studied.

The doubts start with his own team. Doug Burgum and Chris Wright rejected an export ban in public this year. Refiners also cannot withdraw a world diesel price the way steel makers withdrew their own price rise in 1962. The record is narrow too. Only Mexico in 2021 is outside the United States, and two cases are Donald Trump against drug makers.

If a step comes, history says it is likely to be partial and short. Of the three cases where the government acted, only Mexico’s gas price cap was the full step named in the threat. A signed order or a promise from refiners would settle the main question early. A new public rejection by Burgum or Wright would make a step less likely.
