Ukraine war13 Sep 2026

Trump asks Ukraine to spare Russian oil. Both times he asked in 2026, Kyiv struck.

Donald Trump said in Ireland on 13 September that Ukraine must stop hitting Russian diesel plants. He blamed Ukraine, not the Iran war, for $6 diesel.

4 min read

What happened

Donald Trump, speaking to reporters at his Doonbeg golf course in County Clare on Sunday 13 September, said his administration had asked Volodymyr Zelensky to stop drone and missile strikes on Russian sites that produce diesel, and blamed those strikes rather than the US war with Iran for the record price of diesel worldwide.

(Politico Europe 13 Sep)

Donald Trump used a walk round his Doonbeg golf course on Sunday to tell Ukraine to stop hitting Russian diesel production. Asked about diesel reaching a record $6.06 a gallon in the United States on Friday, he said Zelensky could go after other targets but must leave diesel alone because the strikes were causing a world shortage. He said his administration had already put that to Zelensky, and he rejected the idea that the six-month-old war with Iran, which has choked oil shipments from the Gulf, was to blame. (Politico 13 Sep; Reuters 13 Sep; Al Jazeera 13 Sep)

The president's line completes a fortnight of the same message from his cabinet. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on 2 September that the US energy shock was partly the result of Ukraine choosing to blow up Russian refineries, and Energy Secretary Chris Wright told CBS last week that damage to Russian refineries explained the diesel squeeze. A senior European official close to the US-brokered talks told Politico the administration was looking for scapegoats before the 3 November midterms and that any energy ceasefire would have to be mutual. Sergei Lavrov said on Saturday that Russia would not pause its attacks for the duration of talks. The International Energy Agency's 11 September report gives the president part of his case: it said disruption to Russian refining and a near halt to product exports after Ukrainian attacks had compounded the diesel losses from the Gulf. (Politico 13 Sep; NYT 3 Sep; Al Jazeera 13 Sep)

For Britain the ask lands in the middle of a fuel squeeze of its own. Heating oil has gone from about 60p a litre before the Iran war to about 97p, the government has put £53m into heating-oil support, and Saudi Arabia shut its East-West pipeline on Friday after drones from Iraq hit it, sending Brent past $100. Ukraine's refinery campaign is the other side of the ledger: the General Staff claimed the Orsk refinery on 11 August and the Afipsky refinery on 25 August, and Russia has begun importing petrol from India. (BBC 13 Sep; Guardian 12 Sep; Al Jazeera 25 Aug; Guardian 20 Aug)

The Pattern

Requests since 2022 that Ukraine limit its strikes on Russian territory or Russian energy, five in all: four from Washington and one from unnamed partners reported by Zelensky, each recorded by a whitelisted outlet. Held means Ukraine's president, General Staff or a named commander publicly claimed a strike on a Russian refinery, oil depot, pipeline station or oil terminal within 14 days of the request becoming public; claims through unnamed sources do not count. The Read

Trump's need is a price. US diesel set a record above $6 a gallon on Friday, he has promised every adult American $5,000 if Republicans hold both chambers on 3 November, and he has told his own supporters that oil will not come down until after that vote; pinning diesel on Kyiv moves the cause away from the Iran war. Kyiv's need is reach: refinery strikes are the one lever it holds on Russian revenue, Russia is now importing petrol, and Zelensky said on Saturday that Moscow is trying to kill the Ukrainian economy. Moscow's move came first, with Lavrov ruling out any pause during talks. Inside the market window the actors with a move are Zelensky and the General Staff, who choose targets and whether to claim them; the White House, which has an aid and intelligence lever it used in March 2025; and British ministers, with Powell in Kyiv on Sunday telling Europe to take a hard winter to keep the pressure on. (Politico 13 Sep; BBC 10 Sep; Guardian 13 Sep; BBC 5 Mar 2025; BBC 13 Sep)

The weighted case is a claimed strike inside the fortnight. The two 2026 requests, the only ones made since Kyiv began claiming strikes in Russia by name, were each followed by a General Staff claim within a day: Orsk after the Vance call in August, Ust-Luga after the partners' signal in March. August also shows the shape of Kyiv's compliance: it stopped hitting the Kazakh-oil tankers Vance named and kept hitting refineries, and Trump's own wording on Sunday left other targets open. The campaign tempo is one claimed refinery or depot every few days, so the market opens at 74 rather than the two-in-five base rate, whose three misses come from 2022 and 2024, when Kyiv claimed nothing in Russia, and from March 2025. (Guardian 13 Aug; Guardian 14 Aug; BBC 31 Mar; Al Jazeera 25 Aug)

The honest counter-case is March 2025. When the American ask came packaged with a reciprocal energy pause and a live US-led track, Kyiv stopped claiming oil strikes and spent two weeks denying Russian accusations instead. A mutual energy ceasefire is exactly the price the European official named to Politico, and Witkoff and Kushner were discussing next steps with Putin a week ago. The prediction fails if Washington offers Kyiv that trade inside the fortnight, or if Kyiv reverts to the 2024 habit of hitting refineries without claiming them. The White House market fails if the lever stays in speeches; the UK market fails if ministers keep to self-defence language and never name energy. (Guardian 2 Apr 2025; Guardian 5 Sep; Guardian 2 Apr 2024)
