Broadband11 May 2026

238 more exchanges go fibre-only. The copper countdown accelerates.

Openreach's Tranche 24 puts another 1.69 million premises on notice: where full fibre is available, new copper broadband and phone orders stop. The exchange-by-exchange shutdown of Britain's Victorian-era network is now past the 60% mark.

3 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

Openreach publishes Tranche 24 of its FTTP stop-sell programme: 238 exchanges and 1.69 million premises where new copper orders will cease.

Openreach, 11 May 2026
2 · The event

What happened

On 11 May 2026 Openreach published Tranche 24 of its copper stop-sell programme, listing 238 exchanges covering 1.69 million premises. Twelve months after the announcement, new copper-based broadband and legacy phone orders stop in those areas; full fibre becomes the only option for new connections. The trigger is coverage: an exchange qualifies once more than 75% of its premises can order FTTP Openreach, 11 May 2026.

The tranche takes the programme to 1,432 exchanges and 14.2 million premises under stop-sell rules, roughly 61% of Openreach's full-fibre footprint, which itself now passes more than 21 million premises Openreach, May 2026. James Lilley, Openreach's managed customer migrations director, called the programme "a vital step in accelerating the UK's transition to a modern full fibre future".

The commercial logic is blunt: running copper and fibre in parallel is expensive, and copper's ability to support modern services is declining. The stop-sell machine is the quiet, incremental half of the transition. The loud half, the national switch-off of the analogue phone network, has already slipped once.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

Britain has run several national technology switch-offs. The record splits cleanly by structure: incremental, area-by-area migrations tend to land on schedule, while single national deadlines tend to move.

2026
Tranche 24 lists 238 exchanges and 1.69m premises; the stop-sell programme has issued tranches on a steady drumbeat since 2021 without a public reversal.
2024
The nationwide PSTN analogue phone switch-off, originally set for December 2025, is pushed to 31 January 2027 after failures affecting vulnerable telecare users.
2024
UK mobile operators complete the 3G switch-off, with Vodafone, EE and Three finishing their shutdowns broadly on their announced schedules.
2020
Salisbury becomes the UK's first fibre-only exchange trial, with copper stop-sell from December 2020, proving the exchange-level model the tranches now scale.
2012
The digital TV switchover completes in October 2012, 14 years after being announced, delivered region by region rather than in one national jump.
2 of 4
Of four major UK switch-off programmes, two ran to their original schedule and two slipped or stretched. The split is structural, not random: exchange-level and region-level migrations (stop-sell, digital TV regions, 3G) delivered; the single-date PSTN deadline moved by 13 months.

Incremental switch-offs land on time; single national deadlines slip.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case: the stop-sell machine is the most predictable process in UK telecoms. Tranches have appeared roughly monthly through 2026, each obeying the same 75% coverage trigger and 12-month notice, and Openreach's build pace of roughly a million premises a quarter makes both live questions lean YES. The programme is commercially driven: every quarter of parallel copper-and-fibre operation costs BT money, so the incentive to keep publishing tranches is constant.

The honest counter-case: the 22 million threshold is tighter than it looks. Public confirmation of the footprint sits at just over 21 million, and quarterly reporting can lag the live build by weeks; a footprint of 21.8m at the reporting cut-off resolves NO despite the build being on track. And the PSTN slippage is a live warning that the consumer-facing end of this transition, vulnerable users, telecare lines, forced migrations, can still generate the kind of political pressure that pauses even a well-oiled programme.
