Inspectors have until March 2027 to report on Tees Valley. Will the deadline hold?

The government ordered a formal inspection of Tees Valley Combined Authority on 15 October over debt and governance. Inspectors report by March 2027.

4 min read

What happened

The government confirmed a Best Value inspection of Tees Valley Combined Authority under the Local Government Act 1999, citing high debt, leadership and governance. Laura Shoaf CBE leads it, and inspectors report to ministers by March 2027.

A Best Value inspection is the government’s formal check on whether a local authority is run properly, under the Local Government Act 1999. Inspectors appointed by the minister examine the body and report back. The minister then decides whether to do nothing, give directions, or send in commissioners who take over some of its functions. On 15 October the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government ordered such an inspection of Tees Valley Combined Authority, which covers five councils in north-east England and is led by Conservative mayor Ben Houchen.

Laura Shoaf CBE will lead the inspection, with three assistant inspectors to be named shortly. The inspectors will report their findings to Ministers by March 2027, and ministers will carefully consider the report before determining any next steps. The inspection covers governance and decision-making, use of money and audit, and leadership capacity, but is not limited to these.

Local government minister Jim McMahon told the Commons he had material concerns about the Authority’s long-term financial sustainability, given its high levels of debt, increasing borrowing and interest costs, and declining revenue reserves. The authority’s external auditor has not signed off its accounts for 2023/24 and 2024/25 and is considering using its statutory powers. The authority has agreed a formal repayment plan for a loan to South Tees Development Corporation.

They will report their findings to Ministers by March 2027.

GOV.UK, 15 October 2026

This is the third step on a ladder. An independent review in January 2024 made 26 recommendations, and on 3 April 2025 ministers issued a non-statutory Best Value Notice, a warning that carries no powers. McMahon also promised the authority more powers under the government’s devolution plans, with details this Autumn, and said he would update the Commons once the inspection ends. It is the first such inspection of a combined authority led by a mayor; the ten earlier ones were councils and a fire authority.

Sources: GOV.UK (2), BBC, UK Parliament

The Pattern

We looked for cases where a minister personally appointed inspectors under section 10 of the Local Government Act 1999 to check whether a local authority was run properly, and asked whether the first reporting deadline stood. We found ten such inspections since 2014, all ordered by the same ministry. In six the first deadline stood, and in four the minister extended it, three of them twice. Eight of the ten ended with commissioners or envoys given power to act for the authority, and two are still running.

The Read

The deadline is the easier of the two tests. For six of the ten inspections no extension letter was ever published, and Bedford, the most recent, went from appointment to report in four months. But three of the four inspections this ministry opened since May 2024 ran past their first deadline twice, and West Midlands Fire and Rescue Authority, inspected since July 2025, has still not reported. McMahon set the March 2027 date himself, five and a half months out, and left the scope open with the words not limited to.

Commissioners by 31 March are a different question. The minister decides only after the report, and in the seven inspections where the report came first, the decision followed 22 to 78 days later. Only twice did a decision come within 167 days of the appointment: Thurrock in 2022, when a commissioner went in on the same day as the inspectors, and Northamptonshire in 2018. Every finished inspection ended with commissioners or envoys holding powers, so the open question is the date. Tees Valley is also the first mayoral combined authority to be inspected this way.
