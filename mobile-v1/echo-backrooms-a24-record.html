Horror31 May 2026

A 20-year-old broke A24's record. Now the legs get tested.

Kane Parsons was 16 when he uploaded a YouTube horror short from his bedroom. At 20, his feature adaptation of Backrooms opened to $81.4m and made him the youngest filmmaker ever to top the US box office.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

Backrooms stands at roughly $350m worldwide after six weekends, now A24's highest-grossing film, having overtaken Marty Supreme.

Box Office Mojo, 5 Jul 2026
2 · The event

What happened

Backrooms, adapted from Kane Parsons's viral YouTube series about a labyrinth of fluorescent-lit liminal spaces, opened to $81.4m domestic and $118m worldwide over the weekend of 29–31 May — more than triple A24's previous best opening, the $25.5m set by Civil War in 2024 (Deadline, 31 May 2026). The film cost roughly $10m to produce.

Parsons, now 20, became the youngest filmmaker ever to open a film at No 1 in the US, taking a record held since 2012 by Josh Trank, who was 27 when Chronicle debuted top with $22m (Variety, 30 May 2026). The cast — Chiwetel Ejiofor, Renate Reinsve and Mark Duplass — lent prestige weight to what began as a teenager's bedroom project.

By early July the film had climbed to about $350m worldwide — $190m domestic, $159m international — overtaking Marty Supreme to become A24's highest-grossing release ever and the second-biggest horror film of 2026 (Variety, Jun 2026). The weekly grosses are now in steep decay, which is where low-budget horror phenomena are always judged.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

Low-budget horror breakouts follow a brutal shape: enormous multiples on cost, then some of the steepest decay curves in the business. The peak makes the headlines; the tail decides the final number.

2024
Longlegs became Neon's biggest film to date on a sub-$10m budget, finishing at $126.9m worldwide — a huge multiple, a modest absolute ceiling.
2023
Five Nights at Freddy's, another internet-native property, opened to $80m domestic and finished at $297m worldwide despite a simultaneous streaming release.
2017
It opened to $123.4m and finished at $701.8m worldwide — still the ceiling for the genre.
2012
The Devil Inside opened to $33.7m on a $1m budget, then collapsed 76% in weekend two and finished at $101.8m.
1999
The Blair Witch Project, the original internet-fuelled horror hit, turned a $60,000 budget into $248.6m worldwide.
1 of 5
Of five comparable low-budget horror phenomena, only one — It — cleared $400m worldwide. Backrooms already sits at $350m, closer to the line than any of them got this fast, but horror decay curves are unforgiving. The base rate and the head start point in opposite directions.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case on the total: the middle band. Backrooms was adding single-digit millions per weekend by late June — it took $7.3m in the frame Toy Story 5 opened — and summer horror tails compress further once schools break up and tentpoles crowd screens. From $350m in early July, a decaying $4–6m weekly worldwide add lands the film in the $375m–$400m band by 10 August, short of the $400m line that only It has crossed among its comparators. The sequel market is closer to a coin flip that leans no on timing: A24 will almost certainly return to a property this profitable, but the studio has historically announced follow-ups on its own calendar, not the news cycle's.

The honest counter-case: this film keeps beating its comparators. It out-opened every low-budget horror film in history, held better than The Devil Inside-style flameouts, and has an international audience — 45% of its gross — that genre horror rarely reaches. A late platform push, an extended cut, or a strong Asian territory run could add $10–15m the decay model does not see. And on the sequel, Parsons's YouTube series has years of material and A24 has never had a franchise-scale asset before; the commercial logic for confirming quickly, while the film is still in cinemas, is stronger than the studio's arthouse habits suggest.
