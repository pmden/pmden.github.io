Tour de France05 Jul 2026

Vingegaard strikes first. Pogacar is six seconds closer.

Jonas Vingegaard took yellow when Visma-Lease a Bike won the opening team time trial in Barcelona, then defended it on stage 2 as Tadej Pogacar clawed back six seconds through UAE's one-two on Montjuic.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

Vingegaard narrowly defended yellow on Montjuic as Pogacar's late acceleration set up Isaac del Toro's stage win and clawed back six seconds.

ESPN, 5 Jul 2026
2 · The event

What happened

The 113th Tour de France opened on 4 July with a 19.6km team time trial around Barcelona, and Visma-Lease a Bike won it, putting Jonas Vingegaard into the first yellow jersey. Under the stage's format the general classification was set on each rider's individual time: Vingegaard led Filippo Ganna by 8 seconds and Tadej Pogacar by 12 (Olympics.com, 4 July 2026).

Stage 2, 168.5km from Tarragona back to Barcelona, finished on the climb to the Olympic Stadium on Montjuic. UAE Team Emirates-XRG controlled the finale and Pogacar launched the sprint, only to sit up in the closing metres and let teammate Isaac del Toro cross first — handing Del Toro a stage win on his Tour debut (ESPN, 5 July 2026). Pogacar took six bonus seconds out of Vingegaard, who finished fourth and held yellow by the narrowest of margins (ESPN, 5 July 2026).

The route runs 3,321km from Barcelona to Paris, finishing on 26 July, with the first rest day on 13 July after stage 9. Pogacar arrives as the four-time and defending champion; Vingegaard, twice a winner, has beaten him at this race before. The next three weeks decide whether the rivalry's score moves to 5-2 or 4-3.

3 · The pattern

The pattern

Pogacar and Vingegaard have finished first and second at the Tour in some order in every edition they have both completed since 2021. The head-to-head is not one-sided: it is a genuine 3-2 split across their five direct duels, with momentum swinging on team strength and third-week altitude.

2025
Pogacar beat Vingegaard to claim a fourth Tour title, his second in succession.
2024
Pogacar dominated with six stage wins, beating Vingegaard by 6:17 in Nice.
2023
Vingegaard broke the race open in the Combloux time trial and won by 7:29.
2022
Vingegaard cracked Pogacar on the Col du Granon and won his first Tour by 2:43.
2021
Pogacar won his second Tour by 5:20; Vingegaard, a late stand-in leader, announced himself with second place.
3 of 5
Pogacar has won 3 of the pair's 5 head-to-head Tours — a real split, not a trend. The honest reading: whoever leads into the final week has won all five, which makes the early bonus-second skirmishes in Barcelona more than theatre.

Five direct Tour duels since 2021: Pogacar 3, Vingegaard 2 — a genuine split, decided each time in the third week.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case leans Pogacar, and the market's 47% agrees. He is the defending and four-time champion, he has won two Tours in a row, and stage 2 showed UAE strong enough to control a finale and rich enough to gift stages. The six seconds he recovered on Montjuic matter less than how he recovered them: on a punchy uphill finish, Vingegaard could not follow the acceleration. In the five-year history of this rivalry, the rider with the sharper punch in week one has tended to own the mountains in week three.

The counter-case is written in 2022 and 2023. Vingegaard has twice let Pogacar take early time and bonus seconds, then dismantled him at altitude with the stronger third-week engine — by 2:43 and 7:29. He already holds yellow, Visma won the discipline that opened the race, and a 3-2 head-to-head split is the definition of an open contest. A 33% price on a two-time winner wearing the leader's jersey looks light against a coin that has landed his way twice in five throws.
