NATO03 Jul 2026

The declaration is pre-agreed. Trump lands anyway.

All 32 allies have signed off the Ankara summit text at ambassador level, including a €70bn military pledge for Ukraine. Leaders still have to endorse it on 8 July — with a US president who arrives to enforce spending promises, not to celebrate them.

4 min readEcho #116INTERNATIONAL
1 · Today

Trump arrives in Ankara to enforce the 5% spending promises NATO made him at The Hague, amid transatlantic rows over Greenland, Iran and US force posture in Europe.

Al Jazeera, 6 Jul 2026
2 · The event

What happened

NATO's 36th summit convenes in Ankara on 7–8 July, the alliance's second Turkish summit after Istanbul in 2004. The final declaration has already been negotiated by all 32 allies at ambassador level. According to the pre-agreed text, leaders will affirm an 'ironclad commitment' to collective defence under Article 5 and pledge €70bn in military equipment, assistance and training for Ukraine in 2026, with 'at least equivalent levels' in 2027 (NATO, Ankara Summit Declaration).

The declaration also states that 'Iran must never have a nuclear weapon' and counts contributions to Ukraine's defence industry toward the 5% of GDP spending goal agreed at The Hague in 2025. The text is settled — but leaders must still endorse it when the summit closes on 8 July (NATO, Ankara Summit Declaration).

The choreography has complications beyond Trump. The Czech Republic arrives with a constitutional stand-off: Prime Minister Andrej Babiš's government initially refused accreditation to President Petr Pavel, who took the dispute to the Constitutional Court and won a preliminary injunction ordering the government to accredit him. As of late June, both men claimed to lead the Czech delegation (Politico, 24 Jun 2026).

3 · The pattern

The pattern

The question is not whether NATO can draft a declaration — it is whether a pre-agreed text survives two days in a room with 32 leaders, one of whom has threatened to walk out before. The modern record is remarkably one-sided.

2025
The Hague: leaders endorsed the 5% of GDP by 2035 spending pledge in a summit deliberately trimmed to a single working session. Spain claimed a carve-out in advance, then signed the same text as everyone else.
2023
Vilnius: the Ukraine membership language was fought over until the eve of the summit, but the 90-paragraph communiqué was agreed and endorsed by all allies on day one.
2019
London: after Macron declared NATO 'brain dead' and Turkey threatened to block Baltic defence plans, leaders still issued the pre-agreed 9-paragraph London Declaration.
2018
Brussels: Trump forced an emergency session and reportedly threatened to 'go it alone' over spending. The declaration — agreed before the summit — stood unchanged, and allies stayed in.
9 of 9
9 of 9 NATO summit declarations since 2016 were endorsed as pre-agreed. An unbroken record — which is precisely why the residual doubt trades above zero: the one modern break in the genre came at the G7, not NATO, when Trump un-endorsed the Charlevoix communiqué by tweet in 2018.

Every pre-agreed NATO summit declaration since 2016 has been endorsed — through Trump's first term, 'brain dead', and Vilnius brinkmanship.

5 · The read

How to read it

The weighted case: this text is already agreed by every government in the room, including Washington's own ambassador. NATO learned its lesson at The Hague in 2025 — pre-cook everything, keep the summit short, give Trump a win to announce. The €70bn pledge counts toward the 5% goal, which lets European capitals present Ukraine aid as compliance rather than charity. Nine summits, nine endorsed declarations. The 91% call reflects a machine built specifically to produce this outcome.

The honest counter-case: the machine has never been stress-tested against a live war. Unlike 2018 or 2025, this summit happens amid open US-European conflict over Greenland, an ongoing US war with Iran, and a Congress in revolt over war powers. Trump un-signed a fully agreed G7 communiqué from his plane in 2018; a repeat costs him nothing domestically. And a leaders-level endorsement that quietly waters down 'at least equivalent levels in 2027' would resolve this market NO on a technicality the ambassadors did not anticipate.
