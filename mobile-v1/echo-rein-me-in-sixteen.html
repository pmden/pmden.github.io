Fifteen weeks at No 1. One more and the British record falls.

Sam Fender and Olivia Dean's 'Rein Me In' has now spent 15 weeks at the top of the Official Singles Chart, level with Wet Wet Wet's 'Love Is All Around' as the longest-running British No 1 ever. The next chart decides whether 1994 finally gives way.

4 min readEcho #116UK
1 · Today

'Rein Me In' logs a 15th week at No 1, equalling Wet Wet Wet's 'Love Is All Around' (1994) as the longest-running British No 1 single in chart history.

Official Charts Company, 3 Jul 2026
2 · The event

A slow burn becomes a siege

On the Official Singles Chart dated 3 July 2026, 'Rein Me In' by Sam Fender and Olivia Dean recorded its 15th non-consecutive week at No 1, drawing level with Wet Wet Wet's 'Love Is All Around' as the longest-running British No 1 single ever, per the Official Charts Company, 3 July 2026. The song also sits on 37 weeks in the Top 10 — the joint-longest run for any non-Christmas single, level with Harry Styles' 'As It Was' — and 54 consecutive weeks in the Top 40, matching Ed Sheeran's 'Thinking Out Loud'.

The route to the summit was itself a record. The duet spent 35 consecutive weeks in the Top 40 before first reaching No 1, the longest climb to the top in chart history, beating the 19-week ascent of 'Thinking Out Loud', as reported by the Official Charts Company. Along the way it passed Rihanna and Jay-Z's 'Umbrella' (10 weeks in 2007) to become the male–female duet with the most weeks at No 1.

The race now compresses into single Fridays. A 16th week would make 'Rein Me In' the longest-running British No 1 outright, leaving only two targets in all of chart history: Bryan Adams' 16 consecutive weeks from 1991 and Frankie Laine's all-time total of 18 from 1953, a chase Billboard, July 2026 is tracking week by week.

3 · The pattern

What happens after week 15

Only four singles in the 74-year history of the UK chart have reached 15 weeks at No 1. Their fates after the milestone diverge cleanly — two kept going, two were stopped at exactly 15, once by the market and once by the artists themselves.

2016
Drake's 'One Dance' reaches 15 weeks at No 1 — and is knocked off before a 16th, its streaming dominance finally broken at the exact record line.
1994
Wet Wet Wet's 'Love Is All Around' spends 15 weeks at No 1; the band delete the single while it is still on top, ending the run by choice.
1991
Bryan Adams' '(Everything I Do) I Do It for You' runs 16 consecutive weeks at No 1 — still the longest unbroken reign on the chart.
1953
Frankie Laine's 'I Believe' totals 18 weeks at No 1 across three separate spells — the all-time record no single has touched since.
2 of 4
Of the four singles to reach 15 weeks at No 1, two went on past the milestone and two never recorded a 16th week. The base rate is a genuine 50/50 split — though neither blocker (a band deleting its own single, a pre-streaming turnover) maps neatly onto 2026 streaming inertia.

Four singles reached week 15; exactly half of them ever saw week 16.

5 · The read

The Hunch read

The weighted case favours the record falling. Streaming-era No 1s decay slowly, and this one is still setting Top 10 longevity records in the same breath — a song does not sit 37 weeks in the Top 10 while losing momentum. Neither historical blocker applies: no artist deletes a single in 2026, and the July release schedule holds no obvious challenger of 'Cold Water' scale. At 61 per cent for week 16 by 17 July, the market is pricing inertia, and inertia has been the whole story of this record.

The honest counter-case: precisely because the run is historic, the exit can be abrupt. Drake's 'One Dance' looked equally unmovable in week 15 and never saw week 16. A single viral release, a surprise drop, or a coordinated fan campaign for a challenger resolves this NO within one chart cycle — and the multi market's near-even spread across 16, 17 and 18-plus is the market admitting it cannot see past the next two Fridays.
