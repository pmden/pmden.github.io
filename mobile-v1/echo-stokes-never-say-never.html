Stokes says never again. English cricket has heard that before.

Ben Stokes has ruled out a 2027 Ashes return and set his sights on coaching England. Ten months before the squad is named, the ECB is holding the door open.

4 min read

What happened

In his first interview since retiring, on a podcast with Stuart Broad and Jos Buttler, Stokes rules out a 2027 Ashes return and says his long-term ambition is to coach England.

(The Guardian, 4 Aug 2026)

Speaking to Stuart Broad and Jos Buttler on the For The Love Of Cricket podcast, his first interview since stepping away, Stokes ruled out playing in next summer's home Ashes (The Guardian, 4 August 2026). The 35-year-old said he is completing his level three coaching qualification while still playing for Durham, and that his ambition once he stops is to coach England.

His retirement in late June was abrupt. Stokes told the dressing room during the third Test against New Zealand at Trent Bridge that it would be his last match for England, ending a series in which he had been dropped for the second Test after breaching a team curfew, and which England lost 2-1 (Sky Sports, 31 July 2026). It followed a 4-1 Ashes defeat in Australia over the winter.

The ECB is not treating the matter as closed. Four days before the podcast aired, team director Rob Key said he would not be surprised if Stokes came back, that anything is possible with him, and that their relationship is undamaged (Sky Sports, 31 July 2026). England have meanwhile moved on structurally: Joe Root has been reappointed Test captain and Stephen Fleming installed as head coach.

The clock on this question is fixed. The 2027 Ashes opens at Trent Bridge on 18 June 2027, with five Tests running to 2 August (Sky Sports, July 2026). England typically name a Test squad roughly a week to ten days before a series, putting the announcement in early-to-mid June 2027.

The Pattern

England cricket has a long record of talismans who declared themselves finished. The clean cases since 1977 split one way when the dressing room came asking, and the other when it did not. Ian Botham is excluded: his celebrated comebacks were from a ban and from injury, never from a stated retirement.

1977
Geoffrey Boycott made himself unavailable for England in 1974 and stayed away for three years and 30 Tests. Recalled against Australia in 1977, he scored a century on his return at Trent Bridge and his hundredth first-class hundred at Headingley. Came back.
2015
Kevin Pietersen, sacked after the 2013-14 Ashes whitewash, was encouraged by the incoming ECB chairman to play his way back and scored an unbeaten 355 for Surrey. Within a day, director of cricket Andrew Strauss ruled out a recall over trust. Never returned.
2018
Alastair Cook retired with a farewell century at the Oval, then piled up county runs through four years of England opener crises. The recall talk was constant; the recall never came, and Cook never asked. Never returned.
2023
Moeen Ali, retired from Tests since 2021, answered a two-word message from captain Ben Stokes after Jack Leach's injury and un-retired for the entire 2023 Ashes. He retired again the day the series ended. Came back.
2023
Stokes himself retired from one-day internationals in July 2022, calling the schedule unsustainable. Thirteen months later he was in England's World Cup squad, and scored England's then-record ODI score that September. Came back.
3 of 5
Three of five departed talismans returned, but the split has a structure. Every return happened because the England setup initiated it: selectors recalled Boycott, Stokes messaged Moeen, the management courted Stokes back to ODIs. The two refusals held when the hierarchy had moved on (Pietersen) or the player stayed silent (Cook). Here the demand side is already live: Key is publicly holding the door open. The resistance is Stokes himself.

In 3 of 5 clean cases since 1977, the England talisman who walked away ended up back in the squad.

The Read

The 27% is higher than a plain reading of the podcast suggests, and the base rate is why. Stokes has now ruled out the Ashes twice since June, yet he is the man who un-retired from ODIs after calling the schedule unsustainable, and the man whose text message un-retired Moeen Ali. He is still playing professionally, still fit enough to matter, and the returns in this pattern were all initiated by the institution rather than the player. Key began that courtship in public on 31 July, and a home Ashes is the strongest possible pull.

The counter-case is that this departure looks more like Cook 2018 than Stokes 2022. The exit was bruising, the coaching plan is concrete and already in progress, and the regime has reorganised without him under Root and Fleming. A recall also now requires a new captain and a new coach to want the shadow of the old one in their first home Ashes. If Stokes repeats his refusal once the 2027 county season starts, or Fleming names a settled side through the early summer, the probability should fall fast.
